## Resumen

Osteosarcoma (OS) shows marked cellular heterogeneity, and patients often have very different clinical outcomes despite similar clinicopathological features. This variability remains difficult to explain using conventional risk factors alone. Here, we combined single-cell transcriptomic analysis, bulk transcriptomic profiling and machine-learning modeling to identify prognostic signatures related to tumor proliferation and the immune microenvironment. Single-cell RNA sequencing data from GSE152048 (n = 11) were used to map the cellular composition of OS. Within the osteoblastic compartment, we identified a proliferative subpopulation enriched for chromosome segregation, mitotic division and microtubule-related processes. We then used the TCGA-SARC cohort (n = 261) for model development and the GSE39058 osteosarcoma cohort (n = 41) for external validation. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis, differential expression analysis and univariate Cox regression were used to screen genes associated with immune features, proliferative activity and prognosis. These genes were defined as immune-proliferation-related signatures (IPRS). Among 117 machine-learning models, the random survival forest model performed best overall, with a 10-fold cross-validated C-index of 0.755 in the TCGA training cohort, an external validation C-index of 0.782 in GSE39058 and an average C-index of 0.769. The IPRS score separated patients into distinct prognostic groups and remained independently associated with overall survival in multivariate Cox analysis. It was also related to immune and stromal scores, immune-cell infiltration patterns and predicted drug sensitivity. We further selected ALG6 for experimental validation. Knockdown of ALG6 reduced proliferation and colony formation and increased apoptosis in 143B and U2OS cells. Overall, these results support the prognostic relevance of IPRS in osteosarcoma and suggest that ALG6 warrants further mechanistic investigation.
