## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Early oral cancer detection improves survival, but diagnosing early, asymptomatic lesions remains challenging. Screening effectiveness depends heavily on examiner competency, highlighting the need for structured training and calibration. **OBJECTIVE:** This narrative review examines the evolution, current practices, and future directions of examiner training, calibration, and standardization in global oral cancer screening. **REVIEW FINDINGS:** Existing training programs vary widely in content, duration, and assessment. While structured approaches combining theoretical instruction, image-based learning, and supervised clinical training enhance diagnostic accuracy, published studies provide limited details on training protocols, calibration procedures, and competency benchmarks. Formal assessment of inter-examiner reliability, periodic recalibration, and standardized quality assurance are inconsistently reported. Models like OralDETECT demonstrate scalable, competency-based frameworks. Additionally, empowering community health workers through artificial intelligence, mobile health, and adjunctive diagnostic tools provides promising opportunities to expand screening capacity in resource-limited settings. **CONCLUSION:** Establishing an internationally standardized framework for examiner training, competency assessment, and continuous quality assurance is essential to improve screening reliability and reduce global oral cancer mortality. **CLINICAL RELEVANCE:** Structured training and calibration bridge critical diagnostic gaps among primary healthcare practitioners. Integrating these standardized protocols with digital health innovations enhances triage accuracy, overcomes geographic barriers, and optimizes risk-based screening models.
