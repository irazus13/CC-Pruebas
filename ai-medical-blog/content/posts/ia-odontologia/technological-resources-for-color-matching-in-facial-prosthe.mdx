## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** To identify technological approaches reported in the literature for color acquisition, pigment recipe prediction, and color 3D printing for color matching in facial prostheses. **SOURCES:** Searches were conducted in six databases (PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, LILACS, and ACM Digital Library) and in the grey literature (Google Scholar). **STUDY SELECTION:** After duplicate removal, titles and abstracts were screened, and potentially eligible studies were assessed by reading the full text. Studies were included if they reported technological approaches for color matching in facial prostheses. Book chapters, reviews, abstracts, letters to the editor, expert notes, and studies that did not address facial prostheses were excluded. **RESULTS:** Among the 50 included studies, preliminary investigations predominated. Several studies reported good performance of spectral devices under controlled experimental conditions, whereas non-spectral, contactless devices relied on camera-specific color profiles for color acquisition. Pigment prediction methods were reported to produce colors with differences approaching acceptability thresholds; however, these thresholds represent study-specific reference points and should not be interpreted as universal criteria for comparing performance across studies. The use of printer color profiles was associated with improved color-matching performance. However, these techniques remain at an early stage of development, and further improvements in the mechanical and optical properties of printed materials, as well as the assessment of their biocompatibility and compliance with applicable regulatory requirements, are needed. **CONCLUSIONS:** Advances in the technological developments in facial prosthesis coloration suggest a trend toward increasingly precise and individualized prosthetic rehabilitation. Further studies are needed to validate these technologies in clinical settings. **CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE:** This review addresses color-matching technologies across fully digital and hybrid workflows, from color acquisition to final prosthesis fabrication. It highlights emerging trends, methodological limitations, and barriers to clinical translation, including population-specific color variability, printer color gamut limitations, and concerns regarding the biocompatibility of materials.
