## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Precision oral health requires clinicians to consider patient-specific clinical, biological, behavioral, imaging, and preference sensitive data when making clinical decisions. In routine oral health care, however, these data are often fragmented across electronic dental records, imaging platforms, diagnostic tests, narrative notes, and patient-generated sources. Clinical decision support (CDS) systems provide a mechanism for organizing these data and translating them into timely, evidence-informed recommendations at the point of care. **TYPES OF STUDIES REVIEWED:** A narrative review was conducted of peer-reviewed literature relevant to precision dentistry, CDS, dental informatics, artificial intelligence, biomarkers and omics technologies, and patient-generated health data. Priority was given to dental studies, which were supplemented by broader medical CDS literature when dental-specific evidence was limited. **RESULTS:** CDS systems can function as digital infrastructure and generate clinical recommendations based on patient-specific data using evidence-based knowledge and predictive analytics. In dentistry, CDS applications range from rule-based reminders and electronic quality measures to artificial intelligence-assisted radiographic interpretation, risk prediction, biomarker screening, and emerging multimodal platforms. The clinical readiness of these technologies varies substantially, and their value depends on data quality, validation, work flow integration, usability, interoperability, privacy safeguards, and clinician oversight. **CONCLUSIONS AND PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS:** CDS systems can assist clinicians by offering evidence-based, personalized recommendations that synthesize pertinent and available information. These systems are crucial for practicing precision dentistry. Although efforts to enhance the adoption, usability, effectiveness, and acceptance of CDS systems continue, clinicians should always regard CDS systems as tools and retain full responsibility for their decisions and involve patients in the decision-making process.
