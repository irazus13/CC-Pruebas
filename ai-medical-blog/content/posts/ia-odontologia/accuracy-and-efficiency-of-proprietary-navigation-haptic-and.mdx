## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** This study aimed to compare the accuracy and efficiency of three specific proprietary computer-assisted implant surgery systems representing a dynamic navigation system (DNS), a haptic-feedback robotic system (HRS), and an optical guided robotic system (ORS). **METHODS:** A total of 480 dental implants were placed in 120 mandibular and maxillary models, representing both partially edentulous and completely edentulous conditions. The models were allocated to three proprietary systems representing DNS, HRS, and ORS. Implant platform, apex, and angle deviations were calculated and compared among the three groups. Surgical time was also recorded. A linear mixed-effects model was used to compare the accuracy and efficiency among these three implant surgery systems. **RESULTS:** Linear mixed-effects modeling revealed significant differences in performance. For all primary accuracy metrics, including angle, platform, and apex deviations, HRS and ORS demonstrated no statistically significant differences (p&#x2009;&gt;&#x2009;0.05); however, both systems demonstrated significantly lower deviations compared to DNS (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001). Specifically, deviations for HRS, ORS, and DNS were: (0.95&#xb0;&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.67&#xb0; vs. 0.85&#xb0;&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.50&#xb0; vs. 1.37&#xb0;&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.69&#xb0;) for angle (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001); (0.70&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.30&#x2009;mm vs. 0.65&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.36&#x2009;mm vs. 1.06&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.44&#x2009;mm) for platform (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001); and (0.76&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.32&#x2009;mm vs. 0.67&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.38&#x2009;mm vs. 1.04&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.46&#x2009;mm) for apex (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001), respectively. However, HRS exhibited the shortest pre-operative (97.7&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;48.2&#x2009;s) and placement times (221.0&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;27.0&#x2009;s), demonstrating higher efficiency compared to both DNS (269.8&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;41.7&#x2009;s; 312.7&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;49.3&#x2009;s) and ORS (249.1&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;56.9&#x2009;s; 258.0&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;47.8&#x2009;s) (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001). **CONCLUSIONS:** Within the limitations of this in&#xa0;vitro study, all three proprietary systems achieved clinically acceptable accuracy. The tested robotic systems showed lower deviations than the tested DNS, while the HRS demonstrated greater procedural efficiency. These findings are specific to the systems evaluated and should not be generalized to other systems within the same technological categories.
