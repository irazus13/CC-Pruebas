## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly being integrated into orthodontic diagnosis and treatment planning; however, its successful clinical implementation also depends on patients' responses to AI-related applications. This study aimed to describe and compare item-level patterns of self-reported familiarity, perceptions, attitudes, and trust toward AI among individuals seeking orthodontic treatment across Generations X, Y, and Z. **METHODS:** This cross-sectional questionnaire-based study included 117 individuals seeking orthodontic treatment in 2024. Participants were categorized as Generation X (1965-1980; n=36), Generation Y (1981-1996; n=44), or Generation Z (1997-2012; n=37). A study-specific 15-item questionnaire assessed AI-related familiarity, knowledge, attitudes, perceptions, and trust. Categorical data were summarized as frequencies and percentages. Generational differences were evaluated using Pearson's chi-square test or the Fisher-Freeman-Halton exact test, as appropriate. Cram&#xe9;r's V was calculated as a measure of effect size, and the Holm method was applied to adjust for multiple item-level comparisons. Statistical significance was set at an adjusted P&lt;0.05. **RESULTS:** Following standardized pre-questionnaire information, 91.5% of participants reported familiarity with AI. Affirmative responses were 91.7% in Generation X, 84.1% in Generation Y, and 100.0% in Generation Z (nominal P=0.026; Holm-adjusted P=0.286). For the item stating that AI may eventually become more valuable than human knowledge, affirmative responses were 27.8%, 40.9%, and 59.5%, respectively (nominal P=0.023; adjusted P=0.276). For trust in AI-assisted healthcare decisions, the corresponding proportions were 30.6%, 34.1%, and 56.8% (nominal P=0.043; adjusted P=0.430). None of the 13 item-level between-generation associations remained statistically significant after Holm adjustment for multiple comparisons. Thus, the nominal differences are reported as exploratory descriptive patterns only. **CONCLUSIONS:** Following standardized pre-questionnaire information, participants generally reported high self-reported familiarity with AI, but affirmative responses to individual items concerning AI-based decision-making were lower. No item-level between-generation association remained statistically significant after Holm adjustment for multiple comparisons; therefore, the observed unadjusted patterns should be interpreted as exploratory. Descriptively, affirmative responses were more frequent for the item stating that AI could assist the orthodontist than for items concerning AI making decisions, but the questionnaire design does not establish a preference for supportive over autonomous AI.
