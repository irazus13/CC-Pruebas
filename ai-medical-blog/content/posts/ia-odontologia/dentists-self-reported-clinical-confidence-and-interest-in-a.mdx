## Resumen

**BACKGROUND/AIM:** This study investigated dentists' self-reported treatment confidence and their interest in AI-supported assistance in the field of dental traumatology among dentists in Lower Saxony, Germany. **MATERIAL AND METHODS:** A total of 5619 dentists registered with the Lower Saxony Chamber of Dentists were invited via email to participate in an anonymous online survey. The questionnaire included sociodemographic characteristics, frequency of dental trauma management, self-reported treatment confidence, use of existing dental trauma applications, and interest in AI-supported assistance. Responses were recorded using dichotomous, single- or multiple-choice questions and five-point Likert scales. Data were analyzed descriptively and using multivariable linear and logistic regression analyses (&#x3b1;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.05). **RESULTS:** In total, 258 questionnaires (response rate: 4.6%) were included in the analysis. Overall, 41.1% of respondents reported managing traumatic dental injuries at least once per month. The mean self-reported treatment confidence was 3.6&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.8 on a five-point Likert scale (1&#x2009;=&#x2009;very uncertain, 5&#x2009;=&#x2009;very certain; composite score). Overall, 69.4% of respondents expressed an interest in AI-supported assistance. Higher self-reported treatment confidence was significantly associated with longer professional experience (&#x3b2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.01; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.011), male gender (&#x3b2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.24; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.012), a postgraduate qualification in endodontology or pediatric dentistry (&#x3b2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.31; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.019), working in a (university) hospital compared with private practice (&#x3b2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.42; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.019), and a higher frequency of primary management of traumatic dental injuries (&#x3b2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.15; p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001). Interest in AI-supported assistance was positively associated with the use of dental trauma applications (OR&#x2009;=&#x2009;1.98; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.044), whereas greater concerns regarding AI were associated with lower odds of expressing an interest in AI-supported assistance (OR&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.54; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.002). **CONCLUSIONS:** Respondents' self-reported treatment confidence varied across injury types, with the lowest confidence reported for avulsion injuries. Most respondents, particularly those already using dental trauma applications, expressed an interest in AI-supported assistance, with documentation support and treatment guidance being the most desired functions.
