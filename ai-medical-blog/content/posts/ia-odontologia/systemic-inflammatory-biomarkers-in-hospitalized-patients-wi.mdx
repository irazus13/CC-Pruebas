## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** To evaluate longitudinal changes in leukocyte count and CBC-derived inflammatory indices and their association with odontogenic infection severity in hospitalized patients. **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** This retrospective single-centre cohort study included hospitalized patients with odontogenic infections who had complete laboratory data available at hospital admission (T0) and post-treatment assessment (T1). Leukocyte count and the CBC-derived inflammatory indices were evaluated. Patients were classified according to Severity score. Statistical analyses included paired comparisons, Mann-Whitney analysis of &#x394; values (T0&#x2009;-&#x2009;T1), and Multivariable linear mixed-effects models. **RESULTS:** Eighty-two patients were included (43 mild and 39 severe infections). Leukocyte count, SIRI, AISI, and NLR decreased significantly between T0 and T1 in both groups. Regarding the comparison of median changes between groups, these were significantly greater in patients with severe infections for SII (P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.009), SIRI (P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.014), AISI (P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.016), NLR (P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.002), and leukocyte count (P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.003). Multivariable linear mixed-effects models demonstrated significant effects for time for all inflammatory markers except for PLR; significant effects of infection severity were observed for leukocyte count (6.95; P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.010), SII (4.16; P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.045), SIRI (6.64; P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.012)., and AISI (7.99; P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.006); and significant time &#xd7; severity interactions were observed for leukocyte count (9.27; P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.003), SII (5.71; P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.019), and NLR (6,03; P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.016). Neither treatment modality (P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.265) nor antibiotic regimen (P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.140) was significantly associated with infection severity. **CONCLUSION:** Leukocyte count, SII, and NLR showed the most consistent longitudinal changes during hospitalization and appear to be useful complementary inflammatory parameters for monitoring treatment response and clinical recovery in patients with odontogenic infections. SIRI and AISI also demonstrated significant longitudinal changes, although their performance was not consistent for time x severity. **CLINICAL RELEVANCE:** Routine complete blood count parameters and CBC-derived inflammatory indices may complement clinical assessment by monitoring the longitudinal inflammatory response to treatment in hospitalized patients with odontogenic infections.
