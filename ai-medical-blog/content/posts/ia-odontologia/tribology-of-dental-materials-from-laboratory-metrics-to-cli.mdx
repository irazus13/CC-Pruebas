## Resumen

The functional integrity of dental restorations is known by their resilience within the dynamic and hostile tribological ecosystem of the oral cavity. Despite significant advancements in material science, the premature failure of restorations due to wear remains a critical clinical challenge, rooted in a persistent disconnect between controlled laboratory metrics and unpredictable clinical realities. This review provides a comprehensive critical appraisal of dental tribology, delineating the complex interplay of wear mechanisms, attrition, abrasion, erosion, and abfraction, and evaluating the spectrum of current assessment methodologies, from fundamental in vitro simulations to advanced clinical quantification. We identified the primary limitations hindering accurate material prediction, specifically the lack of protocol standardization, the inability to replicate synergistic degradation processes such as tribo-corrosion, and the exclusion of patient-specific variables. To bridge this translational gap, we proposed a strategic roadmap integrating multi-tiered testing standardization, finite element analysis, and machine learning to enhance predictive capabilities. Furthermore, we advocated for the development of clinically relevant in vitro models that incorporate patient-specific parameters, such as occlusal loading profiles and salivary properties. By shifting the paradigm from descriptive testing to predictive, patient-centric material science, this review outlined a transformative pathway for the development of next-generation restorative materials, ultimately optimizing clinical longevity and treatment outcomes.
