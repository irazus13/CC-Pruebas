## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Large-scale analysis of qualitative clinical communication data typically requires substantial manual coding. Natural language processing (NLP) may reduce this burden by converting unstructured physician-patient communication transcripts into structured qualitative data. **OBJECTIVE:** This study aimed to develop, test, and validate an NLP application for identifying quality-of-life factors in cancer care communication. We also compared traditional machine learning (ML), rule-based, transformer-based, and out-of-the-box (OoB) large language model (LLM) approaches. **METHODS:** We analyzed 434 standardized patient encounter transcripts of physician-patient interactions collected from a prior study. A rule-based SpaCy model, traditional ML models, bidirectional encoder representations from transformer (BERT), Robustly Optimized BERT Pretraining Approach (RoBERTa), and the OoB Qwen3 LLM were evaluated using F1-scores. The highest-performing model was incorporated into a web-based NLP application and further assessed through manual validation. **RESULTS:** Transformer-based models demonstrated the strongest performance. RoBERTa achieved the highest initial F1-score of 83%, outperforming BERT at 80%, the OoB Qwen3 LLM model at 39%, and the rule-based SpaCy baseline at 34%. Manual validation of the RoBERTa model produced an F1-score of 91%. The comparatively low performance of Qwen3 indicated that OoB LLM use alone may not produce reliable results for qualitative, domain-specific clinical communication tasks. **CONCLUSIONS:** A fine-tuned transformer model (RoBERTa) was better suited to capturing the complexity of patient encounter transcripts than traditional ML, rule-based approaches, or an OoB LLM. The findings demonstrate the feasibility of using a manually validated, web-based NLP application trained on gold-standard annotations to transform large-scale physician-patient communication transcripts into structured qualitative data. Hybrid systems combining rule-based precision with the contextual capabilities of transformer models may further improve the identification of longer conversational sequences, including emotional expressions, question-answer exchanges, and multitopic utterances.
