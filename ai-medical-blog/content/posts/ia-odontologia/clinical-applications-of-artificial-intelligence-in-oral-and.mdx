## Resumen

The use of artificial intelligence (AI) has expanded rapidly across oral and maxillofacial surgery (OMFS), and yet the scope of its clinical applications and the methodological practices used to evaluate them remain incompletely characterized. To map the extent and nature of this evidence base, this scoping review, conducted in accordance with PRISMA-ScR, charted primary research on clinical AI applications across nine OMFS domains, identified in PubMed/MEDLINE and Scopus and published between January 2020 and August 14, 2026. Of 3760 unique records screened, 1496 studies were included. Annual output rose from 59 studies in 2020 to 407 in 2025, and 64.0% of the corpus was published from 2024 onward. Evidence was unevenly distributed: oral and maxillofacial pathology and oncology accounted for 623 studies (41.6%), more than the next three domains combined, while the four smallest domains together accounted for 203 (13.6%). Charted AI tasks were grouped into nine task families, the largest being detection and diagnostic classification (612 studies) and segmentation and landmarking (272); a single task-and-domain combination, detection within pathology and oncology, accounted for more studies than any other complete family. Retrospectively collected data predominated (1161 studies, 77.6%). A five-indicator Methodological Quality Profile, applied uniformly and reported as a descriptive record of what studies reported rather than as a risk-of-bias assessment, recorded external validation in 156 studies (10.4%), a prospective or randomized design in 63 (4.2%), and a direct AI-versus-clinician comparison in 246 (16.4%), with no directional change in any of these across the review period; 1081 studies (72.3%) recorded none of the three. Output in OMFS AI has grown rapidly without a corresponding change in evaluation practice.
