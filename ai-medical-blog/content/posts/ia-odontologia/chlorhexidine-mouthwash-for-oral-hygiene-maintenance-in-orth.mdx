## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Fixed orthodontic appliances create plaque-retentive environments that complicate oral hygiene maintenance. Chlorhexidine (CHX) mouthwash is widely used as an adjunctive antimicrobial agent, but its efficacy across different concentrations in orthodontic populations requires updated synthesis. Thus we conducted our review to fill this gap by assessing CHX mouthwash in orthodontic patients. **METHODS:** We searched for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) assessing CHX mouthwash in patients undergoing fixed orthodontic treatment in four databases to May 9, 2026. Outcomes included plaque index, gingival index, bleeding percentage, bleeding severity, and probing pocket depth. A random-effects model was used in R software. **RESULTS:** We ultimately had twenty-one RCTs comprising 904 patients that were included. For 0.2% CHX, significant reductions were observed for plaque index (mean difference&#x2009;-&#x2009;0.68, 95% CI&#x2009;-&#x2009;0.99 to -&#x2009;0.38) and gingival index (MD&#x2009;-&#x2009;0.69, 95% CI&#x2009;-&#x2009;0.96 to -&#x2009;0.42). For 0.12% CHX, reductions were smaller and with borderline significance for either outcome. Bleeding severity improved with CHX in both concentrations, while bleeding percentage and probing pocket depth showed no reliable benefit. Heterogeneity was substantial for most outcomes. **CONCLUSIONS:** CHX mouthwash, particularly at 0.2% concentration, provides short-term adjunctive benefits for plaque control and gingival inflammation in orthodontic patients. However, high heterogeneity and potential adverse effects support selective, time-limited use rather than routine prescription.
