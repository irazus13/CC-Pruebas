## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Peri-implant marginal bone loss (MBL) is a critical radiographic parameter for evaluating implant prognosis; however, its assessment on panoramic radiographs remains time-consuming and prone to observer variability. This study aimed to develop and internally validate a two-stage deep learning framework for implant localization and cross-sectional radiographic classification of implant-level peri-implant bone-loss severity on panoramic radiographs. **METHODS:** In this retrospective single-center study, 184 annotated panoramic radiographs, each obtained from a unique patient, were analyzed. In Stage 1, implants were localized using a ResNet-34-based U-Net segmentation model trained on full panoramic images. In Stage 2, implant-centered regions of interest were generated by combining implant bounding boxes with associated bone-loss annotations and applying a 25% contextual margin to preserve the surrounding crestal bone region. The resulting regions were classified as Normal, Mild, or Severe using a DenseNet-169 model. The initial train/validation/test split was performed at the panoramic image level before region extraction, ensuring that all implant-level samples from the same patient remained within the same subset. Stage-2 performance was primarily evaluated using five-fold GroupKFold cross-validation with the panoramic image/patient as the grouping unit. Bootstrap resampling was used to estimate 95% confidence intervals. Calibration analysis, ablation experiments, and Grad-CAM visualization were also performed. Performance was assessed using Dice coefficient, intersection over union, accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro-F1 score, and multiclass ROC-AUC. **RESULTS:** The Stage-1 segmentation model achieved an implant-class Dice score of 0.9368 and an IoU of 0.8811. In five-fold GroupKFold validation, the Stage-2 classifier achieved an accuracy of 0.848&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.035, balanced accuracy of 0.817&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.050, macro-F1 score of 0.806&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.040, and one-vs-rest ROC-AUC of 0.935&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.021. Bootstrap analysis of pooled out-of-fold predictions yielded an accuracy of 0.849 (95% CI: 0.822-0.874), balanced accuracy of 0.811 (95% CI: 0.780-0.841), macro-F1 score of 0.804 (95% CI: 0.773-0.834), and ROC-AUC of 0.934 (95% CI: 0.920-0.948). The highest class-wise performance was observed for the Normal category, whereas the Mild category showed the greatest classification difficulty. Most misclassifications occurred between adjacent severity categories. **CONCLUSIONS:** The proposed framework showed promising internal validation performance for implant localization and radiographic bone-loss severity classification. However, the model should be interpreted as a preliminary radiographic decision-support tool rather than a standalone diagnostic system. External validation, observer reliability assessment, and integration of clinical parameters are required before clinical implementation.
