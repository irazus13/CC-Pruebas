## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Previous research shows that nonsyndromic permanent tooth agenesis is linked with other craniofacial alterations. This study evaluated the ability of machine learning (ML) algorithms to predict nonsyndromic permanent tooth agenesis (excluding third molars) based on two-dimensional frontal facial photographic measurements. **METHODS:** A cross-sectional observational study included a sex- and age-balanced sample of 524 participants: 175 individuals with tooth agenesis and 349 controls. Frontal facial images were obtained with a standardized dental photographic protocol. Seven linear facial measurements were extracted from images using ImageJ software, with calibration based on interpupillary distance. Six ML models were used: logistic regression, decision trees, random forests, artificial neural networks, AdaBoost, and k-nearest neighbors. Model performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), precision, sensitivity, specificity, and Matthews correlation coefficient. **RESULTS:** Among the evaluated models, the artificial neural network showed the best performance (AUC&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.784; accuracy&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.736), followed by the logistic regression model (AUC&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.774; accuracy&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.725). Predictive importance analysis identified zygion-zygion, exocanthion-exocanthion, and endocanthion-endocanthion as the most predictive variables. **CONCLUSIONS:** ML models based on facial measurements from standardized two-dimensional frontal photographs showed moderate discriminative ability for tooth agenesis. These models are not intended to replace clinical or radiographic examination but may be explored as adjunctive tools for preliminary risk stratification. External validation is required before clinical application.
