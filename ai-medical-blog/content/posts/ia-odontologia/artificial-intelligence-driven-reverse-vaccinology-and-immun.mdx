## Resumen

Artificial intelligence (AI) has driven advances in computational biology, enabling the development of recombinant protein-based vaccines for bacterial pathogens in veterinary medicine. These computational tools enable high-throughput analysis of genomic and proteomic data by identifying conserved regions, surface-exposed protein folding structures, epitopes, and immunogenic antigens with cross-protective potential. This systematic review explores recent advances in AI in vaccine design by identifying protective bacterial antigens, assessing antigen variability, determining conserved antigenic regions, and integrating reverse vaccinology in livestock. This review highlights recent in silico strategies and tools and their transformative impact on next-generation animal vaccine technology for controlling emerging and re-emerging infectious diseases.
