## Resumen

Objective: Based on the analysis of global multicenter metagenomic species-level genome bin (SGB), this study aims to identify core cariogenic SGBs across all age groups and provide a basis for the precise diagnosis and age-stratified prevention and treatment of dental caries. Methods: Eight published studies available in the National Center for Biotechnology Information database before August 2024 were included, with a total of 496 oral metagenomic samples enrolled. Samples were stratified into saliva and dental plaque groups across three age segments (&lt;6, 6 to&lt;13, and&#x2265;13 years). Taxonomic profiling, core microbiota identification, and differential analysis of strains were conducted. Results: Analysis at the SGB level revealed that 652 SGBs were detected in plaque samples and 931 SGBs in saliva samples. Specific core SGB clusters were identified in each age group (&lt;6, 6 to&lt;13, and&#x2265;13 years), with 13 SGBs consistently detected across all groups (detection rate&gt;50%). Differential analysis indicated that caries was accompanied by marked ecological dysbiosis. These differential patterns were highly age-and sample-specific. Saliva samples in the group aged&lt;6 years identified the most numerous differential SGBs (227). In plaque samples of aged 6 to&lt;13 years, the caries group was characterized by Streptococcus enrichment versus Capnocytophaga in the healthy group. Notably, in the&#x2265;13-year-old group, the caries-associated microbiome shifted to anaerobic Prevotella species alongside Streptococcus. Heatmap analysis confirmed clear ecological turnovers across all ages, with the sharpest demarcation found in the youngest age group. Conclusions: Analysis at the SGB level unveils microbial dysbiosis patterns with greater precision than traditional species-level profiling, providing a novel theoretical basis for the precision diagnosis and age-specific management of dental caries.
