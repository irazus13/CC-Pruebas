## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Oral cancer represents a significant global health burden, with increasing incidence rates particularly in developing nations. The advent of Artificial intelligence and Machine learning in medical imaging has created unprecedented demand for high-quality, annotated oral cancer imaging datasets. Open-source repositories have emerged as critical resources for advancing diagnostic accuracy, treatment planning, and prognostic modelling. This review appraises the current landscape of open-source oral cancer imaging repositories, analyses the ethical frameworks governing dataset creation and distribution, and proposes future directions for responsible data sharing in oral oncology. **METHODOLOGY:** A comprehensive literature search was conducted across PubMed, Google Scholar, and several medical imaging databases. We evaluated the available open-source datasets, their characteristics, ethical governance structures, technical specifications, strength, limitations and future scope. **RESULTS:** Various open-source repositories such as ORCHID (Oral Cancer Histology Image Database), AIKosh, Kaggle Oral Cancer Dataset, The Cancer Imaging Archive etc., exists for oral cancer imaging. However, significant gaps remain in dataset diversity, annotation quality, and ethical oversight. Key challenges include patient privacy protection, informed consent ambiguities, algorithmic bias from unrepresentative datasets, and inadequate data governance frameworks. **CONCLUSIONS:** The development of ethical, comprehensive open-source oral cancer imaging repositories requires multi-stakeholder collaboration, robust regulatory frameworks, and commitment to principles of fairness, transparency, and patient autonomy. Future directions must prioritize diverse representation, federated learning approaches, and standardized ethical guidelines.
