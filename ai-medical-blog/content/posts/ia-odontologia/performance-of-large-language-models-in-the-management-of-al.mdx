## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** This study comparatively evaluated the clinical decision support capacity of four large language models (LLMs) in dental trauma management, assessing the International Association of Dental Traumatology (IADT) guideline adherence as the primary outcome and communicative quality and readability as secondary outcomes. **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** Thirty-six clinical scenarios covering all trauma types in primary and permanent dentition were developed based on IADT 2024 guidelines and submitted to four LLMs using a standardized prompt. IADT guideline adherence was evaluated with the Dental Trauma Clinical Reliability and Guideline Adherence Index (DeTra-GAI), a 20-item rubric covering five clinical domains (maximum score: 40). Communicative quality was assessed using DISCERN, EQIP, and GQS scales, and readability was evaluated using FRE, FKGL, GFI, and SMOG indices. Two independent experts performed blinded scoring. Statistical analyses included Kruskal-Wallis tests, two-way ART ANOVA, and Spearman correlations. **RESULTS:** Significant performance differences were observed among models (p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.026). ChatGPT-5.1 achieved the highest IADT Compliance Score (39.67&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.99), followed by Gemini 2.5 Pro (39.00&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;2.48), Claude 4.5 (38.69&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;1.98), and Grok 4.1 (35.22&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;9.17). Grok 4.1 showed significantly lower performance in Emergency Management and Patient Safety domains (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001) but scored higher in DISCERN and EQIP transparency dimensions. ChatGPT-5.1 achieved the highest GQS (4.14&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.87) while producing the least readable content (FRE: 23.67); Claude 4.5 demonstrated relatively better readability (SMOG: 7.7&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.39). Trauma type significantly affected all metrics (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001), and dentition stage influenced GQS scores (p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.003). **CONCLUSIONS:** Although LLMs demonstrate substantial alignment with IADT guidelines, communicative clarity, source transparency, and readability vary significantly across models. The primary limitation lies not in clinical accuracy but in insufficient clarity for emergency workflows. Findings should be interpreted within the constraints of text-based scenarios, and clinical decisions must remain under professional oversight.
