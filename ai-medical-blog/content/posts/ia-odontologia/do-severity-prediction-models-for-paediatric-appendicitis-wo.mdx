## Resumen

Background/Objectives: Models predicting complicated appendicitis in children could support selection for non-operative management, but are rarely validated in a second hospital. We examined how severity models transport between hospitals, why they fail and what local recalibration can achieve. Methods: Using two open paediatric cohorts, we developed models in Regensburg (164 operated children with histology-verified appendicitis, 50 complicated) and validated them in D&#xfc;sseldorf (229 operated children, 49 complicated), and vice versa. Laboratory, clinical and ultrasound models, random forest, XGBoost and two clinical scores were compared. We examined heterogeneity in case mix, predictor measurement, documentation and outcome definition, and local updating with 25-150 patients. Results: Internal AUCs of 0.81-0.82 fell to 0.71-0.74 externally; calibration slopes were 0.52-0.70 and risks were overestimated (observed/expected 0.61-0.85), whereas the reverse direction underestimated risk (1.40-1.62). The hospitals differed in age range, referral and management pathways, documentation practices and the histological definition of complicated appendicitis. A larger same-hospital development sample did not improve calibration. Recalibration with 50 local patients (11 events) brought the median calibration slope close to 1, but estimates were imprecise; precise estimation would require several hundred events. Including non-operatively managed children without histology inflated internal AUC from 0.82 to 0.88. Conclusions: In operated children, discrimination was partly preserved between hospitals, but calibration was not. Because outcome definitions, patient selection and documentation differed, poor transportability cannot be attributed to the models alone; the comparability of clinical data and outcomes should be established before models are transferred or recalibrated.
