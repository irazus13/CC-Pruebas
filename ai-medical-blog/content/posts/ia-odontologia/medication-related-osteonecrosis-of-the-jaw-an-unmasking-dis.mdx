## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ) is a rare but serious adverse drug reaction mainly associated with antiresorptive agents. High reporting frequency in spontaneous reporting databases for bisphosphonates and denosumab may mask potential signals for other drugs. **OBJECTIVE:** The aim of the study was to describe reporting patterns and characteristics of MRONJ reports and to identify potential new safety signals using an unmasking disproportionality approach in VigiBase, the WHO global pharmacovigilance database. **METHODS:** We conducted a retrospective disproportionality analysis of individual case safety reports (ICSRs) reporting osteonecrosis of the jaw up to May 22, 2025. Analysis was performed to describe demographic and clinical characteristics. Disproportionality analysis using reporting odds ratios (RORs) (95% confidence intervals) identified potential signals of disproportionate reporting (SDRs), according to a predefined statistical threshold. To reduce masking, analyses were repeated after excluding reports involving bisphosphonates or denosumab. Signals were prioritized based on regulatory labeling and absence of concomitant antiresorptive therapy. **RESULTS:** Among 32,570,624 ICSRs, 26,137 (0.08%) reported MRONJ, predominantly in females (65.0%) and patients aged &#x2265;&#xa0;45 years (71.9%). Most cases were serious (93.2%) and commonly associated with oncologic or bone-related indications. After excluding bisphosphonates and denosumab, 1902 MRONJ reports remained. Unmasked analysis identified 82 SDRs (vs 34 before exclusion), with 43 prioritized signals largely involving antineoplastic and immunomodulating agents. Several drugs not currently labeled for MRONJ showed significant RORs independent of antiresorptive exposure. **CONCLUSIONS:** Beyond confirming established associations with bisphosphonates and denosumab, this unmasking pharmacovigilance approach identified potential new MRONJ safety signals. These findings are hypothesis-generating and warrant further validation through pharmacoepidemiologic studies.
