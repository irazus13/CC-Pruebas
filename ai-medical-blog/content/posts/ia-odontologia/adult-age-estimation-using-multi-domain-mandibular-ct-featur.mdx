## Resumen

Adult age estimation remains difficult because post-maturational skeletal changes are gradual and heterogeneous. We evaluated multi-domain mandibular features extracted from postmortem computed tomography (PMCT) of the head and quantified overall, age-band-specific, sex-specific, and scanner-specific error patterns. This single-institution retrospective study included 1723 individuals aged 20-100&#xa0;years, with one examination per individual. The mandible and teeth-related structures were segmented with TotalSegmentator version 2.12.0; mandibular pose was standardized; and dental metal- and tooth-affected regions were excluded. HU-related features, cortical-thickness proxies, apparent trabecular parameters, medullary-expansion and porosity proxies, and morphometric features were extracted. Candidate feature sets were selected within each outer training set by three-fold inner cross-validation, and HistGradientBoostingRegressor was evaluated by five-fold outer cross-validation. Predicted and chronological age correlated at r&#xa0;=&#xa0;0.628; MAE was 10.34&#xa0;years, RMSE 13.17&#xa0;years, bias -0.02&#xa0;years, and p90_abs 21.67&#xa0;years. MAE was 9.10&#xa0;years in females and 10.97&#xa0;years in males. Bias was +24.38&#xa0;years at ages 20-29 and&#xa0;-&#xa0;15.18&#xa0;years at ages 90-100. The multi-domain model reduced MAE by 1.04-5.22&#xa0;years relative to single-domain models. Coverage of age-band-specific p90_abs ranges was 0.806 when bands were assigned from predicted age, showing inadequate calibration for individual prediction intervals. Multi-domain mandibular analysis from head PMCT may support reproducible forensic screening when accompanied by explicit error profiles and expert assessment; external validation and calibrated intervals remain necessary.
