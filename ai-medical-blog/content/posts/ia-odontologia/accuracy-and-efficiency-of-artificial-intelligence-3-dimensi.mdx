## Resumen

**INTRODUCTION:** This study aimed to develop and validate an artificial intelligence (AI) framework for the automatic identification, segmentation, and quantitative measurement of impacted maxillary canines. Differences in time and accuracy for segmentation and in the linear and volumetric measurements were compared between AI and manual methods. **METHODS:** An AI model based on a mask region-based convolutional neural network (RCNN) with multihead attention (Mask RCNN-MA) was trained using cone-beam computed tomography images from 214 patients with impacted canines. The dataset was divided at the patient level into training (n = 150, 70%), validation (n = 21, 10%), and test (n = 43, 20%) subsets. Segmentation of the maxillary region of interest, canines, and lateral incisors was evaluated using Dice similarity coefficients, sensitivity, specificity, and Hausdorff distance. Linear measurements relative to the occlusal plane and midsagittal plane, volumetric measurements, and canine crown proximity to the lateral incisor root were assessed. **RESULTS:** AI segmentation required significantly less time than manual segmentation. This model showed good agreement with human reference standards for the metrics including Dice similarity coefficients, sensitivity, specificity, precision, accuracy, and Hausdorff distance. Linear measurements revealed no significant differences between methods, whereas volumetric measurements yielded good agreement with intraclass correlation coefficients above 0.90. **CONCLUSIONS:** The proposed Mask RCNN-MA framework provides an acceptable and reproducible method for cone-beam computed tomography segmentation and for linear, volumetric, and proximity measurements. This AI approach enables standardized 3-dimensional evaluation of impacted canines and may serve as a practical adjunct to orthodontic diagnosis and treatment planning.
