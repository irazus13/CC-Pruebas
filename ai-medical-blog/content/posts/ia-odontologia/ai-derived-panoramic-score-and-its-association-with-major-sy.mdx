## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** This study evaluated whether an AI-generated, panoramic radiograph-based score was associated with five selected systemic diseases. **METHODS:** 26,550 panoramic radiographs from two hospitals were analyzed using an AI model that detected teeth and implants and quantified alveolar bone loss. A radiograph-based score (0-100) was derived from missing teeth and weighted bone-loss severity. Associations with diabetes mellitus, hypertension, osteoporosis, ischemic heart disease, and hyperlipidemia were assessed using between-group comparisons, prevalence trends across score bins, and logistic regression adjusted for age, sex, and body mass index. Incremental discrimination over covariates alone was quantified using &#x394;AUC and the DeLong test. **RESULTS:** Patients with each condition had lower scores than controls (all P&lt;0.001), with the largest adjusted difference for diabetes mellitus (&#x3b2;=-6.25). Prevalence of all five conditions showed a decreasing trend across ascending score strata (Cochran-Armitage P&lt;0.001), with the highest Q1-to-Q5 relative risk for ischemic heart disease (4.73). After adjustment, the score remained independently associated with all diseases (AUC, 0.74-0.80), with significant incremental discrimination for diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia (&#x394;AUC =0.0015-0.0154; DeLong P&lt;0.0188) but not ischemic heart disease or osteoporosis. **CONCLUSIONS:** An AI-derived panoramic radiograph-based score was independently associated with five selected systemic diagnoses. Clinical significance An AI-derived score obtained from routinely acquired panoramic radiographs was independently associated with major systemic diseases. This automated, quantitative score may serve as an exploratory image-based index, supporting integrated management between dental and medical care.
