## Resumen

**BACKGROUND/OBJECTIVES:** Generative artificial intelligence is increasingly used by health professions students and by patients seeking health information before consultation. This creates two related educational challenges: supporting critical AI use during learning and preparing future clinicians to respond appropriately to hypothetical AI-informed patients. This study evaluated a teacher-designed seven-step ChatGPT-assisted framework for autonomic pharmacology learning and explored whether principles embedded in the framework were reflected in students' attitudes toward AI-informed patients. **METHODS:** In this matched-cohort educational study, 104 third-year dental students were included. Students who voluntarily participated in the seven-step ChatGPT-assisted framework (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;52) were individually matched with nonusers (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;52) by previous academic achievement and digital-tool familiarity. The primary outcome was performance on a three-item open-ended pharmacology quiz assessing mechanistic reasoning, scored independently by two blinded assessors. Framework participants also completed an eight-item learning-perception questionnaire and a ten-item questionnaire addressing hypothetical AI-informed patients. Exploratory Spearman correlations examined conceptually matched learning- and patient-facing statements. **RESULTS:** Framework participants achieved higher postlearning pharmacology scores than matched nonusers (1.77&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.30 vs. 1.45&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.47; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.0003). The largest difference was observed for questions requiring integration of receptor mechanisms and downstream signaling (+28%; p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001), whereas factual drug-name recall did not differ (p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.47). Students generally recognized both the potential usefulness and limitations of ChatGPT and endorsed professional, nondismissive responses to hypothetical AI-informed patients. The perceived usefulness of ChatGPT for learning and writing was associated with recognition of the risk of patient overreliance on AI-generated diagnoses (&#x3c1;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.32, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.019), while intention to use ChatGPT for learning was associated with endorsement of a serious and professional approach to patients presenting with ChatGPT-generated diagnoses (&#x3c1;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.40, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.004). **CONCLUSIONS:** Participation in the structured framework was associated with stronger postlearning mechanistic pharmacology performance. The novel cross-context analysis identified limited but potentially meaningful alignment between responsible AI use in learning and professional attitudes toward hypothetical AI-informed patients. These exploratory associations do not demonstrate transfer of clinical communication competence, but suggest that verification, contextualization, risk awareness, and human oversight may provide a shared educational foundation for addressing AI use by both learners and patients.
