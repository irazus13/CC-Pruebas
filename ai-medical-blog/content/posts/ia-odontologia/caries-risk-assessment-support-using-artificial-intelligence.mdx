## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** Although artificial intelligence has shown promise in detecting dental pathologies on radiographs, integrating these findings into patient-level disease profiles and caries risk stratification remains challenging. This study aimed to address this gap by combining automated radiographic analysis with fuzzy logic. **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** YOLOv11 based models were trained to automatically detect teeth and pathological findings (caries, restorations, missing teeth) in panoramic radiographs. The DMFT (Decayed, Missing, Filled Teeth) score calculated from the detections and a single anamnesis question regarding the patient's previous treatment history were incorporated into a Mamdani-type fuzzy inference system to generate a quantitative risk score and corresponding risk category (low, medium, or high). The developed model was evaluated using clinical panoramic radiographs from 200 patients. **RESULTS:** The YOLOv11l model achieved a recall of 0.936 in pathology detection. The resulting classifications showed agreement rates of 92.5% and 92.0% with the American Dental Association (ADA) and Caries Management by Risk Assessment (CAMBRA) protocols, respectively. Cohen's kappa values exceeded 0.87. These findings demonstrate the feasibility of integrating radiographic detections and patient information into a structured patient-level assessment. **CONCLUSIONS:** The combination of automated image analysis and fuzzy logic provides a clinical decision-support framework for caries risk stratification based on panoramic radiographs. This approach offers an alternative way to integrate radiographic findings into a structured risk assessment process. **CLINICAL RELEVANCE:** The proposed system integrates automated radiographic analysis and risk stratification into routine dental assessment without requiring additional procedures. It can support clinical decision-making and facilitate communication with patients regarding oral health.
