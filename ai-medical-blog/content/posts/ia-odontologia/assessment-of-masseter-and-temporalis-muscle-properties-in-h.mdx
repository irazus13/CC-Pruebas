## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** To examine the relationship between stiffness measurements obtained using shear wave elastography (SWE) and MyotonPRO (MPRO) within the same muscle and functional state, and to establish sample-specific baseline values for the masseter and temporalis muscles in healthy young adults aged 19-29 years under standardized resting and contraction conditions. **METHODS:** In this cross-sectional study, 100 healthy young adults aged 19-29 years were included. For analytical consistency, MPRO stiffness values were abbreviated as MR-S, MC-S, TR-S, and TC-S, representing the masseter at rest, masseter during contraction, temporalis at rest, and temporalis during contraction, respectively; the corresponding SWE measurements were denoted as MR-SWE, MC-SWE, TR-SWE, and TC-SWE. Holm correction was applied across the 120 correlation tests to account for multiple testing. **RESULTS:** No significant correlation was found between SWE- and MPRO-derived stiffness values for the masseter or temporalis muscles under resting conditions (MR-S vs. MR-SWE, Holm-adjusted p&#x2009;=&#x2009;1.000, r&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.065; TR-S vs. TR-SWE, Holm-adjusted p&#x2009;=&#x2009;1.000, r = -0.100). During contraction, a weak positive correlation was observed for the masseter muscle (MC-S vs. MC-SWE, Holm-adjusted p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.012, r&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.268), whereas the very weak correlation for the temporalis muscle was no longer statistically significant after correction (TC-S vs. TC-SWE, Holm-adjusted p&#x2009;=&#x2009;1.000, r&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.141). Standardized comparison using z-scores showed no significant differences between the two methods for either muscle at rest or during contraction (MR, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.872; TR, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.607; MC, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.608; TC, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.697). **CONCLUSIONS:** Although standardized z-score comparisons showed no significant group-level differences between SWE and MPRO, the absence of significant correlations at rest and the presence of a significant but weak correlation only for the masseter during contraction after Holm correction indicate limited individual-level correspondence between the two methods. These findings suggest that SWE and MPRO may provide complementary rather than directly interchangeable information on masticatory muscle properties.
