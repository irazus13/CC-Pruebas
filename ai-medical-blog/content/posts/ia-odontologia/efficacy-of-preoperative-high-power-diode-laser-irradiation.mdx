## Resumen

**INTRODUCTION:** Local anesthesia is fundamental in dental practice to ensure patient comfort and pain control during invasive procedures. This study evaluated whether preoperative high-power diode laser (HPDL) irradiation reduces the volume of local anesthetic required to achieve effective anesthesia during endodontic treatment of teeth with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIP) without compromising clinical efficacy or patient comfort. **METHODS:** Sixty patients (ASA I-II, aged 18-60 years) diagnosed with SIP in mandibular posterior teeth were randomly allocated into two groups (n = 30): Control Group (CG)- 1.8 mL 4% articaine with epinephrine, and Experimental Group (EG)- HPDL irradiation (810 nm; 1.2 W) at the puncture site prior to administering a volume of 0.9 mL. Primary outcomes were anesthetic success rate and onset latency. Pain intensity (VAS 0-10) was measured during needle insertion, coronal access, and instrumentation. **RESULTS:** In the CG, mean VAS scores were 4.27 at puncture, 2.74 during coronal access, and 1.56 during instrumentation. Anesthetic success was achieved in 76.7% of CG, with 23.3% requiring supplemental anesthesia. The EG demonstrated a 100% anesthetic success rate with 0% requiring supplemental reinforcement (P&lt; .05). Mean VAS scores in the EG were significantly lower: 2.33 at puncture, 1.50 during coronal access, and 0.20 during instrumentation. Profound anesthesia onset within 3 minutes was observed in 60% of EG compared to 40% in CG. **CONCLUSIONS:** Preoperative HPDL irradiation effectively halves the required local anesthetic volume while maintaining 100% clinical efficacy and superior patient comfort during endodontic treatment of mandibular teeth with SIP.
