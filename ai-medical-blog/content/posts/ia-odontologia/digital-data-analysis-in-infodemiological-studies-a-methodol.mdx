## Resumen

The digital transformation of recent decades has fundamentally reshaped how individuals seek, evaluate, and share health information, establishing the internet as a primary source of health knowledge for billions of users worldwide. Against this background, infodemiology has emerged as a scientific field dedicated to studying the distribution and determinants of health information in electronic media and its implications for public health, with the primary objective of identifying knowledge translation gaps between the best available evidence and public information needs to support evidence-informed public health policies and communication strategies. Hence, this methodological overview synthesizes the principal approaches currently employed in infodemiological studies, with particular attention to their application in oral health research, encompassing search engine analytics, network analysis, quality and readability assessment, content analysis, sentiment analysis, and agent-based modeling. Definition, indications, methods, methodological considerations, statistical analysis and data management, and considerations for the interpretation of results are presented for each approach, emphasizing the progressive integration of computational and artificial intelligence-based techniques. Rather than functioning as isolated methods, these approaches constitute complementary components of an integrated analytical framework grounded in transparency and reproducibility, providing researchers with the tools necessary to monitor online health information, characterize misinformation, and inform evidence-based health communication strategies.
