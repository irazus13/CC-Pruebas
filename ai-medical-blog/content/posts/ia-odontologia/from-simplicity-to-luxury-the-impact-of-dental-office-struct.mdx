## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** To evaluate whether the visual appearance of a dental clinic is associated with differences in laypeople's perceptions of the dentist and the dental care provided. **METHODS:** A cross-sectional, quantitative, and descriptive-analytical study was conducted with 120 participants aged between 18 and 65 years. Digital images of three dental offices - simple, intermediate, and luxurious were generated by artificial intelligence and evaluated through a questionnaire comprising six domains: trust, quality of care, hygiene and safety, furniture and equipment, technological updates, and recommendation. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, Friedman and Wilcoxon tests, chi-square, linear correlations, and effect sizes (Cram&#xe9;r's V and r). **RESULTS:** The simple dental office presented substantially less favorable evaluations than the intermediate and luxurious environments. Significant differences were observed across the three environments (Friedman, p &lt; 0.001), whereas direct comparisons between intermediate and luxurious environments showed no statistically significant differences after Bonferroni adjustment. This pattern indicates limited incremental differentiation between the two higher levels of environmental sophistication. **CONCLUSION:** The physical structure of the dental office is a strategic element of credibility, directly impacting patient perception. **CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE:** A well-designed dental office enhances patients' perceptions of trust, quality, and professional competence. An intermediate-standard clinic may provide similar perceptual benefits to a luxurious environment, suggesting that functionality and organization are more important than excessive luxury.
