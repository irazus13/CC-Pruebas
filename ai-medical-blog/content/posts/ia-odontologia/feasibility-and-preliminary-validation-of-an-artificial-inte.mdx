## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Dental anxiety is prevalent among children worldwide and represents a barrier to pediatric dental care, contributing to care avoidance, treatment difficulty, and poorer oral health outcomes. Venham Clinical Anxiety Scale (VCAS) and Visual Facial Anxiety Scale (VFAS), are limited by subjectivity and interobserver variability. This study evaluated an artificial intelligence (AI)-based facial emotion recognition (FER) application for assessing dental anxiety across procedural stages in children undergoing tooth extraction. **METHODS:** A total of 186 children aged 7-13 years who were undergoing dental extraction were enrolled. EmoDent, a SwiftUI iOS application using a convolutional neural network (CNN) model, captured facial expressions at four time points: pre-treatment, before local anesthesia, during extraction, and post-treatment. VCAS and VFAS scores were recorded. Spearman's rank correlation and Cohen's kappa were applied (P &lt; 0.05). **RESULTS:** Emotional responses varied significantly across treatment phases (P &lt; 0.001). Happy (20.4%) and neutral (58.1%) expressions predominated pre-treatment, shifting to anger (49.5%) and sadness (24.7%) during local anesthesia, with partial recovery post-treatment. Concordance with traditional clinical scales was strongest during peak-stress phases. Although extreme trait prevalence mathematically deflated conventional Cohen's kappa (0.146 and -0.010 during local anesthesia), alternative metrics showed high monitoring agreement, with Gwet's AC1 reaching 0.941 versus VCAS and 0.942 versus VFAS. **CONCLUSIONS:** The application demonstrated valid and reliable emotional assessment across all procedural stages and showed strong concordance with conventional scales. It offers a promising non-invasive adjunct, particularly for non-verbal children. Future studies should incorporate multimodal data and clinician feedback.
