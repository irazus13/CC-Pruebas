## Resumen

While anti-human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) drugs, including trastuzumab improve the prognosis of patients with HER2-positive cancers, acquired resistance by cancer cells remains a significant clinical challenge. We had previously reported that YES1 is overexpressed in trastuzumab-resistant breast and lung cancer cells, and that its inhibition restores trastuzumab sensitivity. However, no approved drug specifically targets YES1. In this study, as a drug repositioning approach aimed at rapid clinical application, we constructed a machine learning model capable of learning the interactions between compounds and target proteins and conducted a screening of approved drugs. The strategy identified chlorpromazine and raloxifene as hit compounds. In&#x2009;vitro validation demonstrated that they effectively inhibited YES1 activation at 15&#x2009;&#x3bc;M after 48&#x2009;h. The findings suggested that the identified approved drugs might represent promising therapeutic options for overcoming anti-HER2 drug resistance.
