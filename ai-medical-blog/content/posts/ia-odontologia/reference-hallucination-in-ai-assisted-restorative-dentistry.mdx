## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** Artificial intelligence (AI) chatbots are increasingly used by dental students and faculty for literature review, coursework preparation, and scholarly writing. In restorative dentistry education, where evidence-based decision-making is fundamental, inaccurate AI-generated citations may pose educational risks. This study evaluated the bibliographic reliability of AI-generated references in restorative dentistry topics and examined their potential implications for dental education. **METHODS:** Five large language model (LLM)-based chatbots were tested using ten standardised restorative dentistry prompts representing commonly assigned educational topics. Each chatbot generated 100 references (N&#x2009;=&#x2009;500). References were manually verified using indexed databases and evaluated using the Reference Hallucination Score (RHS; range 0-11). Chatbot performance was compared using the Friedman test, followed by Dunn-Bonferroni post hoc pairwise comparisons. Binary bibliographic error parameters were analysed using generalised linear mixed models, with chatbot type, prompt complexity, and their interaction as fixed effects and prompt as a random intercept (&#x3b1;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.05). **RESULTS:** A significant overall difference in RHS was observed among the evaluated AI chatbots (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001), with post hoc analyses identifying significant differences between specific model pairs. ChatGPT-5.2 and Perplexity AI demonstrated the lowest hallucination severity, whereas Copilot and DeepSeek-v3.2 demonstrated the highest. Prompt complexity did not significantly influence hallucination severity. Errors most frequently involved author names, journal titles, and DOIs. **CONCLUSIONS:** AI-generated references in restorative dentistry demonstrated substantial variability in bibliographic accuracy across the evaluated AI systems. These inaccuracies may pose potential risks to evidence-based learning and academic integrity, highlighting the importance of systematic reference verification and AI literacy in dental education.
