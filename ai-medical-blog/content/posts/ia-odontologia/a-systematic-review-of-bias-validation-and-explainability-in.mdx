## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** To critically evaluate the current role of artificial intelligence (AI) in dental diagnosis across multiple dental disciplines, assessing diagnostic performance, methodological quality, risk of bias, validation, explainability, and clinical applicability. **DATA AND SOURCES:** This systematic review was conducted in accordance with PRISMA guidelines and registered in PROSPERO (CRD42025639340). PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Embase, LILACS, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, Web of Science, and ClinicalTrials.gov were searched up to December-2025. Two reviewers independently performed study selection, data extraction, and risk-of-bias assessment. Risk of bias in diagnostic accuracy studies was assessed using QUADAS-2-tool, evaluating the domains of patient selection, index test, reference standard, flow and timing whilst randomised studies were assessed using Cochrane RoB 2-tool. Reporting quality and transparency of AI-model development and validation were assessed using the selected items from TRIPOD-AI reporting guideline. Methodological quality and risk of bias in AI model development and evaluation were additionally assessed using PROBAST+AI. **STUDY SELECTION:** Ninety-six studies met the inclusion criteria, of which 90.6% were retrospective. Convolutional neural networks (CNN) were the most frequently used architecture (82.3%). Sixteen studies failed to specify the CNN architecture used. Reported diagnostic accuracy ranged from 81% to 99% across dental specialties, with high performance in implantology (92-99%), orthodontics (88-99%), endodontics (87-99%). Panoramic radiographs and cone-beam computed tomography were the most used imaging modalities. QUADAS-2 analysis indicated that high-risk of bias in 55.2% of studies and most lacked external validation, whilst Assessment using selected TRIPOD+AI items identified important gaps in reporting completeness and transparency, particularly in sample-size justification, missing-data handling, protocol availability, study registration, and data or code availability. **CONCLUSIONS:** AI demonstrates substantial technical capability to enhance diagnostic accuracy and efficiency. However, the predominance of retrospective designs, limited external validation, and methodological weaknesses constrain the strength of current evidence. **CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE:** The use of AI is potentially considered as a decision-support tool rather than replacement for clinical judgement.
