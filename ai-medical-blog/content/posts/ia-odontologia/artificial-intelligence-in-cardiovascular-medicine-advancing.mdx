## Resumen

**INTRODUCTION:** One of the main causes of death in the world is Cardiovascular Diseases (CVDs). The number of acute myocardial infarctions and fatalities may be considerably decreased by early detection of Cardiovascular (CV) events. However, the efficacy of conventional data analysis for diagnosis and treatment is hampered by the complexity of the disease and the wide range of affecting factors. Large, multidimensional medical datasets can be processed by Artificial Intelligence (AI) owing to its sophisticated computational capabilities. **METHODS:** This narrative review was conducted through a structured search of PubMed/ MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar to identify peer-reviewed literature on artificial intelligence in cardiovascular medicine. Relevant studies addressing diagnosis, risk stratification, prognostic prediction, cardiovascular imaging, and clinical implementation were critically evaluated with emphasis on methodological quality, external validation, clinical applicability, and implementation challenges. **RESULTS:** AI-based systems have been shown to be more effective at classifying risk, predicting outcomes, and spotting illness trends. Early diagnosis and more customized treatment plans are made possible by these algorithms' ability to examine intricate relationships in large healthcare datasets. Clinical applications have improved auxiliary diagnostic instruments and increased patient care effectiveness. **DISCUSSION:** AI offers more accuracy and flexibility than traditional approaches when handling high-dimensional CVD data. It may reduce the burden of disease and promote tailored treatment regimens. However, data quality, standardization, and ethical concerns for broad use remain issues. **CONCLUSION:** The detection and treatment of CVDs are being revolutionized by AI-powered technology. Their use in clinical settings may improve cardiovascular care and lower the number of heart disease-related deaths worldwide.
