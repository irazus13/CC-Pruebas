## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Three-dimensional postoperative implant assessment is increasingly performed using intraoral scan (IOS) superimposition as a radiation-free alternative to cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT). However, it remains unclear to what extent IOS- and CBCT-based measurements agree and how reproducibility changes across posterior mandibular configurations. **METHODS:** In this paired in vitro method-comparison study, based on the experimental platform of a previous study, 60 implants placed at F&#xe9;d&#xe9;ration Dentaire Internationale (FDI) positions 35, 36, 45, 46 and 47 in bounded edentulous spaces (BES) and distal extension situations (DE) underwent three IOS-based and three CBCT-based postoperative assessments relative to the virtual plan using the Treatment Evaluation Tool of the planning software. Angular, mesiodistal, buccolingual, apico-coronal, and three-dimensional (3D) deviations at implant base and apex were recorded, resulting in 180 paired measurements. Agreement was assessed by Bland-Altman analysis, and within-method reproducibility by the within-implant standard deviation (Sw). IOS variability was additionally separated into within-scan and between-scan components. Non-parametric analyses were used for comparisons by indication and FDI position. **RESULTS:** Mean absolute IOS-CBCT differences were 0.73&#xb0; &#xb1; 0.55&#xb0; for angular deviation, 0.22&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.20&#xa0;mm for the 3D base offset and 0.26&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.22&#xa0;mm for the 3D apical offset. Bland-Altman bias (CBCT&#x2009;-&#x2009;IOS) was small (-&#x2009;0.35&#xb0;; -0.05&#xa0;mm; -0.07&#xa0;mm) with 95% limits of agreement of [-&#x2009;2.01&#xb0;; +1.31&#xb0;], [-&#x2009;0.62; +0.53] mm and [-&#x2009;0.73; +0.58] mm. Within-method reproducibility was superior for IOS in 8 of 9 parameters (e.g., Sw 0.20&#xb0; vs. 0.55&#xb0; for the angle), and IOS variability was dominated by scan-to-scan rather than within-scan re-evaluation. Discrepancies were larger in DE than in BES sites in 7 of 9 parameters (all p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001 for the principal apical and buccolingual components) and were larger at position 47 than at position 35 (3D base p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.004; 3D apex p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.011). **CONCLUSIONS:** In this in vitro setting, IOS- and CBCT-based implant assessment in the posterior mandible showed good agreement with small overall differences, and IOS demonstrated lower within-method variability than CBCT. IOS-based superimposition may therefore serve as a radiation-free complement to CBCT for the postoperative evaluation of single implants in bounded posterior sites. Agreement decreased in distal extension situations and at more distal positions; in these configurations, and whenever clinical decisions depend on sub-millimeter or sub-degree precision, CBCT remains the reference technique. Confirmation in vivo is required before routine clinical use.
