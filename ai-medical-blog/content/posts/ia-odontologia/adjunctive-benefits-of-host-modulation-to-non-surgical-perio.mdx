## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Adjunctive systemic host-modulation therapy (HMT) to non-surgical periodontal therapy has promising clinical benefit. This systematic review evaluated its effect in improving periodontal clinical outcomes versus controls. **METHODS:** A systematic electronic search was conducted in five databases up to April 2025 to identify eligible randomized clinical trials (RCTs) with &#x2265;3-month follow-up. The primary outcome was probing pocket depth (PPD). Secondary outcomes were clinical attachment level (CAL) and bleeding on probing (BoP). Random-effects meta-analysis, network meta-analysis, and mixed-effects linear regression models were employed. Risk of bias was evaluated using the Cochrane RoB 2.0 tool. GRADE was used to assess the certainty of evidence. **RESULTS:** Among 85 included RCTs, meta-analysis across 64 RCTs comprised systemically healthy and medically compromised patients. Significant PPD (low-moderate certainty) and CAL (low-high certainty) improvements were observed with systemic sub-antimicrobial-dose doxycycline (SDD) (PPD: -0.44&#xa0;mm [95% confidence interval {CI}: -0.48, -0.40], p&#xa0;&lt;&#xa0;0.0001; CAL: -0.73&#xa0;mm [95% CI: -1.04, -0.42], p&#xa0;&lt;&#xa0;0.0001) and omega-3 (PPD: -0.78&#xa0;mm [95% CI: -1.45, -0.11], p&#xa0;=&#xa0;0.0232; CAL: -1.06&#xa0;mm [95% CI: -1.89, -0.23], p&#xa0;=&#xa0;0.0124) when administered in healthy patients for &#x2265;6 months. However, non-significant changes were observed in diabetics. Network meta-analysis ranked SDD first in PPD reduction at 3-month (3.00&#xa0;mm [95% CI: 2.54, 3.47]) and 6-month (3.01&#xa0;mm [95% CI: 2.37, 3.66]) follow-ups. Omega-3+low-dose aspirin, probiotics, bisphosphonates, and dietary interventions were not supported by evidence. **CONCLUSIONS:** Although currently available evidence is inconsistent, SDD or omega-3 may complement NSPT when systemically administered for &#x2265;6 months in healthy patients but not in diabetics. **CLINICAL TRIAL REGISTRATION INFORMATION:** CRD420250652488. **PLAIN LANGUAGE SUMMARY:** Treating periodontitis with non-surgical periodontal therapy is effective, but some patients may benefit from additional treatments that help control the body's inflammatory response. This systematic review analyzed 85 randomized clinical trials to determine whether systemic host modulation therapies improve treatment outcomes. Among the available therapies, sub-antimicrobial-dose doxycycline and omega-3 fatty acids, when used for at least 6 months, provided additional improvements in pocket depth reduction and clinical attachment gain in systemically healthy patients. Similar benefits were not observed in patients with diabetes. Overall, the evidence suggests that these therapies may be useful adjuncts to conventional periodontal treatment in selected healthy patients, although further high-quality studies are needed to confirm their long-term effectiveness and identify which patients benefit the most.
