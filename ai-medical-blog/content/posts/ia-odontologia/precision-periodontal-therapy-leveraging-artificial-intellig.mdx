## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Precision medicine recognizes that patients with the same disease exhibit distinct phenotypes that may require different treatment approaches. Despite previous attempts at stratification, periodontal treatment largely follows a "one-size-fits-all" approach, with 30%-50% of patients failing to reach clinical endpoints. Artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) may present a timely opportunity to overcome these limitations. Because personalized treatment depends on accurate prediction of future outcomes, prognostic and causal modeling are essential prerequisites. **METHODS:** We reviewed AI and ML models for predicting individual periodontal treatment outcomes. Only true prognostic models following periodontal therapy were included. Data on prediction targets, input features, model architecture, prediction horizon, validation strategy, and performance were extracted. **RESULTS:** Only three studies have developed true prognostic models for individual treatment outcomes to date. This early evidence suggests that ML-based prognosis from baseline features is feasible at the site and patient levels, with prediction horizons of up to 1&#x2009;year after treatment. More recent studies demonstrate increasingly rigorous development and validation, including assessment of discrimination and calibration and external testing in independent populations. While some prognostic models already incorporate treatment features into their predictions, causal ML approaches capable of estimating individualized treatment effects remain absent. **CONCLUSION:** Although the evidence base remains small and heterogeneous, AI- and ML-based prognostic modeling provides a promising foundation for precision periodontal therapy by enabling individualized prediction of treatment outcomes. The next step is to move toward causal ML methods that estimate individualized treatment effects, enabling truly personalized periodontal treatment selection.
