## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** To evaluate general attitudes toward artificial intelligence (AI) among patients attending an oral and maxillofacial surgery clinic and to investigate factors associated with these attitudes. **METHODS:** A cross-sectional survey was conducted among 200 adult patients attending the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery at Hacettepe University Faculty of Dentistry. Participants completed a demographic questionnaire and the General Attitudes toward Artificial Intelligence Scale (GAAIS). Group comparisons were performed using non-parametric tests, and multivariable linear regression analysis was conducted to identify factors associated with GAAIS scores. **RESULTS:** The mean age of participants was 34.2 &#xb1; 11.8 years, and 52.5% were female. More than half of the participants (55.5%) reported a moderate level of self-perceived AI knowledge, and 78.0% were willing to accept AI-assisted dental treatment. The mean GAAIS score was 60.44 &#xb1; 4.01, indicating relatively favorable attitudes toward AI. Participants willing to accept AI-assisted treatment demonstrated significantly higher GAAIS scores than those unwilling in the univariable analysis (p&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.006). Willingness to accept AI-assisted treatment remained independently associated with higher GAAIS scores after multivariable adjustment (B&#x202f;=&#x202f;1.53, p&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.028), although the overall model explained a limited proportion of variance (adjusted R&#xb2;&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.020). No significant independent associations were observed for age, sex, educational level, previous AI use, or self-perceived AI knowledge. **CONCLUSIONS:** Patients attending an oral and maxillofacial surgery clinic demonstrated relatively favorable attitudes toward AI, and 78.0% were willing to accept AI-assisted dental treatment. Willingness to accept AI-assisted dental treatment was associated with more positive general attitudes toward AI in both univariable and multivariable analyses, although the multivariable model explained only a limited proportion of the variance in GAAIS scores. These findings highlight the importance of transparent patient communication and clinician oversight during the implementation of AI-assisted technologies in oral and maxillofacial surgery. However, the relatively high educational level of this single-center sample may limit the generalizability of these findings to broader dental and OMFS patient populations.
