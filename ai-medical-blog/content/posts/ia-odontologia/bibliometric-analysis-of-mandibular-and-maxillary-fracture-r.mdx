## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** The management of mandibular and maxillary fractures is undergoing a transition from conventional open reduction and internal fixation toward precision surgery driven by three-dimensional printing, computer-assisted navigation, and artificial intelligence. While traditional citation counts reflect past academic impact, they do not necessarily capture the current cutting edge. Bibliometric analysis has therefore gained attention as an objective approach to visualize paradigm shifts and emerging research frontiers. **PURPOSE:** This study aimed to perform a bibliometric analysis of original articles on mandibular and maxillary fractures published over the past 2 decades, in order to clarify citation accumulation, the evolution of research topics, major contributing countries, and emerging digital trends. **STUDY SELECTION:** A title-based search of the Scopus database was conducted on May 10, 2026, for English-language original articles published from 2005 to 2026. The query combined fracture-related terms ("mandibular," "maxillary," "condylar," "subcondylar," "TMJ," and "Le Fort") with treatment-related terms ("fixation," "osteosynthesis," "management," "surgical," and "treatment"). The exclusion criteria comprised animal experiments, in vitro studies, case reports, elective orthognathic procedures without trauma context, oncological reconstructions, and routine implantology studies. Of the 1,168 articles initially retrieved, 842 (72.1%) met the inclusion criteria. **RESULTS:** The mean annual growth rate of publications was 0.99% (simple annual average), with a marked acceleration after 2020. The total number of citations was 31,577 (mean: 12.94 per article). India, China, and the United States ranked highest among corresponding author countries (used as a proxy for national contribution), and the international coauthorship rate was 11.4%. From 2006 to 2012, dominant topics concerned fixation materials and open reduction techniques, whereas keywords such as "three-dimensional printing," "computed tomography," and "navigation" emerged rapidly after 2020, with 3D printing showing the highest citation impact among emerging digital keywords. **CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE:** This bibliometric analysis suggests that published research on mandibular and maxillary fracture management has increasingly focused on digitally assisted workflows in recent years. These findings indicate a shift in research attention rather than proven clinical superiority of newer technologies and may help clarify priorities for future research in oral and maxillofacial trauma.
