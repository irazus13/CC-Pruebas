## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** A proportion of Australians encounter substantial unmet oral health needs despite eligibility for government-subsidised care. Yet, government dental services operate within a fragmented, mixed public-private system with limited capacity&#xa0;to support priority populations. The National Oral Health Plan (2015-2024) responds to this visible and measured need for priority. As the next National Oral Health Plan is developed, these priorities provide a timely basis to consider whether emerging technologies can support access and system performance without reproducing existing inequities. **METHODS:** This narrative review aims to examine the potential uses, evidence for, challenges and implementation considerations of the use of Artificial Intelligence including machine learning, in Australian public-funded dental services, and to examine the implications for access, equity, governance, community co-design and workforce capability. The evidence is interpreted through a multidimensional framework for access to care, while retaining the National Oral Health Plan (2015-2024) as the policy context. **CONCLUSIONS:** Limited research conducted in Australian-based healthcare settings shows evidence for public dental system-level improvements for triage, risk stratification, service planning and workflow, but also identifies material concerns about opportunity cost, human oversight, non-representative data, algorithmic bias, cultural safety, Indigenous Data Sovereignty and clinician education. Artificial Intelligence and machine learning technologies may support more responsive public-funded dental services, but technical performance alone is not evidence of improved access or equitable care. Intentional, values-led implementation requires representative data, Indigenous-led governance, community co-design, transparent evaluation, human accountability and a digitally capable workforce.
