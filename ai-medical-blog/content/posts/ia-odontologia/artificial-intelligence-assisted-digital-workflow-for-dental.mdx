## Resumen

Digital implant dentistry combines the use of intraoral scanning, cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT), virtual planning, static guided surgery, and computer-aided design/computer-aided manufacturing (CAD/CAM). Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly being integrated into these platforms, but AI-generated outputs should be distinguished from deterministic software functions and clinician-controlled decisions. This methods article describes a step-by-step protocol for single-tooth and short-span partially edentulous cases, from data acquisition to definitive restoration. The workflow includes intraoral scanning, clinically indicated CBCT acquisition, surface-to-volume registration, AI-assisted anatomical segmentation with clinician verification, prosthetically driven implant planning, and surgical guide fabrication. Guided implant placement is followed by digital implant impression, AI-assisted crown proposal generation, clinician refinement, CAD/CAM fabrication, and delivery of the definitive restoration. The two cases are presented descriptively, with the first a single-implant maxillary restoration and the second consisting of a three-unit implant-supported fixed dental prosthesis (FDP) in the posterior mandible supported by two implants. Patient reported satisfaction scores were 9.2/10 and 8.8/10 for Cases 1 and 2 respectively. No records were available to define the metric for the positional-deviation values previously stated; thus, the values are not interpreted as entry-point, apical or depth deviations. The cases illustrate the clinical workflow but do not establish comparative accuracy, efficiency, or superiority; all suggestions made by the AI must be validated by the clinician and not replace clinical judgment.
