## Resumen

Robotic assistance can improve the geometric accuracy of dental implant placement, but device-specific post-market safety evidence remains sparse. We characterized publicly reported safety signals for the Yomi dental robotic system using the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Manufacturer and User Facility Device Experience (MAUDE) database. We conducted a retrospective descriptive analysis of MAUDE records in a fixed extract searched on 2 November 2026. Exact openFDA field queries used manufacturer name, Yomi brand, and product codes PLV and QRY; records were merged by MDR report key and screened by device fields. Structured FDA fields were summarized. Lin Wang and Chenglu Ruan independently reviewed all 92 narratives with a finalized dictionary; category-level raw agreement and Cohen's kappa were calculated. Exact-text and conservative cluster sensitivity analyses examined possible non-independence. No incidence or causal estimates were calculated. Ninety-two regulatory reports were included; 86 (93.5%) were classified as injuries, five (5.4%) as malfunctions, and one (1.1%) was not specified. Forty-seven reports (51.1%) had no affirmative patient-problem code. Across 1,012 paired ratings for the 11 principal categories, raw agreement was 100% and Cohen's kappa was 1.00 for every category. Accuracy, calibration, or positioning signals appeared in 57 reports (62.0%) and arm, handpiece, or intraoperative interaction signals in 30 (32.6%). Frequently described responses were implant revision or repositioning (37, 40.2%), freehand completion (30, 32.6%), and grafting or membrane placement (20, 21.7%). Collapsing one text-identical pair yielded 91 units; collapsing six conservative candidate clusters yielded 84 units, with accuracy/calibration/positioning remaining the most frequent signal (56/91 [61.5%] and 52/84 [61.9%], respectively). Manufacturer-received dates were present in 46 reports. Yomi-related MAUDE reports identify recurrent potential failure modes and recovery actions, particularly around plan-to-patient alignment and patient-interface management. They do not establish incidence, comparative safety, confirmed device malfunction, or causality. Proposed verification, stopping, conversion, and interface-release measures should be evaluated prospectively.
