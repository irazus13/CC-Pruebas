## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Candidemia is a rare, life-threatening bloodstream infection that is difficult to predict using conventional risk-stratification methods, often delaying empiric antifungal therapy. **METHODS:** We developed a deep-learning model (PyTorch_EHR) to predict 7-day candidemia risk with retrospective electronic health record data from two large cohorts (Houston Methodist Hospital System [HMHS], USA [6/2016 to 6/2023] and MIMIC-IV, version-3.1, Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Centre, USA [2008 to 2022]), including adult inpatients who underwent at least one blood culture. Model performance was compared with logistic regression (LR), LightGBM, and intensive care unit candidemia scores. We implemented a two-step prediction framework integrating candidemia and 30-day mortality risk models to inform empiric antifungal decision-making. **FINDINGS:** Among 213,404 and 107,507 patients in the HMHS and MIMIC-IV cohorts, candidemia occurred in fewer than 1% (851/213,404 [0.4%] and 634/107,507 [0.6%], respectively). PyTorch_EHR outperformed LR, LightGBM, and existing candidemia scores, particularly in terms of area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC) in HMHS and MIMIC-IV (all p &lt; 0.05). By integrating 30-day mortality risk, the two-step framework identified an additional 20 and 28 candidemia cases beyond the one-step model, increasing coverage to 60.8% [121/199] and 46.3% [68/147] in HMHS and MIMIC-IV, respectively. Many candidemia cases identified by the two-step framework did not receive empiric antifungal therapy and had high 30-day mortality (39.4% [41/104] in HMHS; 61.9% [39/63] in MIMIC-IV). **INTERPRETATION:** A two-step deep-learning framework integrating candidemia and mortality risk may support early identification of high-risk patients and facilitate timely empiric antifungal therapy. Prospective studies are warranted to confirm the findings. **FUNDING:** NIH/NIAID.
