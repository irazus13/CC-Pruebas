## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Artificial intelligence (AI), mainly AI-driven chatbots and large language models (LLMs) is reshaping dental education through adaptive, interactive, and simulation-based learning. While the transition in technology has been rapid, the evidence about the educational effectiveness of these technologies is still very diverse and not entirely directed. **AIM:** To analyze the roles, educational effectiveness, and impact of integrating AI-driven chatbots and LLMs in dental education at the university level with a focus on learning outcomes and implementation practices. **METHODS:** The researchers conducted a thorough search of the electronic databases PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, and Embase for articles published from January 2018 to November 2025. This review met the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 criteria and was made up of original research articles on dental studentsand educators who were using AI-based chatbots as interventions. The outcomes measured were knowledge gain, clinical reasoning, diagnostic accuracy, communication skills, and engagement of learners. Due to the differences in the methodologies used the authors opted for a narrative synthesis approach. **RESULTS:** Six studies met the eligibility criteria and showed potential benefits of AI-driven chatbots and LLMs for diagnostic reasoning, learning performance, engagement, and individualized feedback although methodological and outcome heterogeneity limited direct comparison. **CONCLUSION:** AI-driven chatbots and LLMs may complement university-level dental education, but their effectiveness and safe implementation require further rigorous evaluation.
