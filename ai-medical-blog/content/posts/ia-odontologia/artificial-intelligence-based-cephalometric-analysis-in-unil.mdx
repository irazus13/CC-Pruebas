## Resumen

**INTRODUCTION:** This study aimed to evaluate the agreement between 3 artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted automated and semiautomated cephalometric tracing programs and a clinical reference method (CRM) in the analysis of lateral cephalometric radiographs obtained from patients with unilateral and bilateral cleft lip and palate (CLP). **METHODS:** Lateral cephalometric radiographs obtained from 67 patients (43 unilateral and 24 bilateral CLP) were calibrated and analyzed using NemoCeph (version 24.0.0.3; Nemotec, Madrid, Spain), WebCeph (version 1.5.0; Assemblecircle, Gyeonggi-do, Korea), and AudaxCeph (version 6.7.10.4983; Audax d.o.o., Ljubljana, Slovenia) to obtain 22 measurements. The CRM was the mean of independent digital manual tracings performed by 2 orthodontists. Interobserver reliability and direct AI-CRM agreement were assessed using 2-way mixed-effects, absolute-agreement, single-measure intraclass correlation coefficients [ICC (A,1)], and Bland-Altman analyses. Cleft-type effects were examined secondarily using generalized estimating equations with adjustment for multiplicity. **RESULTS:** CRM interobserver reliability was good or excellent for 20 of 22 measurements; U1-PP and the nasolabial angle showed moderate reliability (ICC range, 0.559-0.984). Direct AI-CRM agreement varied across the measurements and platforms, and the platform with the highest ICC differed according to the measurement being evaluated. All platforms achieved good or excellent agreement for several skeletal and vertical measurements, whereas Nper-A showed moderate overall agreement, and SN-PP showed poor agreement across all platforms (ICC, 0.278-0.389). Maxillary-incisor measurements most frequently showed low agreement. Bland-Altman analyses revealed wide or magnitude-dependent limits of agreement for several measurements, indicating that small mean biases did not imply individual-level interchangeability. Of 22 cleft-type-by-platform interactions, 9 remained significant after false-discovery-rate adjustment (q &lt; 0.05). The direction and magnitude of these differences varied across the measurements and platforms, indicating no consistent group-related agreement pattern. **CONCLUSIONS:** The AI-CRM agreement varied across the measurements and platforms, with good agreement for most sagittal and vertical skeletal measurements, moderate overall agreement for Nper-A, and poor agreement for SN-PP across all the platforms. Lower agreement was observed for measurements involving CLP-affected dental structures and the nasolabial angle. For the nasolabial angle, substantial interobserver disagreement in the CRM limits the attribution of the discrepancy solely to AI performance. These programs may support cephalometric assessment in patients with CLP; however, verification by a clinician remains necessary.
