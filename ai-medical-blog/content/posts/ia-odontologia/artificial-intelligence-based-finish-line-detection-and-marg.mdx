## Resumen

**STATEMENT OF PROBLEM:** Artificial intelligence (AI) has been increasingly incorporated into fixed prosthodontic digital workflows, but evidence regarding finish-line detection, margin handling, and margin-related crown accuracy remains heterogeneous. **PURPOSE:** The purpose of this systematic review was to evaluate AI-based finish-line detection, margin-line extraction, and margin-related accuracy in tooth-supported fixed prosthodontic workflows using a functional meta-synthesis. **MATERIAL AND METHODS:** The PubMed, Scopus, and Embase databases were searched through May 2026. Studies evaluating AI, machine learning, or deep learning for finish-line detection, margin extraction, crown design, margin-aware crown generation, or margin-related accuracy were included. Findings were synthesized according to the functional role of AI, and risk of bias and applicability were assessed using an adapted quality framework. **RESULTS:** A total of 17 studies were included. AI demonstrated measurable performance for margin detection and supported crown-design workflows, frequently reducing design time. Marginal and internal adaptation were generally clinically acceptable or comparable with conventional workflows, although superiority was inconsistent. Quantitative pooling was precluded by substantial methodological and outcome heterogeneity. **CONCLUSIONS:** AI showed technical feasibility for margin-sensitive fixed prosthodontic workflows, but current evidence remains heterogeneous, predominantly early-stage, and insufficient to establish consistent clinical superiority.
