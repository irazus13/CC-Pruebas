## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** To map research on primary and secondary headaches, including its interface with dentistry, in PubMed records entered in 2000-2025. **METHODS:** PubMed records were retrieved with two strategies based on the International Classification of Headache Disorders and restricted to a validated thematic core; citations came from NIH iCite. Output, citation impact and flows, collaboration, co-citation and bursts of Medical Subject Headings were analyzed. **RESULTS:** Of 50,825 records, 40,441 formed the core (precision 92.0%; estimated sensitivity 99.2%). Annual output rose from 747 to 2,522 articles (modeled growth 4.0% annually), with a roughly stable MEDLINE share. The United States led production (29.8%) and China grew fastest. Migraine appeared in 68.7% of articles, secondary headaches in 12.2% and tension-type headache in 8.8%; the latter's share declined (odds ratio per decade 0.69; 95% confidence interval 0.65-0.72). Research shifted from triptans to calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) therapies, with bursts in CGRP antagonists, genetics and machine learning. The dental interface (temporomandibular disorders, bruxism, orofacial pain) appeared in 1.3% of articles; only 1.0% of all articles were in dental journals, which received one third of expected citations. **CONCLUSIONS:** Headache research grew with the biomedical literature and turned toward targeted therapies; tension-type headache is underrepresented given its prevalence, and the dental interface was small, with few articles in dental journals and limited uptake. **CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE:** Headache often coexists with temporomandibular disorders and sleep bruxism, yet few studies address dental aspects. The core journals and gaps identified can help dentists and researchers navigate the literature and identify research priorities.
