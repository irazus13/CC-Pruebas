## Resumen

The aim of this study is to evaluate the accuracy, reproducibility, and guideline adherence of eight large language models (LLMs), including recently published models, in answering questions derived from the European Society of Endodontology S3-level Clinical Practice Guidelines. Fifty questions (20 multiple-choice, 30 dichotomous) were prepared and pilot-tested at 3 different difficulty levels; the gold-standard answer key was developed by two endodontists (Kappa&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.867). ChatGPT-4o, ChatGPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek V3.2-Exp, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Claude Haiku 4.5, Copilot, and Grok 4 were queried using standardized prompts across three independent accounts and at three time points (days 1, 7, and 30). Inter-account and temporal agreement were analyzed using Fleiss' Kappa and Cochran's Q test, while Fisher's exact test examined question format and difficulty level. In cross-model comparisons, non-parametric analyses (Cochran's Q for majority responses and question-level aggregated Friedman tests) were used with the question as the unit of analysis to account for repeated measurements. Overall accuracy (pooled across all administrations) ranged from 86.7% (95% CI 77.8-94.7) to 97.3% (95% CI 94.2-99.6); most models demonstrated significant agreement across assessments and stable performance over time. Accuracy decreased as the strength of the recommendations decreased: 99.8% for strong recommendations and 84.7% for weak recommendations. Inter-model differences were more pronounced in response stability than in peak accuracy, and intra-model performance remained reproducible across accounts and time points. Question format and difficulty had minimal influence. LLMs can generate accurate and reproducible responses to structured guideline-derived questions. These findings suggest an instructional or informative role; clinical decision support needs to be tested using real patient scenarios.
