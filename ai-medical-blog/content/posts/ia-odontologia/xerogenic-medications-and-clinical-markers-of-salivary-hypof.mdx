## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** To determine whether xerogenic medication-class exposure and chair-side salivary markers contribute to risk stratification of objective salivary hypofunction across a broad clinical age spectrum, using conventional regression and explainable machine-learning approaches. **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** This retrospective cross-sectional study included 850 patients (mean age, 60.8&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;15.6 years; 657 [77.3%] female) who underwent salivary flow assessment and oral clinical evaluation at Kyung Hee University Dental Hospital. Patients were categorized into four age groups: &lt;40, 40-64, 65-79, and &#x2265;&#x2009;80 years. Unstimulated salivary flow rate (UFR), stimulated salivary flow rate (SFR), and flow-rate-defined hyposalivation were analyzed using age-group comparisons, multivariable regression models, incremental gradient boosting models, and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP)-based model interpretation. **RESULTS:** Mean UFR was significantly lower in older age groups, ranging from 0.57&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.30 mL/min in patients aged&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;40 years to 0.36&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.24 mL/min in those aged&#x2009;&#x2265;&#x2009;80 years (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001), whereas age-group differences in SFR were less pronounced. SFR-defined hyposalivation was most frequent in the &#x2265;&#x2009;80-year group (17.3%; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.007). In adjusted models, the &#x2265;&#x2009;80-year group had lower UFR than the &lt;&#x2009;40-year group (&#x3b2; = -0.194; 95% CI, -&#x2009;0.272 to -&#x2009;0.115; p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001) and higher odds of SFR-defined hyposalivation (OR&#x2009;=&#x2009;5.47; 95% CI, 1.44-20.78; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.013). Female sex was associated with lower UFR (&#x3b2; = -0.055; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.009) and SFR (&#x3b2; = -0.369; p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001). High-confidence xerogenic medication-class count was independently associated with SFR-defined hyposalivation (OR&#x2009;=&#x2009;1.63; 95% CI, 1.12-2.36; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.010). In incremental machine-learning models, adding salivary pH and buffer capacity produced the largest improvement in discrimination. AUROC increased from 0.468 to 0.723 for UFR-defined hyposalivation (&#x394;AUROC&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.255; DeLong p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001) and from 0.608 to 0.725 for SFR-defined hyposalivation (&#x394;AUROC&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.116; DeLong p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.005). SHAP analysis ranked salivary buffer capacity as the leading contributor for both outcomes, with salivary pH also among the top predictors. **CONCLUSIONS:** Objective salivary hypofunction was not fully explained by age, systemic disease burden, or medication burden alone. Instead, chair-side salivary functional markers, particularly salivary pH and buffer capacity, captured clinically relevant information about current salivary status. These findings support a practical, layered approach to identifying patients who may require objective salivary flow assessment. **CLINICAL RELEVANCE:** Chair-side salivary pH and buffer capacity may help clinicians identify patients who warrant objective salivary flow assessment, particularly when considered together with age, sex, and xerogenic medication-class exposure.
