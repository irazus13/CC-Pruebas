## Resumen

Artificial intelligence is increasingly applied in digital pathology, yet its generalizability in routine diagnostic settings remains challenging. We developed a multimodal framework for classifying head and neck lymphoid lesions using 610 cases from ten institutions. UNI was used for histological feature extraction, while CellViT++ enabled nuclear segmentation and quantitative morphometry, subsequently integrated with clinicopathological variables. The UNI-based model showed limited generalization (accuracy of 0.66, F1 score of 0.65, and Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.66), whereas multimodal integration substantially improved internal performance (accuracy of 0.91, F1 score of 0.91, and AUROC of 0.87) and maintained performance across two external cohorts (accuracy of 0.84 and 0.79 and AUROC of 0.84 and 0.80). Class-stratified SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis revealed distinct feature attribution patterns across diagnostic groups, with nuclear morphometric features contributing prominently to model predictions. Morphology alone imposes a clear performance ceiling in head and neck lymphomas. Multimodal integration provides a more reliable and clinically relevant approach, supporting diagnostic triage in routine practice.
