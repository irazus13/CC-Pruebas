## Resumen

Personalized medicine is on the rise, leading to increasingly granular treatment options. Consequently, pathologists are required to provide more detailed diagnoses to fully use the potential of these novel therapies. The number and complexity of grading systems and biomarker scoring methods are increasing. At the same time, the resulting rise in workload coincides with a growing shortage of pathologists, highlighting the need for new methods to ensure high-quality patient care in the future. This review explores the feasibility and readiness of artificial intelligence (AI) applications to alleviate routine diagnostic tasks in gastrointestinal (GI) pathology. It provides an organ-by-organ overview of both non-neoplastic and neoplastic diseases, with an emphasis on tools that show promise for direct or near-term clinical implementation. We found that numerous studies have investigated a wide range of diagnostic challenges, many of which have yielded encouraging results. While most tools remain in the research phase and often lack multicentre validation, commercially available AI solutions already exist for certain applications, in some cases even with regulatory approval for routine diagnostic use. In conclusion, most AI applications in GI pathology are still at a relatively early stage, and several challenges remain to be addressed. Nevertheless, many studies report promising results and suggest substantial advances in the near future. These developments have the potential to benefit both pathologists and patients by significantly expanding diagnostic capabilities while improving accuracy and reproducibility.
