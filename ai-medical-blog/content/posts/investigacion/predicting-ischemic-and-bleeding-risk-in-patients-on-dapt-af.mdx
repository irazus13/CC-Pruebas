## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Dual antiplatelet therapy after coronary stenting prevents ischemic complications but increases bleeding risk. Individualized assessment of these combined risks may inform treatment strategies. **OBJECTIVES:** This study sought to develop a machine learning algorithm for clinical outcomes after percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with potential to support clinical decisions around antiplatelet therapy. **METHODS:** Data from 11 clinical trials encompassing 19,106 patients who underwent PCI with everolimus-eluting stents were randomly split 3:1 to train and validate 2 separate machine learning algorithms for ischemic (cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, stroke, or stent thrombosis) and bleeding (Bleeding Academic Research Consortium type 3 or 5) risk prediction at 1 year. The Boruta algorithm was used to select the top 11 variables (providing &gt;80% of predictive information) for final model development. An independent cohort of 10,696 patients undergoing PCI at a tertiary-care center (Mount Sinai Hospital) was used for external validation. **RESULTS:** There were 594 (4.1%) ischemic and 448 (3.1%) bleeding events in the training data set. Random survival forest was selected as the best-performing model (mean time-dependent area under the curve of 0.76 for ischemic events and 0.81 for bleeding). Internal and external validation yielded a mean time-dependent area under the curve of 0.72 and 0.70 for ischemic events and 0.72 and 0.71 for bleeding, respectively. The 2 models met the prespecified performance goal in both validation cohorts. **CONCLUSIONS:** A machine learning-based tool exhibited good discriminative ability for the prediction of ischemic and bleeding events at 1 year post-PCI and warrants prospective evaluation in clinical decisions surrounding antithrombotic therapies.
