## Resumen

Leishmaniasis affects approximately 1 million people annually with visceral infections resulting in death if left untreated and the cutaneous disease leading to disfiguring skin lesions. Leishmaniasis is caused by the Leishmania spp. parasites and is difficult to treat as existing drugs are far from optimal. Despite increased efforts to identify better drugs for leishmaniasis, there are few new chemical entities that have entered clinical development, thus highlighting the persistent need for such compounds. Here we describe GNF5885, an orally available and parasite-selective inhibitor of Leishmania cytochrome bc1 complex. GNF5885 was derived through phenotypic optimization of buparvaquone. GNF5885 exhibits unparalleled efficacy in vivo and can efficiently clear parasites from mice in models of visceral and cutaneous infections. Furthermore, its ability to clear parasites rapidly with short treatment regimens makes it particularly desirable for treating patients residing in regions endemic to leishmaniasis. Cryo-EM structure of the Leishmania cytochrome bc1 complex with GNF5885 at 2.8 &#xc5; resolution revealed that the inhibitor binds in the Qi ubiquinone-binding pocket. Our data provide validation of the Leishmania cytochrome bc1 complex as a promising therapeutic target for the treatment of leishmaniasis and underscore the possibility of developing a short-course oral treatment for this neglected disease. In addition, the availability of the cytochrome bc1 complex costructure with GNF5885 opens an opportunity to rationally design new cytochrome bc1 complex inhibitors suitable for clinical development.
