## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Pediatric influenza is an important public health concern. Chinese patent medicines are used in its treatment, but relevant clinical evidence is dispersed across guidelines, expert consensus statements, and clinical studies. The multidimensional knowledge required for syndrome-based prescribing complicates information retrieval and standardized clinical use. **OBJECTIVE:** This study aimed to develop a traceable knowledge graph of Chinese patent medicines for pediatric influenza, integrate it with a large language model (LLM) to support evidence-based question answering, and evaluate answer quality and preliminary usability. **METHODS:** We systematically searched for clinical practice guidelines, expert consensus statements, and clinical studies. Evidence segmentation, ontology development, LLM-assisted extraction with manual verification, terminology normalization, and knowledge integration were used to construct a Neo4j knowledge graph. Structured graph retrieval was combined with retrieval-augmented generation to develop a question-answering system. A standalone LLM and the knowledge graph-enhanced LLM (KG-LLM) each answered 80 standardized questions. Three clinical experts independently evaluated anonymized, randomly ordered answers for core task accuracy, completeness, and unsupported factual content. Five physicians participated in an exploratory usability evaluation using the System Usability Scale (SUS), study-specific Likert items, and self-reported evidence-location outcomes. **RESULTS:** We included 142 publications and generated 1294 evidence units. The knowledge graph contained 3211 nodes and 19,671 relationships, linking structured knowledge to evidence units and source publications. Across 80 paired questions, KG-LLM achieved higher accuracy scores than the standalone LLM (mean 3.05, SD 1.88 vs mean 2.02, SD 1.54; Holm-adjusted P&lt;.001) and higher completeness scores (mean 3.11, SD 1.84 vs mean 2.42, SD 1.62; adjusted P=.002). Among 78 pairs with determinate assessments, unsupported factual content was identified in 11 standalone LLM answers (14.1%) and no KG-LLM answers (0/78, 0%; adjusted P=.002). The mean SUS score was 96.50 (SD 2.85). Of 5 physicians, 4 (80%) reported locating both the original publication and the corresponding evidence. **CONCLUSIONS:** The traceable knowledge graph and graph-enhanced question-answering system integrated clinical evidence on Chinese patent medicines for pediatric influenza. KG-LLM achieved higher accuracy and completeness and less unsupported factual content on standardized tasks than the standalone LLM. Preliminary usability findings were favorable, but generalizability, long-term usability, and safety in clinical practice require further evaluation.
