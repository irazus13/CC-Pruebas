## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) has a poor prognosis, and conventional clinical scores have limited accuracy in predicting individualized survival. **PURPOSE:** We aimed to determine whether machine learning (ML) models using routine clinical data discriminate survival better than established clinical scores in HCC and to compare the prognostic profiles of MASLD- and HBV-associated HCC. **METHODS:** We conducted a single-center retrospective cohort study of 233 patients with HCC diagnosed between 2010 and 2026. Patients with valid survival data and &#x2264;20% missingness across 29 pre-specified predictor variables were included. During a median follow-up of 15 months, 143 patients died. Random Survival Forest, XGBoost, a Hybrid ensemble model, and penalized Cox regression were trained using 5-fold cross-validation. Model discrimination was assessed using Harrell's C-index and the fixed-horizon AUROC. The score-excluded V2 analysis removed clinical scores from ML inputs. SHAP values quantified feature contributions. **RESULTS:** The best-performing ML model (Hybrid) achieved a C-index of 0.797, higher than BCLC stage (0.735), Child-Pugh class, and ALBI grade, but only modestly higher than penalized Cox regression with the same variables (0.773). In the score-excluded sensitivity analysis, the Random Survival Forest maintained a strong performance (C-index 0.784). The best fixed-horizon 12-month AUROC was 0.878. SHAP analysis identified AFP, ECOG performance status, albumin, tumor diameter, and total bilirubin as the most influential predictors. Among 57 patients with MASLD-associated HCC, the median survival was 23.0 months vs 54.0 months in 116 patients with HBV-associated HCC (p=0.0328); this difference was not explained by age, comorbidities, tumor characteristics, or treatment. **CONCLUSIONS:** In this single-center cohort, models using routinely available variables discriminated survival in HCC better than conventional clinical scores by combining more information.
