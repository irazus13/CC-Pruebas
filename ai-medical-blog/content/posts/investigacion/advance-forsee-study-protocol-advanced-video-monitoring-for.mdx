## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Postoperative complications remain common after cardiac surgery, while manual nurse-led vital signs monitoring on post-cardiac surgery wards may delay their detection. Early recognition of clinical deterioration leading to complications is therefore essential to reduce post-surgery morbidity, and mortality. Conversely, personalised early discharge for patients with a fast and uneventful recovery may prevent unnecessarily delayed discharge and thereby free up hospital capacity. **OBJECTIVES:** This study protocol describes the planned evaluation of the feasibility and acceptability of continuous video-based monitoring for early detection of postoperative deterioration as well as identification of uneventful recovery in cardiac surgery patients. **METHODS:** This single-centre prospective observational study will include cardiac surgery patients admitted to the cardiothoracic surgery ward, with a target sample size of 300 patients. Video recordings will be made during postoperative ward stay and processed retrospectively to extract heart rate, arrhythmias, and respiration rate as well as contextual parameters (e.g., posture, movement and facial cues). These video-derived features will be used to train and evaluate machine-learning models for early detection of postoperative deterioration and assessment of discharge readiness. **RESULTS:** Patient enrolment is scheduled to start in November 2026 and is expected to be completed by November 2028. The first deterioration detection and discharge readiness models are expected by December 2028. **CONCLUSIONS:** The ADVANCE-FORSEE study was initiated to test the hypothesis that, by using video-based monitoring, we will be able to (1) mitigate the number and impact of postoperative adverse events and (2) identify patients with an uneventful recovery in an early stage. These insights may contribute to the development of tailored and individualised postoperative care pathways.
