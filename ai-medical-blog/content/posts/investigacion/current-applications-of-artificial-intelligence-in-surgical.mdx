## Resumen

**IMPORTANCE:** Craniosynostosis, the premature fusion of cranial sutures, can cause abnormal skull shapes, neurological issues and aesthetic concerns. Surgical correction is standard, but planning is complex and operator-dependent. Artificial intelligence (AI) offers the potential to enhance diagnosis, optimise planning and predict postoperative outcomes. **OBSERVATIONS:** A scoping review following PRISMA-ScR guidelines identified 11 studies involving paediatric patients with craniosynostosis. AI methods included unsupervised machine learning, convolutional neural networks, finite element models and variational autoencoders. Applications encompassed cranial severity scoring, phenotype classification, preoperative surgical simulation, intraoperative guidance and outcome prediction. AI-derived metrics correlated strongly with expert assessments, facilitated patient-specific planning and predicted postoperative aesthetics. **CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE:** AI is a transformative adjunct in craniosynostosis surgery. Clinically, it improves diagnostic accuracy, supports personalised surgical planning and guides intraoperative decision-making. Some studies show AI can contribute to better functional and aesthetic outcomes, showing great promise in the paediatric surgical field. Although AI does not replace clinical expertise, it enhances precision and reproducibility. Future research should prioritise prospective validation, integration into surgical workflows and development of interpretable, surgeon-friendly AI tools to maximise clinical benefit.
