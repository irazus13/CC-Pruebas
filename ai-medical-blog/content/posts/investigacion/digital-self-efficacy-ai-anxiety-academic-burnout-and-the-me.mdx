## Resumen

**AIM:** This study examined the relationships among digital self-efficacy, AI anxiety and academic burnout among nursing students across geographically diverse Egyptian universities and explored the mediating role of digital resilience. To the best of our knowledge, this study is among the first multisite investigations in Egyptian nursing students to test digital resilience as a mediator in the association between digital self-efficacy and academic burnout. **DESIGN:** A multisite descriptive correlational study was conducted with 649 nursing students from four geographically diverse Egyptian universities during the first semester of the 2025-2026 academic year (September to November 2025). This study followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines. **METHODS:** Validated Arabic-translated instruments were utilized, including the Digital Self-Efficacy Scale, AI Anxiety Scale, Maslach Burnout Inventory-Student Survey and Digital Resilience Scale. A stratified random proportional sampling technique was employed to ensure representative allocation across universities and academic years. Confirmatory factor analysis confirmed the factorial validity of all translated instruments. Structural equation modelling (SEM) with 5000 bootstrap resamples examined direct, indirect and total effects among latent variables. Common method bias was assessed using Harman's single-factor test. **RESULTS:** Digital resilience significantly mediated the relationship between digital self-efficacy and academic burnout (indirect effect &#x3b2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;-0.11, 95% CI [-0.23, -0.05], p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.001). Digital self-efficacy demonstrated a strong positive correlation with digital resilience (r&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.83, p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001). Both digital self-efficacy (r&#x2009;=&#x2009;-0.11, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.007) and digital resilience (r&#x2009;=&#x2009;-0.11, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.004) were negatively correlated with academic burnout. AI anxiety showed a small negative correlation with digital self-efficacy (r&#x2009;=&#x2009;-0.13, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.001). AI anxiety did not significantly predict digital resilience (&#x3b2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;-0.05, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.372) nor academic burnout (&#x3b2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.06, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.102). The final structural model demonstrated acceptable fit: &#x3c7;2/df&#x2009;=&#x2009;2.45, CFI&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.96, TLI&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.94, RMSEA&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.05 (90% CI [0.03, 0.07]), SRMR&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.04. R-squared values were 0.34 for digital resilience and 0.18 for academic burnout. A suppression effect was identified: digital self-efficacy showed a positive direct association with academic burnout (&#x3b2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.32, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.002) despite a negative bivariate correlation. **CONCLUSION:** Digital resilience was associated with reduced academic burnout among nursing students in digital learning environments. Digital self-efficacy was positively associated with digital resilience, which in turn was negatively associated with academic burnout. Although digital self-efficacy showed positive direct and total associations with academic burnout, it also demonstrated a significant negative indirect association through digital resilience. Nursing education programs should prioritize the development of digital resilience alongside digital self-efficacy rather than focusing exclusively on AI-related concerns. Future longitudinal research is warranted to establish causal pathways and evaluate intervention effectiveness. **IMPLICATIONS FOR NURSING PRACTICE:** The findings suggest that nursing education programs may benefit from integrating digital resilience training alongside digital skill development to support student well-being. Clinical preceptors and nurse managers should assess and support the digital competencies of novice nurses during orientation and ongoing professional development. No patient or public contribution.
