## Resumen

TREM2 is a lipid-binding receptor expressed in the brain in microglia. Activation of TREM2 is critical for microglia to adopt a disease-associated phenotype. Here we describe VHB937, a human antibody that activates TREM2 by binding to the IgSF domain. In vitro in human iPS-derived microglia, VHB937 expands a phagocytic subcluster at the expense of a pro-inflammatory subcluster, facilitates chemotaxis and phagocytosis, and reduces the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines like SPP1. In vivo, VHB937 reduces degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the MPTP model and accelerates re-myelination in the cuprizone model. Chronic treatment in a mouse amyloidosis and tauopathy model results in reduction of dystrophic neurites with no effect on plaque load and of pathogenic tau species, respectively. We further show in a first-in-human clinical trial that VHB937 engages TREM2 based on reduction of SPP1 in CSF. We conclude that VHB937 has the potential to change microglial state toward a neuroprotective phenotype, offering therapeutic potential for neurodegenerative/neuroinflammatory disorders.
