## Resumen

Aims and Backgrounds: Artificial intelligence (AI) has rapidly expanding applications in diagnosis, surgical planning, and postoperative care. This state-of-the-art review characterizes the growth and use of AI across perioperative care with particular attention to microvascular and free flap reconstruction, and examines how this growth compares with the pace of clinical validation. Techniques: PubMed (MEDLINE), Scopus, and Web of Science were searched through August 10, 2026 for studies evaluating AI in facial plastic surgery, oral and maxillofacial surgery, microvascular head and neck surgery, and facial reanimation. Pediatric-only, non-English, and non-AI-focused studies were excluded. Conclusions and Clinical Relevance: Of 1,249 articles, 66 met inclusion criteria. Preoperative applications accounted for 50% of studies, focusing on surgical planning, image segmentation, and risk stratification. Postoperative applications accounted for 45%, including free flap monitoring and outcome assessment. Intraoperative applications represented 6%. Deep learning and convolutional neural networks were the most common approaches, while few studies included prospective evaluation. AI use has grown rapidly in facial plastic and reconstructive surgery, with uses in preoperative planning, risk prediction, and postoperative free flap monitoring. Despite growth, clinical validation and prospective evaluation remain limited, particularly for intraoperative applications. Research focused on prospective application of technology will determine how AI can be safely incorporated into facial reconstructive surgery.
