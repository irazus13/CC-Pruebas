## Resumen

Background and objective Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly influencing dental diagnosis and aesthetic planning. AI-driven smile design tools allow dentists to replicate aesthetic outcomes using facial and dental data. However, implementation remains limited in Indian undergraduate dental education, particularly in resource-constrained regions. This study aimed to assess the awareness and attitudes of undergraduate dental students in Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala, toward using AI-driven smile design tools. Methods A cross-sectional pilot study was conducted among 30 undergraduate dental students in Thiruvananthapuram using a validated questionnaire distributed via Google Forms. For this study, "awareness" was operationalized as self-reported recognition of AI applications in smile design, and "utilization" was defined as self-reported encounters with patients requesting AI-assisted treatment or personal willingness to adopt AI tools. The survey included multiple-choice, Likert-scale, and dichotomous questions assessing awareness, attitudes, and perceived clinical implications of AI in smile design. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics and chi-square tests. Results A majority of the participants (23/30, 76.6%) had heard of AI applications in smile design. Positive attitudes were prevalent: most participants believed that AI could reduce treatment duration and improve clinical judgment (27/30, 90%), and all participants felt that AI could elevate service quality (30/30, 100%). Additionally, 24/30 (80%) participants agreed that AI positively impacts prognosis, and 26/30 (86.6%) anticipated increased patient curiosity and inflow. Most respondents (27/30, 90%) supported the inclusion of AI in theoretical classes, and 22/30 (73.3%) were willing to modify their clinical setup for AI integration. However, 27/30 (90%) expected potential troubleshooting or errors, and only 11/30 (36.6%) reported encountering patients who requested AI-based treatment tools. Conclusion Dental students in Thiruvananthapuram demonstrated substantial awareness and positive attitudes toward AI-powered smile design tools, recognizing their potential to enhance service quality, treatment efficiency, and patient engagement. However, these findings should be interpreted in light of the pilot design, small convenience sample, and single-district setting. Despite this readiness, concerns regarding technical limitations and current patient demand highlight the need for structured AI integration into dental curricula and supervised clinical implementation.
