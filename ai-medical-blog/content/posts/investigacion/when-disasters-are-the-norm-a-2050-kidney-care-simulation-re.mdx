## Resumen

Recent disasters, including pandemics, armed conflicts, and extreme weather events, have highlighted the fragility of chronic dialysis systems and accelerated a broader shift in disaster nephrology from management and provision of dialysis for acute crush-related kidney injury toward resilience-oriented continuity of care planning for chronic kidney disease and kidney failure. At the World Congress of Nephrology 2026 held in Yokohama, Japan, the Spotlight Session entitled "When Disasters Are the Norm: A 2050 Kidney Care Simulation" explored future-oriented approaches to resilient kidney care. Two disaster scenarios and a future-oriented discussion were presented, involving water shortages, digital outages, and logistical disruption. The session discussed practical adaptation strategies including crisis-adapted dialysis protocols, decentralized and home-based dialysis models, low-technology backup systems, and regional coordination frameworks during resource-constrained conditions. The session also examined the potential future role of artificial intelligence (AI)-based forecasting systems which may support anticipatory dialysis preparedness through improved prediction of extreme weather events. Collectively, these discussions suggest that future disaster nephrology will increasingly require resilience-focused, decentralized, and operationally adaptable kidney care systems capable of sustaining continuity of care under conditions of recurrent disruption and infrastructure instability.
