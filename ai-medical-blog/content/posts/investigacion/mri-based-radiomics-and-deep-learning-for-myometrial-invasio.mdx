## Resumen

Endometrial carcinoma is a common gynecological malignancy in developed countries, with its incidence continuing to rise. Myometrial invasion depth is a key prognostic factor and determinant of the 2023 International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) staging system, directly influencing surgical planning and adjuvant therapy. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) offers excellent soft tissue resolution, but conventional interpretation remains limited by subjectivity and inter-observer variability. Radiomics and deep learning have emerged as promising tools for preoperative assessment. In this narrative review, we synthesize evidence from MRI-based studies, covering T2-weighted imaging (T2WI), contrast-enhanced (CE) MRI, multi-sequence approaches, and comparisons with radiologist performance. Key challenges include overfitting, lack of external validation, reliance on manual annotation, and limited interpretability. Reported performance varied widely. A recent meta-analysis suggested that pooled areas under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUCs) are lower than those reported in the best-performing single-center studies, raising generalizability concerns. This review critically appraises current evidence, compares artificial intelligence (AI) with radiologist performance, and outlines a roadmap toward clinical translation, including multi-center harmonization, automated workflows, and interpretability-by-design.
