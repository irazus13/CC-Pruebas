## Resumen

**INTRODUCTION:** Generalised myasthenia gravis (gMG) significantly affects physical functioning and emotional wellbeing. Many patients with gMG report a disconnect between their lived experience and physicians' perceptions of disease impact, which may impact treatment priorities and shared decision-making. We assessed patient-physician concordance on perceived gMG burden and explored factors contributing to perceptual gaps. **METHODS:** Across five European countries (France, Germany, Italy, Spain, UK), 167 adult patients with gMG and their treating physicians (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;24) independently completed online surveys assessing the perceived impact of gMG on physical functioning and emotional wellbeing, using 0-100 visual analogue scales (VAS). Discordance was defined as an absolute VAS score difference of &#x2265; 20 points. Additionally, 25 patients and 25 physicians participated in semi-structured qualitative interviews exploring lived experiences, consultation dynamics, and treatment decision-making. VAS score agreement was assessed using intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) and Bland-Altman analysis; qualitative interviews were analysed thematically. **RESULTS:** Overall, mean patient and physician VAS scores were similar for physical functioning (57.9 vs 59.6, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.20) and emotional wellbeing (58.7 vs 61.0, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.09), with "substantial" concordance in 81% of patient-physician pairs (ICC: ~ 0.7). Conversely, 19% of patient-physician pairs demonstrated clear discordance, with physicians either over- or underestimating burden. Notably, even among concordant pairs, up to one fifth reported score differences approaching the discordance threshold, reflecting subtle, but potentially meaningful, misalignment. Qualitative insights suggested that short consultations, under-recognition of fluctuating symptoms, and blind spots regarding psychosocial impacts may contribute to perceptual gaps. **CONCLUSIONS:** Despite overall broad patient-physician concordance of VAS scores, meaningful misalignment in perceived gMG burden persists. Qualitative insights highlighted perceptual gaps that may be driven by limited consultation time and under-addressed psychosocial needs. Incorporating structured, symptom-focused dialogue alongside standard assessments may improve recognition of disease burden and support patient-centred gMG management.
