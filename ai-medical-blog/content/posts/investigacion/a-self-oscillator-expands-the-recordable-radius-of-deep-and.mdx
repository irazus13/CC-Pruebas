## Resumen

Visceral physiological signals are pivotal for both disease monitoring and elucidating complex biological processes, but localized physiological signals rapidly attenuate as they propagate through biological tissue. As a result, noninvasive epidermal recording of localized biosignals originating from deep tissues remains highly challenging, particularly in obese individuals. Here, we report body-powered, frequency-multiplexed self-oscillators that substantially increase the recordable radius of biosignals from &lt;2 to 220&#xa0;centimeters. The self-oscillator converts local physiological signals into long-range field potentials that are detectable from the skin surface. In vivo studies demonstrate real-time, simultaneous detection of gastric bleeding and core temperature, with each modality appearing as a distinct spectral peak in the Fourier domain. This scalable approach, thus, establishes a general route for converting internal physiological signals into skin-surface accessible biomarkers, opening avenues for continuous multimodal biosensing in clinical diagnostics and biomedical research.
