## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Hedgehog pathway inhibitors (HHIs) have significantly expanded therapeutic options for patients with locally advanced basal cell carcinoma (laBCC) who are not candidates for surgery or radiotherapy. The aim of this article is to review current evidence and practical clinical applications of HHIs across relevant real-world treatment scenarios. **METHODS:** Available data from pivotal trials, real-world studies, and multidisciplinary clinical experience were reviewed, focusing on four key clinical settings: multiple basal cell carcinomas and Gorlin Syndrome, basosquamous carcinoma, retreatment and rechallenge strategies after treatment discontinuation, and neoadjuvant or cytoreductive approach. **RESULTS:** HHIs demonstrate sustained efficacy across heterogeneous clinical contexts. In patients with Multiple BCCs and Gorlin Syndrome, systemic therapy reduces tumor burden and surgical morbidity. Emerging evidence supports HHI activity in basosquamous carcinoma. Retreatment, drug switching, and rechallenge strategies frequently restore disease control after relapse or intolerance. Neoadjuvant approach may facilitate tumor downstaging and organ preservation in anatomically complex tumors. **CONCLUSIONS:** Optimal management of laBCC requires individualized treatment duration, proactive adverse-event management, and multidisciplinary coordination. HHIs are evolving from salvage therapy toward integral components of long-term disease management.
