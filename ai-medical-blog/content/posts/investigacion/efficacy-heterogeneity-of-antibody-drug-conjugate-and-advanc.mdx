## Resumen

Antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) has expanded the treatment options for advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC); however, treatment responses vary considerably among patients, and target protein expression alone is insufficient to predict clinical benefit. This review examines the potential mechanisms underlying ADC efficacy heterogeneity in NSCLC, focusing on functional targets, the ADC action cascade, and resistance evolution. The therapeutic relevance of targets such as human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), mesenchymal-epithelial transition factor (c-MET), and trophoblast cell-surface antigen 2 (TROP2) depends not only on protein expression but also on oncogenic background, receptor biology characteristics, and intratumoral spatial heterogeneity. These factors highlighting the need to move beyond single-marker protein expression testing toward comprehensive assessment of target functionality. In addition, tumor tissue delivery, receptor-mediated internalization, intracellular trafficking, and payload-mediated cytotoxicity may all influence ADC sensitivity and contribute to resistance development. Following disease progression, resistance may arise from target loss or depletion of target-positive clones, or from reduced payload sensitivity despite target persistence, potentially affecting the efficacy of subsequent ADC with similar payloads. Therefore, treatment decisions after ADC failure require dynamic assessment integrating tissue-based testing and circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) analysis to characterize target status and potential resistance mechanisms, thereby informing strategies such as switching targets, modifying payloads, or implementing combination therapies. Future research should focus on establishing biomarker systems that integrate molecular background, target functionality, drug delivery, and resistance evolution to advance mechanism-driven precision treatment of NSCLC.&#x2029;.
