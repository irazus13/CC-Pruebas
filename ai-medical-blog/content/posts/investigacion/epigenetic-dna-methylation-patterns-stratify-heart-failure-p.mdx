## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Environmental and lifestyle exposures contribute substantially to heart failure (HF) development and progression. DNA methylation integrates cumulative environmental exposures and may provide insight into biological pathways and risk stratification across HF phenotypes. **OBJECTIVES:** The aim of this study was to identify phenotype-specific DNA methylation patterns across HF subtypes and evaluate their association with clinical outcomes. **METHODS:** Individuals with symptomatic HF (universal definition stage C or D; HF with preserved ejection fraction, HF with mildly reduced ejection fraction, and heart failure with reduced ejection fraction [HFrEF]) from the MyoVasc (An Epidemiological Cohort Study to Investigate the Development and Progression of Heart Failure and the Interaction With Vascular Disease) cohort (N = 3,289) and individuals without cardiovascular disease from the population-based GHS (Gutenberg Health Study) (N = 18,700) were analyzed. Genome-wide DNA methylation from whole blood was measured using the Illumina Infinium MethylationEPIC array. Phenotype-specific DNA methylation scores were derived using penalized regression models adjusted for cardiovascular risk factors. **RESULTS:** Distinct and partially overlapping DNA methylation patterns were identified across HF phenotypes, with limited overlap at the CpG level but substantial convergence at the gene level. Phenotype-specific methylation scores discriminated patients with HF from control subjects with high accuracy (area under the curve: 0.97-0.98). Methylation signatures in HF with preserved ejection fraction were associated predominantly with metabolic and inflammatory markers, whereas HFrEF signatures were more strongly linked to markers of myocardial injury and vascular disease. All methylation scores were independently associated with composite of cardiac death or hospitalization for worsening HF and all-cause mortality, with the strongest association observed for the HFrEF score (HR: &#x223c;1.7 per SD). **CONCLUSIONS:** Symptomatic HF phenotypes are characterized by distinct and shared DNA methylation signatures. These epigenetic profiles reflect relevant biological pathways and are associated with adverse clinical outcomes, supporting their potential utility in HF risk stratification and precision medicine.
