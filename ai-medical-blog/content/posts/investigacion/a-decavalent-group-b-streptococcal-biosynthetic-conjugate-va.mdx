## Resumen

Globally, 394,000 cases of Group B Streptococcus (GBS) infection occur each year, leading to significant morbidity and mortality. Vaccination has emerged as an important strategy to limit GBS infections and disease; however, existing vaccine candidates currently in clinical development do not cover all capsular polysaccharide serotypes. Here, we describe the use of a next generation bioconjugation platform to generate a decavalent GBS vaccine (GBS10V) and characterize its immunological evaluation in animals. Using an evolved PglS bioconjugation platform, capsular polysaccharides from all 10 GBS capsular polysaccharide serotypes were individually expressed and bioconjugated to an optimized Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A (EPA6) carrier protein engineered with six sequons. Multiple biochemical analyses confirmed the identity of each polysaccharide serotype bioconjugated to EPA6. Moreover, intact mass spectrometry (MS) and MS/MS spectra confirmed the presence of sialic acid in each repeat unit of GBS polysaccharides attached to the carrier, an attribute critical for eliciting an optimal immune response. The GBS10V vaccine was used to immunize outbred CD-1 mice and New Zealand White rabbits with doses of 1, 2, or 5 &#x3bc;g per GBS serotype. Sera analyses demonstrated the GBS10V vaccine induced high serotype-specific immunoglobulin titers to all 10 serotypes. Most importantly, interim and postimmune sera from rabbits demonstrated potent opsonophagocytic killing, with median titers ranging from 646 to 6,145 for all serotypes by day 42. Together, these findings establish GBS10V as a global GBS conjugate vaccine candidate for all patient populations, and thus, justify its further preclinical development.
