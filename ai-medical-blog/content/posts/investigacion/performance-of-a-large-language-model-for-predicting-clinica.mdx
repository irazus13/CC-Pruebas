## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Large language models (LLMs) can extract information from unstructured clinical text, but it remains unclear whether their ability to assess current status translates into reliable predictions of longitudinal outcomes. **OBJECTIVE:** This study aimed to evaluate DeepSeek's ability to assess baseline clinical and radiographic status in knee osteoarthritis (KOA), its performance in predicting 6-month outcomes, and its interrun reproducibility. **METHODS:** This study used a single-center, prospective observational cohort design to conduct a blinded, retrospective evaluation of the LLM. A total of 128 patients with primary KOA were enrolled, of whom 111 (86.7%) completed the 6-month follow-up and were included in the primary analysis. DeepSeek-V4-Flash and a rheumatologist independently assessed KOA diagnoses, baseline Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC) scores, and Kellgren-Lawrence (KL) grading based on the same standardized interview records and imaging reports and predicted 6-month outcomes. The LLM was run independently 3 times. The actual outcomes determined by another senior physician served as the reference, and the results were compared with the physician's predictions and the persistence model. **RESULTS:** The LLM showed high baseline diagnostic agreement with the physician (105/111, 94.6%) and good agreement on the WOMAC and KL, but 6-month predictions (WOMAC mean absolute error=5.92-6.58; KL exact agreement=74/111, 66.7%-76/111, 68.5%) were worse than those of the physician comparator (mean absolute error=3.86; 96/111, 86.5%) and not superior to those of the persistence model (mean absolute error=5.43). Despite high interrun intraclass correlation coefficients (0.982 and 0.965), exact agreement between runs was poor (35/111, 31.5% and 7/111, 6.3%). **CONCLUSIONS:** The evaluated DeepSeek configuration may support structured baseline KOA assessment. However, its 6-month predictive accuracy and patient-level stability remain limited. Prognostic applications require evidence of added value over a persistence model.
