## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Diabetic retinopathy (DR) is a leading cause of vision loss and is driven by diabetes-induced retinal endothelial dysfunction. However, diabetes-associated transcriptional heterogeneity within retinal endothelial cells (ECs) and its molecular regulators remain incompletely understood. **METHODS:** Retinal ECs were re-analyzed from a previously published single-cell RNA sequencing dataset of db/db and db/m mouse retinas. STAP2 expression was evaluated in db/db and streptozotocin (STZ)-induced diabetic mouse retinas, human proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) tissue, and human retinal microvascular endothelial cells (HRMECs). Gain- and loss-of-function studies assessed endothelial proliferation, migration, angiogenic activity, retinal capillary degeneration, and vascular leakage. Transcriptomic profiling was used to identify downstream mediators. **RESULTS:** Three transcriptionally defined endothelial subclusters were identified, with EC3 predominantly represented in the diabetic group and showing relatively higher expression of signal-transducing adaptor protein 2 (STAP2). STAP2 was increased in diabetic retinas and high-glucose-treated HRMECs. STAP2 knockdown suppressed HRMEC proliferation, migration, and early tube formation in vitro and reduced acellular capillary formation and Evans blue extravasation in vivo, whereas STAP2 overexpression promoted these responses. Transcriptomic analysis identified C-X-C motif chemokine receptor 4 (CXCR4) as a candidate downstream mediator, and CXCR4 silencing attenuated STAP2-induced endothelial responses. **CONCLUSIONS:** These findings identify STAP2 as a previously unrecognized regulator associated with diabetic retinal endothelial dysfunction and reveal CXCR4-associated signaling as a potential mechanism involved in STAP2-mediated endothelial responses. This study provides new insights into endothelial regulation during diabetic vascular injury.
