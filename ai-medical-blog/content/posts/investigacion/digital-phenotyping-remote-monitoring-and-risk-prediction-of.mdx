## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDPs), particularly preeclampsia, are associated with acute cardiovascular morbidity and future heart failure (HF). Current surveillance relies largely on blood pressure, symptoms, laboratory abnormalities, and clinician-triggered evaluation. Digital phenotyping, remote monitoring, electronic health record-based prediction, and machine learning may enable earlier cardiovascular risk identification. **OBJECTIVES:** To systematically identify and narratively synthesize studies evaluating remote monitoring, digital health, machine learning, or risk-prediction approaches for HF-related phenotypes or cardiovascular morbidity in HDPs. **METHODS:** MEDLINE, Embase, and Scopus were searched for studies published from January 2020 through April 10, 2025. Studies evaluating prediction or monitoring approaches in HDPs underwent sequential assessment for HF-related or cardiovascular morbidity outcomes. Screening and full-text reviews were performed by 2 independent reviewers, with disagreements resolved by a third reviewer. Given substantial heterogeneity, findings were synthesized narratively. **RESULTS:** Of 3366 records imported for screening, 6 studies met the inclusion criteria. Studies used clinical prediction models, electronic health record (EHR)-based models, and remote monitoring to evaluate acute cardiovascular morbidity, HFpEF, cardiac remodeling, and cardiovascular readmission. Model discrimination ranged from modest to strong (AUROC approximately 0.70-0.94). Recurrent predictors included severe hypertensive disease, earlier gestational age at delivery, kidney disease, pre-existing cardiac disease, pulmonary hypertension, maternal age, obesity-related measures, blood pressure, and postpartum volume retention. Most prediction models lacked external validation. **CONCLUSIONS:** Preliminary evidence suggests that clinical, EHR-derived, and remotely monitored data may support risk stratification for selected cardiovascular morbidity and HF-related phenotypes after HDPs. Prospective validation, physiological marker integration, and evaluation of risk-guided interventions are needed.
