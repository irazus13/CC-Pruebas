## Resumen

**BACKGROUND AND AIMS:** Despite an expanding therapeutic landscape, an efficacy ceiling persists with advanced monotherapies in ulcerative colitis (UC). We used longitudinal whole-blood transcriptomics to characterize molecular compatibility, therapy-specific effects with potential complementarity, and potential cross-therapy resistance correction. **METHODS:** Adults with endoscopically active UC (Mayo endoscopic sub-score [MES]&#x2009;&#x2265;2) initiating anti-tumor necrosis factor (anti-TNF), vedolizumab (VDZ), tofacitinib (TFC), or ustekinumab (UST) underwent whole-blood RNA sequencing at baseline and/or post-induction endoscopy. Endoscopic improvement was defined as MES &#x2264;1. Response- and resistance-associated signatures were compared across differentially expressed genes (DEGs), enriched biological processes, and transcription factor (TF) activities. Potential correction required an opposite-direction response effect &#x2265;50% of the corresponding resistance effect. **RESULTS:** Among 314 treatment cycles from 234 patients (514 RNA samples), 47.6% achieved endoscopic improvement. Anti-TNF, VDZ, and TFC response-associated signatures were broadly concordant across molecular layers while retaining therapy-specific features. UST showed no significant response-associated DEGs but retained distinct pathway- and TF-level signals. In parallel, cross-therapy response-associated effects opposed resistance-associated features, with correlations of -0.89 to -1.00 for genes, -0.64 to -0.84 for biological processes, and -0.52 to -0.76 for TF activities. Against anti-TNF resistance, VDZ met the potential-correction criterion for 70% of DEGs, 79% of biological processes, and 96% of TF activities, including all 20 resistance processes with the largest absolute enrichment scores. **CONCLUSIONS:** Whole-blood transcriptomics identified compatible shared responses, therapy-specific molecular effects, and potential cross-therapy compensation. These findings generate hypotheses for biologically informed combination strategies requiring prospective validation, rather than evidence of efficacy or synergy.
