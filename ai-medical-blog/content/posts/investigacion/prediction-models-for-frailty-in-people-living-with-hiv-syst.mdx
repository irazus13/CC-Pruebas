## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Frailty is increasingly prevalent among people living with HIV, who experience earlier onset and a higher burden of frailty than the general population. Prediction models that identify people living with HIV at risk of future frailty could enable timely, targeted intervention. However, the evidence on prediction models developed specifically for frailty in people living with HIV has not yet been systematically evaluated. **OBJECTIVE:** This review aims to systematically identify and critically appraise multivariable prediction models for the future occurrence of frailty in people living with HIV and to assess and summarize their methodological quality and predictive performance. **METHODS:** We searched PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, Cochrane Library, ProQuest, and Chinese databases (SinoMed, CNKI, Wanfang, VIP) from inception to July 24, 2026. Studies that developed or validated any multivariable statistical or machine-learning prediction model for frailty in people living with HIV were eligible. Title and abstract screening, full-text evaluation, data extraction (Checklist for Critical Appraisal and Data Extraction for Systematic Reviews of Prediction Modeling Studies [CHARMS]), and risk-of-bias and applicability assessment (Prediction Model Risk of Bias Assessment Tool [PROBAST]) were conducted independently in duplicate. **RESULTS:** The search identified 2523 records (initial search: 2205; updated search: 318); 137 full-text articles were assessed for eligibility, and only 1 study met all inclusion criteria. Frail RISC-HIV (Frailty Risk in the Short-Term for Care of People with HIV) was developed in the Centers for AIDS Research Network of Integrated Clinical Systems (CNICS) cohort (n=4680; training n=3170; validation n=1510) using Bayesian model averaging and LASSO (least absolute shrinkage and selection operator) regression combined with Cox proportional-hazards models to predict incident frailty within 2 years. The model comprised 8 predictors (baseline frailty score, depressive symptomatology [Patient Health Questionnaire-7], prescribed antidepressants, current illicit opioid use, current marijuana use, current antiretroviral therapy, age, and sex) and achieved acceptable discrimination in the validation set (Harrell C=0.76, 95% CI 0.73-0.79), with a sensitivity of 80% and specificity of 61% at a 5% risk threshold; calibration under-predicted the observed risk (predicted 7.9% vs observed 14.7% in the validation set). PROBAST assessment rated the study at high overall risk of bias, driven primarily by the analysis domain. To our knowledge, no external validation of the model was identified, and TRIPOD (Transparent Reporting of a Multivariable Prediction Model for Individual Prognosis or Diagnosis) reporting was incomplete. Quantitative synthesis was not feasible. **CONCLUSIONS:** The evidence base for frailty prediction in people living with HIV currently comprises a single, internally validated model that has not yet been externally validated. This critical evidence gap underscores an urgent need for further model development and rigorous external validation in diverse settings, with standardized frailty definitions and reporting in accordance with TRIPOD.
