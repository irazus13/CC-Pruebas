## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** This study aimed to identify ARGs associated with preeclampsia and to evaluate whether systemic inflammatory mediators genetically linked to ARG expression contribute to disease susceptibility. **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** Placental transcriptomic data (GSE75010) were used to identify differentially expressed ARGs, followed by machine learning-based gene prioritization. A molecular diagnostic model was constructed as a screening tool and validated using GSE35574. Mendelian randomization (MR) analyses were then performed using large&#x2011;scale genome&#x2011;wide association study datasets from eQTLGen and FinnGen Release 12 to investigate genetically supported associations between ARG expression and preeclampsia risk. A two&#x2011;step MR framework was applied to evaluate the mediating role of systemic inflammatory cytokines. **RESULTS:** Differential expression analysis identified 55 ARGs associated with preeclampsia. Random forest analysis further prioritized 24 feature ARGs, which were used to construct a diagnostic model with good discriminatory performance (AUC&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.87). Among these genes, 11 with available cis-eQTL instruments were further analyzed by Mendelian randomization. MR analyses revealed that higher genetically predicted ATG16L1 expression was associated with an increased risk of preeclampsia, whereas higher genetically predicted ATG12 and NPC1 expression were associated with a reduced risk of preeclampsia. Two-step Mendelian randomization mediation analysis demonstrated that interleukin&#x2011;13 (IL&#x2011;13) partially mediated the association between genetically predicted NPC1 expression and preeclampsia risk, accounting for 16.57% of the total effect. **DISCUSSION:** This study provides genetic evidence linking autophagy-related pathways to preeclampsia susceptibility, with NPC1 as a candidate associated with lower risk, partly via IL-13-mediated immune modulation, highlighting the autophagy-inflammation axis for future research.
