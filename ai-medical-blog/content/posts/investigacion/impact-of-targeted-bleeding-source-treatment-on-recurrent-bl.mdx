## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Whether targeted treatment (TT) to potentially cure the cause of bleeding before percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is effective in preventing recurrent bleeding or death after PCI is unknown. **OBJECTIVES:** This study sought to assess whether TT affects recurrent bleeding or survival after PCI. **METHODS:** We included patients from the Cardiobase Bern PCI registry with Bleeding Academic Research Consortium (BARC) 2 to 4 bleeding before index PCI. TT was defined as surgical, interventional, or medical therapy targeted to cure the cause of prior bleeding. The primary endpoint was recurrent BARC 2 to 5 bleeding at the identical location; secondary endpoints were any BARC 2 to 5 bleeding and mortality throughout 1 year (median: 390 days [Q1-Q3: 371-430 days]) after PCI. We report inverse probability weight-adjusted hazard ratios (IPW-HRs) with TT as dependent variable, and as covariates we report the Academic Research Consortium for High Bleeding Risk variables associated with &#x2265;4% 1-year BARC 3 to 5 bleeding, antithrombotic treatment, and bleeding location. **RESULTS:** Of 19,352 patients, 847 (4.4%) had prior bleeding, 358 of 847 (42.3%) with and 489 of 847 (57.7%) without TT. Recurrent BARC 2 to 5 bleeding at the identical location occurred in 8.9% (32/358) with TT vs 5.1% (25/489) without TT (IPW-HR: 1.88; 95% CI: 1.10-3.19; P = 0.020). There were no differences in any recurrent BARC 2 to 5 bleeding (TT 14.5% [52/358] vs no TT of 10.6% [52/489]; IPW-HR: 1.41; 95% CI: 0.95-2.08; P = 0.087) or mortality (TT 14.0% [50/358] vs no TT 12.3% [60/489]; IPW-HR: 1.16; 95% CI: 0.79-1.70; P = 0.455). **CONCLUSIONS:** Among patients with prior bleeding before PCI, despite TT of the bleeding source, the risk of recurrent bleeding remained significantly higher at the identical location when compared with patients without TT, without a reduction in any recurrent bleeding or mortality after PCI. (CARDIOBASE Bern PCI Registry; NCT02241291).
