## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Most evidence on post-radiotherapy cardiotoxicity (CDT) derives from Global North cohorts, and the relative contribution of baseline clinical risk versus cardiac dosimetry remains poorly characterized in Latin American&#xa0;populations. **PURPOSE:** To examine, in a Chilean breast cancer cohort, whether baseline clinical risk factors or cardiac dose-volume metrics better predict post-RT cardiac events, using supervised and unsupervised&#xa0;learning. **METHODS:** Retrospective cohort of 42 breast cancer patients treated with thoracic radiotherapy (RT, median heart dose 2.6&#xa0;Gy; median follow-up 84.4&#xa0;months). CDT was defined per CTCAE&#xa0;v5.0 ( &#x2265; Grade&#xa0;1), excluding Grade&#xa0;1 events coded under the non-specific term "Cardiac disorders - Other, specify", which on review were incidental imaging findings. Dose-volume histogram (DVH) parameters from seven cardiac substructures were extracted. Firth penalized logistic regression was used for prediction, principal component analysis (PCA) and kernel PCA for exploratory clustering, and bootstrap 0.632+ for optimism correction. The 2023 American Heart Association (AHA) PREVENT score was evaluated post&#xa0;hoc. **RESULTS:** Ten patients (23.8%) developed CDT and 16 (38.1%) experienced cardiovascular events. No DVH differences were found between CDT+ and CDT- groups across any cardiac structure (all p &gt; 0.05 ), although the design is powered only for large effects. By contrast, IMRT delivered significantly higher cardiac doses than 3D-CRT across multiple substructures, without a differential CDT rate. Pre-existing cardiovascular disease was the strongest predictor (crude OR&#xa0;=&#xa0;6.50, p = 0.020 ; adjusted OR&#xa0;=&#xa0;2.08, 95%&#xa0;CI 1.03-4.16, p = 0.040 ). The median time to the first CTCAE-graded event was 48.4&#xa0;months. The clinical-only model reached an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.783 versus 0.604 for dosimetry alone; the combined model corrected to 0.649 (95% CI 0.204-0.933). The AHA PREVENT score showed no significant risk gradient (trend p = 0.203 ) but was markedly less susceptible to overfitting than DVH-only models (optimism 0.089 vs. 0.194). **CONCLUSIONS:** In this low-cardiac-dose cohort, baseline clinical variables outperformed cardiac dose-volume metrics in predicting post-RT cardiac events, although the design was not powered to exclude a dose-effect relationship at these exposures. Registry-based baseline risk assessment is therefore the more robust starting point in this setting, and IMRT breast RT planning warrants multi-substructure cardiac dosimetry&#xa0;monitoring.
