## Resumen

**BACKGROUND CONTEXT:** C5 nerve palsy is a common complication of cervical spine surgery. There is currently limited data evaluating prophylactic C4-5 foraminotomy on instrumented posterior cervical decompression and fusion (PCDF), where greater posterior cord migration may theoretically increase traction on the C5 nerve root. **PURPOSE:** This study aimed to compare perioperative outcomes between patients who underwent a C4-5 foraminotomy and those who did not, evaluating the effect of foraminotomy on postoperative C5 nerve palsy incidence, and describe its effects on C5 nerve palsy recovery. **STUDY DESIGN:** Retrospective chart review PATIENT SAMPLE: Patients undergoing PCDF from 2017 to 2022 with and without prophylactic C4-5 foraminotomy. **OUTCOME MEASURES:** C5 palsy incidence, degree of foraminal stenosis, and postoperative motor scores and recovery. **METHODS:** All patients who underwent PCDF with C4-5 foraminotomy were identified and matched 1:1 to control patients who underwent PCDF without C4-5 foraminotomy. Descriptive statistics and bivariate analysis were performed. Statistical significance was set at P&lt;0.05. **RESULTS:** A total of 176 patients were included (88 without foraminotomy and 88 with foraminotomy). After matching, there were no significant demographic or perioperative differences between groups. Foraminotomy patients had higher rates of foraminal stenosis (P=0.001). Foraminotomy was not associated with a lower rate of postoperative C5 nerve palsy incidence (P=0.429) or improved recovery among patients who developed C5 palsy at 6 and 12 months (P&gt;0.28). **CONCLUSION:** In patients undergoing PCDF, prophylactic C4-5 foraminotomy did not appear to be associated with reduced C5 palsy incidence or improved recovery if a palsy occurred. Further studies evaluating motor recovery in patients that underwent foraminotomy and sustained a C5 palsy are warranted.
