## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** To evaluate an otoscopy ensemble for four diagnostic categories in Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children and compare it with specialist consensus. **METHODS:** This retrospective study used 8006 eligible records from two tele-otology programs. Twelve architectures underwent five-fold cross-validation grouped by individual. An equal-weight ensemble was evaluated on Test Set 1 (1619 images) and compared with 13 otolaryngologists across three information tiers in single-site Deadly Ears Test Set 2 (80 rating items, 79 unique images, 65 children). Test Set 2 was designed for inter-rater agreement. Age and sex summaries differed between cohorts but used different denominators; Test Set 2 had more acute otitis media (AOM; 17.5% vs. 4.1%). **RESULTS:** Ensemble accuracy was 85.5% (1384/1619; 95% Wilson's CI 83.7%-87.1%; unweighted &#x3ba;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.778) on Test Set 1 and 86.3% (69/80; 77.0%-92.1%; &#x3ba;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.813) on Test Set 2. Specialist consensus accuracy was 83.8% with images alone, 86.3% with audiological data, and 92.5% with full clinical information. Seven of 23 otitis media with effusion (OME) items were classified as normal. These exploratory comparisons were not powered for superiority or equivalence. **CONCLUSION:** The ensemble showed promising accuracy in retrospective evaluation. The small, single-site comparator cohort and its differing characteristics limit generalization. OME errors and exclusion of uninterpretable images preclude clinical deployment. Prospective multisite validation and workflow evaluation are required.
