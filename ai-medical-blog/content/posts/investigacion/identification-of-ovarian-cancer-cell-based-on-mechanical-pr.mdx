## Resumen

Cellular mechanical properties are emerging as promising biomarkers for tumor diagnosis and for elucidating cancer-related mechanisms. However, most studies treat cells as mechanically homogeneous entities, overlooking regional heterogeneity and its implications for classification. Atomic force microscopy was used to characterize the mechanical properties of hepatocytes, mammary cells, ovarian cystadenoma cells, and SKOV3 ovarian cancer cells. Fluorescent staining was used to analyze the correlation between cytoskeletal topology parameters and mechanical properties. Logistic regression and support vector machine (SVM) classifiers were used to compare the performance of whole-cell, region-specific, and cross-regional models. Mechanical properties distinguished cells representing normal, benign, and malignant phenotypes. For ovarian cells, a model integrating multiple mechanical features achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.95. Region-specific models yielded AUCs of approximately 0.85, demonstrating pronounced spatial heterogeneity in cancer-cell mechanics and region-dependent remodeling of cytoskeletal branching architecture. In cross-cell-type analysis, an SVM trained on 7702/HepG2 achieved an AUC greater than 0.90 when applied to ovarian cells, indicating substantial generalizability of mechanical features across cell types. These results support the cross-cell type generalization of mechanical properties and suggest the potential feasibility of establishing a mechanical fingerprint reference across cell types. Furthermore, the difference between perinuclear and cytoplasmic regions reveals the complexity of mechanical regulation in ovarian cancer cells and provides a new perspective for understanding cellular dynamics.
