## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** Tumour necrosis factor (TNF) and interleukin (IL)-17 are central drivers of axial spondyloarthritis (AxSpA). We previously identified activated CD4+ tissue-resident memory Th17 cells as the principal source of IL-17A/F in AxSpA synovium. We sought to define the cellular source of TNF and mechanisms driving the CCL20+ myeloid phenotype in joint and blood. **METHODS:** Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) of synovial tissue from 5 patients with AxSpA was combined with immunofluorescence, synovial-fluid scRNA-seq, and flow cytometry, as well as comparisons with rheumatoid arthritis datasets. Monocyte-derived macrophages from healthy controls (HCs) and patients with AxSpA were cocultured with activated autologous CD4+ T cells, with blocking and cytokine experiments defining mechanisms of CCL20+ induction. Circulating monocytes were assessed by scRNA-seq and functional assays. **RESULTS:** CCL20+ macrophages were the predominant source of TNF in AxSpA synovium, with TNF protein confirmed in tissue and synovial fluid. Activated CD4+ T cells induced the CCL20+ phenotype through CD40L-CD40 signalling and an IL-1&#x3b2;/TNF-&#x3b1; autocrine circuit in HC-derived and AxSpA-derived cells. Baseline AxSpA monocytes showed a higher CCL20+ signature than HC monocytes, but this difference largely disappeared after T-cell-mediated induction. Under stimulation, AxSpA monocytes also produced more TNF, IL-6, IL-1&#x3b2;, and IL-23 than HC monocytes, a difference attenuated following IL-1/TNF priming. **CONCLUSIONS:** CCL20+ macrophages are a major CD40L- and IL-1&#x3b2;/TNF-&#x3b1;-driven source of TNF in AxSpA synovium. AxSpA circulating monocytes exhibit heightened basal CCL20+ signatures and inflammatory responsiveness that converge towards HC responses after inflammatory priming, supporting circulating CCL20+ monocytes as a marker of IL-1&#x3b2;/TNF-&#x3b1;-driven myeloid inflammation.
