## Resumen

Fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23) is a bone-derived hormone whose activity is coordinately regulated by proteolysis, phosphorylation, and glycosylation. The protein &#x3b1;-Klotho and the glycosaminoglycan heparan sulfate cooperate to promote asymmetric receptor dimerization, providing a structural basis for targeted intervention. Excessive FGF23 signaling can cause renal phosphate wasting and impaired bone mineralization. In chronic kidney disease (CKD), early elevations in FGF23 serve a compensatory role, whereas sustained exposure to high levels may contribute to myocardial injury. The clinical use of burosumab has demonstrated the therapeutic value of FGF23 neutralization in X-linked hypophosphatemia (XLH) and tumor-induced osteomalacia (TIO). C-terminal peptide antagonists and small-molecule inhibitors offer additional approaches to modulating FGF23 signaling. However, their development faces challenges including short peptide half-lives, insufficient selectivity of receptor inhibitors, and limited evidence on the long-term safety of investigational agents. This review summarizes the post-translational modifications and signaling mechanisms of FGF23. We highlight the structural basis for asymmetric receptor dimerization cooperatively mediated by &#x3b1;-Klotho and heparan sulfate, as well as the distinct physiological and pathological roles of FGF23 across disease contexts. Finally, we discuss the mechanisms of action and limitations to clinical translation of the FGF23-neutralizing antibody burosumab, C-terminal peptide antagonists, and targeted small-molecule inhibitors. These insights provide a mechanistic foundation for developing next-generation therapeutics that precisely modulate FGF23 signaling.
