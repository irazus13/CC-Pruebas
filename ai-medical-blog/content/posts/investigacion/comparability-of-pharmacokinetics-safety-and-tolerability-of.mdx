## Resumen

This phase 1, randomized, open-label, single-dose, parallel-group study aimed to describe the pharmacokinetic (PK) comparability and safety of canakinumab administered as a single 200&#xa0;mg subcutaneous injection via pre-filled syringes assembled within a needle safety device (PFS-NSD) by HCP and auto-injector (AI) by subject self-administration versus two injections of 150&#xa0;+&#xa0;50&#xa0;mg via PFS-NSD by HCP. Healthy adults (18-55 years) were allocated to three arms: canakinumab 150&#xa0;+&#xa0;50&#xa0;mg (PFS-NSD; n = 78), canakinumab 200&#xa0;mg (AI; n = 76), and canakinumab 200&#xa0;mg (PFS-NSD; n = 76). Multiplicity was addressed as two tests were compared with the same reference. Primary PK parameters were comparable across arms. The geometric mean ratios and 95% CI values of AUCinf, AUClast, and Cmax were within bioequivalence limits of 0.80-1.25 for both comparisons: (a) canakinumab 200&#xa0;mg AI versus canakinumab 150 + 50&#xa0;mg PFS-NSD and (b) canakinumab 200&#xa0;mg PFS-NSD versus canakinumab 150&#xa0;+&#xa0;50&#xa0;mg PFS-NSD. Median Tmax was comparable: 7, 9, and 7 days for canakinumab 150&#xa0;+&#xa0;50&#xa0;mg PFS-NSD, 200&#xa0;mg AI, and 200&#xa0;mg PFS-NSD, respectively. Exploratory post-hoc analyses, assessing the impact of pause during the pandemic (N&#xa0;=&#xa0;131 subjects recruited pre-pandemic) and evaluating partial AUC4weeks, suggested similar results. Canakinumab was well-tolerated, and no treatment-induced immunogenicity was observed.
