## Resumen

Background Mild hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE) comprises approximately 30%-50% of neonatal encephalopathy cases, yet neurodevelopmental outcomes remain difficult to predict using clinical assessment alone. Purpose To develop and externally test a diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI)-based machine learning model for predicting 24-month neurodevelopmental outcomes in neonates with mild HIE. Materials and Methods This prospective four-hospital study consecutively enrolled neonates from November 2019 through November 2023, with 24-month follow-up completed in November 2025. The primary outcome was adverse neurodevelopment at 24 months, defined as any Bayley Scales of Infant and Toddler Development, third edition, composite score below 85; cerebral palsy; or epilepsy requiring ongoing treatment. Clinical-only, imaging-only, and combined clinical-imaging models were developed from 49 DKI parameters and seven clinical variables. Model discrimination was assessed with the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), and curves were compared with paired DeLong tests and false discovery rate adjustment. Results Overall, 740 neonates were included (mean gestational age, 38.3 weeks &#xb1; 1.3 [SD]; 404 [54.6%] female). Adverse outcomes occurred in 182 of 510 development cohort participants (35.7%) and 80 of 230 external test set participants (34.8%). In the external test set, the combined model achieved an AUC of 0.92 (95% CI: 0.89, 0.95), with a sensitivity of 86% (69 of 80) and specificity of 85.3% (128 of 150). It exceeded the clinical-only model (AUC, 0.85; false discovery rate-adjusted P = .004) and imaging-only model (AUC, 0.86; false discovery rate-adjusted P = .005). Prespecified risk stratification yielded a negative predictive value of 100% (61 of 61) for low-risk infants and a positive predictive value of 88% (38 of 43) for high-risk infants. Conclusion In neonates with mild HIE, a combined clinical and DKI model demonstrated higher discrimination for predicting 24-month neurodevelopmental outcomes than did clinical-only or imaging-only models. &#xa9; The Author(s) 2026.
