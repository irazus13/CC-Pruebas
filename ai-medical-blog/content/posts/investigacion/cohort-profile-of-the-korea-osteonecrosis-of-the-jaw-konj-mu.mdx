## Resumen

**INTRODUCTION:** We describe the establishment and profile of the Korea Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (KONJ) cohort, an ongoing multicenter registry and biobank for longitudinal clinical and omics research on medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ). **MATERIAL AND METHODS:** Initiated in 2018 and expanded to four Korean tertiary centers, KONJ combined a Main clinical registry of retrospective and prospective records with a prospectively enrolled Core cohort. A web-based registry captured medication histories, dental events, disease status, and treatment. The Core protocol specified sampling at diagnosis or around surgery and post-cure for MRONJ. Tissue collection depended on the procedure. Staging followed American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons criteria. **RESULTS:** At the 30 August data lock, KONJ comprised 982 unique patients, including 389 in the Core cohort. The clinical analysis included 702 patients: 477 with MRONJ, 200 at risk, and 25 with stage 0 findings. Among patients with MRONJ (mean age, 74.7 &#xb1; 9.7 years), 90.6% were women and 84.5% had osteoporosis. Denosumab was the most frequent documented agent (26.8%), and stage 2 accounted for 74.2%. Tooth extraction and dental implants were associated with MRONJ in 43.4% and 20.8%, respectively, and 93.7% underwent surgery. First-timepoint specimens were collected from 185/207 Core participants with MRONJ and 76/86 at-risk participants. **DISCUSSION:** KONJ combines ongoing clinical registration with protocol-defined biospecimen sampling at clinically relevant time points. MRONJ in this tertiary-center cohort predominantly affected older women with osteoporosis. It provides a foundation for clinical, biomarker, and omics studies of MRONJ development, treatment, and healing.
