## Resumen

The diagnosis of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) requires clinical evidence of both upper motor neuron (UMN) and lower motor neuron degeneration. Some UMN-predominant ALS patients demonstrate intracranial corticospinal tract (CST) hyperintensity (ALS-CST+) on T2- and proton density-weighted images, and exhibit faster disease progression than those without CST hyperintensity (ALS-CST-). We analyzed 149 radiomic features from brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) obtained at 1.5&#x202f;T for routine clinical evaluation. This study examined whether grey matter (GM) or white matter features classify ALS-CST+ and ALS-CST- patients. MRI T1-weighted and diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) scans were acquired in 15 neurologic controls and 47 UMN-predominant ALS patients identified as ALS-CST+ or ALS-CST-. Monofractal and multifractal (MF) analyses were performed on GM and CST. Using DTI, we conducted graph theory network analysis, CST tractography, and perivascular space analyses. The 149 radiomic measures, along with age and gender were included as features in machine learning (ML) classifiers. Across eight ML algorithms, XGBoost classified ALS subgroups from controls and among themselves with 98% accuracy, sensitivity, specificity and F1 score. Local interpretable model-agnostic explanations method identified variables of importance. MF features, specifically frontal lobe GM capacity dimension D(Q)&#x202f;=&#x202f;0 and cerebellar &#x394;&#x3b1;, which reflects MF spectrum width, played a vital role in subgroup classification. Our results suggest that GM in these two regions is differentially affected in ALS subgroups and that MF analysis offers advantages over cortical thickness or voxel-based morphometry. Such MF measures may serve as non-invasive neuroimaging biomarkers for diagnosing UMN-predominant ALS patients.
