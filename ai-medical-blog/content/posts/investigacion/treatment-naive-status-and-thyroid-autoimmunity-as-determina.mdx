## Resumen

ObjectiveEarly biochemical thyroid outcomes after iodine-131 (131I) therapy for Graves' disease are heterogeneous. We aimed to identify baseline determinants of 3-month biochemical outcome and assess whether the FT3/FT4 ratio, thyrotroph thyroxine resistance index (TT4RI), or machine-learning algorithms added predictive value.MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed 637 consecutive patients with clinically documented Graves' disease treated with 131I between November 2022 and November 2024. Graves' disease was identified from contemporaneous endocrinologist-recorded diagnoses rather than uniform thyrotropin-receptor antibody confirmation. Three-month thyroid status was classified as persistent overt hyperthyroidism, euthyroidism, or hypothyroidism using institutional biochemical reference ranges; remission was defined as euthyroidism or hypothyroidism. A prespecified multivariable logistic model included age, sex, treatment-naive status, disease duration, goiter grade, administered dose, 24-hour radioactive iodine uptake, thyroid peroxidase antibody (TPOAb), and thyroglobulin antibody. Cross-validated prediction models assessed derived indices and machine-learning algorithms.ResultsAt 3 months, 464 patients (72.8%) achieved remission and 369 (57.9%) were hypothyroid. Treatment-naive status and higher TPOAb were independently associated with remission (OR 1.67, 95% CI 1.06-2.62; TPOAb OR 1.11 per log-unit, 1.01-1.23) and hypothyroidism (OR 1.95, 1.29-2.97; TPOAb OR 1.16, 1.06-1.27). Lower goiter grade and higher administered dose were also associated with both outcomes. Discrimination was modest (remission AUC 0.59; hypothyroidism AUC 0.62). Derived indices and machine-learning algorithms did not consistently improve clinically meaningful prediction; an isolated positive continuous NRI for hypothyroidism was not supported by improved AUC or IDI. By 6 months, 273 patients, all hypothyroid at 3 months, had not returned; among reassessed 3-month hypothyroid patients, 21% reverted to euthyroidism.ConclusionsTreatment-naive status and TPOAb were associated with early biochemical outcome after 131I therapy, but baseline prediction remained limited. Early hypothyroidism should be interpreted as a 3-month biochemical state rather than definitive ablation.
