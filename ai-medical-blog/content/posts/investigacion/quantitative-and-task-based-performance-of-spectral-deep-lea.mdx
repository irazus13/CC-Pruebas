## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Coronary CT angiography (CCTA) is an established noninvasive test for coronary artery disease, but balancing image quality and radiation dose remains challenging. Dual-layer spectral CT (DLCT) enables reconstruction of virtual monoenergetic images (VMI) that enhance coronary artery contrast, but are inherently more sensitive to noise, making this trade-off especially critical. Deep learning reconstruction (DLR) improves image quality and enables radiation dose reduction in CCTA, but its performance on spectral reconstructions from dual-layer CT (DLCT) remains unexplored. **PURPOSE:** This phantom study compares spectral DLR at five different noise reduction strengths with hybrid-iterative reconstruction (hybrid-IR) at one fixed, clinically used noise reduction strength across conventional and VMI at multiple reduced dose levels. **METHODS:** An anthropomorphic thorax phantom with a coronary insert containing two calcifications (100 and 800&#xa0;mg CaHA/mL) was scanned on a DLCT system at six dose levels (volumetric CT dose index 12.2-1.4 mGy) using a clinical sequential CCTA protocol. Data were reconstructed with one clinical hybrid-IR protocol, and DLR at five noise reduction strengths (Smoother, Smooth, Standard, Sharp, Sharper) for conventional images and VMI at 7 energy levels (40-70&#xa0;keV). Image noise, luminal contrast-to-noise ratio (CNRlumen), noise power spectrum means frequency (NPSmean), task transfer function at 50% (TTF50), and detectability index (d') of coronary plaques were assessed. Statistical significance was inferred when 95% confidence intervals did not overlap. **RESULTS:** At full dose, DLR significantly reduced noise up to 64% in conventional images and 67% in 40&#xa0;keV VMI, while increasing CNRlumen up to 300% and 230%, respectively, compared with hybrid-IR. DLR Standard, Sharp, and Sharper significantly increased NPSmean by 7%-13% for conventional images and 40&#xa0;keV VMI, whereas DLR Smoother shifted noise toward lower frequencies, reducing NPSmean by 22%-32%. All DLR strengths significantly improved spatial resolution of calcified plaque, with TTF50 increasing 9%-29% relative to hybrid-IR. VMI at 40&#xa0;keV with DLR significantly increased d' by 307% for high-density and 283% for low-density calcifications compared to conventional hybrid-IR. At 50% dose reduction, VMI at 40&#xa0;keV with DLR Smooth improved CNRlumen (+ 46%), and TTF50 (+ 18%) and d' (+ 24%) of the high-density calcification relative to full-dose hybrid-IR 40&#xa0;keV VMI, whereas confidence intervals overlapped for NPSmean and for TTF50 and d' of the low-density calcification. **CONCLUSIONS:** On dual-layer CT, spectral DLR outperforms hybrid-IR on both conventional and VMI images in noise, CNR, spatial resolution, and plaque detectability. At 50% dose reduction, DLR Smooth on 40&#xa0;keV VMI exceeded full-dose hybrid-IR on 40&#xa0;keV VMI in CNR, and in spatial resolution and detectability of high-density calcifications, with overlapping confidence intervals for noise texture and for low-density calcifications. Patient studies are needed to validate clinical translation.
