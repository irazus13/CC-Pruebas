## Resumen

Osteoarthritis (OA) affects more than 500 million people worldwide, yet disease-modifying therapies remain unavailable. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have transformed understanding of OA genetic susceptibility, progressing from early single-cohort analyses to multi-ancestry meta-analyses involving 2 million individuals and prioritizing hundreds of loci and 700 effector genes. This review summarizes the development of OA GWAS and examines how genetic discoveries are being translated into biological mechanisms and precision medicine. We discuss how integrating GWAS with fine-mapping, molecular quantitative trait loci (QTLs), chromatin interaction maps, single-cell and spatial omics, proteomics and metabolomics links non-coding signals to effector genes, regulatory programs and disease-relevant cell states. These genes converge on biological modules involving developmental patterning, extracellular matrix homeostasis, metabolism, immune-inflammatory signaling, circadian regulation and neuro-glial pathways, linking inherited susceptibility to joint structure, tissue homeostasis and symptoms. We further review translational applications, including Mendelian randomization, GWAS-informed drug target prioritization, polygenic risk scores for risk stratification and precision prevention, and AI-enabled multimodal models for phenotype refinement, subgrouping and trial enrichment. Finally, we highlight key challenges, including phenotype heterogeneity, ancestry imbalance, incomplete variant-to-function mapping and limited clinical validation. Addressing these challenges is essential for translating OA GWAS into globally relevant, mechanism-based and precision-oriented prevention and treatment.
