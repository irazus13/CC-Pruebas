## Resumen

ObjectiveSupraglottic airway devices are alternatives to endotracheal intubation under general anesthesia. However, their performance in older patients remains understudied, particularly in different head positions.MethodsThis prospective randomized controlled study included 120 patients aged &#x2265;75 years undergoing elective urologic surgery. Patients were randomly allocated into four groups based on the device type and head position during insertion: (a) i-gel with neutral head position; (b) i-gel with right head rotation; (c) classic laryngeal mask airway with neutral head position; and (d) classic laryngeal mask airway with right head rotation. The primary outcome was the insertion time. Secondary outcomes included insertion attempts, oropharyngeal leak pressure, and perioperative variables, which were assessed in patients with successful device placement.ResultsThe overall insertion success rate was 94.2% (113/120). The i-gel was associated with a significantly shorter insertion time and higher oropharyngeal leak pressure than the classic laryngeal mask airway (both p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.001). Two-way factorial analysis revealed that device type significantly influenced insertion time and oropharyngeal leak pressure, whereas head position and device-by-head position interaction exerted no significant effects. No significant differences were observed in the number of insertion attempts, fiberoptic view scores, peak airway pressure, recovery times, hemodynamic variables, or perioperative and postoperative complications.ConclusionsIn patients aged &#x2265;75 years undergoing elective surgery, the i-gel was associated with a shorter insertion time and higher oropharyngeal leak pressure than the classic laryngeal mask airway. Head position did not significantly influence insertion time, oropharyngeal leak pressure, or peak airway pressure. These findings suggest that airway device selection exerts a greater impact on supraglottic airway performance than head position, whereas the i-gel may offer practical advantages in selected clinical settings.Trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov (https://clinicaltrials.gov), Identifier: NCT07027293, registered on 27 May 2025.
