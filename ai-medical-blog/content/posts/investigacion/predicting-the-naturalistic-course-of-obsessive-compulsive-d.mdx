## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) during adolescence follows heterogeneous trajectories, from remission to persistence. Early identification of these patterns is crucial for optimizing clinical management. This study examined whether behavior symptoms or neuroimaging data could predict the persistence of OCD diagnosis at two-year follow-up. **METHODS:** 492 children between 9-10 years old with OCD were selected from Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) study. They were classified as persisters or remitters by clinical status at two year follow-up. Baseline Child Behavior Checklist (CBCL) item scores and multimodality MRI measures were used to examine behavioral and neuroimaging differences associated with follow-up OCD diagnostic status. We further applied machine learning models to predict individual symptom persistence using CBCL scores alone, MRI features alone from single modality or multimodal data, and combined CBCL and MRI features. **RESULTS:** Group-level CBCL and neuroimaging analyses identified significant behavioral, structural and functional brain differences at baseline between persisters and remitters. The best-performing CBCL model demonstrated limited discriminative ability (AUCbest&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.638). In comparison, the best MRI-derived model, utilizing resting-state fMRI (rsfMRI) features, performed slightly above chance (AUCbest&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.577). Integrating rsfMRI features with CBCL improved performance only marginally and non-significantly (AUCbest&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.665). Integrating rsfMRI features with CBCL did not significantly outperform the CBCL-only model, although the performance was numerically higher (AUCbest&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.665). Feature-importance analyses suggested that the most clinically informative signals were compulsive behavior and functional connectivity linking large scale networks with widely distributed subcortical regions. **CONCLUSION:** These findings suggest that OCD persistence follow-up OCD outcome is related to distinct clinical symptoms and brain measures. However, the accuracy to predict diagnosis is limited, indicating that OCD persistence naturalistic course is difficult to predict from clinical and neuroimaging data evaluated here.
