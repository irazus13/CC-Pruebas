## Resumen

**INTRODUCTION:** A positive blood culture is a clinically heterogeneous finding that may represent true bloodstream infection, transient bacteremia or contamination-diagnostic uncertainty that complicates early clinical decision-making. We aimed to develop and evaluate machine learning models to predict 30-day all-cause mortality in adults with a positive blood culture. **METHODS:** Retrospective cohort study using MIMIC-IV. Adult hospitalizations with at least one positive blood culture were included (first positive culture per admission); potential contaminants were retained to reflect real-world uncertainty. Demographic, clinical, laboratory, organ support and microbiological variables were used as predictors. Nine supervised machine learning algorithms were trained with repeated 10-fold cross-validation, evaluated in an independent test cohort and calibrated; thresholds were optimized with the Youden index. **RESULTS:** Of 5731 episodes, 742 patients (12.9%) died within 30 days. Test-set ROC AUC ranged from 0.759 to 0.801. After threshold optimization, the random forest model showed the best overall performance (sensitivity 0.788, specificity 0.707, balanced accuracy 0.748; Brier score 0.0999, calibration slope 1.03). ICU admission at onset, mechanical ventilation, bilirubin, urea and the Charlson comorbidity index were the main predictors. **CONCLUSIONS:** Machine learning models can provide clinically meaningful early prediction of 30-day mortality in patients with a positive blood culture using routinely available data. Focusing on the moment of blood culture positivity rather than established sepsis syndromes, this approach supports early risk stratification in a diagnostically heterogeneous population.
