## Resumen

The postpartum period involves substantial brain changes, but whether these represent pathological aging or adaptive plasticity remains unclear. We applied machine learning-based brain age prediction to quantify postpartum brain aging trajectories in a controlled longitudinal study. Brain age gap, the difference between MRI-predicted and chronological age, was assessed in 55 women (25 postpartum and 30 nulliparous controls). Postpartum women were assessed at approximately 3 and 12&#x2009;months postpartum; controls were assessed at matched intervals. Postpartum women showed elevated brain age gap at 3&#x2009;months (2.5&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;2.7&#x2009;years) compared with controls (0.3&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;3.6&#x2009;years; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.01, Cohen's d&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.69), which normalized by 12&#x2009;months. Within-group analysis revealed significant brain age gap decrease in postpartum women (-3.3&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;3.5&#x2009;years; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.008), while controls showed minimal change; the between-group difference in longitudinal change did not reach statistical significance (p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.12, Cohen's d&#x2009;=&#x2009;-0.58). Across all participants, brain age gap correlated with working memory performance (&#x3c1;&#x2009;=&#x2009;-0.30; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.03), depressive symptoms (&#x3c1;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.38; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.004), and subjective cognitive complaints (&#x3c1;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.28; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.04) at baseline, providing preliminary evidence of clinical relevance. Explainability analysis using Shapley additive explanations identified subcortical structures as the network most selectively associated with the postpartum elevation in brain age gap at 3&#x2009;months (p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.006, Cohen's d&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.82), with normalization by 12&#x2009;months. Together, these findings indicate transient brain age gap elevation in postpartum women without statistical confirmation of a trajectory differing from controls. With brain age gap providing a quantitative index of postpartum brain structural change, these preliminary findings are consistent with adaptive neuroplasticity, suggesting that such change may be temporary rather than reflecting permanent impairment.
