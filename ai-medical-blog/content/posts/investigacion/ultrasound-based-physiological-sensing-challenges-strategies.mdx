## Resumen

Ultrasound is well suited to repeated deep-tissue imaging, but it rarely measures local biochemical conditions directly. Stimuli-responsive chemistry enables materials to respond to physiological cues through physicochemical changes, yet these changes are not necessarily detectable by ultrasound. Multiscale structural organization can bridge this gap by translating and amplifying molecular or nanoscale responses into larger-scale material changes that produce detectable acoustic signals. The field is progressing from proof-of-concept demonstrations of material responses towards integrated sensing platforms. Advances across these platforms include bioresorption, multiparametric sensing, and repeated measurements over defined periods in animal models. This review examines how responsive chemistry and multiscale structural design jointly enable ultrasound-readable physiological sensing. It also considers the requirements for reliable operation in vivo. Looking ahead, integration with wearable and conformal ultrasound interfaces, soft robotic systems, and AI-assisted decoding could enable longitudinal, personalized, and multiparametric sensing across internal organs and tissue interfaces.
