## Resumen

Cardiovascular ultrasound is particularly suitable for artificial intelligence: it is widely available, information-rich, dynamic, and strongly dependent on acquisition quality and operator interpretation. Contemporary computational methods can guide image acquisition, recognize views, segment chambers, quantify ventricular function, characterize myocardial mechanics, and detect disease signatures. In heart failure, multimodal architectures can combine echocardiographic images and Doppler data with clinical variables, biomarkers, electrocardiograms, and omics to support patient-level phenotyping and prognostic assessment, while emerging foundation models enable adaptation across multiple tasks. The most credible near-term model is augmented echocardiography: automation of repetitive measurements combined with transparent quality control and expert clinical interpretation.
