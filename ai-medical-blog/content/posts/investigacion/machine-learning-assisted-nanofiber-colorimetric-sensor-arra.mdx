## Resumen

The detection of foodborne pathogens constitutes a public health imperative, yet conventional methods often lack the simplicity and non-destructive capability required for practical applications. This study presents a new strategy that integrates a nanofiber mat-based colorimetric sensor array (NFMs-based CSA) with machine learning (ML) to discriminate high-risk foodborne pathogens via their representative volatile organic compounds (VOCs). Electrospun polyacrylonitrile and polyamide 6 NFMs serve as sensor carriers, offering high porosity, large specific surface area and tunable surface chemistry, which collectively enhance the sensitivity and responsiveness. Under simulated cold-chain storage at 4&#x202f;&#xb0;C, the NFMs-based CSA generates distinct colorimetric fingerprint patterns in response to characteristic VOCs released from Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, Salmonella enterica and Listeria monocytogenes inoculated into pork samples. Among the employed ML algorithms, the random forest model achieved 100% classification accuracy across all test groups, with clear differentiation between the control and each pathogen-inoculated sample. This work establishes a non-destructive and robust analytical platform for the discrimination among four foodborne pathogens, highlighting its potential for food-safety surveillance applications.
