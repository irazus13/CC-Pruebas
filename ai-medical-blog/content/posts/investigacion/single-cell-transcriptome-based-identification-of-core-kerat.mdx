## Resumen

Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic relapsing inflammatory skin disorder driven by epidermal barrier impairment and keratinocyte dysfunction. Current therapies are limited by insufficient target specificity and high recurrence rates. This study aimed to identify keratinocyte-derived biomarkers and develop novel targeted agents for precise diagnosis and treatment of AD. Single-cell RNA sequencing and weighted gene co-expression network analysis were performed to screen for key AD biomarkers, and machine learning algorithms with ROC curve analysis were applied to evaluate their diagnostic performance. Immunohistochemistry confirmed LYPD3 protein upregulation in AD lesional skin. In silico knockdown simulations at single-cell resolution elucidated LYPD3-mediated signaling pathways. Structure-based virtual screening and molecular dynamics simulations identified candidate LYPD3-targeted compounds, and cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA) and biolayer interferometry (BLI) validated target engagement and direct binding. An in vitro keratinocyte inflammation model and an in vivo MC903 (calcipotriol)-induced AD mouse model were established to assess therapeutic efficacy. LYPD3, KRT6C, and FABP5 were specifically overexpressed in AD keratinocytes, showed excellent diagnostic performance that was validated in a fully independent external cohort, and correlated closely with cutaneous immune infiltration. LYPD3 depletion disrupted epidermal barrier integrity and keratinocyte differentiation pathways. CETSA confirmed target engagement, and BLI demonstrated direct binding between Rebastinib and LYPD3. Rebastinib inhibited keratinocyte hyperproliferation and inflammation in vitro and significantly ameliorated epidermal lesions while suppressing Th2-type inflammatory responses in AD mice. These findings identify three keratinocyte-specific AD biomarkers and support Rebastinib as a promising LYPD3-targeted therapeutic candidate, offering new insights into precision diagnosis and targeted therapy of AD.
