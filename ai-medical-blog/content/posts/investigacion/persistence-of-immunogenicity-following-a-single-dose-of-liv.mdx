## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** The long-term immunogenicity of a single dose of live-attenuated hepatitis A virus (HAV) vaccine has previously been demonstrated, with substantial seroprotection maintained for up to 15 years after vaccination. The present study further evaluated the persistence of immunogenicity and seroprotection 20 years after vaccination. **METHODS:** This single-center follow-up study assessed the immunogenicity of a single dose of H2-strain live-attenuated hepatitis A vaccine (BIOVAC-A) in participants who received the vaccine in 2004. Any participant who had received any additional dose of hepatitis A vaccine was excluded. At 20-year follow-up, total anti-HAV antibodies were estimated using enzyme-linked fluorescence assay (ELFA) and anti-HAV IgM were estimated using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Seroprotection rates were assessed using two cutoff levels for total anti-HAV antibodies (&#x2265;20&#x2009;mIU/mL and &#x2265;10&#x2009;mIU/mL). Trends in geometric mean titers (GMTs) of anti-HAV antibodies over the last 20-years were evaluated. **RESULTS:** Of the 143 participants vaccinated in 2004, 99 could be followed up after 20 years. 12 were excluded and 87 participants (64.4% males), with mean (SD) age of 24.6 (2.6) years, were enrolled in the 20-year follow-up assessment. Seroprotection rates were 71.3% and 87.4% at anti-HAV cutoffs of &#x2265;20 mIU/mL and &#x2265;10 mIU/mL, respectively. None of the participants were positive for anti-HAV IgM and no breakthrough HAV infections were reported. **CONCLUSION:** A single&#xa0;dose of live attenuated HAV vaccine confers a durable protection and supports the potential utility of a single-dose strategy in endemic settings.
