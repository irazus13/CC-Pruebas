## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** The predictive role of the Estimated Insulin Sensitivity (e-IS) index, serving as a surrogate marker for insulin resistance, in cardiovascular disease (CVD) and mechanisms remains underexplored in nondiabetic populations. We aimed to evaluate the association of e-IS with CVD incidence and its predictive utility for CVD risk. **METHODS:** We analyzed a national prospective cohort of participants without CVD or diabetes at baseline from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study and an external validation cohort from the Second Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University. We used Cox proportional hazards models and restricted cubic splines to explore relations. Mediation analyses were performed to explore whether inflammation and kidney disease mediated the relation between e-IS and CVD outcomes. In addition, we used machine learning to develop an e-IS-based CVD prediction model, which underwent external validation. **RESULTS:** A total of 1,083 incident CVD cases occurred. Lower e-IS levels were associated with greater CVD risk, with no evidence of a nonlinear association based on restricted cubic splines. Mediation analysis revealed that high-sensitivity C-reactive protein and estimated glomerular filtration rate accounted for 3.06% and 5.47% of the association with CVD, respectively. Integrating e-IS into a basic model significantly improved the concordance statistic for CVD (0.708 vs 0.684) and yielded robust net reclassification (net reclassification improvement, 0.318; P &lt; .001). The machine learning model achieved area under the curve values of 0.738 for CVD, 0.754 for heart disease, and 0.719 for stroke, respectively, with consistent external validation. **CONCLUSIONS:** Our findings indicate that e-IS might be an independent predictor of CVD in nondiabetic populations, mediated in part by high-sensitivity C-reactive protein and estimated glomerular filtration rate.
