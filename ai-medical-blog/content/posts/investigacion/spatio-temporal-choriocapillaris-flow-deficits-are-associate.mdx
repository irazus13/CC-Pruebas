## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** To assess whether baseline choriocapillaris (CC) flow deficits (FD%) are associated with local geographic atrophy (GA) progression-conversion of retina outside existing loss (OL) to ellipsoid zone loss (EZL) and retinal pigment epithelium loss (RPEL), distinguishing observed direct (OL to RPEL) from observed sequential (OL to EZL to RPEL) transitions-and with visual function. **METHODS:** In a prospective cohort (2017-2024), optical coherence tomography (OCT), OCT angiography, and best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) were evaluated annually. Transitions were modeled with patient-clustered cause-specific Cox and generalized additive models. Gradient-boosted classifiers assessed prediction with and without FD%. Visual function used visit-level mixed models. **RESULTS:** We analyzed 102 eyes (58 patients). Higher baseline FD% was associated with earlier conversion in all pathways. The gradient was steepest for observed direct OL to RPEL conversion (time-averaged hazard ratio = 3.94; 95% confidence interval [CI], 3.16-4.92; FD% &gt;30% vs. &lt;10%; 6.13 at one year, 1.91 at five years) versus OL to EZL (2.30) and EZL to RPEL (1.83). Machine-learning models discriminated well (area under the curve = 0.84-0.93) without incremental value from FD%. Among 83 eyes with at least two BCVA measurements, higher FD% was associated with faster decline and earlier &#x2265;15-letter loss in foveal-sparing eyes after adjustment and multiplicity control; estimates in non-foveal-sparing eyes were imprecise and opposite in direction. **CONCLUSIONS:** CC flow deficits are associated with local GA progression, especially observed direct conversion, and with visual decline in foveal-sparing eyes. FD% added limited predictive value beyond structural biomarkers but remained an independent vascular correlate of conversion risk that may complement OCT-based risk stratification.
