## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** The aims of this study were to investigate audiology students' experiences of using an artificial intelligence (AI)-enabled virtual client platform (VCP) that simulated interactions with adult audiology clients within a simulated learning environment (SLE) and to examine changes in self-reported confidence and anxiety following participation. **METHOD:** Twenty-six students enrolled in a Master of Audiology Studies program completed four weekly 1-day SLE placements incorporating AI-enabled VCP simulations of adult audiology appointments. Participants completed surveys before and after the simulations assessing confidence in communicating with clients and anxiety related to interacting with real clients and computer simulations. Postsimulation surveys also examined perceptions of the AI VCP and included open-ended questions. Quantitative data were analyzed using nonparametric statistics, and qualitative responses were examined using a simple content analysis. **RESULTS:** Students reported significantly increased confidence postsimulation in the areas of interacting professionally and ethically, meeting and greeting clients, obtaining case histories, and using interviewing skills during case-history taking. Reported anxiety levels were low before and after simulation, and only the reduction in anxiety about using simulations to practice providing feedback reached significance. Students perceived their experience of the AI VCP simulation positively, including feeling well prepared for its use and finding it useful for reinforcing skills learned in lectures. Qualitative responses highlighted benefits related to communication skills, case-history taking, clinical decision making, confidence building, and flexible opportunities for practice. **CONCLUSIONS:** AI-enabled VCPs appear to be a useful adjunct to audiology education, particularly for developing confidence in professional communication, client engagement, and case-history-taking skills. Further research should determine whether these perceived benefits translate into objective improvements in clinical performance.
