## Resumen

RNA molecules adopt diverse secondary structures for distinct functions. Among them, the left-handed RNA double helix (Z-RNA) regulates innate immunity and has been implicated in autoimmune disorders. However, accurate, fine-scale Z-RNome profiling remain lacking, and its role in autoimmune diseases like systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is poorly understood. Here we develop AMAZE-seq for fine-scale profiling of the human Z-RNome. AMAZE-seq leverages the Z&#x3b1; domain for specific recognition of Z-RNA and a fused APOBEC1 enzyme to edit Z-RNA regions for subsequent detection. Using AMAZE-seq, we reveal that Z-RNAs predominantly form on intramolecular double-stranded RNA rich in pyrimidine/purine alternations, with nearly half originating from non-repetitive regions, substantially expanding the known repertoire of Z-RNA-forming regions. Knockout and rescue experiments support the role of ADAR1 p150 in suppressing Z-RNA through both binding and RNA editing. AMAZE-seq profiling of SLE patient-derived cells reveals a dysregulated Z-RNome. Mechanistically, elevated Z-RNA formation activates ZBP1 and induces RIPK3-MLKL-mediated necroptosis and caspase-dependent apoptosis, potentially triggering the release of immunogenic Z-RNA species and aberrant immune responses in SLE patients. Our results define the landscape, features, regulatory mechanisms, and disease relevance of Z-RNA, establishing AMAZE-seq as a transformative tool for dissecting Z-RNA biology in health and disease.
