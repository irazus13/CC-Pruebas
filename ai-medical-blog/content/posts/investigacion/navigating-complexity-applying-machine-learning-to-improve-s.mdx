## Resumen

**INTRODUCTION:** Shock wave lithotripsy (SWL) remains a cornerstone of non-invasive urolithiasis treatment, yet its success is highly variable and difficult to predict using traditional linear statistical models. Traditional models often fail to account for the complex, non-linear interactions between stone characteristics and procedural variables. This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of machine learning (ML) algorithms in predicting stone-free status (SFS), post-SWL renal colic, and the requirement for subsequent ureteroscopy (URS). **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** A retrospective analysis was conducted on a multicentric database of 1498 patients treated with SWL over a six-year period. A total of 23 clinical features, including patient demographics, stone characteristics (size, density, and location), and surgical parameters, were utilized as inputs. We compared 15 different ML algorithms (including LightGBM, CatBoost, and Random Forest) and a multitask Artificial Neural Network against conventional logistic regression. Models were evaluated using validation accuracy, confusion matrices, cross-imbalance metrics (AUC-ROC, AUPRC), and F1-scores. "SFS" was defined as the primary endpoint, while secondary endpoints were represented by "Need for secondary URS" and occurrence of "Post-SWL colic". **RESULTS:** On logistic regression analysis, stone characteristics (size, density) resulted as the most important predictors of treatment failure. Treatment parameters (sessions, power) reflected procedural difficulty, and SWL success decreases with increasing stone complexity. ML models consistently outperformed traditional logistic regression across all endpoints. For the primary endpoint (SFS), LightGBM achieved an AUC-ROC of 0.83 (95% CI: 0.79-0.86) and an AUPRC of 0.88 (95% CI: 0.84-0.91). For secondary endpoints, the Need for secondary URS was predicted with high discrimination by LightGBM (AUC-ROC&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.92, 95% CI: 0.89-0.95; AUPRC&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.78, 95% CI: 0.71-0.84) and CatBoost (AUC-ROC&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.89, 95% CI: 0.85-0.92; AUPRC&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.73, 95% CI: 0.65-0.80). For Post-SWL colic (2.7% prevalence), LightGBM achieved an AUC-ROC of 0.88 (95% CI: 0.82-0.93) and an AUPRC of 0.42 (95% CI: 0.31-0.54), reflecting the realistic discrimination limits for rare post-procedural events. **CONCLUSION:** ML models, particularly gradient boosting algorithms, demonstrate potential in predicting outcomes after SWL. While SFS remains inherently difficult to predict due to extra-procedural factors, ML provides accurate assessments of treatment failure and complications. These tools could support more personalized clinical decision-making, helping to identify patients who may benefit from primary endoscopic intervention rather than SWL.
