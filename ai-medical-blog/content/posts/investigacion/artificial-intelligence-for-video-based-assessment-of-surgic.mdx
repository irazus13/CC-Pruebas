## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Robot-assisted minimally invasive surgery requires specific skills of the surgeon beyond the mastery of conventional laparoscopic techniques. However, efficient and objective methods for evaluating these skills remain limited. Artificial intelligence (AI) offers potential for a more objective, video-based performance assessment. This systematic review aims to evaluate the current evidence of using AI for evaluation of surgical skill in real robotic surgery and outline potential avenues for future research. **METHODS:** We systematically searched Ovid Medline, Embase, Clarivate Web of Science, ERIC Institute of Education Sciences and Scopus databases from inception to 10 July 2025, for studies evaluating AI-based technical skill assessment using videos of robot-assisted surgery performed on real patients. Study characteristics, AI approaches, assessment targets, reference standards, and model performance were extracted. Risk of bias and applicability were assessed using PROBAST&#x202f;+&#x202f;AI. Owing to differences in assessment constructs and reported performance measures, findings were synthesised using a structured narrative approach. **RESULTS:** In total, 2116 articles were identified, of which 5 met our eligibility criteria. AI approaches and assessment targets varied substantially across studies, including prediction of global technical skill scores, analysis of instrument-derived performance metrics, procedure-specific assessment, and surgical error detection. Reference standards included OSATS, GEARS or modified GEARS, PLACE, and procedural experience. Reported performance measures represented different constructs and were not directly comparable across studies. PROBAST&#x202f;+&#x202f;AI identified important methodological limitations, and no study reported external validation using an independent dataset. **CONCLUSIONS:** AI shows potential for video-based technical skill assessment in real robotic surgery, but current evidence is limited and heterogeneous. Future research should prioritise standardised annotation, multicentre intraoperative datasets, and external validation to support reliable translation into clinical practice.
