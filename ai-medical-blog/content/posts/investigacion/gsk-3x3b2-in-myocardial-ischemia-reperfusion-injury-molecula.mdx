## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** To synthesize current evidence on the role of glycogen synthase kinase-3&#x3b2; (GSK-3&#x3b2;) in myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury (MIRI) and evaluate its potential as a therapeutic target. **METHODS:** This review examines the mechanistic involvement of GSK-3&#x3b2; in MIRI, focusing on mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening, apoptosis, inflammation, autophagy, and associated signaling pathways. Recent pharmacological advances in GSK-3&#x3b2; inhibition and their translational potential were also evaluated. **KEY FINDINGS:** GSK-3&#x3b2; is a central regulator of the pathophysiological response to MIRI. Its temporal inactivation during reperfusion confers cardioprotection through pathways including PI3K/Akt and mTOR, whereas context-dependent activation may exacerbate myocardial injury. Recent GSK-3&#x3b2; inhibitors show therapeutic promise, although challenges including isoform selectivity and systemic toxicity remain. **CONCLUSIONS:** GSK-3&#x3b2; represents a complex yet promising therapeutic target in ischemic heart disease. Future research should prioritize precision therapies and biomarker-guided interventions to improve the clinical translation of GSK-3&#x3b2;-targeted strategies.
