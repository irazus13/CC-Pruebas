## Resumen

Noninvasive ventilation (NIV) and high-flow nasal therapy (HFNT) are central to the management of acute and chronic respiratory failure, yet their monitoring remains largely descriptive and reactive. Artificial intelligence (AI) has the potential to transform this paradigm by enabling dynamic, predictive and individualised approaches to respiratory support. This narrative review summarises current knowledge and future perspectives of AI applications in NIV and HFNT monitoring. AI techniques, including supervised and unsupervised machine learning, deep learning, and hybrid approaches, can integrate multimodal data across different timescales, from high-frequency ventilator signals to longitudinal telemonitoring data. Current applications include early detection of treatment failure, outcome prediction, patient phenotyping and automated detection of patient-ventilator asynchrony. While these approaches have shown promising performance in retrospective and proof-of-concept studies, their implementation in routine clinical practice remains limited. Key challenges include data heterogeneity, limited sample sizes, insufficient external validation and issues related to interpretability and integration into clinical workflows. Regulatory and ethical considerations further highlight the need to ensure transparency, safety and clinician trust. Future developments are expected to move towards adaptive and closed-loop systems capable of continuously optimising respiratory support. Ultimately, AI should be viewed as a tool to augment clinical expertise, supporting a shift towards more anticipatory, personalised and responsive respiratory care.
