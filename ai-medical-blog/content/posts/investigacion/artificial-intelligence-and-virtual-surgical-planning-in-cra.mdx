## Resumen

Artificial intelligence (AI), 3-dimensional modeling, and virtual surgical planning (VSP) are increasingly integrated into craniofacial reconstruction, yet the literature spans both surgical and computational disciplines. The authors characterized the global research landscape at this intersection using Web of Science Core Collection records and VOSviewer network analysis. A search performed on June 13, 2026 identified 1296 publications from 1965 through 2026. Publication output accelerated markedly after 2010, with surgery, dentistry/oral surgery, and computer science among the leading Web of Science categories. Keyword mapping demonstrated a transition from conventional reconstructive concepts and intraoperative navigation toward machine learning, image analysis, and other AI-related methods. International and institutional networks showed prominent contributions from the United States and China, with expanding participation from other regions. The most-cited records also included foundational computer-vision studies, underscoring the interdisciplinary nature of the literature and the overlap between clinical facial reconstruction and computational facial modeling. Overall, AI-enabled virtual surgical planning is an increasingly important component of craniofacial research, but bibliometric findings should be interpreted in light of terminology overlap between surgical and computer-vision literature. Future work should emphasize clinically validated algorithms, transparent reporting, and multicenter collaboration to support safe translation into patient-specific craniofacial care.
