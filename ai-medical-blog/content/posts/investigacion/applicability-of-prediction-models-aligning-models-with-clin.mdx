## Resumen

Doctors increasingly rely on models that predict a patient's risk of future health problems. These models only work well when the patients they were built on resemble the patients being treated now, in illness type, care setting, and stage of disease. This is easy to see when the mismatch is obvious: a model built on hospital patients may not suit those seen in a family doctor's office. The same problem is harder to appreciate when it comes from where a patient sits on a chronic illness journey. Using severe chronic kidney disease as an example, this review shows that a model built from patients who have recently progressed to this more severe stage works better for patients at that same point, while a model built from patients who have lived with the disease for years works better for those further along. This review offers practical steps to keep predictions aligned with the patients they serve.
