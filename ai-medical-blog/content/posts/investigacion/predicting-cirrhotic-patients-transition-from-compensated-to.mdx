## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Cirrhosis is a chronic liver disease, manifested by fibrosis and regenerative nodules. It can be classified clinically as compensated or decompensated. Accurate predictions of patients' transition from compensated to decompensated cirrhosis are important but difficult because of the multifactorial nature of disease progression, comorbid conditions, and patient heterogeneity. **OBJECTIVE:** To effectively identify at-risk patients with cirrhosis and facilitate timely interventions across chronic diseases and patient populations, this study aimed to develop a deep learning-based method to estimate patient transition from compensated to decompensated cirrhosis. The proposed method seeks to provide superior predictive utilities and help physicians derive actionable clinical insights by modeling comorbidity relationships and symptom interdependencies that influence the progression of cirrhosis among patients. **METHODS:** The proposed method was guided by clinical knowledge, considers patient demographics and medications, and leverages a graph structure to capture important comorbidity interplays. This method was augmented with post hoc interpretability to help clinicians understand its predictions for clinical decision-making and patient management. Using clinical data from 29,915 patients with cirrhosis, we evaluated the proposed method in comparison with 12 prevalent methods. **RESULTS:** The proposed method attained an average area under the curve (AUC) of 0.871 (SD 0.004), F-measure of 0.678 (SD 0.010), recall of 0.741 (SD 0.022), and precision of 0.663 (SD 0.005). It showed a 6.09% improvement in AUC and a 13.95% improvement in F-measure over the best-performing benchmark method. Supported by post hoc interpretation analyses, this method can provide physicians with interpretable visualizations of the underlying comorbidity and symptom dynamics to better support their decision-making and adoption. **CONCLUSIONS:** Empirical results underscore the value of demographic similarity, comorbidity graphs, and extended loss functions for identifying patients at higher risk of decompensation by capturing finer-grained information from essential patient features that tend to be overlooked by most existing methods. The proposed method not only supports physicians' clinical decision-making and patient management but can also enhance health care organizations' resource allocation efficiency and cost containment.
