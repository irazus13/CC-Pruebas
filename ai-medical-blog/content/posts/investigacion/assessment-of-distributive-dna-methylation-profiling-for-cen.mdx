## Resumen

**CONTEXT.&#x2014;:** Implementation of machine learning-based central nervous system (CNS) tumor classifiers remains limited, particularly in low- and middle-income countries, where many centers lack the infrastructure, expertise, or financial resources required for clinical deployment. Distributive testing offers a practical pathway for broader adoption, allowing centers without in-house capabilities to access validated classification pipelines. Understanding sample and data quality and classifier performance in the local patient population is a critical initial step in determining the feasibility of this approach. **OBJECTIVE.&#x2014;:** To evaluate whether methylation data generated in a Brazilian site could support tumor classification, using a US-based clinical-grade methylation classifier. **DESIGN.&#x2014;:** We conducted retrospective and prospective analyses of preanalytic parameters, methylation testing quality metrics, and classifier outputs on data generated in Brazil. Quality metric distributions from study samples were compared with in-house clinical data. Classifier performance was assessed by reviewing concordance with histopathology and available ancillary testing information. **RESULTS.&#x2014;:** A total of 151 unique CNS tumor samples, representing major tumor families, were evaluated. Preanalytic metrics of the study samples were comparable to those from the US-based laboratory. Some differences in methylation testing metrics reflected instrumentation variation. Confident classifications matching histologic diagnosis were achieved in 141 of 151 samples (93.4%), with ancillary testing and quality control data supporting the classifier call in 6 additional cases. Classifier-histopathology concordance showed no significant correlation with preanalytic or methylation metrics. **CONCLUSIONS.&#x2014;:** Samples processed in Brazil met US-comparable quality standards, supporting robust classifier performance and feasibility of distributive methylation testing.
