## Resumen

Despite radical cystectomy and advances in perioperative treatment, patients with muscle-invasive bladder cancer&#xa0;remain at risk of recurrence. Circulating tumour deoxyribonucleic acid (ctDNA) offers a means of detecting molecular residual disease before recurrence becomes apparent on imaging. However, detecting disease earlier is only clinically useful if the result can guide treatment and improve outcomes. This narrative review examines the evidence supporting postoperative ctDNA testing, with particular emphasis on IMvigor010, IMvigor011 and TOMBOLA, alongside the limitations of current assays and their implications for multidisciplinary practice. IMvigor010 did not demonstrate a disease-free survival (DFS) benefit from adjuvant atezolizumab in an unselected population, although subsequent exploratory analyses suggested a potential benefit among ctDNA-positive patients. IMvigor011 subsequently demonstrated improved DFS (9.9 versus 4.8 months; hazard ratio (HR) 0.64, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.47-0.87) and overall survival (32.8 versus 21.1 months; HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.39-0.90) with atezolizumab compared with placebo in patients with postoperative ctDNA positivity. TOMBOLA demonstrated the feasibility of serial ctDNA monitoring and early intervention, although its single-arm design limits conclusions regarding treatment efficacy. Importantly, favourable outcomes in patients with persistently negative ctDNA do not establish that adjuvant treatment can safely be omitted. Assay sensitivity, timing of sampling, cost and previous exposure to immunotherapy remain important considerations. Current evidence supports ctDNA-guided treatment in the population studied in IMvigor011, while de-escalation and treatment sequencing require further prospective evaluation. This review proposes a decision-oriented distinction between prognostic detection, evidence for biomarker-triggered treatment and the still-unproven safety of biomarker-based treatment omission, with particular attention to applicability after contemporary perioperative therapy.
