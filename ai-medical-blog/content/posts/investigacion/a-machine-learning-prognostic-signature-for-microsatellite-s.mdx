## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Endometrial cancer (EC) prognosis varies, particularly in microsatellite-stable (MSS) subtypes lacking effective immunotherapy strategies. **METHODS:** This study integrated bulk RNA-seq and weighted gene coexpression network analysis (WGCNA) to identify immune-related genes (IRGs), followed by a machine learning framework (117 algorithms) to construct a prognostic model. Complementary validations were performed using multiple omics datasets, including survival analysis, immune infiltration assessment, and single-cell transcriptomic profiling. **RESULTS:** A three-gene (GAL, PGR, and THRB) model stratified patients into high-risk and low-risk groups, with high-risk correlating significantly with MSS status. Exploratory scRNA-seq analysis described cell type-specific expression patterns of PGR and THRB in one anti-PD-1 responder and one nonresponder; these observations were hypothesis-generating and not confirmatory. Drug sensitivity analysis computationally identified candidate agents (e.g., dasatinib and foretinib) for MSS/high-risk subgroups; these in silico findings are hypothesis-generating and require experimental validation before clinical translation. **CONCLUSION:** This model provides a computationally derived candidate tool for prognosis and immune contexture-associated prognostic stratification in EC, awaiting further external validation. GAL and THRB (candidate adverse-risk genes) and PGR (a candidate favorable marker) may serve as research targets for MSI and MSS subtypes and potential prognostic biomarkers.
