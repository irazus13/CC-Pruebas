## Resumen

**INTRODUCTION:** Urothelial carcinoma (UC) is a common malignancy of the urinary tract, and its prognosis is highly dependent on stage at diagnosis. However, a substantial proportion of muscle-invasive UC is diagnosed de novo at presentation, and diagnostic delay remains prominent, partly attributable to nonspecific symptoms and the invasive nature of current diagnostic procedures. **OBJECTIVES:** This study aimed to develop context-specific classification models based on urinary metabolites for screening UC in asymptomatic populations and for differential diagnosis in individuals presenting with urinary symptoms. **METHODS:** Untargeted metabolomic profiling was performed on 496 urine samples from a multicenter cohort (193 UC, 222 healthy controls [HC], and 81 benign urological conditions [BUC]). Pathway enrichment and metabolite network analyses were conducted to characterize metabolic alterations. Robust predictive features were selected from differential metabolites using the intersection of four machine learning approaches, and classification models were developed and evaluated in the validation set. **RESULTS:** A total of 83 differential metabolites were identified in the UC vs. HC comparison and 7 in the UC vs. BUC comparison, with two shared downregulated metabolites, indole-3-pyruvic acid and 2-aminonicotinic acid. Distinct metabolic profiles were observed across comparison settings by pathway and metabolite network analysis. A support vector machine model (11 metabolites combined with clinical variables) achieved an AUC of 0.951(95% CI, 0.924-0.977) for UC vs. HC classification, and a logistic regression model (6 metabolites combined with clinical variables) achieved an AUC of 0.891(95% CI, 0.829-0.954) for UC vs. BUC classification in the validation set. Both models showed good calibration, with Brier scores ranging from 0.09 to 0.13. **CONCLUSION:** This study developed non-invasive urinary metabolite-based models for UC detection, showing potential to improve early diagnosis and clinical management.
