## Resumen

The mechanisms driving cancer immune escape remain incompletely understood. Our study reveals that alternative RNA splicing is hyperactivated in small cell lung cancer (SCLC), particularly in the classic neuroendocrine subtypes, and is associated with poor prognosis. ASCL1, a defining transcription factor of SCLC, directly binds to and up-regulates the expression of multiple splicing factors, including PRMT5. Elevated levels of these splicing factors correlate with an increase in skipped exon (SE) events. Notably, the SEs in SCLC exhibit strong immunogenicity and are linked to immune suppression. Genetic or pharmacological inhibition of PRMT5 significantly suppresses SCLC progression both in vitro and in vivo. Transcriptome analyses demonstrate that PRMT5 deficiency reduces the prevalence of SEs and mutually exclusive exons (MXEs), thereby maintaining exons with high immunogenicity. Consistent with these findings, the clinically safe PRMT5 inhibitor GSK3326595 synergizes with anti-PD1 antibody to treat SCLC by repressing alternative RNA splicing and enhancing immune responses. We conclude that hyperactive alternative RNA splicing, which excludes immunogenic exons, serves as a key mechanism for immune escape in SCLC, highlighting a potential therapeutic vulnerability for this aggressive disease.
