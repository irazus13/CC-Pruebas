## Resumen

With the widespread use of low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) in lung cancer screening, pulmonary subsolid nodules (SSNs) are being detected with increasing frequency. Persistent SSNs have malignant potential, but their natural history varies, with most lesions growing slowly and some remaining stable for prolonged periods. For SSNs with a low risk of progression, early surgical intervention may lead to overtreatment. Therefore, avoiding overtreatment while identifying the lesions with progression risk in a timely manner and determining the appropriate timing of intervention has become an important challenge in the clinical management of SSNs. Based on their imaging characteristics and natural history, active surveillance has gradually become an important management strategy for low-risk SSNs. Active surveillance appears clinically feasible in appropriately selected patients who can adhere to regular follow-up. However, during long-term surveillance, perceived illness uncertainty, risk perception, anxiety, and patient-clinician communication may influence patients' adherence to follow-up and treatment decisions. Patient education, risk communication and psychological support may help alleviate psychological distress and improve patients' understanding of their condition, although direct evidence supporting these interventions in patients undergoing long-term active surveillance for SSNs remains limited. This review summarizes the clinical evidence and implementation strategies for active surveillance of SSNs, and research on patients' psychological changes and behavioral decision-making during surveillance, with the aim of providing insights into individualized long-term management of SSNs.&#x2029;.
