## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Circulating C-terminal agrin fragment (CAF22) is a biomarker of neuromuscular junction degradation and sarcopenia. However, its relationship with objective measures of physical performance has not been systematically evaluated. This systematic review and meta-analysis examined associations of circulating CAF22 concentrations with gait speed and Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB) scores in aging and chronic disease populations. **METHODS:** PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar were searched to identify studies reporting associations between circulating CAF22 concentrations and gait speed and/or SPPB scores. Correlation coefficients were extracted, transformed to Fisher's z values, and pooled using random-effects meta-analysis. Risk of bias, heterogeneity, subgroup differences, and publication bias were assessed. **RESULTS:** Twenty-one studies met the inclusion criteria, contributing 33 cohorts for gait speed (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;3136) and 28 cohorts for SPPB (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;2773). Higher circulating CAF22 concentrations were significantly associated with slower gait speed in controls (r&#x2009;=&#x2009;-&#x2009;0.309, 95% CI -&#x2009;0.371 to -&#x2009;0.244%) and disease cohorts (r&#x2009;=&#x2009;-&#x2009;0.309, 95% CI -&#x2009;0.361 to -&#x2009;0.254%), yielding an overall pooled correlation of r&#x2009;=&#x2009;-&#x2009;0.308 (95% CI -&#x2009;0.346 to -&#x2009;0.269%). CAF22 concentrations were also inversely associated with SPPB scores in controls (r&#x2009;=&#x2009;-&#x2009;0.335, 95% CI -&#x2009;0.415 to -&#x2009;0.250%) and disease cohorts (r&#x2009;=&#x2009;-&#x2009;0.368, 95% CI -&#x2009;0.422 to -&#x2009;0.312%), with an overall pooled correlation of r&#x2009;=&#x2009;-&#x2009;0.353 (95% CI -&#x2009;0.397 to -&#x2009;0.307%). No significant subgroup differences were detected. **CONCLUSIONS:** Elevated circulating CAF22 concentrations are consistently associated with poorer physical performance across healthy and chronic disease populations, supporting CAF22 as a promising biomarker of functional decline.
