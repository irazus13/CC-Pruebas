## Resumen

Background/ObjectivesPersonalized nutrition may support a healthy lifestyle and improve prediabetes management, but most digital tools remain open loop. We developed a clinician-governed digital-twin framework for adaptive nutrition decision support and a prospective clinical evaluation pathway.MethodsThe framework integrates continuous glucose monitoring (CGM), heart-rate variability (HRV), activity, sleep, and dietary data within an individualized state-space model. Technical/computational feasibility was assessed using one deterministic 42-day reference simulation and six additional prespecified 42-day phenotype/failure stress scenarios (seven scenarios in total). Synthetic generation rules, random seeds, estimator settings, action constraints, and executable reference code are specified for reproduction. A future 24-week, three-arm multicentre pragmatic randomized trial with an embedded four-week micro-randomized component is specified using SPIRIT 2025 and SPIRIT-AI principles. No human participant was enrolled in the simulation component.ResultsIn the 42-day reference run, 126 nominal decision opportunities were generated and 97 (77.0%) met the prespecified availability rule. Thirty-five adaptive recommendations were delivered, 52 available decisions resulted in no adaptive change, 29 decisions were withheld for insufficient inputs, and 10 candidate actions were blocked by hard constraints and routed to fallback; no hard-constraint violation was delivered. CGM missingness was 5.65%. A programmed 10-minute CGM lag was recovered as 10 minutes; alignment reduced CGM-to-latent-state RMSE from 3.53 to 3.01 mg/dL, and the UKF/RTS glucose-state RMSE was 1.17 mg/dL (1.26 mg/dL within missing-CGM windows). All seven scenarios (reference plus six additional stress conditions) completed without numerical failure or delivered hard-constraint violations.ConclusionsThe results support end-to-end software-integration feasibility under explicitly programmed synthetic conditions and provide a reproducible basis for prospective validation. They do not establish physiological validity, clinical effectiveness, or safety. The future trial has not commenced or enrolled participants; prospective registration, ethics approval, final statistical review, and informed consent will precede enrollment.
