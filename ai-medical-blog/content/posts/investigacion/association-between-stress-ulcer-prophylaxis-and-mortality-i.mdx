## Resumen

ObjectiveStress ulcer prophylaxis (SUP) is widely used in intensive care units (ICUs), but its association with outcomes in trauma patients remains uncertain. We examined the association between SUP and mortality in adult trauma patients.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study included adult trauma patients in MIMIC-IV (2008-2022). SUP was defined as proton pump inhibitor or histamine-2 receptor antagonist use for &#x2265;3 days. The primary outcome was in-hospital mortality. Propensity score matching (1:1, caliper 0.2 of the standard deviation of the propensity score logit) and sequential multivariable regression were used.ResultsAmong 2,999 patients (median age 66.7 years; 63.6% male), 2,286 (76.2%) received SUP. After matching (409 pairs), SUP was associated with higher in-hospital mortality (OR 1.98, 95% CI 1.30-3.02), 90-day mortality (HR 1.67, 95% CI 1.26-2.22), 1-year mortality (HR 1.51, 95% CI 1.18-1.94), and hospital-acquired pneumonia (OR 4.77, 95% CI 2.52-9.02). Clostridioides difficile infection was not significant after full adjustment. SUP was also associated with longer ICU (adjusted mean difference 2.88 days, 95% CI 2.08-3.68) and hospital stay (8.24 days, 95% CI 6.42-10.07). In inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) sensitivity analyses, most associations were attenuated; only in-hospital mortality and length of stay remained significant.ConclusionsSUP use was associated with higher mortality, hospital-acquired pneumonia, and longer stay in the matched analysis, but findings were sensitive to the method of confounding adjustment. These results are hypothesis-generating and may reflect residual confounding by indication and illness severity rather than a causal effect.
