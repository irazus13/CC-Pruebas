## Resumen

Medical data annotation is a critical yet contingent process that (re)configures ambiguous medical decisions and data representations into ground truths for artificial intelligence (AI). Extending critical data, AI, and STS research on uncertainty in data annotation, this article develops the concept of zones of (un)certainty to capture the epistemic time-spaces through which annotators transform uncertainty and dissonance into stabilized ground-truth data for medical AI. Drawing on interviews with AI and medical researchers at two Dutch University Medical Centres and complemented by observations at an interdisciplinary workshop on annotation practices, we empirically unfold three such zones: first, we identify an epistemic time-space where ground-truthing is approached spatially as practitioners decompose and recompose data layers, views, and metadata to reach "good enough" certainty. Second, we analyze temporal zones of drift where micro-judgments are routed through negotiations with colleagues, experts, AI suggestions, and automated evaluations. Third, we examine thresholding practices in which uncertain cases are gradually compressed into singular labels. Taken together, this article provides insights into the temporal-spatial dimensions and thresholds that annotators use to settle on ground-truth decisions. Consequently, we open a broader analytical vocabulary for studying how uncertainty and dissonance are operationalized for AI-based medicine and beyond.
