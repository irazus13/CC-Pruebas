## Resumen

Breast cancer remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide, making early and accurate diagnosis essential.. Mammography is widely used for breast cancer screening; however, distinguishing benign and malignant lesions remains a challenging due to subtle visual differences. . This study proposes a mammogram classification approach combining Smooth Support Vector Machine (SSVM) with zoning-based feature extraction. The framework consists of three main stages: image preprocessing, zoning-based feature extraction, and SSVM classification. Experiments were conducted using 322 mammogram images from the Mammographic Image Analysis Society (MIAS) database, comprising 207 benign and 115 malignant cases. Zoning was applied to extract regional intensity features, which served as input to the SSVM classifier. Experimental results achieved 97.50% training accuracy and 65% testing accuracy. The findings indicate that integrating zoning feature extraction and SSVM can capture relevant mammogram characteristics for breast cancer classification. However, the lower testing accuracy suggests potential limitations related to dataset size and feature representation. Future work will focus on integrating deep learning-based feature extraction to improve classification robustness and generalisation performance.
