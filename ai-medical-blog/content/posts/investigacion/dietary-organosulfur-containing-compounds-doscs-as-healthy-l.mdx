## Resumen

Aging is a complex biological process characterized by a gradual decline in physiological functions and increased risk of chronic diseases. The framework of "hallmarks of aging" provides a foundation for identifying actionable targets to delay aging and extend healthspan. Among dietary interventions, bioactive compounds may modulate aging-related pathways. Dietary organosulfur-containing compounds (DOSCs), abundant in Allium vegetables, Brassicaceae species and moringa, and other natural sources (e.g., stink and djenkol beans, shiitake mushrooms), have attracted significant attention due to their pleiotropic health-promoting properties. Historically used in traditional medicine, DOSCs are now recognized for their antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, cardioprotective, and neuroprotective activities. Our review conducts a comprehensive analysis of current knowledge on DOSCs as potential antiaging agents. We first outline their classification, dietary sources, and derivatives formed during food processing (particularly heat treatment and fermentation), highlighting how culinary practices modulate their concentrations and biological efficacy. Additionally, preclinical studies in model organisms and clinical investigations demonstrate that DOSCs can attenuate oxidative damage, improve mitochondrial function, modulate inflammatory signaling, and enhance resilience against age-related decline. Mechanistic insights suggest that DOSCs influence multiple hallmarks of aging. Despite promising findings, challenges remain regarding their bioavailability, interindividual variability, and limited long-term human data. Therefore, innovative strategies such as novel delivery systems, personalized nutrition, combination therapies with established geroprotective agents, and AI-driven compound discovery may accelerate the translational potential of DOSCs. Collectively, our review underscores DOSCs as a versatile and underexplored class of secondary dietary bioactive compounds with significant potential in extending lifespan and mitigating age-related disorders.
