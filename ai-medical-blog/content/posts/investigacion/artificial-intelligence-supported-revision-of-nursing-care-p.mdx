## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Nursing students often produce care plans that are plausible yet generic, with weak linkage among diagnoses, interventions and outcomes. Teacher-mediated, bounded generative AI may support revision while preserving clinical judgement. **AIMS:** To assess whether an AI-supported Reflect-Compare-Revise workflow improves nursing care plan quality and diagnosis-intervention-outcome alignment using a standardised newborn vignette. **METHODS:** A one-group pretest-posttest study involved 40 second-year nursing students at a public university in the Philippines. Students wrote an unaided care plan, then revised it using the structured workflow. Two blinded nurse-educator raters scored paired plans using the Quality of Diagnoses, Interventions and Outcomes instrument. **RESULTS:** Post-revision documentation quality improved across all domains, with the largest raw gains in diagnostic formulation and nursing-sensitive outcomes. Interventions and outcomes remained the weaker downstream domains, with outcomes lowest at posttest. Diagnosis-intervention-outcome alignment and specificity increased, although explicit cross-linking remained inconsistent. **CONCLUSION:** This study adds preliminary evidence for nursing education: higher scores after guided AI-supported revision were associated with clearer and more coherent clinical reasoning in care planning. Policy recommendations are to permit bounded AI use with standardised prompts, revision logs/checklists, rubric-based feedback and verification safeguards to protect documentation integrity and patient safety.
