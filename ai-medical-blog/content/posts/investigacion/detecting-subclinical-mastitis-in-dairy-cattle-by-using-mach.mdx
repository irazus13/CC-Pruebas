## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Early detection of subclinical mastitis in dairy cattle is essential to aid pattern diagnosis and the implementation of control strategies to reduce new infection rates. This observational cross-sectional study aimed to evaluate the ability of machine learning techniques to detect subclinical mastitis in dairy cattle using production and health indicators. **METHODS:** Six machine learning algorithms were evaluated: support vector machine, logistic regression, decision tree, random forest, XGBoost and multilayer perceptron neural network. Three response classifications were tested. Data were partitioned by cow, with 80% of cows used for development and hyperparameter tuning through 30 repeated cow-grouped holdouts and 20% of cows used for independent testing. SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) analysis identified influential variables in development data. **RESULTS:** The random forest model, using positive microbiological culture and/or somatic cell count &#x2265;200 &#xd7; 103&#xa0;cells/mL to define affected quarters, performed the best, achieving a sensitivity of 0.6322, balanced accuracy of 0.6177, F1-score of 0.5729 and ROC-AUC of 0.6774 in the independent test dataset. Electrical conductivity, milk yield and mastitis detection index showed the greatest mean absolute SHAP contributions. **LIMITATIONS:** Most models showed modest sensitivity, and the single-herd design limited external generalisability. **CONCLUSION:** Routinely recorded automatic milking system variables showed potential to support mammary-quarter-level classification of subclinical mastitis.
