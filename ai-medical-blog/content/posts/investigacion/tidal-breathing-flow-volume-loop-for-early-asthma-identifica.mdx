## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Conventional tidal breathing parameters are often insensitive to mild early airway obstruction. In addition, existing artificial intelligence (AI) models may generate overconfident errors in clinically heterogeneous cases. **METHODS:** This retrospective study (2022-2025) included 1121 children aged 1-3&#x2009;years from West China Second University Hospital, Sichuan University. Data from the Jinjiang campus were used for model development (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;805) and data from the Huaxi campus served as an independent spatial validation cohort (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;316). We extracted novel geometric morphological and derived functional features from TBFVL and used machine learning model to evaluate a conventional-feature baseline model versus an enhanced model incorporating novel features. We further proposed and evaluated the Adaptive Conformal Three-way Decision framework (ACT-Decision) to separate high-uncertainty cases from automatic decision-making. **RESULTS:** The final feature set comprised 20 newly derived features, 8 conventional parameters and 1 demographic variable. In the Huaxi validation cohort, the enhanced model using combined features outperformed the baseline model using only conventional features, achieving an accuracy of 0.788 (95% CI: 0.741, 0.829), ROC-AUC of 0.867 (95% CI: 0.827, 0.902) and F1-score of 0.798 (95% CI: 0.744, 0.841). SHAP analysis confirmed contributions from both conventional and newly derived features. In the Huaxi test subset, ACT-Decision automatically classified 74.78% of children with an accuracy of 0.849 (95% CI: 0.787, 0.896). **CONCLUSIONS:** TBFVL-derived features improved discrimination between children aged 1-3&#x2009;years with clinically diagnosed asthma and healthy controls. ACT-Decision framework enabled selective deferral of higher-uncertainty predictions for clinician review.
