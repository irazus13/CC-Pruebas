## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** Posttransplant diabetes mellitus is a key complication after kidney transplant, linked to increased cardiovascular, infection, graft loss, and death risks. Regional data on posttransplant diabetes mellitus remain absent in the Arab world. Regional factors like consanguinity, pharmacogenomics, and hypomagnesemia are not included in current risk models. **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** We performed a systematic review and meta-analysis following PRISMA 2020 and MOOSE guidelines, registered with PROSPERO. We searched PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science through March 1, 2025, for studies involving adult Arab kidney transplant recipients without preexisting diabetes. Random-effects meta-analyses estimated 12-month posttransplant diabetes mellitus incidence and risk factors. We also assessed evidence certainty. **RESULTS:** Our analyses included 16 studies comprising 3622 kidney transplant recipients from Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Egypt, Sudan, and multicenter Arab cohorts. The pooled 12-month incidence of post-transplant diabetes mellitus was 19.8% (95% CI, 16.2-23.8%; I&#xb2; = 89% ). Significant risk factors (as shown by odds ratio) included age per decade (1.49), body mass index &#x2265;25 kg/m&#xb2; (1.92), prediabetes (4.28), hypertension (2.56), hepatitis C virus seropositivity (2.31), and hypomagnesemia &lt;1.9 mg/dL (5.11), with tacrolimus versus cyclosporine showing marginal increase in risk; exclusion of a Sudanese outlier strengthened this association but remained lower than in Western populations. No study evaluated consanguinity. Evidence certainty ranged from moderate (for age, body mass index, hypertension, and hepatitis C virus) to low/very low for other factors. **CONCLUSIONS:** Although traditional risk factors for posttransplant diabetes mellitus remain relevant, tacrolimus showed a weaker association, possibly due to genetic variants such as CYP3A5. Hypomagnesemia represented a key modifiable risk factor. Our findings support a precision-risk framework that incorporates regional data and call for multicenter Arab registries integrating genomics, glycemia, and machine learning to improve risk stratification of posttransplant diabetes mellitus. Prospective multicenter studies are needed to validate findings and establish causal relationships.
