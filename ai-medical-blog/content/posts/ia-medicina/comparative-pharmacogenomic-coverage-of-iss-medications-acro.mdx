## Resumen

Precision health is a rapidly advancing field that has the potential to improve mission planning for long duration spaceflight and strengthen the predictive capabilities of individualized crew health risks beyond Earth. While the pharmacotherapeutic inventory of the International Space Station (ISS) is a critical component of astronaut healthcare, the current understanding of pharmacogenomics (PGx), or how genetic allele variants can impact drug efficacy, in the context of space medicine remains limited. This study explores the potential safety and efficacy impact of PGx to the current space formulary (i.e. ISS medical kit (MedKit) containing 111 formulations) using the FDA Table of Pharmacogenetic Associations, a list of regulated PGx interactions, ClinPGx, a clinically accepted PGx knowledge base, and the drug-gene interaction database (DGIdb), a research-driven platform that uses large language models to compile pharmacogene variant information. Our analysis indicates that the FDA Table of Pharmacogenetic Associations provides PGx coverage for 11% of the medications in the ISS MedKit, while ClinPGx summarizes PGx coverage for 42% of the medications in the ISS MedKit and DGIdb reports on PGx interactions for 86% of the formulary. This comparison dissects the gap between regulatory, clinical, and AI-enabled PGx annotations, highlighting the potential for increased risk profiles as more research data becomes available to support clinical actionability on drug-gene interactions. Additionally, this analysis scrutinizes several of the most frequently occurring pharmacogenes interacting with medications in the ISS MedKit, including CYP family genes, ATP-binding cassette (ABC) genes, G6PD, ACE, et cetera, which are involved in drug transport, drug metabolism, and pharmaceutical response, all of which can be altered by spaceflight stressors. Thus, a comprehensive understanding of the pharmacogenomic landscape of the ISS formulary is vital for mitigating the risks associated with pharmacological interventions in space to ensure astronaut health and safety during long duration missions.
