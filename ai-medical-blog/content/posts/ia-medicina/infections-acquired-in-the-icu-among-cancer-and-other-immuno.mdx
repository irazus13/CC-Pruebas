## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** To compare the cumulative incidence of ICU-acquired infections (ICU-AIs) across distinct immunosuppression diagnostic categories (solid cancer, hematological cancer, other immunosuppression) and nonimmunosuppressed patients, accounting for competing risks of death and discharge. **DESIGN:** Retrospective cohort study using prospectively collected data. **SETTING:** A total of 50 participating ICUs in Brazil. **PATIENTS:** A total of 38,126 adult patients admitted between October 2019 and December 2021 with an ICU stay exceeding 48 hours and no infections diagnosed within the first 48 hours. **INTERVENTIONS:** None. **MEASUREMENTS AND MAIN RESULTS:** The primary outcome was the incidence of the first ICU-AI during the ICU stay, evaluated using Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard ratios (sHR). In the multivariable competing-risk analysis, patients with hematological malignancies (sHR, 1.64; 95% CI, 1.01-2.65) and other immunosuppressive conditions (sHR, 1.61; 95% CI, 1.02-2.54) were associated with an increased cumulative incidence of ICU-AI, while solid malignancy was not significantly different from non-immunosuppressed patients (sHR, 0.88; 95% CI, 0.68-1.14). **CONCLUSIONS:** Patients with hematological malignancies and other immunosuppressive conditions face a significantly higher risk of ICU-AIs.
