## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being explored as clinical decision-support tools; however, their performance across different levels of surgical complexity remains unclear. This study compared the performance of experienced general surgeons and contemporary LLMs in the management of inguinal hernia. **METHODS:** Twenty-five inguinal hernia-related clinical questions were developed based on current European Hernia Society and HerniaSurge recommendations and categorized into three domains: Basic Guideline-Based Management, Complex Clinical Decision-Making, and Complications &amp; Emergency Management. Responses were obtained from 43 board-certified general surgeons and four LLMs (ChatGPT-5.3, Gemini-3.1, DeepSeek-v3.2, and Claude Sonnet 4.6). All responses were independently evaluated by three expert surgeons using a 5-point Likert scale. Group comparisons were performed using Friedman tests followed by Conover post hoc analyses with Holm correction. **RESULTS:** No significant differences were observed in the Basic Guideline-Based Management domain (&#x3c7;2(4)&#x202f;=&#x202f;4.477, p&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.345). Significant differences were identified in the Complex Clinical Decision-Making domain (&#x3c7;2(4)&#x202f;=&#x202f;18.073, p&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.001) and the Complications &amp; Emergency Management domain (&#x3c7;2(4)&#x202f;=&#x202f;11.108, p&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.025). In both domains, Gemini-3.1 achieved the highest scores and scored significantly higher than human participants and ChatGPT-5.3 in selected pairwise comparisons. Overall performance differed significantly among groups (&#x3c7;2(4)&#x202f;=&#x202f;26.983, p&#x202f;&lt;&#x202f;0.001, Kendall's W&#x202f;=&#x202f;0.270). Gemini-3.1 achieved the highest overall score (4.45&#x202f;&#xb1;&#x202f;0.51), followed by DeepSeek-v3.2 (4.05&#x202f;&#xb1;&#x202f;1.01), Claude Sonnet 4.6 (4.05&#x202f;&#xb1;&#x202f;0.99), human participants (3.85&#x202f;&#xb1;&#x202f;0.47), and ChatGPT-5.3 (3.36&#x202f;&#xb1;&#x202f;0.95). Post hoc analyses demonstrated significantly higher overall performance for Gemini-3.1 compared with both human participants and ChatGPT-5.3. **CONCLUSIONS:** LLM performance in inguinal hernia management varied according to clinical complexity. While no significant differences were observed for guideline-based questions, performance differences emerged in complex and emergency-related scenarios. Gemini-3.1 achieved the highest and most consistent scores across the evaluated domains; however, these findings reflect performance within a standardized text-based benchmark and should not be interpreted as evidence of superior real-world clinical competence.
