## Resumen

Evidence-Based Medicine (EBM) is the doctrine intended to establish medical decision-making. It leans on three fundamental pillars: the best available scientific and medical knowledge, the physician's experience, and the patient's preferences. Following the writings of Aristotle&#xa0;-&#xa0;specifically the Nicomachean Ethics&#xa0;-&#xa0;this text examines the EBM doctrine through the lens of Aristotelian virtues ethics. In particular, the analysis seeks to demonstrate that in the age of artificial intelligence (AI)&#xa0;-&#xa0;where the medical state of the art is easily accessible and synthesizable via generative AI&#xa0;-&#xa0;the application of EBM's first scientific pillar is now being partially delegated to AI. Consequently, the physician's role&#xa0;-&#xa0;without requiring them to abandon the acquisition of medical knowledge&#xa0;-&#xa0;will shift to focus more on EBM's other two pillars. As their theoretical wisdom (sophia) becomes maybe less essential than in the past, current and future physicians could use this opportunity to cultivate practical wisdom&#xa0;-&#xa0;termed phronesis by Aristotle. This human virtue is indispensable for effectively applying general scientific recommendations to the contingencies and inherent unpredictability of each specific medical situation.
