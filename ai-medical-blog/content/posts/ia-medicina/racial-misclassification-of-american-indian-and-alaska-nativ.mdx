## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** Racial misclassification in vital statistics data is common among American Indian and Alaska Native (AI/AN) populations and varies by geographic region. A previous study showed racial misclassification varies by cause of death (COD) among the non-Hispanic AI/AN (NH-AI/AN) population nationally. We examined how racial misclassification by COD varied geographically in data from 2020. **METHODS:** We used the 2020 US Cancer Statistics American Indian and Alaska Native Mortality Database, which links the Indian Health Service patient registration database to death records to correctly classify AI/AN individuals misclassified as another race. We calculated death rates per 100 000 population for 10 common CODs in 2020 among NH-AI/AN people. Pre-linkage and post-linkage death rates and percentage misclassification were calculated by race, sex, COD, and region. Analyses of death rates and percentage misclassification were restricted to NH-AI/AN people living in purchased/referred care delivery area counties. We calculated death rates for non-Hispanic White people for comparison. **RESULTS:** Overall, percentage misclassification of NH-AI/AN race by COD ranged from 6.3% for COVID-19 to 20.3% for Alzheimer disease. Percentage misclassification varied by geographic region, COD, and sex. Infectious diseases, such as influenza/pneumonia and COVID-19, had a lower percentage misclassification than several chronic diseases including heart disease, chronic lower respiratory disease, Alzheimer disease, and cancer. **CONCLUSIONS:** Racial misclassification among NH-AI/AN people varied by COD, sex, and region. Future work could explore causes for the differential misclassification by geographic region and COD among NH-AI/AN populations.
