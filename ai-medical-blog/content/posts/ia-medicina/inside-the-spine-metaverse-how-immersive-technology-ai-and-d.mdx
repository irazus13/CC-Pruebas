## Resumen

Extended reality (XR), artificial intelligence (AI), and digital twins are converging across medicine. Few fields stand to gain more than spine care: the spine is anatomically complex and 3-D, lies within millimeters of the spinal cord, and requires care across the entire lifespan, from childhood scoliosis to traumatic injury and age-related degeneration. Applied to the spine, this convergence forms what we describe as a Metaverse for spine care, an integrated and immersive environment for visualizing, modeling, and treating spinal conditions. Within it, XR provides headsets through which clinicians and patients can view and manipulate 3-D anatomy; AI interprets imaging and helps anticipate how a condition will progress; and the digital twin is a continuously updated virtual model of an individual patient, built from that patient's own imaging and clinical data. We examine applications across the care pathway, including patient education, surgical planning, surgical guidance, surgical training, rehabilitation, and pain management. We then assess the principal barriers to routine clinical adoption, foremost the difficulty of operating in real time, and outline future directions, including more predictive digital twins and brain-spine interfaces. Together, these advances point toward a connected, patient-specific continuum of spine care.
