## Resumen

Children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) often demonstrate impaired postural stability. However, assessment of postural stability in this population can be challenging because prolonged standing trials may increase spontaneous fidgeting and reduce data quality. Convolutional neural network (CNN) may offer a practical approach for identifying ASD-related postural control patterns using short-duration center of pressure (COP) data. The purpose of this study was to evaluate the classification performance of a CNN for distinguishing children with ASD from healthy controls using short duration COP data. 49 children, including 20 typically developing children and 29 children with ASD, completed quiet-standing trials on a force plate. COP data were analyzed using CNN models trained separately with 5&#x202f;s, 10&#x202f;s, and 15&#x202f;s COP data inputs. Classification accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score were calculated to assess the model performance. In general, classification performance improved as COP input duration increased. Using 5&#x202f;s COP data, the CNN model achieved a classification accuracy of 77.6&#x202f;%. When the duration was increased to 10&#x202f;s, accuracy improved substantially to 96.7&#x202f;%. Extending the duration to 15&#x202f;s resulted in a minor increase in accuracy to 98.8&#x202f;%. These findings indicate that short-duration COP contains discriminative information in this sample. Larger multisite samples and external validation are required before clinical application.
