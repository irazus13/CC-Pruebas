## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** To evaluate large language model (LLM) performance in multidisciplinary tumor board (MTB) decision support for head and neck cancer (HNC). **STUDY DESIGN:** Blinded comparative study. **SETTING:** Single tertiary academic center using real-world tumor board cases. **METHODS:** One hundred de-identified HNC cases were transcribed from institutional MTB presentation materials. GPT-4 and GPT-5 outputs, generated with and without in-context guideline grounding (RAG) using National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) 2025 guidelines, were evaluated by five blinded fellowship-trained head and neck surgeons. Outputs were scored across five domains: staging accuracy, treatment recommendation accuracy, prognosis accuracy, guideline adherence, and overall clinical usefulness using a five-point Likert scale with predefined anchors. Mixed-effects regression models accounted for clustering by case and reviewer. **RESULTS:** All configurations demonstrated high performance, with composite scores &gt;4.45/5. GPT-5 without RAG achieved the highest mean score (4.82), followed by GPT-4 without RAG (4.55), GPT-5 with RAG (4.53), and GPT-4 with RAG (4.45). Configurations without RAG outperformed those with RAG (&#x394;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.22, P&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;.001), and GPT-5 outperformed GPT-4 (&#x394;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.15, P&#x2009;=&#x2009;.008). Mixed-effects modeling confirmed a significant effect of model version (&#x3b2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;.17, P&#x2009;=&#x2009;.001), while RAG was not associated with improved performance (&#x3b2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;-.06, P&#x2009;=&#x2009;.25); a negative model-by-RAG interaction did not reach significance (&#x3b2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;-.13, P&#x2009;=&#x2009;.07). **CONCLUSION:** LLMs demonstrate strong expert-rated performance in HNC tumor board decision support. However, na&#xef;ve guideline-based RAG was associated with lower performance, suggesting that poorly optimized retrieval may introduce irrelevant context in complex decision-making. These findings underscore retrieval design as a critical determinant of safe clinical AI deployment and reinforce the need for clinician oversight.
