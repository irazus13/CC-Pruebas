## Resumen

Diabetic retinopathy (DR) remains a leading cause of preventable vision loss, yet the pathways patients navigate to be screened are fragmented and inconvenient, and structural barriers-not patient neglect-drive poor adherence. This commentary explores how artificial intelligence (AI)-enabled autonomous screening, embedded in primary care, can reshape the patient and family experience while improving clinical outcomes. Drawing on published evidence and early implementation experience from Saudi Arabia, where DR is common among people with diabetes, we present actionable insights for healthcare leaders. We emphasize experience-related gains-same-visit results that reduce waiting-related anxiety, fewer trips and unnecessary referrals, faster time to treatment, and clearer communication-alongside outcomes and cost. We address implementation challenges including clinician and patient trust, automation and confirmation bias, workflow integration, equity, and preserving human connection in AI-mediated care. Recommendations emphasize embedding screening in existing diabetes visits, pilot-to-enterprise scaling, transparent patient communication, and measuring patient-reported experience. AI-enabled DR screening offers a compelling, patient-centered model for specialty care transformation under value-based frameworks such as Saudi Vision 2030.
