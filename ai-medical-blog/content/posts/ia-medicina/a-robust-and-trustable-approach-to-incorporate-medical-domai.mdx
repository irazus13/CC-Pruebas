## Resumen

Many AI models for disease prediction remain difficult to integrate into clinical practice because model complexity can limit transparent interpretation. This study develops a domain-informed, low-dimensional machine-learning framework for diabetic retinopathy (DR) screening using routinely collected electronic health record and laboratory data. Medical-domain information was extracted from PubMed using a Term Frequency-Relative Citation Ratio (TF-RCR) procedure, and the selected feature set was constrained to remain cognitively manageable. The final model uses eight variables - age, glucose, nephropathy, neuropathy, HbA1c, hemoglobin, albumin, and creatinine - and its predictions are examined using post-hoc SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) feature attribution. The data contained a 97,786-patient cohort, including 16,865 DR cases (17.25%). After a 64%/16%/20% training/validation/test split, SMOTE was applied only to the training subset, while validation and test sets retained the observed prevalence. On the untouched test set (n = 19,558), the eight-feature XGBoost model achieved 95.35% accuracy, 91.17% sensitivity, 96.22% specificity, an F1-score of 87.12%, AUROC of 97.24%, and AUPRC of 94.55%. Same-cohort comparisons with logistic regression, a 24-feature XGBoost model, a multilayer perceptron (MLP), and an FT-Transformer showed that the eight-feature model retained competitive discrimination while using a substantially smaller feature set. The framework provides a reproducible approach for combining literature-derived knowledge with empirical data while keeping the final model concise and its feature contributions available for post-hoc interpretation.
