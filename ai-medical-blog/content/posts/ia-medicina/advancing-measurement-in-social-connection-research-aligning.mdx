## Resumen

Advancing social connection requires identifying priority research questions and determining whether measurement approaches adequately address them. We identified research questions and gaps, prioritized areas for investigation, and evaluated whether existing measurements can address next steps. We conducted an interdisciplinary convening. Attendees included 81 individuals from academia, community-based organizations, the private sector, and those with lived experience. Using an iterative, facilitated engagement design, attendees identified key research questions and measurement challenges in social connection research, shared perspectives on their relative importance, and assessed their feasibility based on the field's perceived readiness and ability to answer them. Results were synthesized and refined into measurement-oriented research priorities. Attendees identified 64 questions across six domains: assessment/screening; conceptual clarity; interventions; cross-sector integration; technology; and differences across populations. Twenty-seven questions (42%) were deemed feasible, including identifying generational differences, potential medicalization, strengths-based measurement, and meaningful thresholds for social connection. Twenty-four (38%) were moderately feasible, including technology/AI, systems integration, intervention pathways, and modalities of social connection. Thirteen (20%) were deemed challenging, including measuring social connection in dementia, structural/environmental barriers, healthcare screening/referrals, and measuring beyond the individual level. Most high-priority questions were considered feasible with current measurement tools. However, challenging priorities highlight gaps and opportunities for measurement innovation.
