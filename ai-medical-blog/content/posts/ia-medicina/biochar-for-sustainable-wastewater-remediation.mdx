## Resumen

Biochar, produced from industrial and agricultural waste, has evolved from an agricultural soil amendment to an effective and sustainable material for wastewater treatment. Engineered/modified biochar produced using chemical or physical methods possesses a high surface area, porosity, stability, and a variety of surface functional groups and active sites for removing a wide spectrum of organic and inorganic contaminants, including heavy metals, nutrients, and antibiotics. However, its performance depends on feedstock characteristics and pyrolysis conditions. Slow pyrolysis optimized with artificial intelligence and machine learning technologies is increasingly adopted because of its operational simplicity, high yield, and economic feasibility. Alkali treatment enhances surface functionality, whereas acidic modification promotes the formation of oxygen-containing groups. Experimental evidence indicates that the solution pH is a critical parameter influencing heavy metal adsorption onto biochar, and nanomaterial-based biochar composites can further improve treatment efficiency and cost-effectiveness. Recent advances are shifting toward tailor-made, regenerable biochar integrated into membranes, electrodes, photocatalysts, biofilters, and nutrient-recovery systems. Despite its potential, challenges remain in long-term performance, regeneration, spent-biochar management, and full-scale comparison with activated carbon. Overall, biochar supports environmental, social, and governance principles and sustainable water management while contributing to circular economy strategies and the United Nations' sustainable development goals.
