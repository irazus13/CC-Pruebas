## Resumen

Conversational artificial intelligence (AI), or chatbot, technology offers a scalable way to deliver health information, but its deployment in low- and middle-income countries is constrained by linguistic diversity, limited internet connectivity, user trust, and the lack of context-specific evaluation frameworks. This study describes the development, contextualization, and evaluation of a cervical cancer (CC) awareness chatbot tailored for women living with HIV in Nigeria. Using a co-design, mixed-method implementation approach, we engaged subject-matter experts and end users. We collected questions and answers through a moderated WhatsApp group across six health facilities over three months. We curated a dataset of 431 questions from 659 participants and automatically expanded it to 1,300 question-answer pairs. We implemented a hybrid offline-online architecture and integrated the chatbot into the existing AVIVA mobile application. Offline, natural language processing and cosine similarity mapped user queries to pre-generated questions, with a 'no response' fallback when no sufficiently similar question was found. Evaluation involved conversation log analysis, monthly expert quality assurance, and qualitative assessment of user experience. Over three months, monthly interactions increased from 58 to 133, while the offline non-response rate declined from 32% to zero. However, high-match automated query scores (&#x2265;95% text similarity) fell from 75.9% in month one to 66.2% in month three, while expert-rated appropriate responses rose from 38.7% to 68.8%. This suggests that although users increasingly phrased questions differently from preloaded questions, clinical appropriateness improved, likely reflecting iterative, expert-led content refinement. Participants reported high satisfaction but identified poor connectivity, phone incompatibility, and the need for local languages and voice notes. Contextualizing conversational AI through co-design shows promise for delivering accurate medical information in low-resource settings. Reliability, however, depends on rigorous query-set construction, dataset vocabulary, and localization. This study offers a replicable co-design methodology and an offline-capable chatbot architecture for cervical cancer awareness in resource-constrained settings.
