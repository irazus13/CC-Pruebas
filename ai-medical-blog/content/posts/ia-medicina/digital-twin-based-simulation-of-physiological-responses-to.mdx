## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Digital twin-based simulation, in which a patient-specific counterfactual outcome is constructed from observational data, could enable individualised treatment effect estimation from routinely collected clinical data. Applications in critical care remain scarce, however, and it is unclear how well such methods capture the complex physiological responses to ICU interventions. We adapt an established digital twin framework to simulate multiple short-term physiological effects of prone positioning, an intervention with well-documented effects on oxygenation in mechanically ventilated patients. **METHODS:** In this retrospective study using AmsterdamUMCdb, mechanically ventilated patients were separated into a treatment group (first prone positioning event) and control group (no documented proning). Using pre-intervention data, we trained a recurrent neural network-based encoder to learn patient-specific latent representations. For each proned patient, a synthetic twin was constructed as a sparse, weighted mixture of control patients. Treatment effects were estimated as the difference between observed post-proning outcomes and the simulated counterfactual across five signals: PaO2/FiO2 ratio, mechanical power, ventilatory ratio, dynamic compliance, and estimated cardiac output. Simulation fidelity was assessed through placebo testing, and treatment effect heterogeneity was examined by baseline PF ratio. **RESULTS:** We included 4,935 patients (469 proned, 4,466 never proned). Prone positioning was associated with a significant overall increase in PaO2/FiO2 ratio (ATT:&#x2009;+&#x2009;51.2&#xa0;mmHg, 95% CI [41.6, 60.9], p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001), with the largest effect at lower PF ratios (PF&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;100 and 100&#x2009;&#x2264;&#x2009;PF&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;200), and no significant effect at PF&#x2009;&#x2265;&#x2009;200. Mechanical power increased (+&#x2009;12.6&#xa0;J/min [11.2, 14.1], p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001) and dynamic compliance decreased (-&#x2009;26.4 mL/cmH2O [&#x2009;-&#x2009;28.9,&#x2009;-&#x2009;23.8], p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001) across all strata, while ventilatory ratio rose modestly (+&#x2009;0.11 [0.04, 0.18], p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.002). Estimated cardiac output showed no significant overall effect. **CONCLUSIONS:** Digital twin-based simulation can produce clinically plausible estimates of the multi-outcome physiological effects of prone positioning. The oxygenation findings align with established trial evidence, while the simultaneous characterisation of respiratory and haemodynamic effects illustrates the approach's potential for a more comprehensive understanding of ICU interventions from observational data.
