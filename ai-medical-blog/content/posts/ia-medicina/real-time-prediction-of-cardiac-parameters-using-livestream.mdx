## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** The aim of this study is to develop a workflow for acquiring and analyzing livestream echocardiography images from a GE HealthCare Vivid E95 system, a real-time artificial intelligence (AI)-based automatic left ventricle segmentation, and cardiac parameter prediction. The system provides automated echocardiography image analysis independent of electrocardiogram signals. **METHODS:** A software pipeline was developed to acquire and analyze livestream echocardiography images from the ultrasound system. A UNet neural network model was trained using publicly available datasets (CAMUS, EchoNet-Dynamic) and an in-house dataset (Physical Activity and Fitness in Childhood Cancer Survivors). AI models were trained using these three datasets and three loss functions, with each model evaluated independently on all three test datasets. The model with the highest Dice score on the test datasets was selected and used to process livestream echocardiography images throughout the entire cardiac cycle. The resulting segmentation masks were used to compute the cardiac parameters. **RESULTS:** The offline-trained UNet models achieved an average Dice score of 0.9509. The livestream echocardiography image prediction showed average ejection fractions of 48%, 48% and 56%, corresponding to the Simpson's single-plane, area-length and Bullet methods, respectively. In contrast, EchoPAC (the GE HealthCare software tool to compute cardiac parameters) showed an average ejection fraction of 55% in nine volunteers with no known cardiac diseases. **CONCLUSION:** This study shows the feasibility of AI-assisted echocardiographic image analysis based on livestream echocardiography. However, the accuracy of the obtained cardiac parameters needs improvement to align with clinical standards.
