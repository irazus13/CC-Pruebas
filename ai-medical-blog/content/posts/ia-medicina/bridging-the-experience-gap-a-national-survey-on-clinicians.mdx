## Resumen

**BACKGROUND/AIMS:** With the accelerated integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into clinical workflows, understanding the practical barriers to its adoption is crucial. This study aimed to examine the effect of direct clinical experience with AI on physicians' perceptions of its clinical impact and their professional autonomy, legal liability, and institutional support. **METHODS:** A nationwide online survey was conducted from October 16 to 21, 2025, among 2,125 physicians registered with the Korean Medical Association. The respondents were divided into AI-experienced (AIex_(+), n = 1,013) and AI-inexperienced (AIex_(-), n = 1,112) groups. **RESULTS:** The AIex_(+) group reported a significantly higher positive clinical impact compared to the AIex_(-) group (6.8 &#xb1; 2.5 vs. 6.0 &#xb1; 2.5, p &lt; 0.001). While concerns regarding professional autonomy were similar (p = 0.244), confidence in the legal liability framework was critically low in both groups (2.4 &#xb1; 1.7 vs. 2.3 &#xb1; 1.6, p = 0.028). Experienced users expressed a significantly greater demand for government infrastructure (p &lt; 0.001), AI education (p = 0.023), and reimbursement support (p &lt; 0.001). Furthermore, the AIex_(+) group identified ambiguity regarding legal liability (69.1%) and insufficient data quality (51.5%) as primary barriers, whereas the AIex_(-) group primarily cited a lack of information (54.4%) and limited accessibility (48.2%). **CONCLUSIONS:** Clinical experience with AI is associated with variations in physician concerns, from foundational access to systemic and structural anxieties. Sustainably integrating medical AI requires a bifurcated strategy of prioritizing education for nonusers to lower entry barriers while establishing robust legal frameworks and fair reimbursement systems to support experienced users.
