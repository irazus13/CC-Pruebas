## Resumen

Flexible wearable biosensors are reshaping health monitoring by enabling continuous, noninvasive access to physiological and biochemical information during daily life. Beyond device miniaturization, their clinical value depends on whether signals acquired at specific body sites can be reliably linked to meaningful biological states. However, anatomical heterogeneity, dynamic skin deformation, biofluid variability, unstable tissue-device coupling, and environmental interference continue to limit the accuracy, reproducibility and translational potential of wearable biosensing systems. In this review, we summarize the design principles of wearable biosensors with a focus on signal transduction mechanisms, site-specific specificity, and wearability. We then review the physiological and biochemical detection methods tailored to the epidermis, hand, and head. Furthermore, we highlight how integration, artificial intelligence, and multimodal architectures enhance performance under diverse physiological and environmental conditions. By providing a comprehensive framework for designing precise, site-adaptive wearable sensors, this work underscores their potential in personalized healthcare, early disease detection, and proactive physiological management.
