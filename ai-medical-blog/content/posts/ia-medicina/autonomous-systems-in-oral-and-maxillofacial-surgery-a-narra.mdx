## Resumen

Robotic and artificial intelligence (AI) systems are increasingly described as autonomous in oral and maxillofacial surgery (OMS), although the operational meaning of that term varies widely across devices and procedures. This narrative review aims to classify robotic systems in OMS by level of autonomy and evidence stage, describe AI-driven surgical planning tools, and examine the regulatory status and liability implications of both. A PubMed search combining OMS procedural and anatomical terms with autonomy descriptors was conducted on March 2, 2026, supplemented by manual citation tracking, and findings were synthesized narratively. Dental implant placement accounts for most clinical activity. The single US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-cleared OMS system provides haptic guidance only and does not execute any step independently. Other systems, none currently FDA-cleared, carry out the osteotomy and implant placement along a surgeon-approved plan and have been reported in patient series across single-tooth, full-arch, zygomatic, pterygoid, and sinus augmentation procedures. Beyond implants, a laser osteotome has performed Le Fort I cuts in a multicenter patient trial. Systems demonstrating autonomous mandibular tumor resection, maxillary repositioning, robotic orthognathic execution, and soft tissue tumor resection remain preclinical. AI-driven tools can segment craniomaxillofacial anatomy, identify landmarks, and generate surgical plans with accuracy comparable to expert reference standards, but their outputs are generally not linked to robotic execution in current OMS workflows. Competency standards, society guidelines, and specialty-specific consent language have not developed in parallel, possibly leaving the operating surgeon as the default bearer of risk when employing these technologies.
