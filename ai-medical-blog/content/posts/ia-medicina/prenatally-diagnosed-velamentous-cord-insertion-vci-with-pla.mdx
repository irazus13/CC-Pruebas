## Resumen

Velamentous cord insertion (VCI) is a rare placental abnormality where umbilical vessels traverse fetal membranes before connecting to the placenta, increasing the risk of compression and injury. Although velamentous cord insertion can lead to complications like fetal growth restriction and preterm birth, many pregnancies result in favorable outcomes. A case is presented of a 33-year-old primigravida with prenatally diagnosed velamentous cord insertion at 21 weeks' gestation. The patient underwent a planned cesarean delivery at 37 weeks due to her medical history of arrhythmias requiring a cardiac pacemaker. The cesarean was performed under general anesthesia, yielding a 2,160 g male neonate, with postoperative recovery being uneventful for both mother and child. Follow-up at 40 days postpartum showed the patient was in good health. The report underscores the importance of prenatal diagnosis and individualized delivery planning, emphasizing that velamentous cord insertion does not automatically necessitate cesarean delivery; decisions should be based on comprehensive maternal and fetal clinical evaluations.
