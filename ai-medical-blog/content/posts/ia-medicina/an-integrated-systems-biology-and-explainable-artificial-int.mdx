## Resumen

Preeclampsia (PE) is a heterogeneous pregnancy disorder characterized by placental dysfunction and immune dysregulation. Translation of transcriptomic biomarkers has been limited by inconsistent feature selection, inadequate external validation, and poor model interpretability. We developed an explainable, externally validated, transcriptomics-driven artificial intelligence framework for biomarker discovery, PE diagnosis, and exploratory severity stratification. Placental transcriptomic datasets were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus. Differential expression analysis and weighted gene co-expression network analysis were integrated with consensus feature selection using Elastic Net, LASSO, SVM-RFE, and Boruta. Selected biomarkers were evaluated using conventional machine learning, deep learning, ensemble, and hybrid models with repeated stratified cross-validation and independent cross-platform external validation. Model performance and interpretability were assessed using ROC and precision-recall analyses, DeLong's test, permutation testing, SHAP, and permutation importance. Integrative analyses identified 166 candidate genes and prioritized a six-gene signature comprising CGB5, LEP, LIMCH1, LRRC1, PAPPA2, and SIGLEC6. Logistic Regression achieved the highest external performance (ROC-AUC: 0.8516, 95% CI: 0.7913-0.9119; PR-AUC: 0.8572). DeLong's test showed no significant differences from the best-performing deep or hybrid models. Explainability analyses consistently identified LEP, LRRC1, PAPPA2, and SIGLEC6 as major predictive contributors. Exploratory early versus late-onset PE stratification achieved an ROC-AUC of 0.7617. The proposed framework identified a reproducible six-gene placental signature with robust cross-platform predictive performance and biological interpretability. Integrating consensus biomarker selection, external validation, and explainable artificial intelligence may facilitate the reproducible development of transcriptomic biomarkers for precision medicine in PE.
