## Resumen

BackgroundHealthcare's digital transformation depends on artificial intelligence (AI) and high-performance computing (HPC), yet no healthcare-specific framework consolidates the competences required for their adoption.ObjectiveTo identify, classify and synthesise the competences healthcare professionals need to implement AI and HPC in clinical practice.MethodsFollowing PRISMA 2020, six databases (PubMed, IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect, EBSCOhost, Emerald Insight, Taylor &amp; Francis Online) were searched in June-July 2025 for 2020-2025. Two reviewers independently screened records and extracted data; three reviewers categorised competences by inductive thematic synthesis. Methodological quality was appraised with design-specific instruments (AMSTAR 2, CASP, JBI, CREDES), and a sensitivity analysis excluding low-quality studies tested robustness.ResultsOf 2255 records, 17 studies were included, yielding 22 competence categories. The most frequent were AI fundamentals (88.2%), ethical and legal issues (70.6%), data analysis and interpretation (64.7%) and evaluation of AI tools (58.8%). No HPC-related competence was reported in any included study, despite HPC terms in the search strategy.ConclusionsThe 22 competences are consolidated into four dimensions (technical and data-centric, ethical and legal, human-centred and interpersonal, and organisational and cognitive leadership), mapped to three proficiency levels and to defined target groups. The absence of HPC competences is a distinct research gap.
