## Resumen

Melanoma remains a malignancy requiring novel interventions. While triggering ferroptosis has emerged as a promising anti-tumor strategy, the targetable molecular vulnerabilities governing this process remain incompletely understood. Here, through artificial intelligence (AI)-driven&#xa0;virtual screening, we identified the coumarin derivative Nodakenin (NK) as a potent inhibitor of nuclear receptor subfamily 4 group A member 1 (NR4A1), an orphan nuclear receptor highly expressed in malignant melanoma cells. Transcriptomic, biochemical, and structural analyses revealed that NK triggers ferroptosis in melanoma cells. Mechanistically, NK functions as a molecular glue that enforces proximity between NR4A1 and the E3 ubiquitin ligase tripartite motif-containing protein 28 (TRIM28). This recruitment promotes K48-linked polyubiquitination of NR4A1 at the K381 residue, leading to its rapid proteasomal degradation. Consequently, the targeted depletion of NR4A1 abolishes its transcriptional transactivation of the defense gene the defense gene lipocalin 2 (LCN2), resulting in intracellular free iron overload, lipid reactive oxygen&#xa0;species (ROS) accumulation, and lethal ferroptosis. Crucially, NK dramatically suppresses tumor growth and liver metastasis in vivo and disrupts the viability of patient-derived organoids (PDOs), with these therapeutic efficacies being dependent on the ferroptosis pathway. Collectively, this study identifies a novel molecular glue targeting NR4A1 and unveils the TRIM28/NR4A1/LCN2 axis as a promising therapeutic vulnerability for melanoma.
