## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** To evaluate whether color fundus photograph-derived vascular retinomics predicts progression from single vascular disease to polyvascular disease. **METHODS:** This prospective cohort study included UK Biobank participants with baseline single vascular disease and eligible color fundus photographs. Progression was defined as incident involvement of a previously unaffected coronary, cerebrovascular, or peripheral arterial bed. After LASSO-Cox and Boruta feature selection, eight survival-learning algorithms were compared. The best-performing model was refitted to derive the Retinal Polyvascular Disease Risk Index (RPVDRI). Performance was evaluated in the testing cohort and benchmarked against conventional clinical factors and SMART2. **RESULTS:** Among 2592 participants, 334 progressed during a median follow-up of 11.39 years. A 25-feature vascular retinomic panel was retained, and a gradient boosting machine performed best. In the testing cohort, the RPVDRI showed a graded association with progression after multivariable adjustment (highest vs. lowest tertile: hazard ratio, 1.97; 95% confidence interval, 1.03-3.74; P = 0.040; P for trend = 0.029). The RPVDRI achieved 3-, 5-, and 10-year areas under the curve (AUCs) of 0.636, 0.716, and 0.628, respectively. Adding the RPVDRI to conventional clinical factors improved 5-year AUC by 0.113 (P = 0.002), whereas the corresponding improvement beyond SMART2 was 0.037 (P = 0.466). Shapley additive explanations linked predictions to multidomain features and co-attribution. **CONCLUSIONS:** The RPVDRI was independently associated with polyvascular disease progression and may complement clinical risk assessment in secondary prevention, particularly in scalable or opportunistic settings where comprehensive clinical information is limited. **TRANSLATIONAL RELEVANCE:** Fundus-derived vascular retinomics may support precision secondary prevention by noninvasively stratifying residual polyvascular progression risk.
