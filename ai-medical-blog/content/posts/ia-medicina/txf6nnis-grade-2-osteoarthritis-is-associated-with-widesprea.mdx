## Resumen

Hip dysplasia can alter periarticular muscle biomechanics, but the extent and distribution of muscle changes across the hip and thigh remain incompletely characterized. This retrospective cross-sectional study evaluated 72 patients with unilateral symptomatic hip dysplasia and a pain-free contralateral hip who underwent eccentric rotational acetabular osteotomy. Preoperative computed tomography images were analyzed using an artificial intelligence-based three-dimensional segmentation system to quantify bilateral muscle volume and mean attenuation in 19 muscles or muscle regions, summarized into six functional groups. Total assessed muscle volume and overall muscle attenuation were lower on the symptomatic side. After false discovery rate correction, symptomatic-side reductions remained significant for quadriceps muscle volume and gluteus maximus mean CT attenuation among the six functional muscle groups. After adjustment for clinical and radiographic factors, T&#xf6;nnis grade 2 osteoarthritis was associated with a 6.99-percentage-point greater symptomatic-side reduction in total assessed muscle volume compared with grades 0 and 1 and with greater volume reductions across all six functional groups. T&#xf6;nnis grade 2 was not independently associated with overall muscle attenuation asymmetry, and acetabular morphology was not associated with either primary outcome. These findings suggest that, among patients with symptomatic hip dysplasia undergoing eccentric rotational acetabular osteotomy, more advanced osteoarthritis is associated with widespread muscle volume reduction. STATEMENT OF CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: Recognition of widespread periarticular hip and thigh muscle involvement in patients with more advanced osteoarthritis may support more comprehensive preoperative muscle assessment and inform future studies of perioperative rehabilitation.
