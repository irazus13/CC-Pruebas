## Resumen

**AIM:** To develop and evaluate a dental virtual standardized patient (D-VSP) integrating natural-language intent recognition, a high-fidelity haptic simulator, and chairside visuals, to establish its face validity as a training tool for endodontic clinical reasoning and decision-making, and to examine its construct validity as a summative assessment instrument. **METHODOLOGY:** The D-VSP delivered a structured clinical workflow comprising six sections: history-taking, clinical examination, documentation, ancillary testing, diagnosis, and treatment planning. Dental students from the Stomatology College of Chongqing Medical University were classified into four pre-specified groups according to their self-reported clinical experience. All participants completed two virtual standardized patient cases: uncomplicated crown fracture (UCF) and symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIP). They then filled out two questionnaires assessing perceived usability and fidelity on 5-point Likert scales. Statistical analysis employed one-way analysis of variance or the Kruskal-Wallis test, with Bonferroni correction applied for multiple comparisons. The significance level was set at p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05, and effect sizes were reported as eta-squared (&#x3b7;2). **RESULTS:** A total of 149 students were recruited (data recovery rate: 96.64%). All groups rated the D-VSP highly for its usability and fidelity. SIP test scores differed significantly among the groups (H&#x2009;=&#x2009;10.914, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.012, &#x3b7;2&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.057). In addition, significant differences were found in the clinical examination section (H&#x2009;=&#x2009;8.219, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.042, &#x3b7;2&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.037) and the diagnosis section (H&#x2009;=&#x2009;22.063, p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001, &#x3b7;2&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.136) of the SIP test. For the UCF case, only the clinical examination section showed a significant difference (H&#x2009;=&#x2009;8.496, p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.037, &#x3b7;2&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.039). **CONCLUSION:** This study established face and construct validity for the D-VSP, supporting its potential as a tool for teaching and assessing clinical reasoning in endodontics.
