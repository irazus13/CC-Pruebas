## Resumen

Polytraumatic brain injury (pTBI) involves concurrent injuries to the brain and other body regions, demanding a multidisciplinary approach to pain management and rehabilitation. Here, we evaluated pTBI-induced disruption of endogenous pain modulation in male and female rats, and therapeutic impact of monominergic drugs and voluntary exercise. We established a rat pTBI model combining the lateral fluid percussion model of brain trauma and tibia fracture with pin fixation. We then evaluated mechanical nociceptive reactivity, descending control of nociception (DCN) and response to selective noradrenergic (NRI) and serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRI), as well as &#x3b1;1-, &#x3b1;2-adrenoceptor, and 5-HT7 serotonin receptor antagonists. pTBI caused chronic hindpaw mechanical hypersensitivity up to 180&#x202f;days post-injury (DPI) bilaterally in females and unilaterally in males. Acute NRI treatment elevated noradrenaline and reduced hindlimb hypersensitivity in both sexes, an effect reversed by an &#x3b1;1- adrenoceptor antagonist. While NRI pretreatment remained effective through 180 DPI in females, efficacy diminished in males by 49 DPI. In males, hindlimb hypersensitivity was instead reduced by enhancing serotonin signaling with an SSRI acting via the 5-HT7 receptor. DCN response was present in both male and female pTBI rats following SSRI or NRI pretreatment, respectively. In female pTBI rats, voluntary exercise initiated at 3 DPI restored normal sensitivity by 21 DPI and maintained an intact DCN response up to 180 DPI; these gains were reversed an &#x3b1;1- adrenoceptor antagonist. Overall, pTBI causes chronic mechanical hindlimb hypersensitivity treatable with sex-specific reuptake inhibitors, while early exercise restores long-term noradrenergic pain modulation in females.
