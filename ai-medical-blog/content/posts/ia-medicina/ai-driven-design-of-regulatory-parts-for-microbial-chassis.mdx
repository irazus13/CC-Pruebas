## Resumen

Synthetic biology aims to design, modify, or synthesize new biological parts, genetic networks, and metabolic pathways from the bottom up, and even to create artificial cells with specific functions, offering transformative solutions in medicine, chemical engineering, energy, and other fields. In constructing complex biological systems, the underlying biological parts serve as the core basic components. Conventional methods for obtaining parts rely mainly on mining and characterizing natural organisms, which are not only time-consuming and labor-intensive but also depend on lengthy empirical trial-and-error, making it difficult to meet diverse industrial demands. Using artificial intelligence (AI) to study parts enables the precise mining of implicit mapping relationships between massive biological sequences and their functions, thereby allowing efficient prediction of part expression levels and breaking through the constraints of natural species to design de novo high-activity parts that do not exist in nature. Building upon this, this paper systematically reviews the latest advances in the AI-assisted design of biological parts for microbial chassis. It first outlines the advantages of underlying AI algorithms in feature extraction and generation of biological sequences. Then, it details the specific applications and breakthroughs of AI technologies in engineering and de novo designing two core categories-transcriptional regulatory parts and translational regulatory parts-across different chassis cells. Finally, the article discusses the current challenges, such as data sparsity and weak cross-species generalization, in AI design and provides an outlook on a new blueprint for efficient part development that integrates physics-based models, large language models, and fully automated laboratories in the future. This review provides theoretical references for accelerating the rational design of biological parts and advancing the intelligent and industrial development of synthetic biology.
