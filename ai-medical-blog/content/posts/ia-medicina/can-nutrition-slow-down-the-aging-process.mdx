## Resumen

Aging is a complex biological process involving genomic, molecular, cellular, tissue, and systemic changes. In recent years, research evidence has accumulated showing that different dietary patterns can significantly influence the rate of aging at all these levels, the risk of developing age-related diseases, and life expectancy. Diets such as the Mediterranean diet, vegetarian diet, ketogenic diet, DASH, and eating patterns including intermittent fasting have been shown to reduce inflammation, improve metabolic markers, slow telomere shortening, reduce oxidative damage, and modulate other components of the aging process. Observational and interventional studies, including twin studies and long-term cohort studies, demonstrate that pro-healthy diets (rich in vegetables, fruits, fish, and whole grains) are associated with a slower epigenetic age compared to chronological age, reduced inflammation, and improved health profiles-both in younger and older adults. In contrast, Western dietary patterns characterized by processed foods, sugars, and saturated fats accelerate the aging process and increase the risk of age-related diseases. Technological advances, especially the introduction of artificial intelligence (AI) into nutritional research, are ushering in a new era of personalized medicine. AI enables the analysis of big data, identification of hidden patterns between diet, genetics, microbiome, and diseases, prediction of the future effects of dietary changes, and even the construction of personalized nutritional models based on each individual's genome, transcriptome, and microbiome. The vision for the future is that everyone will have a personal AI-based virtual assistant, which will create precise nutrition plans and predict how each dietary component will affect the aging process, disease risk, and quality of life. This will allow managing our aging process intelligently and data-driven, aiming not only for health maintenance, but also for longevity and aging in good health.
