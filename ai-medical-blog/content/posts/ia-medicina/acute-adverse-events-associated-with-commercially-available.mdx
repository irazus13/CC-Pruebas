## Resumen

**BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES:** Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cell therapy is increasingly used to treat a variety of diseases, but limited data exist on acute adverse events (AEs) associated with these infusions. We aimed to evaluate AEs associated with Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved CAR-T cell infusions in adult and pediatric patients. **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** This was a 5-year retrospective study carried out at a multi-hospital center. Demographic information, disease state, infusion data, medical history, and pre-infusion vitals were extracted from the electronic health record system. Acute AEs were graded per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) criteria. Associations between AEs and collected variables were analyzed using binary logistic regression and machine learning (random forest classifier) methods. **RESULTS:** Among 339 adult infusions, 37 AEs were identified (11%): idecabtagene vicleucel 7/40 infusions, lisocabtagene maraleucel 1/39, ciltacabtagene autoleucel 12/104, tisagenlecleucel 5/24, brexucabtagene autoleucel 3/26, and axicabtagene ciloleucel 9/106. Most AEs were grade 1 (54%). Eleven reactions were grade 2, four reactions were grade 3, and two reactions (hypotension, bronchospasm/shortness of breath) were grade 4. No grade 5 AEs occurred. In adults, dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) exposure normalized to body weight was associated with a higher acute AE risk (p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.007 [binary logistic regression]), supported by machine learning analysis. Among 30 pediatric infusions, one grade 1 hypotensive reaction occurred (~3%). No further statistical analyses were performed in the pediatric cohort because of the limited number of events. **CONCLUSION:** FDA-approved CAR-T cell infusions demonstrated a low rate of acute AEs, supporting the safety of current products. The association between increased DMSO exposure and acute AE risk identifies a potential, actionable target for further risk reduction.
