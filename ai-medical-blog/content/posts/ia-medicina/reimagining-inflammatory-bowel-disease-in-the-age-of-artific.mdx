## Resumen

Artificial intelligence (AI) is rapidly evolving from task-specific algorithms to multimodal foundation models capable of integrating heterogeneous biological and clinical data across the health-care continuum. In inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), this transformation offers an unprecedented opportunity to move beyond fragmented analyses toward a unified understanding and management of disease. Artificial intelligence is emerging as the connective infrastructure linking scientific discovery, therapeutic development, patient care, education, and health-care delivery systems into continuously learning ecosystems. In this viewpoint, we explore how AI may reshape IBD over the next decade through 5 interconnected domains. Advances in multimodal AI accelerate biological discovery by integrating multiomics, spatial biology, and foundation models to identify mechanistic disease endotypes. Simultaneously, AI can shorten the path from molecular discovery to therapeutic innovation by supporting target identification, predicting protein structure, drug positioning, and adaptive clinical trial design. These scientific advances converge at the bedside, where multimodal AI may transform patient care from reactive management to predictive precision medicine through integrated diagnostics, digital biomarkers, real-time monitoring and individualized risk prediction. Beyond clinical care, AI is redefining medical education by enabling adaptive learning, explainable decision support, and democratized specialist expertise, while contributing to sustainable health care through optimized resource allocation, workforce support, and continuously learning systems. Realizing this vision will require robust governance, interoperability, prospective validation, and equitable implementation. If responsibly deployed, AI holds the potential to redefine IBD diagnosis, classification, therapeutic strategies and management within an intelligent ecosystem able to accelerate discovery, personalize care, and improve outcomes for patients and health-care systems alike.
