## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Tumor budding (TB) is a histopathological marker of tumor aggressiveness and an established prognostic factor in colorectal cancer. In rectal cancer, however, TB is usually assessed on biopsy or resection material, and a reliable result may not be available when preoperative management is being considered. **PURPOSE:** To develop and internally validate a radiomics model for preoperative discrimination of low-grade versus intermediate/high-grade TB in patients with rectal cancer who proceed to upfront surgery without neoadjuvant treatment. The primary model was selected according to the highest repeated cross-validation AUC, with alternative feature sets and algorithms evaluated exploratorily. **METHODS:** This retrospective study included 139 patients with pathologically confirmed rectal cancer who had received no neoadjuvant treatment. TB was assessed on postoperative resection specimens. T1WI, T2WI, arterial-phase contrast-enhanced T1WI, and DWI were analyzed. Radiomics features were extracted from the tumor core, a combined tumor-plus-2-mm expansion, and an isolated external 2-mm ring. Seven feature sets and four machine-learning algorithms were evaluated; the primary model was selected according to the highest repeated cross-validation AUC. Feature filtering, imputation, selection, and scaling were performed within the training portion of every split. Model performance was estimated using stratified five-fold cross-validation repeated five times and patient-level out-of-fold (OOF) probabilities. **RESULTS:** The primary core-ROI Elastic Net model achieved a repeated cross-validation AUC of 0.825 (range 0.809-0.839 across repetitions), with PR-AUC 0.781, sensitivity 0.604, specificity 0.851, and Brier score 0.173. Its patient-level mean OOF AUC was 0.829 (2,000 paired bootstrap replicates; 95% CI, 0.755-0.896). At a probability threshold of 0.50, patient-level sensitivity was 0.607 and specificity was 0.867. In conditional paired bootstrap comparisons, the core model showed higher AUCs than the clinical, peritumoral-ring, clinical-plus-expanded, clinical-plus-core, and clinical-plus-core-plus-ring models (BH-adjusted P&#xa0;=&#xa0;0.004-0.005). The difference versus the expanded ROI was smaller and did not reach statistical significance after adjustment (P&#xa0;=&#xa0;0.052). **CONCLUSION:** Multiparametric MRI radiomics features from the tumor core showed promising internal discrimination of low-grade versus intermediate/high-grade TB among patients who underwent upfront surgery without neoadjuvant treatment. The model may support preoperative TB risk stratification in this population, but external validation is required before clinical use.
