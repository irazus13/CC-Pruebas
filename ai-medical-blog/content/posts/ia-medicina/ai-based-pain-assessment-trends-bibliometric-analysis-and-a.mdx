## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** The purpose of this study was to map the development and thematic structure of Scopus-indexed research on artificial intelligence (AI)-based pain assessment published between 2015 and 2025 and to develop a conceptual model supporting its clinical translation. **DESIGN:** A quantitative bibliometric and scientometric analysis was conducted and combined with author-developed conceptual framework development. **DATA SOURCES AND ANALYS:** English-language articles and conference papers published between January 1, 2015, and December 31, 2025, were retrieved from Scopus using search terms related to AI, machine learning, deep learning, digital sensing, and pain assessment. Following automated filtering, duplicate removal, and title-abstract screening for eligibility, 720 of the 2,550 initially retrieved records were retained for analysis. Keyword normalization was subsequently performed as an analytical preprocessing step to standardize terminology before bibliometric and thematic analyses. Bibliometrix/Biblioshiny and VOSviewer were used to analyze publication performance, annual trends, geographical distribution, keyword frequencies, co-occurrence relationships, and thematic clusters. **RESULTS:** The 720 included publications appeared across 408 sources and demonstrated an annual growth rate of 12.85%. The mean document age was 3.35 years, and the mean citation count was 12.39 citations per document. Publication output increased from 14 to 21 publications per year during 2015-2017 to 132 publications in 2024 and 137 in 2025. Five thematic clusters were identified: patient-centered biopsychosocial context; multimodal AI and physiological signal processing; perioperative outcomes and pharmacological prediction; multidimensional pain phenotyping and predictive modeling; and standardized assessment and psychometric validation. **CONCLUSIONS:** Research activity in AI-based pain assessment increased substantially during the study period and encompassed interconnected computational, physiological, clinical, behavioral, and methodological themes. However, important gaps remain in external clinical validation, dataset standardization, population diversity, explainability, and real-world implementation. A four-phase conceptual model is proposed, covering data-infrastructure development, algorithm design, clinical and ethical validation, and sustained implementation. **CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS:** The proposed model may help clinicians, researchers, healthcare organizations, and technology developers evaluate and plan the responsible integration of AI-supported pain-assessment systems. Clinical implementation should maintain professional oversight and prioritize patient-centered outcomes, transparent model behavior, diverse population validation, data protection, and compatibility with existing clinical workflows. The bibliometric findings describe the research landscape and should not be interpreted as direct evidence of clinical effectiveness.
