## Resumen

Background: This pilot survey explored the views of clinicians and service users regarding the use of AI in a specific diagnostic scenario of OME. Methods: An online survey was conducted in collaboration with a Patient and Public Involvement and Engagement panel and advertised through research and clinical networks, social media platforms. Participants (N = 95; 21-72 years) included clinicians (59%) and service users (32%; patients and carers) with experience of OME. Quantitative and qualitative data were collected and analysed, comparing the views of the two groups across the two contexts. Results: Participants demonstrated a generally positive view of AI in OME diagnostics and in healthcare. Clinicians and service users reported comparable levels of trust and acceptance, although clinicians expressed greater concerns about flexibility and accuracy. Perceived key benefits included time and cost savings, while transparency and trust appeared as pivotal facilitators for acceptance. Frequent concerns centred on operational reliability and ethical considerations, with less emphasis on data confidentiality. Conclusions: The findings indicate broad support for AI applications both in OME diagnosis and more widely in healthcare, contingent on addressing concerns about accuracy, human oversight, and ethical use. Recommendations include focused communication strategies emphasising transparency and human oversight in the clinical decision-making processes.
