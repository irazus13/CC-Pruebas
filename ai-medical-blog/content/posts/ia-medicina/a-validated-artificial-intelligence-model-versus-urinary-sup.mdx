## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** To develop and validate an artificial intelligence (AI) model that can identify kidney stone composition more accurately than the EQUIL2 urine supersaturation (SS) algorithm. **PATIENTS AND METHODS:** Patients with both a 24-hour urine SS panel and a kidney stone composition analysis conducted within 90 days of each other between September 24, 2015, and March 15, 2024, were identified (n=13,000 unique patients). Kidney stones were classified as either majority calcium oxalate (CaOx), majority calcium phosphate (CaP), uric acid, or other. A model was developed to predict kidney stone classification using demographic characteristics plus urinary SS panel results. This model was subsequently validated on a held-out data set of paired measurements collected between March 16, 2024, and March 15, 2025 (n=1653). The urinary SS for CaOx, hydroxyapatite (CaP), brushite (CaP), and uric acid were calculated using EQUIL2 according to routine clinical practice and expressed using the delta Gibbs free energy scale. **RESULTS:** The AI model was significantly more accurate in the validation data set for identifying stone composition compared with SS alone (accuracy, 0.73 vs 0.33; P&lt;.01). Additionally, the model outperformed SS alone in the validation data set for correctly identifying kidney stone composition in all analyses stratified into clinically relevant subgroups by age and sex. **CONCLUSION:** In all kidney stone categories and within various subgroups, the AI model outperformed the EQUIL2-calculated SS as well as previously published AI models in the correct prediction of CaOx, CaP, and uric acid kidney stones when using the 24-hour urine SS panel.
