## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** The distribution and abundance of smooth muscle within the human common bile duct (CBD) remain incompletely characterised, with previous studies relying predominantly on qualitative assessment. This study aimed to characterise smooth-muscle architecture within the human &#x200b;CBD using complementary qualitative assessment and semi-automated quantitative digital image analysis. **METHODS:** &#x200b;CBD specimens from 106 pancreaticoduodenectomies and 3 total pancreatectomies were stained for desmin, smooth muscle actin (SMA) and calretinin and analysed using QuPath ( https://qupath.github.io ). **RESULTS:** Desmin-positive smooth muscle was identified in all specimens and was predominantly scattered (44.0%) or interrupted (26.6%), while focal (25.7%) and continuous circumferential (3.7%) patterns were less frequent. Median desmin-positive area was 1.86% (IQR 1.17-2.79%), compared with 33.55% (IQR 25.96-43.54%) for SMA. Qualitative desmin classification demonstrated moderate concordance with quantitative desmin-positive area (&#x3c1;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.515, p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001). Smooth-muscle abundance and distribution did not differ significantly between malignant and non-malignant specimens and were not associated with preoperative bilirubin or CBD wall thickness. Malignant specimens demonstrated greater CBD wall thickness than non-malignant specimens despite similar smooth-muscle abundance. **CONCLUSIONS:** These findings demonstrate that smooth muscle is consistently identifiable but predominantly sparse and discontinuously distributed within the surgically resected human &#x200b;CBD. Semi-automated digital image analysis provides an objective approach to its quantification; however, studies incorporating disease-free controls and functional assessment are required to determine the physiological significance of these findings.
