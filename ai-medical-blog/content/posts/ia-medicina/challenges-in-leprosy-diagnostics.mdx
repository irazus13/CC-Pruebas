## Resumen

**INTRODUCTION:** Leprosy remains a public health challenge, with persistent diagnostic delays particularly affecting patients with paucibacillary, oligosymptomatic forms, and pure neural disease, in whom clinical manifestations may be subtle and conventional tests have limited sensitivity. **AREAS COVERED:** This narrative review examines the current diagnostic landscape of leprosy, focusing on clinical and dermato-neurological assessment, bacteriological examination, histopathology, serological assays, and molecular methods. It also discusses emerging diagnostic approaches, including multimarker strategies, point-of-care testing, digital health, and artificial intelligence. The literature was reviewed in conjunction with international diagnostic recommendations, with emphasis on evidence relevant to early diagnosis, decentralized testing, active case finding, and the identification of individuals at risk of disability. **EXPERT OPINION:** Clinical and dermato-neurological assessment remains the cornerstone of diagnosis, while laboratory methods provide complementary information but have important limitations, particularly in paucibacillary disease. No single diagnostic method can reliably detect all clinical forms of leprosy. Improving early diagnosis will therefore require integrated, context-adapted strategies that combine clinical assessment with complementary technologies, strengthen diagnostic capacity at decentralized levels, promote active case finding, and improve healthcare networks to reduce diagnostic delays and prevent disability.
