## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Traditional formative assessment development in specialized medical education is time-intensive and often poorly aligned with learning outcomes, particularly in highly technical domains such as patient immunomonitoring. Evidence on the feasibility and quality of AI-generated assessments in such specialized biomedical training remains limited. **OBJECTIVE:** This study aimed to evaluate the feasibility, implementation efficiency, expert review outcomes, and learner perceptions of a Claude 3.5 Sonnet-based approach for generating formative self-assessment questions from video-recorded lectures, contextualizing efficiency against lecturers' retrospective estimates of conventional manual question-writing time. **METHODS:** Using backward design and A-SMART (action-oriented, specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-based) criteria, we processed 63 segments from 8 video-recorded lectures on cytokine profiling, flow cytometry, and immune-cell phenotyping. Claude 3.5 Sonnet (Anthropic), guided by specialized educational prompts, generated assessment questions from transcribed lecture content. Each question was independently reviewed by subject-matter experts for accuracy, relevance, cognitive-level appropriateness, and alignment with learning outcomes, and was then accepted without revision, revised with minor edits, or deleted. We tracked processing and review times and surveyed 12 course participants (70.6% response rate) on their perceptions. **RESULTS:** The large language model generated 439 assessment questions (7.0 per segment); 342 (77.9%) were accepted without edits, 37 (8.4%) after minor revision, and 60 (13.7%) were deleted, for an overall acceptance rate of 86.3% (n=379; 95% CI 82.8%-89.2%). Processing averaged 37 seconds per segment vs an estimated 4956 seconds for conventional manual question development, based on lecturers' retrospective estimates, corresponding to 99.3% time savings for generation alone and 84.4% when expert validation was included. Most questions were medium (n=251, 57.2%) or hard (n=165, 37.6%) difficulty, with medium-difficulty items showing the highest acceptance rate (228/251, 90.8%). Students rated helpfulness in understanding content and knowledge-gap identification (4.1/5 each); 83.3% (10/12) found the questions helpful for understanding content, and 75.0% (9/12) reported they helped identify knowledge gaps. **CONCLUSIONS:** AI-assisted assessment generation from video lectures was feasible, was associated with substantial implementation-efficiency gains relative to lecturers' retrospective estimates, achieved high question retention following expert review, and was positively perceived by participating learners in a specialized medical education context. Future studies should determine whether this approach also improves learning outcomes, knowledge retention, or summative performance. This workflow offers a potentially transferable implementation model for formative assessment design, with relevance to health professional education across resource settings, pending evaluation in other disciplines and institutions.
