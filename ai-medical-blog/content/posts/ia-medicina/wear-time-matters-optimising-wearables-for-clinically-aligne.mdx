## Resumen

Wearable devices hold transformative potential for healthcare, yet their clinical impact is undermined by suboptimal long-term adherence. Although 24/7 always-on monitoring remains the default for maximising data completeness, achieving sustained wear could be limited by discomfort, inconvenience, or perceived lack of relevance, particularly during longitudinal monitoring. The clinical utility of wearables also depends on intelligent systems that filter, contextualise, and prioritise the resulting data into timely and actionable insights for individuals and clinicians. In this Viewpoint, we reframe wear time as a strategic design opportunity rather than a technical requirement. We provide illustrative examples of varying wear time requirements across clinical contexts: for instance, continuous monitoring is valuable for detecting intermittent episodes of paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, whereas periodic blood pressure monitoring may be appropriate for managing stable hypertension. Our systemic analysis identified nine interconnected dimensions influencing wear time adherence, spanning clinical contexts, human factors, and technological reliability. Building on these insights, we propose a strategic implementation framework grounded in human-centred design, adaptive precision, and intelligent engagement. By aligning wear time strategies with specific clinical contexts and individual characteristics, wearables can evolve from passive data collectors into personalised health companions that balance clinical precision with real-world usability, fostering sustainable adherence and clinically meaningful insights.
