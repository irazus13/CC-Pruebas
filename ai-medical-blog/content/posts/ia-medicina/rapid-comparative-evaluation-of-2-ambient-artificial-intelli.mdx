## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** To evaluate ambient Artificial Intelligence (AI) scribe systems (AAS) across multiple domains using a rapid learning health system (LHS) approach, including provider satisfaction, efficiency, documentation quality, usability, and patient sentiment. **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** This LHS evaluation compared 2 AAS systems (Abridge and Nabla) among 100 providers over 90 days. Evaluation incorporated a cluster randomized crossover comparison, analysis of use and EHR-based workflow outcomes, and standardized simulated encounters to assess documentation quality and safety. Outcomes included provider satisfaction, efficiency, note quality, usability, and patient sentiment. **RESULTS:** In standardized simulated encounters, both systems achieved 76% note element accuracy with minimal risk for harm. In the randomized crossover comparison, Abridge demonstrated higher usability scores (78.5 vs 73) and was preferred 3:1 over Nabla, which had higher automated speech recognition error rates. Across 16&#x2009;479 clinical encounters, AAS was used in 35.7% of visits. Total EHR time decreased by approximately 5 minutes per day; same-day chart closure increased by approximately 5% during the initial crossover period, but this effect was attenuated. Provider experience was positive (Net Promoter Score [NPS] 53). **DISCUSSION:** Both systems demonstrated comparable documentation accuracy and modest efficiency gains while improving clinician satisfaction. Despite similar performance, clinicians reported differences in usability and preference. Adoption below 40% reflected the trade-off between documentation support and verification burden. **CONCLUSION:** Rapid, multidimensional evaluation within a LHS can assess AAS across domains of implementation, efficiency, clinician experience, and documentation quality and safety. Ongoing monitoring of efficiency, documentation quality, and use patterns is essential.
