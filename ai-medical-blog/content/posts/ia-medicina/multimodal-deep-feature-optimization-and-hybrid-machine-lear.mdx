## Resumen

Diabetic foot ulcer (DFU) is a serious complication of diabetes that can result in infection, amputation, impaired quality of life, and increased healthcare costs. Accurate identification of DFU severity is therefore important for timely clinical management. This study presents a hybrid machine learning framework for DFU severity classification using a publicly available dataset containing 2,674 DFU images. Deep features were extracted from the available foot images and integrated using an autoencoder-based feature optimization approach to reduce redundancy and retain informative representations. Random Forest (RF) and Support Vector Machine (SVM) classifiers were subsequently employed for severity classification. Stratified group-fold cross-validation was used to provide patient-level evaluation and reduce potential information leakage between training and validation subsets. The proposed framework achieved an accuracy of 96.84%, precision of 0.91, recall of 0.89, F1-score of 0.90, AUC of 0.97, and specificity of 0.94. The results demonstrate the ability of the optimized feature representation and hybrid classifiers to discriminate among DFU severity grades (Grades 1-3). Performance remained relatively stable across the internal validation folds, although external generalizability has not been established. The findings indicate that autoencoder-based feature optimization combined with machine learning classification can provide an effective computational approach for automated DFU severity assessment. Further validation using independent, clinically characterized datasets is required before potential clinical deployment.
