## Resumen

As the earliest extant pictorial artwork depicting acupuncture-moxibustion-related medical customs, Jiu Ai Tu (Illustrations of Moxibustion and Acupuncture) provides invaluable visual historical materials for the study of the folk healthcare ecosystem during the Song Dynasty. From the perspective of medical iconography, the paper introduces a systematic interpretation of the physician-patient relationship, perceptions of disease, and therapeutic techniques of acupuncture and moxibustion depicted in this painting. By examining the identities of practitioners of traditional Chinese medicine, patient conditions, and various therapeutic approaches, including direct moxibustion with multiple cones, acupuncture for pus drainage, and external application of medicated plasters, this study reveals the interactive relationship between folk acupuncture practice during the Song Dynasty and the records preserved in medical texts. Furthermore, from a social historical standpoint, it explores the medical ecosystem of physician-patient relationship in the folk, the divergent fates of acupuncture-moxibustion therapies across different social strata, and the influence of the public dissemination of medical knowledge during the Song Dynasty, thereby providing a perspective from social history of knowledge for understanding the diverse dimensions of the medical society during the Song Dynasty.
