## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Clinician use of telehealth and ambient technologies remains uneven. Using the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), we examined what drives intention to use these tools, whether intention translates into use when access is partly assigned by role, and how trainees differ. **METHODS:** We conducted a cross-sectional survey of attending physicians, advanced practice providers (APPs), residents, and medical students at a large integrated health system in 2026, using an abbreviated, adapted UTAUT instrument and self-reported weekly use. Analyses included nonparametric tests and maximum-likelihood path models. **RESULTS:** Of 90 respondents, 37 (41.1%) were residents, 31 (34.4%) attending physicians, 14 (15.6%) medical students, and 8 (8.9%) APPs. Behavioral intention was high (mean 4.19/5) and did not differ by role (p = 0.241). In the hypothesized path model (n = 71), performance expectancy (&#x3b2; = 0.39) and social influence (&#x3b2; = 0.45) predicted intention (both p &lt; 0.001). Intention did not predict use (&#x3b2; = 0.07, p = 0.537), whereas facilitating conditions (FCs) did (&#x3b2; = 0.33, p = 0.006). This model fit poorly; adding a path from FCs to intention improved fit without changing these findings. Medical students reported lower FCs than attending physicians (2.71 vs. 4.12; Holm-adjusted p = 0.008) without significantly different intention. The most common free-text barriers were limited access and training. **CONCLUSIONS:** Clinicians and trainees reported high intention to use telehealth and ambient monitoring. FCs, not intention, were associated with use and were lowest among medical students.
