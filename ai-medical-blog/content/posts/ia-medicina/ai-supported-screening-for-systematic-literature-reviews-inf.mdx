## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** This study aims to improve the efficiency of title and abstract screening in systematic literature reviews (SLRs) supporting European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR) recommendations and points to consider, by developing and validating stopping criteria for active learning-assisted screening that achieve &#x2265;95% recall (ie, sensitivity). **METHODS:** Simulation studies analysed 40 development and 5 validation datasets (202,177 records) from completed and ongoing EULAR SLRs, using active learning models (h3, u3, and u4) in ASReview. Time-to-event analyses estimated the proportion of records that required screening to reach 95% recall and the minimum safe streak length (longest sequence of irrelevant records before reaching 95% recall). Empirical stopping criteria were derived, tested on independent datasets and evaluated for workload reduction and time savings. **RESULTS:** Datasets averaged 4493 records (SD, 2851), with a mean of 67 (SD, 46) records included. The u4 model was optimal for balancing screening efficacy and computational efficiency. Target recall was achieved after screening 35% of records or reaching sequences of 15% nonrelevant records. A combined criterion (26% screened plus 10% irrelevant streak) provided an alternative stopping criterion. Validation met target recall. Screening workload was reduced by 67.1%. **CONCLUSIONS:** Empirically derived stopping criteria using an active learning tool can substantially accelerate SLR screening while maintaining high recall (&#x2265;95%), allowing reviewers to skip about two-thirds of records during title and abstract screening. Artificial intelligence-assisted screening can substantially reduce evidence synthesis workload, thus supporting more efficient development of EULAR recommendations and points to consider.
