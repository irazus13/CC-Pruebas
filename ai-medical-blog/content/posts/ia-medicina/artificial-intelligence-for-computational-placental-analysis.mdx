## Resumen

Artificial intelligence (AI) could support placental examination where diagnostic resources are limited, but evidence for clinical deployment remains uncertain. We conducted a rapid scoping review, reported in accordance with PRISMA-ScR, of PubMed, IEEE Xplore, and the ACM Digital Library for studies published between 1 January 2015 and 31 December 2025. Of 1,441 unique records screened, 124 met the eligibility criteria, and 36 additionally demonstrated applicability to low-resource settings and were charted at full text. Most charted studies analysed ultrasound (27 of 36) and addressed segmentation and classification tasks using convolutional architectures, principally U-Net and its variants. Digital photography, a relatively accessible post-delivery modality, was analysed in six studies; 13 studies used data from low- or middle-income countries. While recent work has begun to explore multimodal learning, self-supervised approaches, and model efficiency, key challenges persist, including limited labelled data, variability in imaging conditions, and a lack of standardised evaluation datasets and frameworks. We also identify critical gaps in real-world deployment: computational constraints were addressed in eight charted studies and on-device evaluation reported in one, evidence of clinical workflow integration was limited, and external or cross-site validation was reported in five. This review highlights the need for data-efficient, robust, and clinically grounded AI systems and outlines future directions towards scalable and equitable computational placental analysis in global health contexts.
