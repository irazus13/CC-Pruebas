## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** To compare reoperation rates, complications, health care costs, and conversion to total knee arthroplasty (TKA) after partial meniscectomy versus meniscal repair. **METHODS:** A retrospective cohort study was performed using the PINC AI Healthcare Database to identify adults 18 and older who underwent isolated partial meniscectomy or meniscal repair between 2016 and 2022. Patients with concomitant ligament reconstruction or prior knee arthroplasty were excluded, and patients with pre-existing osteoarthritis were not excluded. Propensity score matching on age, sex, comorbidities, payer category, and index year was used to balance demographic and clinical characteristics between groups. Outcomes included 2-year reoperation rates, 30-day postoperative complications, and conversion to TKA. Mean cumulative health care costs were assessed at the index procedure, 9&#x2009;months, and 2&#x2009;years postoperatively. A Markov decision model estimated cumulative costs over a 20-year horizon using transition probabilities and cost inputs derived from the matched cohort. **RESULTS:** After matching, 56,439 patients were included (37,530 partial meniscectomy; 18,909 meniscal repair), with a mean age of 38.2 (interquartile range 26-47). Meniscal repair was associated with a higher 2-year reoperation rate compared with partial meniscectomy (7.3% vs 5.8%; P&#x2009;&#x2264;&#x2009;.001). Partial meniscectomy showed a higher 2-year conversion rate to TKA (0.9% vs 0.5%; P&#x2009;&#x2264;&#x2009;.001). Thirty-day complication rates were similar between groups (1.7% vs 1.7%; P&#x2009;=&#x2009;.847). Mean cumulative costs were higher for meniscal repair at the index procedure ($8222 vs $4835; P&#x2009;&#x2264;&#x2009;.001), 9&#x2009;months ($9394 vs $5930; P&#x2009;&#x2264;&#x2009;.001), and 2&#x2009;years ($10,383 vs $7056; P&#x2009;&#x2264;&#x2009;.001). Over a modeled 20-year horizon, meniscal repair was associated with lower cumulative costs driven by reduced progression to TKA. **CONCLUSIONS:** Meniscal repair is associated with higher early reoperation rates and greater short-term health care costs compared with partial meniscectomy but shows a lower rate of conversion to TKA and lower modeled cumulative costs over the long term. **LEVEL OF EVIDENCE:** Level III, retrospective cohort study.
