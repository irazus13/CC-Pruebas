## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Coughing is a nonspecific respiratory sign that is common in horses with asthma. Cough quantification is currently subjective and labor-intensive, leaving its diagnostic and monitoring potential underexplored. **HYPOTHESIS/OBJECTIVES:** (1) Develop a wearable audio recorder for horses; (2) collect recordings of coughing horses and annotate coughs; (3) train a machine learning model to automate cough detection; and (4) describe temporal patterns of coughing and determine pathophysiologic correlations in horses with asthma. **ANIMALS:** Seventy horses were recruited from a teaching herd, clinical cases, and via social media. Sixty had coughing for &#x2265;3&#xa0;weeks and valid recordings. **METHODS:** In our prospective observational and analytical study, coughs were recorded using a digital device, annotated by a single observer, and used to develop a machine-learning-based cough-detection pipeline. Machine learning output was analyzed using generalized linear mixed models to determine circadian patterns and pathophysiologic associations of coughing. **RESULTS:** We annotated 252 of 1260 recorded hours. We labeled 8555 sound events, 2780 of which were coughs of the instrumented horse. Our machine learning model detected coughs with a sensitivity of 0.95 and a specificity of 0.9993. In horses with asthma, coughing frequency was associated with neutrophilic lung inflammation and showed a diurnal pattern with peak rates around 3&#xa0;am. **CONCLUSIONS AND CLINICAL IMPORTANCE:** The combination of robust hardware for long-term recordings and high-performance machine learning algorithms has the potential to transform coughing from a relatively crude clinical sign to a quantifiable endpoint in research and clinical practice.
