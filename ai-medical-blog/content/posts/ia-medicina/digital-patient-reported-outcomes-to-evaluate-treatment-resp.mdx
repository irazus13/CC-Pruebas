## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** Patient-reported outcomes (PROs) are valuable tools for improving long-term patient outcomes and providing patient-centered care. PROs can be used to investigate real-world treatment effectiveness in patients with a chronic inflammatory disease. **METHODS:** This was a real-world cohort study with digital patient-reported data collection by patients with ulcerative colitis (UC) using TrueColours-IBD, a web-based platform for prospective monitoring of symptoms, quality of life, and outcomes in Oxford. Effectiveness of filgotinib was compared between advanced therapy-na&#xef;ve (ATN, i.e. filgotinib 1st line, n&#x2009;=&#x2009;44) and advanced therapy-experienced (ATE, i.e. prior biological/small-molecule therapy, n&#x2009;=&#x2009;56) patients using the following outcomes, evaluated post-induction (8-12&#x2009;weeks) and at 6 and 12&#x2009;months: clinical remission (Simple Clinical Colitis Activity Index (SCCAI) &#x2264;2), clinical response (&#x2265;2 point SCCAI reduction from baseline), corticosteroid-free remission (defined as clinical remission without any prednisolone or budesonide in the 12&#x2009;weeks preceding that time point), and treatment discontinuation (defined as stopped prescription of the medication). **RESULTS:** There were no significant differences between the ATN and ATE groups regarding clinical remission, response, and corticosteroid-free remission at any time point, with P values &gt; 0.1. More than half of the patients in each group achieved clinical remission and response at all time points. Filgotinib discontinuation rates within 12&#x2009;months were comparable (P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.095). **CONCLUSION:** This study shows that PROs can serve as an important tool in evaluating real-world treatment outcomes in patients with UC using filgotinib. The results demonstrate good subjective clinical effectiveness, regardless of prior advanced therapy exposure.
