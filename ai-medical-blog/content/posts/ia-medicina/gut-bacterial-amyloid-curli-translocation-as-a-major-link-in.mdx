## Resumen

**INTRODUCTION:** Emerging evidence links alterations in the gut microbiota to Alzheimer's disease (AD) progression via the microbiota-gut-brain axis. In this study, we propose that persistent gut dysbiosis from young adulthood may trigger central amyloid beta (A&#x3b2;) pathogenesis at a later age via enteric nervous system and systemic communications. **METHODS:** Novel Object Recognition test and Y-maze were used to determine cognitive impairment (CI) longitudinally in Tg2576 familial and sporadic huAPP (hA&#x3b2;-KI) AD mice with gut inflammation or infection. 16S-rRNA-gene-amplicon sequencing to determine gut microbiota composition. Human and mouse AD plasma samples were used to perform proteomics analysis. **RESULTS:** Gut inflammation with dextran sulfate sodium or gut infection with curli-producing Escherichia coli caused dysbiosis-associated elevated luminal curli with increased translocation through plasma-derived extracellular vesicles (pEVs) in AD mice. Increased pEV-curli correlates with CI. Patients with mild CI who progressed to AD showed elevated pEV-curli. **DISCUSSION:** These findings identify a causal role of curli in host A&#x3b2; progression and highlight curli-pEVs as a potential early biomarker.
