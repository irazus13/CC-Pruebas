## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Left atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO) is an alternative to oral anticoagulation for stroke prevention in patients with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation who are unsuitable for long-term anticoagulation. Ischemic stroke may still occur after LAAO, but its mechanisms remain incompletely defined. **OBJECTIVES:** This study aimed to determine the incidence, mechanisms, outcomes, and associated factors of ischemic stroke following percutaneous LAAO. **METHODS:** This single-center retrospective cohort study included consecutive patients who underwent LAAO at a tertiary center in Hong Kong (June 2009-July 2024). The primary outcome was post-LAAO ischemic stroke. **RESULTS:** Among 482 patients (median age, 73.0 years, 33.6% women, median follow-up, 4.89 years), 38 developed ischemic strokes (incidence 7.88%, 1.44 per 100 person-years). Mechanisms were heterogeneous: cardioembolic (39.5%), noncardioembolic (34.2%), periprocedural (5.3%), and undetermined (21.0%). Although 93.8% (n = 452) of patients underwent follow-up transesophageal echocardiogram (TEE), only half of patients with stroke underwent repeat TEE. In this subgroup, peridevice leak was present in 63.2% (n = 12; major leaks &gt;3 mm in 50%; new leaks in 50%), and new device-related thrombus was detected in 15.8% (n = 3). Oral anticoagulation was resumed in 65.8% (n = 25), with subsequent bleeding in 40% (n = 10) and recurrent ischemic stroke in 12% (n = 3). Background congestive heart failure was associated with post-LAAO ischemic stroke (adjusted HR: 3.05; 95% CI: 1.44-6.47; P = 0.004). **CONCLUSIONS:** Residual ischemic stroke risk after LAAO remains substantial, with heterogeneous mechanisms. Repeat TEE after stroke was infrequent despite common peridevice leaks identified. Background heart failure was associated with post-LAAO ischemic stroke, implicating residual appendage abnormalities and cardiovascular substrate.
