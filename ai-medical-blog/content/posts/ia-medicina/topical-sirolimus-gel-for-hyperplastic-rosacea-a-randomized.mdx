## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Hyperplastic rosacea is difficult to treat. Sirolimus may be a topical option. **OBJECTIVE:** To evaluate topical sirolimus gel and explore two-dimensional-to-three-dimensional approximate protrusion volume (APV) assessment. **METHODS:** In this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 65 patients applied 0.2% sirolimus gel (n = 29) or placebo (n = 36) twice daily. The primary endpoint was an Investigator's Global Assessment (IGA) score &#x2264;1 after a 12-week cycle. Secondary and exploratory endpoints included Clinician's Erythema Assessment (CEA) success, artificial intelligence-estimated APV change, subgroup analyses, and correlations between APV and clinical scores. **RESULTS:** IGA success was 72.4% versus 33.3% (adjusted relative risk, 1.949; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.153-3.294; P = .013), and CEA success was 69.0% versus 30.6% (adjusted relative risk, 2.778; 95% CI, 1.627-4.743; P &lt; .001). APV reduction was greater with sirolimus (-61.556 units; P &lt; .001). Baseline APV correlated with IGA (&#x3c1; = 0.386; P = .001) and CEA (&#x3c1; = 0.314; P = .011). No serious adverse events occurred. **LIMITATIONS:** Single-center design, small sample, short follow-up, unvalidated algorithm, and no systemic absorption, quality-of-life, or satisfaction assessments. **CONCLUSION:** Topical sirolimus gel showed exploratory clinical and APV signals; confirmatory trials are needed.
