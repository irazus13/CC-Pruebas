## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** Obesity is a heterogeneous, chronic metabolic disease driven by dysregulation of energy homeostasis across interconnected central nervous system, gastrointestinal, adipose, endocrine, immune, and mitochondrial networks. Despite the established roles of lifestyle intervention and metabolic/bariatric surgery, limitations in accessibility, durability, and interindividual response have stimulated the development of mechanism-based pharmacotherapies. This review critically examines emerging therapeutic strategies targeting integrated energy-regulatory pathways and evaluates their translational potential for precision obesity management. **METHODS:** Current evidence was synthesized across major biological systems governing appetite, energy expenditure, and metabolic adaptation. Central melanocortin, Neuropeptide Y (NPY)/ Agouti-related peptide (AgRP), serotonergic, dopaminergic, and endocannabinoid pathways were considered alongside gut-derived incretin, amylin, Peptide YY (PYY), ghrelin, Fibroblast Growth Factor (FGF), and bile acid signaling. Emerging approaches targeting adipose remodeling, thermogenesis, mitochondrial energetics, AMPK-mTOR-sirtuin signaling, inflammation, and fibrosis were also evaluated. Genetic, epigenetic, and artificial intelligence-based approaches to patient stratification were incorporated to assess prospects for precision therapy. **RESULTS:** Obesity pharmacotherapy is shifting from conventional appetite suppression toward coordinated modulation of interconnected metabolic pathways. Incretin-based and multi-agonist therapies provide the strongest clinical evidence for substantial weight reduction and metabolic improvement. However, treatment discontinuation, weight regain, adverse effects, cost, and heterogeneous therapeutic responses remain major challenges. Strategies targeting adipose tissue dysfunction, mitochondrial metabolism, thermogenesis, immune-metabolic signaling, and fibrosis offer additional opportunities for disease modification, although many remain in preclinical or early clinical development. Increasing evidence for biological heterogeneity supports individualized therapeutic selection. **CONCLUSION:** The emerging therapeutic paradigm positions obesity as a systems-level disorder requiring mechanism-informed, individualized intervention. Integrating CNS-gut-adipose-mitochondrial biology with multi-omics, biomarkers, and artificial intelligence may enable more durable and precise treatment. Long-term safety, durability, affordability, equitable access, and clinical validation remain essential for translation.
