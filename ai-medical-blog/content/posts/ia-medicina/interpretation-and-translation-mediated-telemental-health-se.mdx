## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Individuals with limited English proficiency (LEP) or limited host-language proficiency (LLP) represent a growing, medically underserved population at disproportionate risk for poor mental health outcomes. Most mental and behavioral health services provided to individuals with LEP or LLP are delivered by providers who do not speak the patient's preferred language, due to a shortage of bilingual mental health professionals. Since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, the number of telehealth-delivered outpatient mental health (telemental) visits has increased, yet little is understood about the provision of interpretation and/or translation-facilitated telemental health care in populations with LEP and LLP. **OBJECTIVE:** This study is a scoping review that aimed to characterize the existing evidence on interpretation- and translation-mediated telemental health services for adults with LEP or LLP. We examined study characteristics, populations, and settings represented, telemental health modalities used, reported barriers and facilitators, and the methodological quality and gaps within the current evidence base. **METHODS:** Included studies focused on adults receiving interpreter and/or translation-mediated telemental health services. All studies were published in the English language, which may limit generalizability. Studies with bilingual providers, nonbehavioral health care, mobile health (mHealth) applications, or deaf or hard-of-hearing populations were excluded. Online databases were used to identify papers published through May 2026, from which we selected 15 publications from 137 full-text articles retrieved for full-text screening. **RESULTS:** A total of 60% (9/15) of publications reported qualitative or mixed method designs, while 40% (6/15) were quantitative. Most were conducted with Spanish-speaking populations. Of the 15 studies, 12 achieved &gt;4 out of 5 using the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT). Thematic analysis yielded the following four themes across the articles reviewed: (1) facilitators of access, (2) barriers to access, (3) therapeutic process considerations, and (4) recommendations to improve quality of and access to interpreter- or translation-mediated telemental health care in LEP and LLP populations. **CONCLUSIONS:** As previous reviews focused primarily on in-person mental health care, this scoping review is among the first to synthesize evidence specifically on interpreter and translation-mediated telemental health for individuals with LEP or LLP. In addition, its inclusion of literature published after COVID-19 allowed for an examination of how the literature has evolved from the prepandemic period to the post-2020 telemental health expansion. Findings underscored the following needs: (1) address access barriers to telemental health care in populations with LEP and LLP, which persist following the postpandemic increase in telemental health adoption; (2) standardize training for clinicians and interpreters; and (3) update clinical guidelines in the context of rapid telehealth uptake in behavioral health care. It also highlighted the potential impact of emerging AI technologies for real-time translation in mental health care, calling for further research on their ethical and clinical implications.
