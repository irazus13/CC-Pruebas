## Resumen

**AIM:** To examine attitudes towards the use of artificial intelligence technologies in nursing and associated factors among a convenience sample of registered nurses across multiple regions in China and to describe connections among individual attitude components. **BACKGROUND:** Artificial intelligence is becoming increasingly embedded in nursing practice. As key users and implementers of these technologies, nurses' attitudes are critical to successful AI adoption. However, the factors associated with these distinct attitude dimensions and the connections among individual attitude components remain insufficiently understood. **DESIGN:** A multiregional online cross-sectional survey. **METHODS:** From March 27 to April 1, 2026, 1828 registered nurses were recruited using convenience and snowball sampling through WeChat. Most participants were recruited from hospitals in Liaoning Province, particularly Shenyang, while 346 (18.9%) participants were from provinces or municipalities outside Liaoning. Data were collected using a confidential online questionnaire assessing AI attitudes, demographic and occupational characteristics, job demands, job resources (nursing work environment), external social resources (perceived social support) and technology-related beliefs and emotional responses (general technophobia). An item-level network analysis described conditional associations among the 15 attitude items. Univariate comparisons, Pearson correlations and separate multiple linear regression models characterised positive and negative attitudes and their associated factors. **RESULTS:** The mean positive- and negative-attitude scores were 33.12&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;7.00 and 15.97&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;5.60, respectively. In the item-level network, 'I hope to acquire the skills needed to use artificial intelligence technologies' showed the highest expected influence centrality. In adjusted analyses, higher work environment scores (B&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.090, 95% CI 0.065-0.115) and social support scores (B&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.091, 95% CI 0.065-0.118) were associated with higher positive-attitude scores. Higher general technophobia scores were associated with higher negative-attitude scores (B&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.122, 95% CI 0.105-0.139). The models explained modest proportions of variance (adjusted R2&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.123 and 0.136, respectively). **CONCLUSIONS:** Positive and negative attitudes towards AI showed distinct patterns of association in this multiregional convenience sample. The findings may inform further evaluation of the potential roles of nurses' learning needs, supportive work conditions and AI-related concerns in nursing management. **IMPLICATIONS FOR NURSING MANAGEMENT:** Nursing managers may consider evaluating AI-related education, clear ethical and operational guidance and supportive work environments as potential components of AI implementation strategies.
