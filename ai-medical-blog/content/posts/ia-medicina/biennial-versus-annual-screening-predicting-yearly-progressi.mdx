## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** To use machine learning models to classify diabetic patients into low-risk (candidate for biennial screening) and high-risk (needing annual screening) categories based on their likelihood of worsening diabetic retinopathy (DR) only (excluding progression of diabetic macular edema) within 1 and 2 years from tabular data. **DESIGN:** Retrospective cohort study. **PARTICIPANTS:** Deidentified electronic health record data of 32 542 patients with diabetes mellitus from the United States Veterans Health Administration Technology and Eye Care Services teleophthalmology program. **METHODS:** Eight prediction approaches were evaluated, spanning rule-based methods, traditional machine learning, and deep learning architectures. **MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES:** Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), sensitivity, specificity, expected calibration error, and Brier score, with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). **RESULTS:** Sixteen thousand six hundred and six veterans with diabetes and no DR at baseline were included in the 1-year cohort and 15 397 in the 2-year cohort. Predictive models demonstrated clinically meaningful risk stratification across both prediction horizons. A deep learning model (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers for Healthcare) achieved the strongest discrimination, with an AUROC of 0.91 (95% CI, 0.85-0.96) at 1 year and 0.84 (95% CI, 0.76-0.91) at 2 years. When constraining sensitivity to 90% to capture &#x2265;9 in 10 patients who will progress, Extreme Gradient Boosting correctly identified 53% of nonprogressors as low risk at 1 year and 52% at 2 years, suggesting that more than half of low-risk patients could be deferred from routine screening without substantially compromising the detection of true progressors. Performance across both horizons was achieved using only routinely collected tabular variables, without retinal imaging. **CONCLUSIONS:** Machine learning models can effectively identify low-risk diabetic patients suitable for biennial screening intervals without retinal imaging. These models may also help prioritize patients who have not yet been screened given that DR frequently remains undiagnosed. Together, our findings support the feasibility of transitioning a meaningful proportion of low-risk patients to biennial screening, with the potential to reduce unnecessary examinations and optimize resource allocation in high-burden health systems without compromising the detection of clinically significant disease progression. **FINANCIAL DISCLOSURES:** Proprietary or commercial disclosure may be found in the Footnotes and Disclosures at the end of this article.
