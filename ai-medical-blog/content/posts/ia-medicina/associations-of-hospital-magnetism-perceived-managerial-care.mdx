## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** To examine the associations between hospital magnetism (HM) and new nurses' AI literacy (AIL) and to test the parallel indirect associations of perceived managerial care (PMC) and technology acceptance (TA). **METHODS:** A three-wave survey was conducted among 556 new nurses from 10 tertiary hospitals in China (September 2025-March 2026). HM was measured at T1, PMC, and TA at T2 and AIL at T3. PROCESS Model 6 was initially specified to test H4. Because PMC and TA were measured at the same time point, a mediator-order sensitivity analysis was conducted; both the hypothesized and reversed serial orders yielded significant indirect effects, indicating that H4 was not supported. The parallel mediation model (PROCESS Model 4) was therefore adopted as the primary final interpretable and exploratory model. Analyses used 5000 individual-level bootstrap samples in PROCESS for indirect effects, cluster-robust standard errors (df&#x2009;=&#x2009;9), and a separate hospital-level cluster bootstrap for indirect effects, with Wild Cluster Bootstrap sensitivity analyses. **RESULTS:** HM, PMC, TA, and AIL were significantly positively correlated. In the exploratory parallel mediation model, HM was directly associated with AIL (&#x3b2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.177, 95% CI [0.082, 0.273]). In the individual-level PROCESS bootstrap, significant indirect associations were found through PMC (indirect association&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.322, 95% CI [0.260, 0.388]) and TA (indirect association&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.092, 95% CI [0.040, 0.144]); the hospital-level cluster bootstrap yielded similar results. **CONCLUSION:** Among new nurses in 10 tertiary general hospitals, HM was associated with AIL, both directly and indirectly through PMC and TA in an exploratory parallel mediation analysis. Serial mediation was not supported. These findings suggest that organizational factors, supportive management, and technology-related perceptions may be relevant to AI-related competencies among new nurses and may have implications for nursing workforce readiness in AI-driven healthcare environments. The sample was limited to tertiary general hospitals; therefore, the findings should not be generalized to secondary or community hospitals without further validation. **IMPLICATIONS FOR NURSING MANAGEMENT:** Nursing managers in tertiary hospitals may consider strategies aimed at HM, supportive managerial practices, and TA in efforts related to AIL among new nurses. AI-related training, continuous technical support, and resource provision may be relevant to developing a digitally competent nursing workforce. Future longitudinal or experimental studies including secondary and community hospitals are needed to confirm these associations.
