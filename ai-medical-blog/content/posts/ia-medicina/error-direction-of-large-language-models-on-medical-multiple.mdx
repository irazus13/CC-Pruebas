## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Large language models (LLMs) attain high accuracy on medical examinations, yet evaluations rely predominantly on single-best-answer multiple-choice questions and report only overall accuracy. Multiple-response items, termed X-type in China, require selecting every correct option and remain understudied. Whether models err by omitting correct options or by commissioning incorrect ones has yet to be assessed within an opportunity-standardized framework. **OBJECTIVE:** This study aimed to quantify the accuracy penalty of multiple-response (X-type) items relative to single-select items and to determine, after standardizing for the unequal numbers of correct and incorrect options, whether LLMs lean toward omission or commission. **METHODS:** We analyzed 1644 text-based items from 10 examination cohorts (2017-2026) of the Chinese National Postgraduate Entrance Examination in Clinical Medicine, comprising 1344 single-select items of types A1, A2, A3, and B, and 300 X-type items. Five LLMs, DeepSeek V4 Pro, Kimi K3, Doubao Seed-2.1-pro, GPT-5.6 Sol, and Gemini-3.6 Flash, were tested zero-shot bilingually, yielding 16,440 planned responses. Item-type effects were estimated using generalized estimating equations with cluster-robust standard errors, adjusting for model, language, discipline, and cohort. Opportunity-standardized error direction was analyzed 2 ways: an option-level model treating each option as 1 judgment record and the omission-bias index. **RESULTS:** Of 16,440 planned responses, 16,426 (99.91%) were evaluable. X-type accuracy (2527/3000, 84.23%) was markedly below single-select accuracy (12,990/13,426, 96.75%; adjusted odds ratio at GPT-5.6 Sol in Chinese 0.17, 95% CI 0.11-0.26; P&lt;.001), a gap of 12.52 percentage points observed and 13.55 adjusted, with no statistically significant item type &#xd7; model or item type &#xd7; language interaction (P=.19 and P=.82). Raw counts suggested more strict omissions (245/3000, 8.17%) than overselections (201/3000, 6.70%), but X-type items averaged 2.79 correct vs 1.21 incorrect options per item, a 2.3-fold imbalance in error opportunities. After opportunity standardization, the per-opportunity false-selection rate was 6.69% (242/3620), roughly twice the 3.57% per-opportunity omission rate (299/8380; rate ratio 1.87, 95% CI 1.28-2.74; adjusted odds ratio at the reference model and language 0.38, 95% CI 0.20-0.70; P=.002), so the tilt is modest on the absolute scale and close to 2-fold on the relative scale. Strict omission showed no between-model difference (all Holm-adjusted pairwise comparisons, P&#x2265;.46). **CONCLUSIONS:** LLMs incur a substantial accuracy penalty on medical multiple-response items. The apparent omission dominance in raw counts is an artifact of answer-key opportunity imbalance; after opportunity standardization, models show a commission tilt that is modest on the absolute scale (3.12 percentage points per opportunity) and close to 2-fold on the relative scale, and that holds in every model-language condition and in the answer-key stratum whose opportunity base is exactly balanced. Medical LLM benchmarks should report accuracy stratified by item type and error direction on an opportunity-standardized basis.
