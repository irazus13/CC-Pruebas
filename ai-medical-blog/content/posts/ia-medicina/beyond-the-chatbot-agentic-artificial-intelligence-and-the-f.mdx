## Resumen

Artificial intelligence (AI) discourse in infectious disease (ID) has focused on benchmarking large language model chatbots against clinicians, a useful but narrowing inquiry. Agentic AI is a different paradigm: goal-directed systems that pursue clinical objectives over time, integrating data, invoking tools, monitoring results, and adapting without repeated prompting. This Viewpoint defines the anatomy of an agent, situates it within a 3-level framework (predictive, conversational, agentic), and examines how agents could transform 6 domains of ID practice: clinical care, antimicrobial stewardship, clinical microbiology, infection prevention and control, research, and epidemiology and pandemic preparedness. Individual components have proven value; integrated systems remain untested. No validated agentic system has been deployed in clinical ID; error propagation, algorithmic bias, cost, absent validation frameworks, undefined liability, and consequences for the workforce and its training must be addressed first. The ID community has a narrow window to shape these systems before decisions are made without it.
