## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** This study aims to compare image feature identification and diagnostic performance between artificial intelligence-aided image-enhanced endoscopy (using the Storz Professional Image Enhancement System, SPIES) and conventional white-light endoscopy (WLE) for sinonasal inverted papilloma (SIP). **METHODS:** A total of 163 patients were investigated using Storz Professional Image Enhancement System (SPIES) endoscopy (SPIES Spectral A[SA]/Spectral B[SB]) and WLE. From each patient, one or two images (only in the presence of bilateral lesions) were obtained, and a large number of potential features were extracted from WLE, SA, and SB. Independent t-tests and LASSO methods were used to feature selection. Machine learning (ML) models were trained and evaluated for each channel of images. **RESULTS:** The Random Forest (RF) algorithm on SPIES SB mode achieved superior performance: accuracy&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.86, sensitivity&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.89, specificity&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.83, AUC&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.86, and Kappa&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.71. SB significantly outperformed SA (accuracy&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.77) and WLE (accuracy=0.75). ROC analysis confirmed SB's diagnostic superiority (area under curve [AUC]: SB&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.86 vs. SA&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.77, WLE&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.75). **CONCLUSIONS:** SPIES SB mode demonstrated the highest diagnostic accuracy and AUC for SIP identification, highlighting its potential as a superior imaging modality. SPIES endoscopy exhibits superior diagnostic accuracy and predictive performance compared to conventional WLE in the diagnosis of SIP. However, the single-center design and limited sample size necessitate validation through multicenter studies with larger cohorts.
