## Resumen

Nuclear medicine has a long-standing role in imaging of infection and inflammation because a single radiotracer administration can survey multiple organ systems and detect pathophysiologic abnormalities that may precede changes on anatomic imaging. Whole-body acquisition can localize an occult source of systemic illness, define the extent of infection beyond a known primary site, including septic emboli and metastatic foci, and map the distribution and activity of multisystem inflammatory disease for treatment planning and response assessment. This review examines whole-body scintigraphy, SPECT/CT, PET/CT, and PET/MRI, with emphasis on indication-specific protocols, field-of-view selection, and incremental diagnostic value. We discuss settings in which regional or dedicated acquisitions are appropriate, which are often obtained in addition to whole-body imaging. We highlight representative clinical applications including peripheral and spinal osteomyelitis, fever of unknown origin, cardiovascular infection, large-vessel vasculitis, and sarcoidosis. We also note the limitations of nuclear medicine imaging and the need to correlate it with clinical parameters and, when available, to compare it with anatomic imaging modalities. Lastly, we will provide insight into future directions in the field, including increasing incorporation of long-axial-field-of-view PET, emerging new radiotracers, and the use of artificial intelligence.
