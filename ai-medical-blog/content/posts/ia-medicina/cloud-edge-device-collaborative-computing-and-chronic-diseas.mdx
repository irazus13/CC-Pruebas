## Resumen

Continuous monitoring, timely intervention, and personalized long-term management are necessary for chronic diseases. Conventional healthcare systems often fail to assess patients' health status in real time. Cloud-edge-device collaborative computing combines Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) devices, edge computing, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence (AI) to enable distributed processing and remote healthcare delivery. This scoping review aimed to map the current evidence on the application of cloud-edge-device collaborative computing in the management of chronic diseases. The Joanna Briggs Institute methodology and the PRISMA Extension for Scoping Reviews guidelines were used in this scoping review. PubMed, Scopus, the Cochrane Library, Web of Science, Embase, Google Scholar, and selected grey literature sources were searched from database inception to 1 July 2026, with results limited to studies published from 2000 onward. Original cloud-edge-device collaborative computing studies focused on chronic disease management were included, and data were analyzed narratively. Ten studies, including experimental implementations, system development studies, conceptual frameworks, and one randomized controlled trial published between 2015 and 2025, were included. Cloud-edge-device architectures were applied in the management of diabetes (n = 5), monitoring of cardiovascular disease (n = 1), management of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD; n = 1), and multi-disease/multi-morbidity applications (n = 3). IoMT devices continuously obtained physiological data, while edge/fog layers processed local data, reduced latency, and made real-time decisions. Cloud platforms stored and analyzed large-scale data and trained the models. Artificial intelligence methods, especially machine-learning algorithms, predicted and assessed disease risks. However, only one study was a clinical trial involving patients; most studies used benchmark datasets, volunteers, or simulations, meaning real-world clinical validation remains limited. Cloud-edge-device collaborative computing has been used to integrate distributed computing, IoMT, and AI for chronic disease management, but current evidence is mainly technical, and its clinical benefits remain largely unestablished.
