## Resumen

Noncanonical amino acids are essential molecular building blocks for expanding biochemical functionality and structural diversity, and have demonstrated broad application potential in areas such as innovative drug development, biomaterial construction and enzyme engineering. However, their conventional production has relied primarily on total chemical synthesis, which typically involves complex synthetic routes, limited stereoselectivity and serious environmental pollution, making it difficult to meet the demands of large-scale and sustainable manufacturing. Consequently, biomanufacturing has emerged as a green, efficient, and economically viable approach for producing noncanonical amino acids. This review systematically summarizes recent advances in the biomanufacturing of noncanonical amino acids from three key perspectives: their application value, production bottlenecks and specific biomanufacturing strategies. The focus is on summarizing core technologies, including the reconstruction and "hijacking" of endogenous metabolic pathways, substrate promiscuity and broad utilization of enzyme substrates, rational design of key enzymes and artificial intelligence-assisted directed evolution, cofactor regeneration enhancement, modular assembly of single-enzyme and multi-enzyme cascade reactions, as well as fermentation process scale-up and throughput improvement. Furthermore, in the context of the current bioeconomy landscape, this review analyzes the challenges and industrialization potential of biomanufacturing strategies for noncanonical amino acids, aiming to provide a comprehensive reference for future research and industrial development.
