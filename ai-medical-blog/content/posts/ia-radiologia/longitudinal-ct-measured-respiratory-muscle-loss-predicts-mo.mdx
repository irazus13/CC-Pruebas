## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** The impact of changes in chest wall respiratory muscle volume on outcomes in septic patients remains unclear. The objective of this study was to develop and validate a nomogram incorporating longitudinal respiratory muscle volume parameters and clinical variables to predict mortality in patients with sepsis. **METHODS:** This multicentre study retrospectively enrolled 793 septic patients, divided into training (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;361), internal (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;143) and external (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;289) validation cohorts. Changes in chest wall respiratory muscle volume were measured on two non-contrast chest CT scans &#x2265;&#x2009;5&#x2009;days apart. The nomogram was constructed using Cox regression and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator regression in the training cohort, followed by validation in the internal and external cohorts. The predictive performance of the nomogram was compared with that of the acute physiology and chronic health evaluation II (APACHE II) score using integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) values. Patients were stratified into low-, moderate- and high-risk groups according to two optimal cutoff values of the nomogram score, and survival differences were analysed using Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests. **RESULTS:** The median age was 69&#x2009;years (interquartile range, 58-79), and 540 patients (68.10%) were male. Greater chest wall respiratory muscle volume loss was independently associated with increased mortality (hazard ratio&#x2009;=&#x2009;1.05, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.04-1.07, p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001). The nomogram achieved time-dependent areas under the curve (AUCs) of 0.77 (95% CI: 0.71-0.82) for 28-day, 0.81 (95% CI: 0.77-0.86) for 3-month and 0.86 (95% CI: 0.82-0.90) for 1-year mortality in the training cohort, corresponding to AUCs of 0.70 (95% CI: 0.59-0.81), 0.78 (95% CI: 0.70-0.87) and 0.82 (95% CI: 0.74-0.89) in the internal validation cohort, and 0.74 (95% CI: 0.67-0.81), 0.78 (95% CI: 0.72-0.84) and 0.79 (95% CI: 0.73-0.84) in the external validation cohort. The nomogram improved discrimination over the APACHE II score, with IDI values ranging from 0.036 to 0.276 across cohorts and time points (all p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05). Kaplan-Meier analysis showed significant survival differences among the low-, moderate- and high-risk groups defined by the nomogram in all three cohorts (all log-rank p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001). **CONCLUSIONS:** Chest wall respiratory muscle volume loss was associated with mortality in patients with sepsis. A nomogram incorporating longitudinal respiratory muscle volume changes improved mortality prediction and risk stratification beyond the APACHE II score.
