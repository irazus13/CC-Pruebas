## Resumen

**UNLABELLED:** Variability in periprosthetic DXA limits reproducibility and clinical translation. This multisociety RAND/UCLA consensus provides a standardized framework for ppDXA acquisition, analysis, ROI definition, longitudinal LSC-based interpretation, and structured reporting for hip and knee arthroplasty. The framework supports auditable measurement and reporting, does not advocate universal routine ppDXA screening, and is not intended to establish outcome-validated periprosthetic BMD thresholds. **PURPOSE:** Periprosthetic dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (ppDXA) is increasingly used to measure areal bone mineral density (BMD) around hip and knee implants and to characterize longitudinal changes associated with periprosthetic bone remodeling. However, variability in acquisition, analysis, region-of-interest (ROI) definition, metal-artifact handling, and reporting limits reproducibility and hinders clinical and research implementation. This multisociety consensus aimed to develop a standardized methodological framework for ppDXA acquisition, analysis, reporting, and longitudinal interpretation in hip and knee arthroplasty. **METHODS:** A multinational collaboration of scientific societies from Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, and Spain convened a 21-member multidisciplinary panel with expertise in orthopedic surgery, clinical densitometry, medical physics, bone biology, epidemiology, biostatistics, and consensus methodology. A targeted evidence review and narrative synthesis informed candidate statements on the ppDXA workflow. Statements were rated, discussed, and refined using the modified RAND/UCLA Appropriateness Method. Recommendations with high agreement were organized into six conceptual domains: quality assurance and precision; patient preparation and positioning; scan acquisition; ROI definition and analysis; longitudinal assessment; and reporting and clinical contextualization. **RESULTS:** The consensus establishes minimum and optimal standards for site-level quality assurance, implant documentation, joint-specific positioning, scan parameters, metal-removal settings, implant-anchored ROI schemes, ROI-specific precision assessment, least significant change (LSC)-based longitudinal interpretation, structured reporting, and clinical contextualization. A Minimum Dataset for Reporting and practical ROI templates are provided. An exploratory therapy-aware Green-Amber-Red interpretive framework is proposed to support structured contextual interpretation and prospective evaluation of periprosthetic BMD trajectories, in conjunction with technical validity, clinical findings, imaging, systemic bone health, and treatment context. **CONCLUSIONS:** These multisociety RAND/UCLA consensus recommendations provide a standardized framework to reduce avoidable technical variability, improve reproducibility, and support auditable use of ppDXA in research and selected clinical workflows. It does not advocate universal routine ppDXA screening for all patients undergoing hip or knee arthroplasty and is intended to standardize measurement and reporting rather than to establish outcome-validated diagnostic, prognostic, or therapeutic periprosthetic BMD thresholds.
