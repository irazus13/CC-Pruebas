## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Needle insertion accuracy in robot-assisted percutaneous puncture is critical for clinical outcomes, especially when traversing multi-layered tissues like muscle and organs. **METHODS:** This study proposes a novel ultrasound-guided model predictive control method for needle steering. It incorporates a mechanics-based model that accounts for muscle anisotropy and tissue heterogeneity, integrated with ultrasound-based trajectory reconstruction to predict needle deflection. The control strategy is designed to minimise insertion error, the number of rotations, and rotation depth. **RESULTS:** Experiments in muscle-liver tissue show the controller achieves sub-millimetre accuracy ( &#x2264; 1 mm error) for given tasks while minimising tissue trauma. **CONCLUSIONS:** The proposed method significantly enhances the insertion accuracy and reduces tissue damage, as indicated by the reduced number and depth of needle rotations, for robot-assisted percutaneous in anisotropic, heterogeneous tissue. These results provide a validated foundation for future clinical translation of the proposed control framework.
