## Resumen

**INTRODUCTION:** Burnout is a major concern across medical professions, with radiologists and radiation oncologists among the most affected. Artificial intelligence (AI) has the potential to reduce burnout by decreasing workload; however, previous research has shown inconsistent associations between AI use and burnout. These relationships may depend on clinicians' motivation for using AI. **METHOD:** A 6.5&#x2009;min anonymous online survey was distributed to members of the Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Radiologists (RANZCR) over a 2&#x2009;month period (May-July 2025). Measures included demographics and work-related variables (hours, level of training), burnout, AI use (frequency and task type), and motivation to use AI (based on Self-Determination Theory). Motivation was examined as a moderator of the relationship between AI use and burnout. **RESULTS:** Responses were received from 121 clinicians (90 radiologists, 31 radiation oncologists), including 21 trainees. Clinically significant burnout was reported by 42.1% (51/121) of respondents. Ninety-one (75.2%) respondents reported using AI, primarily for image analysis and interpretation (radiologists) and treatment planning (radiation oncologists). There was no overall significant relationship between AI use and burnout. However, AI use was positively associated with burnout among those reporting lower autonomy in AI use. This relationship was not mediated by need satisfaction or frustration. **CONCLUSION:** AI use is common among RANZCR members, and the burnout rate is prevalent. These preliminary findings highlight the potential importance of supporting autonomous motivation when implementing AI, while recognising that larger and more context-specific studies are required to confirm this relationship.
