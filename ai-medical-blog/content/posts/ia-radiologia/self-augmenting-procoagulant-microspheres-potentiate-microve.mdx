## Resumen

Transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) persists a cornerstone therapy for hypervascular tumors such as hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), yet clinical limitations remain due to inefficient occlusion of tumor microvessels and hypoxia-induced drug resistance. Here, self-augmenting procoagulant microspheres (SPM) were developed for sustained co-delivery of thrombin (Th) and hypoxia-activated prodrug AQ4N (Th/AQ@SPM) to potentiate TACE therapy in HCC. SPM induced primary embolization by obstructing major tumor vasculature, affording vascular disruption and procoagulant factor exposure to establish a localized hypercoagulable microenvironment. Th-triggered secondary thrombosis through platelet aggregation and fibrinogen-to-fibrin conversion within residual vasculature extended occlusion to inter-microsphere spaces and distal microvessels beyond direct microsphere reach. Concurrently, hypoxia-potentiated activation of AQ4N to its cytotoxic form enabled targeted chemotherapy within the ischemic tumors. In rabbit renal and auricular artery models, Th@SPM demonstrated significant microvessel embolization through dual-phase vascular occlusion. Orthotopic VX2 liver tumor models revealed that TACE treatment with Th/AQ@SPM achieved superior tumor suppression without adverse toxic effects. Thus, Th/AQ@SPM establishes a self-augmenting embolic cascade that couples progressive microvascular occlusion with hypoxia-activated chemotherapy, providing a potent strategy to overcome key limitations of conventional TACE for HCC.
