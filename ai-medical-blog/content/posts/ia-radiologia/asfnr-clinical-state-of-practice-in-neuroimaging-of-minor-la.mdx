## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Minor ischemic stroke (MIS) with anterior large vessel occlusion (LVO) is a distinct stroke subtype in which mild neurological deficits may mask a substantial volume of threatened but salvageable brain tissue. Despite a baseline NIHSS&#x2264;5, nearly one-third of patients experience poor functional outcomes, often due to early neurological deterioration (END). **CURRENT EVIDENCE AND ONGOING TRIALS:** Patients with LVO-MIS were markedly underrepresented in landmark thrombectomy trials, resulting in limited evidence to guide treatment. Retrospective studies have identified imaging biomarkers of END, including proximal occlusion, clot length, collateral status, and perfusion mismatch, but the benefit of endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) remains uncertain. Ongoing randomized controlled trials, including STEP and MOSTE, are expected to define the role of EVT and imaging-based patient selection in this population. **PURPOSE:** To summarize the current state of neuroimaging in anterior circulation LVO-MIS and provide a practical imaging workflow and reporting checklist for diagnosis, prognostication, and treatment selection. **KEY POINTS:** CTA remains the primary modality for LVO detection and collateral assessment, while CT and MR perfusion identify salvageable tissue and predict END beyond baseline NIHSS. MRI further refines infarct core, perfusion deficit, and thrombus characterization. Current AI-based LVO detection tools improve stroke triage but require careful human oversight because proximal M2 and non-terminus ICA occlusions may be missed. **CONCLUSION:** Pending results from ongoing trials, tissue-based imaging with CTA, CT perfusion, and MR perfusion can complement clinical assessment to improve risk stratification and individualized treatment decisions. Advances in quantitative imaging and artificial intelligence are expected to further enable precision imaging-guided management of LVO-MIS.
