## Resumen

Claudin 18.2 (CLDN18.2) is a promising therapeutic target for gastric, gastroesophageal junction (GEJ), and pancreatic cancers, with CLDN18.2-targeting agents yielding encouraging clinical outcomes; however, intrinsic and acquired drug resistance is still a key obstacle limiting long-term clinical benefits and disease control. The intricate molecular mechanisms underlying resistance have not yet been fully elucidated, and clinically feasible strategies to reverse it are urgently needed. This review establishes a five-tiered resistance framework (dysregulated expression and aberrant subcellular localization of CLDN18.2, metabolic reprogramming, intracellular bypass signaling compensation, tumor microenvironment [TME] remodeling, and immune evasion) to summarize previously fragmented resistance mechanisms. It also elucidates newly discovered core resistance mechanisms: O-GlcNAc glycosylation-induced cytoplasmic retention of CLDN18.2, trogocytosis-mediated transfer of cell membrane CLDN18.2 (mem-CLDN18.2) from tumor cells to cluster of differentiation (CD)8+ T cells, and spatial intratumoral heterogeneity-related target loss. Furthermore, it compares distinct resistance profiles across diverse therapeutic modalities and summarizes validated and emerging predictive biomarkers, artificial intelligence (AI)-based risk assessment models, and clinical advances involving the use of novel agents and rational combination regimens to overcome resistance. It also comprehensively synthesizes cutting-edge findings on CLDN18.2-targeting drug resistance, forming an integrated system covering mechanisms, biomarkers, and translational strategies. This review could offer a theoretical reference for overcoming clinical drug resistance, improving the precision treatment of digestive system malignancies, and promoting the research and development of next-generation CLDN18.2-targeting drugs.
