## Resumen

ObjectiveAccurate, automated classification of brain tumor subtype from MRI remains constrained by a trade-off between diagnostic accuracy and computational efficiency, and by the fact that attention mechanisms borrowed from natural-image tasks are not tailored to the intensity and texture properties of medical MRI. This study addresses the gap by developing and evaluating a Tumor-Aware Feature Refinement (TAFR) module.MethodsThis retrospective, algorithm development-and-validation study used a publicly available, de-identified secondary MRI dataset of 7,023 T1-weight contrast-enhanced images (5,712 training/1,311 test) spanning the four classes above.ResultsThe proposed model achieved 99.77% test accuracy, 99.75% precision, 99.75% recall, and 99.75% F1-score, with perfect classification (100%) of glioma and no-tumor cases and only 3 misclassifications across 1,311 test images. This exceeded all six baselines, including the strongest prior result (YOLOv7-based, 99.50% accuracy), while using 7.2M parameters versus 138M for VGG16 (a 19-fold reduction) and an inference time of approximately 15ms per image.ConclusionDomain-specific attention tailored to MRI texture and intensity characteristics can match or exceed general-purpose architectures at a fraction of the parameter cost. TAFR-based screening may help reduce reporting delays and unnecessary follow-up investigation, subject to external validation on independent, multi-institutional cohorts before clinical deployment.
