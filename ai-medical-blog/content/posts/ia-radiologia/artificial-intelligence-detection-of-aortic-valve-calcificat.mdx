## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Routine noncontrast chest computed tomography (CT) may reveal aortic valve calcification (AVC) outside aortic stenosis evaluation. Its prevalence, burden, and documentation remain uncertain. Artificial intelligence (AI) may enable systematic detection. **OBJECTIVES:** The study aims to evaluate AVC prevalence and burden, clinical correlates, radiology documentation, and AI-assisted case finding. **METHODS:** This retrospective single-center study screened 511 consecutive noncontrast, non-electrocardiographically gated chest CT examinations in adults aged &#x2265;50 years. After 15 protocol exclusions and 56 software-designated nonevaluable examinations, 440 underwent quantification by 2 blinded readers. AVC was defined as &gt;10 AU using a 130-Hounsfield-unit threshold adjusted for blood-pool attenuation and image noise when needed. Locked HealthAVC V1.0 was a secondary comparator. **RESULTS:** Manual quantification identified AVC in 172 of 440 scans (39.1%; 95% CI: 34.6%-43.7%); 97 of 440 (22.0%) had &#x2265;100 AU. Inter-reader agreement at &gt;10 AU was 99.5% (kappa = 0.990). Age (adjusted OR: 1.92 per 5 years; 95% CI: 1.63-2.26) and male sex (adjusted OR: 2.09; 95% CI: 1.32-3.30) were associated with AVC. Reports documented 27 of 172 positive scans (15.7%; 95% CI: 11.0%-21.9%). AI detected 169 of 172 manually confirmed cases (98.3%) among 440 evaluable scans, including 142 of 145 undocumented cases. Of 496 submitted scans, 56 (11.3%) were nonevaluable. End-to-end sensitivity counting positive rejected scans as missed was 89.9%. Among 86 AI-positive patients with echocardiography, 10 (11.6%) had mild-or-greater aortic stenosis. **CONCLUSIONS:** AVC was common but infrequently documented on routine chest CT. AI detected most cases among evaluable scans; 11.3% were nonevaluable, yielding 89.9% end-to-end sensitivity. Prospective studies should assess clinical pathways.
