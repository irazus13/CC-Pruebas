## Resumen

The basal ganglia (BG) are conserved brain regions essential for motor control, learning, emotion, and cognition and are implicated in neurological and psychiatric disease, yet a unified cross-species taxonomy of BG cell types is lacking. We present a multiomic consensus atlas of 1.8 million nuclei from humans, macaques, and marmosets spanning eight BG structures, integrating gene expression, open chromatin, and spatial profiling. Alignment across species and to mouse and human whole-brain atlases identifies 61 homologous cell types conserved over 76 million years. Dorsal-ventral spatial identity is more conserved than D1/D2 receptor or striosome/matrix identity, and striatum (STR) D2 SMYD2-HTR7 spiny projection neurons have distinct molecular, electrophysiological, and morphological features. Cis-regulatory analysis reveals conserved sequence grammars that encode cell identity and inform viral targeting, and cell-type-resolved mapping localizes psychiatric and neurodegenerative risk to specific BG cell populations. This work provides a foundational resource for BG evolution, function, and disease.
