## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** PSMA-targeted PET/CT (PSMA-PET/CT) is increasingly used for prostate cancer staging, selection of patients for PSMA-directed radioligand therapy, and longitudinal assessment of treatment response. Multiple quantitative frameworks, including the Prostate Cancer Molecular Imaging Standardized Evaluation (PROMISE) criteria, have been proposed to standardize interpretation, yet limited studies have established the test-retest repeatability and reliability of quantitative PSMA-PET measures, which is a prerequisite before any parameter can be used to define treatment eligibility or to distinguish true response from progression. We evaluated the repeatability of deep learning-enabled lesion-level SUV-based and volume-dominated features, together with the PROMISE-criteria-based lesion index (LI), to provide repeatability benchmarks for future studies of response assessment and treatment selection. **METHODS:** Twenty-two men with metastatic prostate cancer underwent two 18F-DCFPyL PSMA-PET/CT scans 1 to 7 days apart with no intervening therapy. Lesions were detected, segmented, localized to PROMISE anatomic regions, and quantified using the FDA-cleared and deep learning-enabled aPROMISE platform; false-positive detections were manually removed, and lesions were matched one-to-one between scans. After excluding two patients without matched lesions, 20 patients with 292 matched lesion pairs were analyzed. SUVmax, SUVmean, SUVpeak, SUVtotal, lesion volume, and LI were assessed. Repeatability was quantified using the within-subject coefficient of variation (wCOV), intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC(2,1)), and log-scale Bland-Altman 95% limits of agreement (LOA), with volume-threshold, lesion-site, and scan-interval sensitivity analyses. **RESULTS:** Among the SUV-based features, SUVmean was the most repeatable (wCOV&#x2009;=&#x2009;7.04%, ICC(2,1)&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.984, 95% LOA&#x2009;=&#x2009;-&#x2009;17.1% to 22.7%), while the volume-dominated features SUVtotal and lesion volume showed greater variability (wCOV&#x2009;=&#x2009;24.92% and 24.53%, respectively). LI demonstrated the highest repeatability of all evaluated measures (wCOV&#x2009;=&#x2009;3.88%, ICC(2,1)&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.986, 95% LOA&#x2009;=&#x2009;-&#x2009;9.4% to 12.2%). Volume thresholding generally narrowed the limits of agreement and reduced variability, and the relative ordering of feature repeatability was largely preserved across scan intervals. **CONCLUSIONS:** SUVmean was the most repeatable SUV-based feature, supporting its further prospective evaluation for lesion-level response and progression assessment, whereas PROMISE criteria-based LI demonstrated the highest repeatability overall, supporting further evaluation as a standardized measure of PSMA expression and potential quantitative imaging biomarker, including for treatment-eligibility decisions. By establishing the test-retest repeatability of these measures, this study provides repeatability benchmarks for quantitative 18F-DCFPyL PSMA-PET/CT features and supports their further evaluation as imaging biomarkers in prostate cancer.
