## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Accurate differentiation between benign and malignant spinal tumors is critical for treatment planning, yet manual interpretation of T2-weighted fat-suppressed MRI remains subjective, with substantial inter-observer variability. This study aimed to develop a deep learning model, the multi-scale deep residual network (MSDR-Net), for automated benign-malignant classification. **METHODS:** A total of 500 T2-weighted fat-suppressed MR images of pathologically confirmed spinal tumors (389 benign, 111 malignant) were retrospectively collected from two institutions. Data were partitioned into training (70%), validation (20%), and independent test (10%) sets, and five-fold cross-validation was performed. Leave-one-center-out external validation was also performed (development: Institution A, n = 300; evaluation: Institution B, n = 200). The proposed MSDR-Net incorporates multi-branch residual blocks that combine parallel multi-scale convolutional paths with efficient channel attention modules and was benchmarked against six established architectures. **RESULTS:** On the independent test set (N&#xa0;=&#xa0;50; 12 malignant cases), MSDR-Net achieved 92.0% accuracy, 91.7% sensitivity (95% CI 61.5%-99.8%), 92.1% specificity, and an AUC of 0.921, the highest point estimates among evaluated architectures. The model was well calibrated (Brier score 0.072), with net benefit over treat-all strategies across thresholds of 0.10-0.60. Derived from a small test cohort, these estimates carry substantial uncertainty and await confirmation in larger, prospective cohorts. External-cohort accuracy was 86.5% (AUC 0.875). Ablation experiments showed that removing either component degraded performance, with a modest super-additive interaction; given their substantially smaller capacity, the single-path variants' deficit cannot be attributed solely to removing multi-scale processing. The model has 55.4M parameters and 12.3-ms inference per image. **CONCLUSION:** MSDR-Net effectively captures multi-scale discriminative features of spinal tumors on T2-weighted fat-suppressed MRI, achieving high classification accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency. The model shows promise for clinical deployment as an auxiliary diagnostic tool for orthopedic surgeons and radiologists.
