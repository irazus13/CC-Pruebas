## Resumen

Wernicke's encephalopathy is an acute neurological emergency caused by thiamine deficiency and is an underrecognized complication of hyperemesis gravidarum. Prolonged vomiting during pregnancy can lead to severe nutritional deficiencies, increasing the risk of developing this potentially life-threatening condition. We report the case of a 35-year-old woman who experienced severe hyperemesis gravidarum, resulting in three hospital admissions and approximately 19 kg of weight loss. At 15 weeks' gestation, following the diagnosis of a missed miscarriage, she developed blurred vision, ptosis, dizziness, and an unsteady gait. Examination revealed bilateral horizontal nystagmus and impaired right eye abduction, without confusion. Based on these clinical findings and characteristic MRI abnormalities involving the medial thalami and mammillary bodies, a diagnosis of Wernicke's encephalopathy was made, with some biochemical features suggestive of refeeding syndrome. Treatment with intravenous Pabrinex, followed by oral thiamine and dietary support, resulted in complete neurological recovery and resolution of the imaging abnormalities. This case highlights the importance of early recognition and prompt parenteral thiamine replacement in patients with prolonged vomiting and neurological symptoms, even without the complete classical triad.
