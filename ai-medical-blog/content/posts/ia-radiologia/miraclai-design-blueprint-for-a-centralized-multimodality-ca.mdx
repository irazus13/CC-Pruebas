## Resumen

Centralized imaging core laboratories have become an indispensable pillar of contemporary cardiovascular imaging research in artificial intelligence (AI), underpinning the scientific integrity of large multicentre trials and international registries. Their primary missions are to eliminate inter-centre variability, enforce standardized endpoint assessment and ensure the reproducibility and interpretability of imaging-derived measurements. Over the past two decades, these platforms have evolved from modality-specific units into integrated, multimodal infrastructures capable of harmonizing heterogeneous data streams across cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, computed tomography, echocardiography, coronary angiography and intracoronary imaging, electrocardiography and nuclear imaging. In parallel, the transformative expansion of AI in cardiovascular imaging has fundamentally reshaped the strategic mandate of centralized imaging core laboratories, positioning them as the indispensable custodians of high-quality, structured and auditable imaging datasets for the development and validation of next-generation AI tools. This state-of-the-art review provides a comprehensive framework for the design and operation of a centralized multimodal cardiovascular imaging core laboratory, structured around five critical domains: (1) data flow management, from acquisition harmonization to pseudonymized secure transfer and expert-labelled analysis; (2) reader organization and standardized image analysis workflows; (3) multi-level quality control, with an emphasis on inter- and intra-observer reproducibility; (4) governance, regulatory compliance and contractual frameworks with academic and industrial partners and (5) methodological recommendations for the development and validation of AI tools in cardiovascular imaging, with a particular emphasis on the role of centralized core laboratories as the essential infrastructure for this process. We further address the human resources and information technology requirements needed to operate such a platform, the sample sizes required to develop and validate AI tools and the specific risks that AI introduces into core laboratory practice. We further present the MIRACL.ai laboratory as an illustrative design blueprint of an academic multimodal cardiovascular imaging core laboratory, demonstrating how a unified research platform can simultaneously support conventional imaging endpoint adjudication and the development of robust, generalizable AI applications.
