## Resumen

Synthetic data generation has become an increasingly important component of methodological development in magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS), particularly in contexts where ground-truth in&#xa0;vivo measurements are limited, noisy, or inaccessible. This review provides a structured overview of current practices in synthetic MRS data generation, with emphasis on the physical, biochemical, and computational assumptions that underpin commonly used models. A decomposition of synthetic MRS data is presented in terms of core building blocks, including basis sets, signal formation models, and metabolite parameter distributions such as concentration and relaxation properties. This framework is extended to advanced components required for realistic data synthesis, including baseline and nuisance signals, spatial encoding effects such as coil sensitivities and field inhomogeneities, k-space sampling and gradient imperfections, as well as sample-specific and temporal dynamics. Beyond single-voxel modeling, synthetic data generation across multiple MRS modalities is discussed, including functional MRS, diffusion-weighted MRS, and spectroscopic imaging, with emphasis on modality-specific challenges such as physiological variability, motion, and confounding signal contributions. The role of synthetic data in clinical and preclinical research, acquisition optimization, software validation, and emerging artificial intelligence applications is further examined, with particular attention to domain shift and representational realism. Current practices in validation, reporting standards, and data accessibility are also reviewed, and key gaps limiting reproducibility and interoperability across studies are identified. Overall, this work provides a unified conceptual framework for synthetic MRS data generation, clarifies underlying assumptions across modeling choices, and outlines open challenges toward improving realism, standardization, and utility in both methodological and translational applications. Conducted by the MRS Synthetic Data Working Group under the Code &amp; Data Sharing Committee of the MRS Study Group of the International Society for Magnetic Resonance in Medicine (ISMRM), this review encompasses the existing literature, supplemented by the collective experience and in-house methodologies of its contributors, providing a community perspective that extends beyond the published record.
