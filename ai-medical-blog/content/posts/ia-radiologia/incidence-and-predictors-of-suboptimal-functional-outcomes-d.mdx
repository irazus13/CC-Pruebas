## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Successful vessel reperfusion with persistent clinical deficit occurs in 40-60% of adults undergoing mechanical thrombectomy but has never been characterised in childhood stroke. We aimed to determine the incidence and predictors of so-called futile recanalisation, in which the occluded artery is reopened but the patient does not recover good function, in paediatric mechanical thrombectomy for acute ischaemic stroke. **METHODS:** We retrospectively analysed prospectively collected data from the Save ChildS Pro registry, an international, multicentre, observational registry that included children aged 28 days to 18 years presenting with acute ischaemic stroke across 45 centres in 12 countries between Jan 1, 2020, and Aug 31, 2023. Patients who underwent endovascular thrombectomy and had successful recanalisation (expanded Treatment in Cerebral Infarction [eTICI] grade &#x2265;2b) were included. The primary outcome was suboptimal functional outcome despite successful recanalisation, defined as a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score greater than 2 at 90 days, on a scale from 0 (no symptoms) to 6 (death) on which 0-2 denotes functional independence; three additional thresholds (mRS score &gt;1, Pediatric Stroke Outcome Measure [PSOM] &gt;1&#xb7;0, and PSOM &gt;0&#xb7;5) were analysed as sensitivity analyses. Adjusted associations between candidate predictors (age, baseline Pediatric National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale [PedNIHSS] score, baseline Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score [ASPECTS], and final eTICI grade) and each outcome were estimated using Firth-penalised multivariable logistic regression. Save ChildS Pro is registered with the German Clinical Trials Registry (DRKS00018960). **FINDINGS:** 101 patients (54 [53%] males and 47 [47%] females; median age 11&#xb7;0 years [IQR 6&#xb7;0-15&#xb7;0]) had successful recanalisation with 90-day follow-up and comprised the analytical cohort. A suboptimal outcome (mRS score &gt;2) occurred in 18 (18%) of 101 patients. On Firth-adjusted multivariable analysis, lower final eTICI grade was significantly associated with suboptimal outcome (adjusted OR 0&#xb7;44 [95% CI 0&#xb7;23-0&#xb7;84], p=0&#xb7;012), and younger age was associated in the same direction (0&#xb7;87 [0&#xb7;78-0&#xb7;97] per year, p=0&#xb7;0098); baseline PedNIHSS score and baseline ASPECTS were not independently associated with a suboptimal outcome. Findings were directionally consistent across the three sensitivity thresholds. **INTERPRETATION:** In this analysis, 18% of children with successful recanalisation had a suboptimal functional outcome at 90 days, markedly lower than the proportion reported in adults (40-60%). Lower recanalisation quality and younger age were independently associated with suboptimal outcome, whereas baseline PedNIHSS score and ASPECTS had limited prognostic value. These findings highlight the need for longer-term studies and challenge the direct translation of adult prognostic frameworks to paediatric stroke, supporting age-specific approaches. Given the limited event count, these findings should be considered exploratory and require external validation. **FUNDING:** None.
