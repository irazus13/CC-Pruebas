## Resumen

Sixty years after its initial description, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) remains defined by bilateral radiographic opacities and impaired oxygenation rather than by its underlying biology. This syndromic definition encompasses considerable clinical and biological heterogeneity, which may contribute to the ineffectiveness of pharmacotherapy trials to date. Subphenotyping aims to address this heterogeneity by identifying more homogeneous patient groups that differ in prognosis and/or treatment response. In this narrative review, framed by the research questions outlined in the 2023 European Society of Intensive Care Medicine guidelines, we synthesize current approaches to ARDS subphenotyping across complementary domains: clinical (etiology, rapidly improving ARDS), physiological (gas exchange, recruitability, elastance, and ventilatory ratio), morphological (focal versus non-focal radiological patterns), and biological (individual injury biomarkers and the hyperinflammatory and hypoinflammatory subphenotypes). We examine their pathobiological basis, temporal dynamics, and retrospective evidence for heterogeneity of treatment effect, and consider how parsimonious biomarker panels, machine-learning classifiers, and adaptive basket trial designs may support clinical implementation. Prospective validation across diverse populations, including resource-limited settings, remains a key priority.
