## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Hepatic proton density fat fraction (PDFF) is often reported as a whole-liver mean, without a population reference for individual fat distribution. **PURPOSE:** To construct longitudinal, sex-specific three-dimensional PDFF atlases and assess regional fat loss, sex differences in heterogeneity, and metabolic associations. **STUDY TYPE:** Prospective; secondary analysis of a randomized lifestyle intervention trial. **POPULATION:** A total of 127 adults with obesity imaged at baseline ( V 1 , 73 female [F], 54 male [M]), after an 8-week low-caloric diet ( V 2 , 49F/32M), and after 12-month weight maintenance ( V 3 , 40F/32M). **FIELD STRENGTH/SEQUENCE:** 3T; six-echo chemical shift-encoded gradient-echo. **ASSESSMENT:** Sex- and visit-specific atlases were built by deformable registration; z-maps quantified voxel-wise deviation from the matched atlas. Six heterogeneity features (standard deviation [&#x3c3;x], coefficient of variation [CV], entropy [ H ]; z- skewness, z-kurtosis, volume fraction z&#x2009;&gt;&#x2009;2) were compared between sexes and correlated, cross-sectionally and longitudinally, with hepatocellular, glycemic, adiposity markers and triglycerides, and longitudinally with steatosis remission (PDFF &lt;&#x2009;5%). **STATISTICAL TESTS:** Welch t-test; Spearman correlation (&#x3c1;); Benjamini-Hochberg correction within each test set. Significance at adjusted p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.05. **RESULTS:** Liver PDFF decreased significantly from V 1 to V 2 in males (13.6%&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;7.0% to 6.6%&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;2.5%) and females (9.1%&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;6.2% to 5.9%&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;3.3%) with regionally non-uniform, sex-specific patterns. At V 1 , &#x3c3;x and H were significantly higher in males and CV, z-skewness and z-kurtosis in females; at V 2 , &#x3c3;x (males) and z-skewness and z-kurtosis (females) remained significant; no feature differed at V 3 (adjusted p&#x2009;=&#x2009;[0.583-0.953]). Cross-sectionally, features correlated with hepatocellular, glycemic and adiposity markers, more extensively in females. Longitudinally, significant correlations were almost limited to adiposity (V1 to V3: CV&#x2009;&#xd7;&#x2009;visceral fat volume in females, &#x3c1;&#x2009;=&#x2009;-0.65; CV&#x2009;&#xd7;&#x2009;visceral PDFF in males, &#x3c1;&#x2009;=&#x2009;-0.67); apart from H &#xd7; gamma-glutamyl transferase in females, no feature tracked hepatocellular, glycemic or steatosis remission outcomes (adjusted p&#x2009;=&#x2009;[0.051-0.999], [0.092-0.999], and [0.073-0.887]). **DATA CONCLUSION:** Hepatic fat loss showed spatially heterogeneous and sex-specific patterns. Heterogeneity reflected metabolic state cross-sectionally but mainly adiposity change longitudinally. **EVIDENCE LEVEL:** 2. **TECHNICAL EFFICACY:** Stage 1.
