## Resumen

Effective stroke prevention relies on identifying at-risk individuals before neurological symptoms arise, yet this is severely limited by the absence of scalable screening tools. To address this, we developed RetCEVD, a diagnostic framework that uses rapid, non-invasive retinal Optical Coherence Tomography Angiography (OCTA) for auxiliary assessment of ICVD. At its core, RetCEVD introduces a memory-guided mutual information optimization strategy that maximizes shared disease-relevant information within the same diagnostic category while suppressing redundant or confounding dependencies across different categories. This dual information constraint encourages compact, discriminative representations of subtle retinal microvascular abnormalities. Trained and validated on a multi-center cohort of 24,438 retinal images, RetCEVD addresses three primary tasks: auxiliary detection of existing ICVD, etiological subtyping (large artery atherosclerosis vs. small vessel disease), and current symptomatic-status classification (asymptomatic vs. symptomatic LAA). The model achieved AUCs of 0.95 internal and 0.94 external for ICVD detection, 0.83 internal and 0.81 external for etiological subtyping, and 0.75 internal and 0.76 external for current symptomatic-status classification. The two-year ALAA follow-up provided supplementary longitudinal validation for subsequent stroke events (AUC, 0.71). By comprehensively evaluating the model's clinical interpretability, including its learned pathological features and alignment with established clinical patterns, we provide novel insights into retina-based stroke evaluation. Ultimately, RetCEVD may serve as an efficient, low-cost screening and triage tool to identify individuals who may benefit from confirmatory neurovascular imaging and specialist assessment.
