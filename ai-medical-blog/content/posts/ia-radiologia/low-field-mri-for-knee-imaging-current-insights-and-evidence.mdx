## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Low-field magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) has regained interest for musculoskeletal imaging due to advances in magnet design, hardware optimization, and artificial intelligence (AI)-based reconstruction, offering improved accessibility, patient comfort, and sustainability. **PURPOSE:** This systematic review aimed to explore and summarize the current status of low-field MRI for knee investigations. **STUDY TYPE:** Systematic review on MEDLINE, Cinhal, EMBASE, Web of Science, Scopus, and Google Scholar databases. PROSPERO protocol (CRD42024544354). **POPULATION:** Approximately 3400 participants (8-89&#x2009;years) encompassing healthy volunteers, community-based cohorts, and patients with diverse knee pathologies were evaluated. Few studies incorporate phantom experiments. **FIELD STRENGTH/SEQUENCE:** This review covered a range of very-low-field (&lt;&#x2009;0.1&#x2009;T), conventional low-field (0.1-&lt;&#x2009;0.29&#x2009;T), and upper-range low-field (&gt;&#x2009;0.3&#x2009;T-0.55&#x2009;T) with standard, extremity, open, and weight-bearing/standing systems. SE/FSE and GRE sequences, 3D GRE quantitative protocols, and AI-based were used. **ASSESSMENT:** Two reviewers independently extracted data on knee protocols, technical parameters, diagnostic performance, and reported advantages and limitations. **STATISTICAL TESTS:** Study quality was assessed using appropriate JBI critical appraisal tools. A narrative synthesis and descriptive analysis of the findings from the included studies are provided due to the heterogeneity of the intervention and outcome. **RESULTS:** From 3726 records, 31 were included. Low-field MRI demonstrated high diagnostic performance for meniscal and ligamentous injuries but lower sensitivity for early cartilage degeneration. Weight-bearing MRI provided distinct functional information but not direct equivalence to standard MRI. AI-enhanced reconstruction was evaluated in a limited number of single-center, vendor-specific studies and was associated with improved image quality and diagnostic confidence. Important limitations included lower signal-to-noise ratio, reduced spatial resolution, protocol heterogeneity, and limited pathology-validated evidence. **DATA CONCLUSION:** Low-field MRI may provide a clinically useful complementary option for selected knee meniscal and ligamentous indications. However, heterogeneous evidence, influenced by field strength, scanner design, sequences, and reference standards, limits claims of equivalence with high-field MRI, particularly for subtle cartilage lesions. Advances in hardware and AI have improved image quality and efficiency; however, evidence is limited and larger standardized multicenter studies are required. **EVIDENCE LEVEL:** 5. **TECHNICAL EFFICACY:** Stage 5.
