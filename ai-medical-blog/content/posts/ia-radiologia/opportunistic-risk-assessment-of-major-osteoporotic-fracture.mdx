## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Bone mineral density (BMD) and the Fracture Risk Assessment Tool (FRAX) incompletely capture fracture risk in the osteopenic range, where much of the fracture burden occurs. Routine chest radiographs are acquired across a broad range of patients and may contain information relevant to fracture risk, offering a potential opportunistic complement without additional imaging burden. **METHODS:** We developed a convolutional neural network survival model in 53,109 adults at a tertiary hospital and externally validated it in 85,284 patients from two independent institutions. Eligibility required a chest radiograph obtained within 1 year of dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry scans, so all participants had been referred for densitometry. The primary outcome was incident major osteoporotic fracture. Discrimination was assessed by using the concordance index (C-index) and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) at 2, 3, 5, and 10 years, compared with FRAX with BMD and with the lowest BMD T-score. **RESULTS:** In the external validation cohorts (mean 58.6 years of age; 68.9% female; and follow-up of 77 months), the deep-learning (DL) model achieved a C-index of 0.843 (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.837 to 0.850) and a censoring-adjusted 10-year AUROC of 0.855 (95% CI, 0.846 to 0.863), which was higher than that of FRAX with BMD (C-index, 0.779, 95% CI, 0.770 to 0.787; 10-year AUROC, 0.785, 95% CI, 0.775 to 0.794; both P&lt;0.01). Adding FRAX to the DL model did not meaningfully improve discrimination (C-index, 0.847, 95% CI, 0.841 to 0.853). In the osteopenia subgroup (n=35,358), the DL model achieved a 10-year AUROC of 0.779 (95% CI, 0.764 to 0.795) versus 0.659 (95% CI, 0.641 to 0.676) for FRAX with BMD (P&lt;0.01). This advantage was consistent across fracture types and BMD categories. **CONCLUSIONS:** A survival DL model applied to routine chest radiographs identified patients at high fracture risk with discrimination exceeding that of FRAX, with the greatest benefit in patients with osteopenia. As all participants had been referred for densitometry, absolute-risk and case-finding estimates require prospective evaluation in an unselected routine-radiography population.
