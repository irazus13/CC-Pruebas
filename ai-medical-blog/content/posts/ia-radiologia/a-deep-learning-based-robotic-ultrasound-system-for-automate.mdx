## Resumen

Ultrasound (US) imaging is central to the assessment of musculoskeletal disorders, but robotic systems still rely on operators for initial joint localization and scan-region selection, limiting standardization. We present an AI-guided robotic US framework that combines two-step deep-learning alignment with optical refraction compensation for underwater joint scanning. The fully automated workflow performs underwater localization, per-joint US probe centering, and inter-joint path generation. Within 2 minutes, it captures cross-sectional and sagittal images of a whole hand, covering five metacarpophalangeal joints, the thumb interphalangeal joint, and four proximal interphalangeal joints. The alignment network estimates the optimal scan center with 1-2 mm accuracy despite initial errors up to 1 cm. In static-target validation, the optical model reduces refraction-induced localization error by 88%. Independent expert grading of 200 joint-orientation sweeps yielded a mean image-quality score of 3.33 &#xb1; 1.08/5, and 77.5% were rated good or better.
