## Resumen

**AIMS:** Vestibular schwannoma (VS) is a common benign intracranial tumor for which Gamma Knife radiosurgery (GKRS) is an important treatment option. However, post-treatment changes in tumor volume vary considerably and can complicate follow-up and response assessment. This study developed a framework that uses deep learning-based segmentation and MRI radiomics to predict long-term treatment response to GKRS for VS. **METHODS:** We developed an nnU-Net model to automate VS segmentation on contrast-enhanced T1-weighted MRI. Patients with long-term follow-up after GKRS were classified into four response patterns: direct regression, delayed regression, stable disease, and growth. A two-stage machine-learning framework was then constructed. Stage 1 distinguishes regression from non-regression, while Stage 2 differentiates direct from delayed regression. Pretreatment MRI radiomics features were combined with clinical and dose-related variables and used to train multiple machine-learning models. The models were evaluated using five-fold cross-validation, and SHAP analysis was used to interpret the results. **RESULTS:** The nnU-Net model achieved a mean Dice similarity coefficient of 91.16&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.47% on the external test dataset. Among the 155 patients, 53 showed direct regression, 83 delayed regression, 9 stable disease, and 10 tumor growths. In Stage 1, the Balanced Random Forest algorithm achieved accuracy of 74.19&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;7.21%, an AUROC of 72.43&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;10.18%, and AUPRC of 93.08&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;4.27%. In Stage 2, the Support Vector Machine achieved accuracy of 64.05&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;4.16%, an AUROC of 66.70&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;6.21%, and AUPRC of 57.10&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;7.30%. SHAP analysis identified radiomics and dose-related features as important predictors. **CONCLUSION:** Deep learning-based segmentation combined with pretreatment MRI radiomics, clinical variables, and dose-related parameters may provide useful information for predicting long-term response after GKRS for VS. This framework could support more individualized follow-up planning and help clinicians distinguish delayed regression from treatment failure.
