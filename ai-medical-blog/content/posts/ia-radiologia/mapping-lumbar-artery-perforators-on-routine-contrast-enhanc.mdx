## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** Imaging data on the lumbar artery perforators (LAPs) are limited and, for Vietnamese subjects, absent. We assessed whether contrast-enhanced abdominopelvic multidetector CT (MDCT) acquired for unrelated indications-with no extra imaging-can characterise LAP distribution and morphology and yield the first coordinate-based perforator map in this population. **METHODS:** We retrospectively analysed contrast-enhanced abdominopelvic MDCT of 108 subjects (216 hemi-backs) acquired for unrelated indications (256-detector scanner, 0.5&#xa0;mm reconstruction). On multiplanar, maximum-intensity-projection, and volume-rendered images, each perforator was assigned a source level (LA1-LA4), a diameter at deep-fascia penetration, and cutaneous emergence coordinates on an Oxy system. Within-subject non-independence was handled with the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) and cluster-robust regression. **RESULTS:** Nine hundred seventy-seven perforators were identified. Diameter increased from LA1 to LA4 (LA4-LA1 difference 0.83&#xa0;mm, 95% CI 0.72-0.95; overall mean 1.98&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.54&#xa0;mm), whereas number was non-monotonic-lowest at LA2 (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;121, below LA1 n&#x2009;=&#x2009;167) and rising sharply to LA3 (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;247) and LA4 (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;442). Diameter did not differ by side (p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.38) or sex (p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.90). The emergence point shifted toward the inter-iliac-crest line from LA1 to LA4 (p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.001), forming two paravertebral clusters densest in the lower lumbar region. Clustering was mild (ICC 0.056), leaving conclusions unchanged. **CONCLUSION:** Routine abdominopelvic MDCT can map lumbar artery perforators without additional imaging. LA3-LA4 are the dominant sources in number and calibre, comparable between sides and sexes; the resulting coordinate-based map-the first for a Vietnamese population-provides a reproducible search window for preoperative localisation, pending intraoperative validation.
