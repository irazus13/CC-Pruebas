## Resumen

**BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE:** &#xa0;Accurate acetabular coverage assessment is essential for diagnosing adult hip dysplasia. Computed tomography (CT) best evaluates 3-dimensional acetabular morphology but is typically acquired supine, while symptoms arise during weightbearing. This study evaluates how sagittal pelvic tilt affects CT-based acetabular parameters in adults with hip dysplasia and whether pelvic tilt causes clinically relevant measurement changes. **METHODS:** &#xa0;3D-models from supine CT of 29 adults with hip dysplasia (lateral center-edge angle (LCEA) &#x2264; 25&#xb0;) yielded 39 affected hips. LCEA, inclination (AI), anterior/posterior/horizontal acetabular sector angles (AASA, PASA, HASA), acetabular version (AV), extrusion index (EI), and total and loadbearing coverage were measured. Pelvic tilt was simulated in 5&#xb0; increments from 30&#xb0; anterior to 30&#xb0; posterior around the hip centers and parameters were remeasured in each position. Differences &lt; 2.5&#xb0; were considered clinically irrelevant. **RESULTS:** &#xa0;Median supine LCEA was 21&#xb0;, AI 50&#xb0;, AASA 43&#xb0;. Across -30&#xb0; to +30&#xb0; tilt, AASA/loadbearing coverage decreased, PASA/AV increased, LCEA/EI showed parabolic patterns, and HASA/total coverage remained stable. From supine to simulated upright, paired mean differences were: LCEA -1&#xb0; (95% confidence interval [CI] -2 to 0), AASA -4&#xb0; (CI -5 to -3), PASA +4&#xb0; (CI 4 to 5), HASA 0&#xb0; (CI -1 to 1), AV +4&#xb0; (CI 3 to 4), AI +1&#xb0; (CI -1 to 3), EI +2% (CI 0 to 4), total coverage 0% (CI 0 to 0), and loadbearing coverage -2% (CI -2 to -2). **CONCLUSION:** &#xa0;Functional sagittal pelvic tilt resulted in generally small changes in CT-based acetabular measurements in dysplastic hips, which may be clinically relevant in borderline cases, particularly for AASA, PASA, acetabular version, and EI, whereas LCEA and AI were not significantly affected.
