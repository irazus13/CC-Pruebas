## Resumen

Multiparametric MRI (mpMRI) improves detection of clinically significant prostate cancer (csPCa) but is limited by inter-reader variability. Artificial intelligence (AI) has been increasingly applied to prostate segmentation and lesion detection, with heterogeneous results. Concerns remain regarding generalizability, data set bias, and validation. This review summarizes recent evidence on AI-assisted prostate MRI interpretation, identifies knowledge gaps, and outlines directions for future research. A systematic literature search of PubMed and Google Scholar was performed for studies published between January 2023 and April 2025 evaluating AI-assisted interpretation of biparametric or multiparametric prostate MRI. Reference lists were manually reviewed. Eligible studies assessed diagnostic performance of AI alone or in combination with radiologists. Eighteen studies met inclusion criteria from 157 screened. Standalone AI systems achieved areas under the curve (AUCs) ranging from 0.77 to 0.91, with sensitivities of 65.4% to 96% and specificities of 77.6% to 91%, occasionally outperforming radiologists. Hybrid AI-radiologist models improved inter-reader agreement, reduced interpretation time by up to 50%, and enhanced detection accuracy for equivocal (PI-RADS 3) lesions. AI assistance particularly benefited junior and general radiologists, improving diagnostic performance, confidence, and reproducibility. In active surveillance cohorts, AI demonstrated greater accuracy than radiologists for detecting disease progression (AUC=0.86 vs. 0.69), although specificity limitations persisted. Overall, AI integration into prostate MRI workflows demonstrates substantial potential to improve detection efficiency and standardization. Broader clinical adoption will require large-scale, multicenter validation, harmonized imaging protocols, and rigorous outcome-based evaluation.
