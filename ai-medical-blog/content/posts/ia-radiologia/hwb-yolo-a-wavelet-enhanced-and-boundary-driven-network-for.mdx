## Resumen

Ultrasound is the most commonly used imaging modality for diagnosing thyroid nodules. Thyroid ultrasound images typically suffer from severe speckle noise, low lesion contrast, and high similarity to surrounding tissue textures, which significantly interfere with deep learning models' ability to extract lesion features. To address these limitations, we designed a thyroid nodule ultrasound detection model named HWB-YOLO. Initially, the Hybrid Anisotropic Difference Convolution (HADC) module is incorporated to tackle the orientation-dependent nature of medical structures, effectively boosting the model's directional sensitivity. Subsequently, to counteract high-frequency attenuation and noise interference during downsampling, the Wavelet-Enhanced Transition Block (WETB) module is utilized to simultaneously preserve edge details and suppress noise during feature reconstruction. Lastly, addressing the challenges of low foreground-background contrast and blurred boundaries in ultrasound images, the Boundary Contrast-Driven Feature Aggregation (BCDFA) module is developed to improve lesion discriminability through the differential modeling of foreground and background features. To validate the effectiveness of the proposed method, extensive comparative experiments were conducted on four thyroid ultrasound image datasets. Across five independent runs, HWB-YOLO achieved mean mAP50 values of 94.3% &#xb1; 0.1%, 97.3% &#xb1; 0.2%, and 97.9% &#xb1; 0.1% on the TN3K, DDTI, and TNSCUI datasets, respectively. On the private SZThyroid dataset, it achieved a nodule mAP50 of 81.2% &#xb1; 0.2%. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed model achieves competitive detection performance and low computational overhead.
