## Resumen

Digital pathology (DP) is evolving from an image-based diagnostic modality into a quantitative platform for computational precision oncology. This transition is particularly relevant in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), where limited tissue specimens must support histological classification, immunohistochemistry, broad molecular profiling and an expanding range of therapy-linked biomarkers. Artificial intelligence (AI) can assist conventional tasks such as tissue segmentation, cell detection, quality control and biomarker quantification, but it can also derive image-based signatures associated with molecular alterations, spatial immune organization and treatment response. This review focuses primarily on NSCLC and examines the continuum from foundational image-analysis tasks and AI-assisted PD-L1 scoring to histology-based molecular prediction, computational companion diagnostics, pathology foundation models and multimodal AI. We discuss recent lung-specific evidence, including computational triage of actionable alterations, the emerging TROP2 quantitative continuous scoring paradigm and selected protein biomarkers associated with antibody-drug conjugates. We also consider the integration of pathology with genomics, transcriptomics, liquid biopsy, radiology and longitudinal clinical data. Particular attention is given to the barriers separating algorithmic performance from clinical utility: uneven adoption of whole-slide imaging, pre-analytical and scanner variability, missing or asynchronous multimodal data, propagation of upstream measurement errors, laboratory information-system interoperability, prospective validation, regulation, reimbursement and equitable access. Emerging paradigms such as digital twins and agentic AI are discussed as research directions rather than established clinical tools. We argue that the principal value of AI in NSCLC will not be autonomous diagnosis, but the reliable conversion of fragmented data streams into transparent, auditable and clinically actionable patient representations.
