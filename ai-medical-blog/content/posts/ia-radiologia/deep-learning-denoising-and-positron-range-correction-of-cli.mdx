## Resumen

PET image quality degradation due to positron range effects in high-energy positron-emitting radionuclides such as Gallium-68 (68Ga) can be mitigated by applying positron range correction (PRC). Deep learning (DL) models have been shown to improve image quality by reducing image noise. This study aimed to evaluate a DL model for simultaneous denoising and 68Ga-specific PRC in clinical [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TOC PET scans. Three different U-Net-based DL models were trained to map reduced-count PET images reconstructed without PRC (25%, 33%, or 50% of acquired counts) to full-count PET images reconstructed with tissue-independent PRC (FC-PRC). The generated reduced-count-enhanced images (RCE-25%, RCE-33%, RCE-50%) were evaluated using the mean standardized uptake value (SUVmean) in liver and lung, noise levels in liver, and maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) and contrast-to-noise ratio (CNR) of soft tissue lesions. Three experienced nuclear medicine physicians assessed the overall image quality (OIQ), overall diagnostic confidence (ODC), and lesion detectability (sensitivity, specificity, accuracy). Compared to FC-PRC, RCE-33% and RCE-50% exhibited similar noise, whereas RCE-25% showed substantial residual noise. CNR was reduced for RCE-33% (-5.6%, p=0.001) and RCE-25% (-8.0%, p&lt;0.001), but was comparable for RCE-50%. All reduced-count-enhanced images demonstrated lower SUVmax (RCE-25%: -3.8%, p=0.02; RCE-33%: -7.0%, p&lt;0.001; RCE-50%: -5.2%, p&lt;0.001). RCE-33% and RCE-50% achieved non-inferior OIQ and ODC compared to FC-PRC, while RCE-25% demonstrated only non-inferior ODC. All reduced-count-enhanced images showed high sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy (&gt;90%). The proposed DL-based approach achieved comparable noise and image quality down to 50% of counts. Although it did not outperform the reconstruction-based PRC, its benefit lies in simultaneous denoising and PRC in a single step.
