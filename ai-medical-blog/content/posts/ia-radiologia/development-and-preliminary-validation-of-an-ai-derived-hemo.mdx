## Resumen

**INTRODUCTION:** The objective assessment of technical skill remains a significant challenge in surgical training and quality control. Intraoperative hemorrhage, a direct consequence of tissue handling, may serve as a quantifiable proxy for surgical proficiency. This study aims to develop and validate an artificial intelligence-derived metric based on intraoperative hemorrhage for objective skill assessment in laparoscopic cholecystectomy. **METHODS:** Frames were sampled at 1 fps (every 25th frame) from videos recorded at 25 fps, and the 70 curated cases were split at the video (case) level into training, validation, and test sets (8:1:1; 56/7/7 videos). An external test set included 30 cases from Zhujiang Hospital. Hemorrhagic regions were manually annotated on 84,680 frames from 56 training videos. A PoolNet-based model quantified hemorrhagic pixels per frame during critical view of safety. Model outputs were validated against surgeon assessments (Fleiss' &#x3ba; = 0.72) and operative metrics, with the modified Global Operative Assessment of Laparoscopic Skills (mGOALS) scale as the skill reference. Mean hemorrhagic pixels were correlated with skill scores, ratings, and maneuver frequencies. **RESULTS:** The hemorrhage identification model achieved dice scores of 0.819 &#xb1; 0.054 (internal) and 0.685 &#xb1; 0.031 (external). The proficiency metric negatively correlated with expert skill scores (Spearman's r = -0.93, P &lt; 0.001), and strongly positively with subjective bleeding assessments (&#x3c1; = 0.89, P &lt; 0.001) and hemostasis frequency (&#x3c1; = 0.94, P &lt; 0.001). It effectively stratified surgeons by skill, with high-skill operators having fewer hemorrhagic pixels (Kruskal-Wallis H = 21.0-31.1, P &lt; 0.001). **CONCLUSIONS:** The developed model provides an objective, automated hemorrhage metric that, in this preliminary validation, aligned with expert skill ratings and operative behavior during laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Pending difficulty-adjusted, multicentre validation, it may serve as a potential adjunct for postoperative, competency-based feedback rather than as a stand-alone measure of surgical skill.
