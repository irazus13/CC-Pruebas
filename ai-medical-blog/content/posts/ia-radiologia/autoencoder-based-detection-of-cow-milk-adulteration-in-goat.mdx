## Resumen

Cow milk adulteration remains a challenge in goat milk authentication because goat and cow milk are compositionally similar. This study developed a 1,8-ANS-assisted fluorescence spectroscopy approach combined with one-class anomaly detection for controlled cow milk adulteration in goat milk. Models were fitted only to pure goat milk spectra and evaluated using laboratory mixtures containing 5-50% cow milk. Overall metrics were calculated from held-out predictions pooled across adulteration levels and reported with 95% confidence intervals. The one-class autoencoder (OCAE) showed the best overall performance, with an accuracy of 0.906 and an F1-score of 0.917, and outperformed VAE, OC-SVM, and K-means in paired bootstrap comparisons for most metrics, particularly precision, specificity, and F1-score. Level-specific testing showed consistent performance across the evaluated range, including the lowest level of 5%. Reconstruction-error analysis highlighted model-sensitive regions at 416-424 and 592-600&#x202f;nm. These results support a feasible screening strategy under controlled-mixture conditions.
