## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Imaging-based AI technologies, particularly deep learning and radiomics, offer a novel approach to improving the accuracy of lymph node metastasis (LNM) diagnosis. **OBJECTIVE:** This study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of imaging-based AI models compared with radiologists in detecting preoperative LNM in esophageal cancer (EC). **METHODS:** A systematic literature search was conducted across 5 databases (PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, and IEEE Xplore) up to August 19, 2026. Eligible studies assessed imaging-based AI for preoperative LNM detection in EC (pathology reference standard). The protocol was prospectively registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO CRD420251169109). The PROBAST+AI (Prediction Model Risk of Bias Assessment Tool with AI) tool was used for quality assessment. Bivariate random-effects models were used to calculate pooled sensitivity, specificity, diagnostic odds ratio (DOR), and area under the curve (AUC); between-study heterogeneity was described using &#x3c4;&#xb2; and I&#xb2; (interpreted descriptively rather than categorically); homogeneity of study effects was tested with the Q statistic, and 95% prediction intervals (PIs) were derived for all pooled estimates. Head-to-head comparisons used a random-effects meta-analysis of within-study paired differences (restricted maximum likelihood with Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman adjustment). **RESULTS:** A total of 18 studies were included. Across the 17 internal validation sets (2485 participants), AI models demonstrated a pooled sensitivity of 0.77 (95% CI 0.73-0.81; 95% PI 0.62-0.87), specificity of 0.79 (95% CI 0.73-0.84; 95% PI 0.48-0.94), a DOR of 12.88 (95% CI 9.57-17.33; 95% PI 4.5-37.0), and an AUC of 0.83 (95% CI 0.81-0.86; 95% PI 0.80-0.86). In the 10 head-to-head studies, AI showed higher sensitivity than radiologists (a paired difference of +0.27, 95% CI 0.11-0.37; 95% PI -0.26 to 0.45; P=.006) and higher discrimination (DOR ratio 4.65, 95% CI 2.53-8.56; 95% PI 0.97-22.42; P&lt;.001), whereas specificity did not differ (+0.06, 95% CI -0.08 to 0.16; 95% PI -0.39 to 0.26; P=.30). Pooled arm-specific AUCs were 0.86 (95% CI 0.82-0.89; 95% PI 0.79-0.91) for AI and 0.67 (95% CI 0.59-0.75; 95% PI 0.44-0.84) for radiologists. In 4 external validation studies, pooled sensitivity was 0.66 (95% CI 0.46-0.81; 95% PI 0.31-0.90), specificity was 0.85 (95% CI 0.75-0.91; 95% PI 0.63-0.94), and the DOR was 10.42 (95% CI 3.50-31.01; 95% PI 0.6-142.0). These estimates remain subject to substantial heterogeneity and design-related bias. **CONCLUSIONS:** Imaging-based AI models demonstrated promising pooled diagnostic performance for preoperative LNM detection in EC. By pairing AI with radiologists' performance in the same cohorts, reporting PIs alongside CIs, and separating internal from external validation, this review provides a more realistic assessment than previous average-effect estimates. In predominantly retrospective and heterogeneous cohorts, AI had higher pooled sensitivity and discrimination than radiologists, whereas specificity did not differ; the selective extraction of the model with the highest AUC and very low certainty of evidence precludes conclusions about clinical superiority. These findings support AI as a decision-support tool, warranting prospective, multicenter external validation and prespecified human-AI comparisons rather than replacing radiologists.
