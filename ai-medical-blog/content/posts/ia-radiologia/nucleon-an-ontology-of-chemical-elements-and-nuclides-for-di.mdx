## Resumen

Radiopharmaceutical information in imaging systems is typically recorded as free text or as flat code system entries that include little or no computable information about radionuclides' physical properties. This article introduces the Nuclide and Element Ontology (NUCLEON), a machine-processable knowledge resource that represents the 118 known chemical elements and 5408 nuclides, including 2019 metastable isomers, with computable nuclear properties. The ontology declares each element and nuclide as both a class and an individual: the class structure generates a taxonomy that aligns with vocabularies of chemistry and clinical medicine, and the individual entities support quantitative assertions, such as half-life. Each of 7691 decay branches is an individual specifying its mode of decay and branching ratio; 7399 branches have an explicit link to their decay product (daughter) nuclide, derived from the decay mode rather than transcribed. Ground-state data were taken from the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) LiveChart of Nuclides, isomer data from the NUBASE2020 evaluation, and the energies and yields of 3990 radiation emissions of clinically coded radionuclides and their decay chains from International Commission on Radiological Protection (ICRP) Publication 107; symbols and names were verified against standard chemical nomenclature. NUCLEON is linked to the Ontology of Chemical Elements, SNOMED Clinical Terms, and DICOM radiopharmaceutical isotope and PET radionuclide code sequences, allowing a nuclide to be resolved directly from a DICOM header. The ontology incorporates 215,862 triples across 5533 classes and 17,253 named individuals. Automated checks confirmed the arithmetic consistency of atomic, neutron, and mass numbers, and none of the derived decay products disagreed with ICRP Publication 107 or the Evaluated Nuclear Structure Data File. Element names are provided in seven languages. NUCLEON is available at https://bioportal.bioontology.org/ontologies/NUCLEON .
