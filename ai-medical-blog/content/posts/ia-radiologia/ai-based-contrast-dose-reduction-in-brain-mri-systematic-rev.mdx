## Resumen

**OBJECTIVES:** To evaluate the performance of AI algorithms in reducing gadolinium (Gd) administration in brain MRI by generating synthetic post-contrast images from low-dose inputs. **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** Comprehensive searches of PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Embase were conducted through April 17, 2026. Study screening, eligibility assessment, quality evaluation, and data extraction were performed independently by two reviewers. Pooled peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM) values were calculated using a random-effects model. Heterogeneity was quantified using the I&#xb2; statistic. These analyses were additionally followed by evaluations of the reported qualitative assessments. **RESULTS:** Twelve studies met the inclusion criteria, most of which used convolutional neural network architectures. The pooled PSNR was 32.91&#x2009;dB (95% CI: 31.37-34.46; I&#xb2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;99.08%) across seven studies, and the pooled SSIM was 91.81% (95% CI: 89.33-94.30; I&#xb2;&#x2009;=&#x2009;99.69%) across six studies. Subgroup analyses stratified by contrast-dose reduction level, deep learning model type, and input modality did not identify consistent contributors to heterogeneity. Given substantial between-study heterogeneity, pooled estimates were interpreted descriptively. Across the qualitative assessments, synthetic images were generally rated as diagnostically acceptable or comparable to full-dose images for most tasks, although subtle differences in lesion conspicuity and artifact patterns were reported. **CONCLUSION:** AI models demonstrate promising technical performance for generating synthetic post-contrast brain MRI with high voxel-wise similarity to full-dose images at substantial contrast-dose reductions. However, marked heterogeneity, limited external validation, and the lack of clinically oriented diagnostic outcomes limit the interpretability and generalizability of current evidence. **KEY POINTS:** Question There is a need to determine how effectively artificial intelligence can reduce gadolinium use in brain MRI by generating synthetic post-contrast images from low-dose inputs. Findings Across 12 studies involving 3846 patients, AI-generated post-contrast brain MRI achieved pooled peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM) values of 32.91&#x2009;dB and 91.81%, respectively, with substantial between-study heterogeneity. Clinical relevance AI-assisted synthetic post-contrast imaging may substantially reduce gadolinium exposure in neuroradiology while preserving diagnostic confidence. However, high heterogeneity highlights the need for cautious interpretation and further multicenter standardization before clinical adoption.
