## Resumen

Corneal confocal microscopy (CCM) is a rapid, non-invasive ophthalmic imaging technique that enables detailed visualization and quantification of small corneal nerve fibres. Although initially developed for the assessment of diabetic neuropathy, its application has broadened substantially, with increasing evidence supporting its value in a range of central neurodegenerative diseases. This narrative review synthesizes current findings on the use of CCM in dementia, multiple sclerosis, stroke, burning mouth syndrome, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, trigeminal neuralgia, migraine and Parkinson's disease. Across these conditions, CCM consistently identifies measurable alterations in corneal nerve fibre density, length, branching and morphology, as well as changes in corneal immune cell populations. These structural abnormalities frequently correlate with disease severity, functional decline and clinical progression. In some disorders, CCM demonstrates diagnostic performance comparable to or exceeding existing biomarkers, and offers distinct advantages as a non-invasive, repeatable and objective measure that can detect early or subclinical neurodegeneration. Corneal confocal microscopy also shows potential as a prognostic and monitoring tool, particularly in conditions with limited reliable biomarkers. It may help stratify risk, track therapeutic response, and provide surrogate endpoints for clinical trials. Despite these strengths, several limitations remain, including modest sample sizes in some studies, variability in image interpretation, and difficulties differentiating between neurodegenerative conditions with overlapping patterns of nerve loss. Emerging automated analysis methods and artificial intelligence-based algorithms may help address these challenges by improving accuracy, reproducibility, and scalability. Overall, CCM represents a promising technique that bridges ophthalmology and neurology. A growing body of literature supports its utility across multiple neurodegenerative diseases, and further large-scale, longitudinal studies may help establish its role within routine clinical assessment and research.
