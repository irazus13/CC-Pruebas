## Resumen

This study conducts a controlled evaluation of four freely accessible Large Language Models-ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and DeepSeek-by testing their ability to answer questions from Italian Continuing Medical Education (CME) courses, with attention to accuracy, consistency across domains, and associated ethical concerns. A total of 47 CME courses were selected, and their multiple-choice questionnaires were submitted to the LLMs. A passing threshold of 75% was used to define successful course completion. Performance was assessed based on pass rates and the percentage of correct answers. DeepSeek scored the highest (87%), followed by ChatGPT (85%), Gemini (83%), and Perplexity (81%). LLMs performed more reliably on generalist courses than on highly specialized ones. Despite promising results, limitations include the absence of a deep statistical analysis, lack of a control group, and technical constraints with some models. Ethical concerns and risks such as hallucinations and misinformation require cautious use and human oversight. Responsible integration of LLMs into medical education requires clear standards and coordination among AI developers, educators, clinicians, legislators, and academic authorities.
