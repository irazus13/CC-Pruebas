## Resumen

Radiology has traditionally evaluated physician work using operational measures such as work relative value units, examination volume, and report turnaround time. These served as practical surrogates for physician contribution because operational workflow and diagnostic reasoning were historically performed together by the interpreting radiologist. Artificial intelligence (AI) is dissolving that coupling. AI increasingly performs routine operational tasks, yet radiologists must now evaluate AI-generated content, resolve discordance between AI output and their own interpretation, contextualize AI findings for patients and referring clinicians, and remain accountable for the result. By reducing operational effort while adding evaluative effort, AI may paradoxically increase rather than reduce higher-order cognitive work, and operational productivity and physician contribution diverge. This article introduces the Cognitive Value Framework, a conceptual theory of how radiologists-in academic and private practice alike-create Professional Value in AI-enabled practice. Its premise is that the primary professional product of radiology is clinically actionable diagnostic judgment rather than the report. Professional Value is generated through five interacting Domains of Professional Contribution: Operational, Cognitive, Clinical, Communication, and Accountability. Cognitive Friction is the central mechanism, distinguishing Productive Friction, which generates Professional Value, from Waste Friction, which does not. The Framework complements rather than replaces existing productivity metrics and offers a hypothesis-generating foundation for future investigation.
