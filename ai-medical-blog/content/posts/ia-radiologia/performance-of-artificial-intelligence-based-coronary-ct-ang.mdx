## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) provides accurate anatomical assessment of coronary stenosis severity but has limited ability to determine lesion-specific ischemia as assessed by invasive fractional flow reserve (FFR). We aimed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of an artificial intelligence-based deep learning CCTA-derived FFR model (CT-FFRAI) in cohorts undergoing CCTA and invasive FFR as part of a study protocol and a real-world cohort undergoing invasive FFR based on suspected coronary artery disease on CCTA in routine clinical care. **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** Diagnostic performance of CT-FFRAI (CorEx, SPIMED-AI) was evaluated using an intention-to-diagnose approach and compared with CCTA using 50% and 70% stenosis thresholds, with invasive FFR as reference standard. In the PACIFIC-1 study cohort, 208 patients underwent CCTA and invasive FFR assessment of all major coronary vessels irrespective of CCTA findings. The real-world cohort included 202 patients with at least one CCTA-defined lesion &#x2265;70% stenosis who underwent ICA with invasive FFR. **RESULTS:** In the PACIFIC-1 cohort (612 vessels), per-vessel diagnostic accuracy was comparable between CT-FFRAI (77%), when using a CCTA based &#x2265;50% stenosis threshold (76%; P = 0.689), and when using a &#x2265;70% stenosis threshold (74%; P = 0.183). In the real-world cohort (325 vessels), CT-FFRAI demonstrated higher per-vessel diagnostic accuracy (63%) compared with CCTA using &#x2265;50% and &#x2265;70% stenosis thresholds (52% and 57%, respectively; P &lt; 0.001 and P = 0.034). Net reclassification improvement for CT-FFRAI beyond CCTA alone using &#x2265;50% and &#x2265;70% stenosis thresholds was 7.9% (P = 0.256) and 17.7% (P = 0.012) in the PACIFIC-1 cohort, compared with 23.2% (P &lt; 0.001) and 13.6% (P = 0.070) in the real-world cohort, respectively. **CONCLUSIONS:** CT-FFRAI may outperform anatomic assessment alone, with diagnostic accuracy depending on the prevalence of obstructive coronary artery disease and the range of stenosis severities among vessels undergoing invasive FFR assessment.
