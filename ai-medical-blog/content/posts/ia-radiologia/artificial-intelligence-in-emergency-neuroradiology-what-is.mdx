## Resumen

Artificial intelligence (AI) in emergency neuroradiology is currently used mainly for worklist prioritization and selected detection tasks rather than autonomous diagnosis. The best current evidence for clinical utility in detection is in intracranial hemorrhage and acute ischemic stroke, especially large vessel occlusion, with additional emerging use in cervical/thoracolumbar fracture detection and incidental extracranial findings on computed tomography angiography head/neck. Across these areas, AI generally shows good diagnostic performance and can improve workflow metrics. However, evidence that AI improves hard patient outcomes, particularly mortality, disability, or long-term functional status remains limited, inconsistent, and largely observational rather than prospective.
