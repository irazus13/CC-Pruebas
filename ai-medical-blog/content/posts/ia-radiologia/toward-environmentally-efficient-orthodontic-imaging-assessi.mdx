## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** This study evaluates the impact of various JPEG compression levels (1.0, 0.8, 0.5, and 0.3) on the stability and accuracy of cephalometric analyses performed by a manual expert and three commercial AI (artificial intelligence) software packages (CranioCatch, CEPHX, and WEBCEPH). **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** Forty digital lateral cephalometric radiographs were compressed into four quality levels using a standardized web application (ImageMagick v7.1.0). A total of 10 measurements, categorized into skeletal, dental, and soft-tissue parameters, were analyzed. Measurement stability was assessed using the Friedman test, while agreement with the manual reference standard was evaluated using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) and mean absolute error. **RESULTS:** Stability was maintained across most parameters up to the 0.3 compression level (70% quality reduction) (P &gt; .05). Significant discrepancies were found between AI engines relative to the manual standard. The highest variation occurred in the Nasolabial angle, with a 20.95&#xb0; difference between the manual expert and WEBCEPH. Baseline agreement with the manual standard ranged from low to excellent across parameters (ICC 0.50-0.96), with the highest agreement for interincisal angle and U1/SN, and the lowest for SNA, nasolabial angle, and U1 to NA (mm). While skeletal parameters showed high inter-software consistency, soft-tissue and dental inclination assessments revealed a significant lack of standardization. **CONCLUSION:** High JPEG compression (down to 0.3) produced no clinically meaningful measurement changes in high-quality radiographs, providing a 70%-80% file size reduction supporting "Green Radiology" goals. However, baseline diagnostic variations between AI platforms necessitate consistent single-software use for longitudinal tracking.
