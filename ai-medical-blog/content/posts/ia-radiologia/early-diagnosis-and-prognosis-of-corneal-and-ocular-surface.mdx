## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** This study aims to elucidate the expanding role of artificial intelligence (AI) in the early diagnosis of corneal and ocular surface diseases. AI has the potential to enhance diagnostic precision, streamline clinical workflows, and facilitate disease detection before clinical manifestations become evident. **METHODS:** A narrative literature review was conducted to assess the impact of AI and machine learning in corneal disease preclinical diagnosis. A comprehensive search in PubMed was performed using key terms such as "artificial intelligence," "machine learning," "deep learning," "corneal diseases," and "early diagnosis." Eligible studies were those published in English over the last decade that focused on AI-driven diagnostic approaches and prognostic modeling. Case reports, nonoriginal studies, and those with minimal AI integration were excluded. Data extraction emphasized AI methodologies, imaging techniques, diagnostic performance metrics, and clinical relevance. **RESULTS:** AI-powered machine learning models analyze vast imaging datasets, detecting subclinical abnormalities imperceptible to conventional diagnostic methods. This capability is particularly significant in early keratoconus detection, dystrophies, infectious keratitis, conjunctival neoplasia, and other corneal pathologies such as dry eye or Steven-Johnson disease, where subtle morphological changes precede clinically apparent disease. **CONCLUSIONS:** The integration of AI into corneal disease diagnostics and management could mark in the future a transformative shift in Ophthalmology, offering precision, efficiency, and scalability. By facilitating early disease detection and individualized patient assessments, AI minimizes diagnostic inconsistencies and accelerates clinical decision making. Continuous algorithm refinement and real-world validation are crucial to maximizing AI's clinical utility. To fully harness AI's potential, multidisciplinary collaboration among ophthalmologists, data scientists, and regulatory bodies is imperative to address standardization, ethical concerns, and implementation barriers. As AI-driven technologies continue to evolve, they will redefine the standard of care in corneal disease diagnosis, therapeutic precision, and patient outcomes.
