## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** Dental imaging is routinely based on two-dimensional radiographs. There is increasing use of 3D imaging, mostly cone-beam CT and computed tomography accompanied by increasing radiation dose. Recent developments tailor radiation-free 0.55T magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) in combination with dedicated coil systems and MRI sequences to the teeth and jaw. We aim to describe the usage of this new technical development on several clinical cases. **METHODS:** We use a 0.55T MRI equipped with a 7-channel dental coil and dedicated MRI sequences for the teeth and the jaws. We focus on the technical feasibility of dental-dedicated MRI (ddMRI) for the diagnosis of jawbone cysts, dental trauma, and periapical inflammation. Furthermore, we illustrate its application in surgical planning of third molar removal, dental implant placement, and the effect of AI-based acceleration. **RESULTS:** The cases illustrate visual delineation of odontogenic cysts. Dental trauma patients may benefit from the delineation of inflammatory apical edema. Also, ddMRI is suitable for surgical planning, including third molar removal and dental implant placement, in a first case. Finally, artificial-intelligence-based acceleration algorithms can help reduce scan time by more than 50%. **CONCLUSIONS:** ddMRI with dedicated sequences is a promising technical development for the aforementioned indications. Further prospective studies are needed for validation.
