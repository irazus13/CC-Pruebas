## Resumen

Artificial intelligence (AI) is rapidly transforming radiology research and is progressively influencing the scholarly publication process itself. Large language models (LLMs) are being increasingly used for manuscript writing. At the other end of the spectrum, AI can assist with manuscript screening, statistical and methodological assessment, language editing, reference checking, reviewer selection, and the generation of peer-review comments. LLMs have the potential to improve the efficiency of journal review by reducing repetitive tasks and helping reviewers identify reporting deficiencies. However, their integration also introduces substantial concerns regarding confidentiality, hallucination, bias, reproducibility, authorship, accountability, and manipulation of the peer-review process. In this article, the authors examine the potential benefits and limitations of AI-assisted peer review in radiology journals and propose a framework in which AI serves as a controlled decision-support tool rather than an autonomous reviewer. Human reviewers and editors should retain responsibility for scientific judgment, confidentiality, and final editorial decisions. This approach harnesses AI's efficiency while maintaining accountability.
