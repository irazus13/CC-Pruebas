## Resumen

To assess whether native-resolution cascaded refinement improves the reliability of automated lower-limb landmark localization beyond mean error. This retrospective study included 965 full-length lower-limb radiographs from distinct Osteoarthritis Initiative participants (675 training, 144 validation, 146 internal evaluation). Sixteen landmarks were localized by 512&#x2009;&#xd7;&#x2009;512 heatmap regression and refined within 256&#x2009;&#xd7;&#x2009;256 native-resolution regions. Analyses covered landmark-specific errors, alignment measurements, cross-dataset and prosthesis stress tests, image perturbations, and failure recognition using 12 inference-only features. Paired bootstrap intervals retained within-radiograph clustering. Evaluation used reference-informed image preparation and documented image-coordinate scales. Refinement reduced mean radial error from 2.433 to 1.987 image-grid mm. Cases with mean error greater than 3&#xa0;mm decreased from 13.0% (19/146) to 3.4% (5/146), and landmarks within 3&#xa0;mm increased from 71.5% (1671/2336) to 80.4% (1877/2336). Lateral femoral condyles accounted for 40/106 residual landmark errors above 5&#xa0;mm. Mean absolute mechanical lateral distal femoral and medial proximal tibial angle errors decreased by 0.620&#xb0; and 0.276&#xb0;, respectively; change intervals for hip-knee-ankle angle and mechanical axis deviation included zero. The risk model improved failure ranking during validation but did not outperform ensemble disagreement on internal evaluation. Native-resolution cascaded refinement improved tail reliability, with measurement benefits concentrated in joint-orientation angles. Inference-time features did not provide a generalizable failure-warning mechanism.
