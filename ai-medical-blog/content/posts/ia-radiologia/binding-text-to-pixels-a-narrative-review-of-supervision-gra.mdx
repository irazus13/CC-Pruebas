## Resumen

Chest radiographs come with abundant free-text reports but scarce lesion-level annotation, so report text increasingly supervises localization. This structured narrative review, written for model developers, benchmark curators, and evaluators of clinical AI, covers phrase grounding, weakly supervised localization, language-guided segmentation, grounded report generation, and multimodal large language models to September 2026. We propose a five-level granularity ladder, organizing the field by where text binds to the image, not by architecture, as an explanatory framework and testable hypothesis, and show that separating train-time binding from inference-time output exposes the most informative systems. A resource audit classifying each corpus's spatial signal as reader-drawn, image-derived, or report-derived reveals a structural gap: at MIMIC-CXR scale, lesion masks exist only when generated from the reports that supervise the models, and independent reader-drawn masks for multiple lesion types cover 234-902 images, roughly 400 to 1600 times fewer. On the shared MS-CXR benchmark, three studies report one model at 21.5 to 26.6 mean intersection over union, a spread exceeding most between-method differences. Grounded outputs are intended to make AI assertions checkable at the point of care, though prospective evidence is lacking. We specify an anatomy-referenced evaluation, propose federated subgroup evaluation, and recommend reserving reader-drawn masks for evaluation.
