## Resumen

Head and neck tumors arise in anatomically complex regions where MRI plays a central role in defining tumor extent, deep invasion, perineural spread, skull-base involvement, nodal disease, and post-treatment changes. Although conventional MRI remains the foundation of routine assessment, recent advances in acquisition, reconstruction, and quantitative imaging have expanded its clinical and biological capabilities. This review summarizes current developments in advanced MRI for head and neck tumors, with emphasis on both image-quality improvement and quantitative tumor characterization. Motion-robust acquisition, distortion reduction, high-resolution imaging, and artificial intelligence (AI)/deep-learning-based (DL) reconstruction can improve lesion conspicuity, anatomical sharpness, geometric fidelity, and scan efficiency, thereby strengthening morphology-based assessment. Among quantitative techniques, diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) analysis are the most established, whereas advanced diffusion models, perfusion imaging, synthetic quantitative mapping, and molecular imaging techniques such as chemical exchange saturation transfer (CEST) remain at varying stages of clinical validation. These approaches may provide complementary information on tumor microstructure, perfusion, tissue composition, metabolism, heterogeneity, treatment response, and prognosis. Overall, advanced MRI currently appears most valuable for refining anatomical interpretation and adding selectively validated quantitative or functional information rather than replacing morphology-based assessment. Further standardization, reproducibility assessment, multicenter validation, and demonstration of incremental clinical value will be required before many emerging biomarkers can be incorporated into routine decision-making.
