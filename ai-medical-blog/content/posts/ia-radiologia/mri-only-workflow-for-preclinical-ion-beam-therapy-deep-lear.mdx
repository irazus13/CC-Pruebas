## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** To develop and validate a deep-learning-based magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)-only workflow for preclinical mouse brain irradiations with ion beams and to compare with a rigidly aligned population-averaged CTs (sCTsavg). **METHODS:** A 3D U-Net was trained on 69 paired ultra-high-field (15.2T) T1-weighted MRIs and micro-CTs (&#xb5;CTs) from 17 BALB/c mice. Two BALB/c and two C57BL/6 mice were used for independent testing. Two training losses were compared and selected based on cross-validation. Quantitative performance was assessed using mean absolute error (MAE), structural similarity index (SSIM), and bone Dice similarity coefficient (DSC). Treatment plans with X-rays, protons, and carbon ions were analyzed using dose-volume parameters and 3D gamma analysis of the physical dose distribution. **RESULTS:** For image generation, the intra-strain test set reached an MAE of (138&#xb1;10)HU and a bone DSC of 0.75-0.80, slightly below the sCTavg baseline (0.79-0.80). On the inter-strain test set (C57BL/6), the results were inferior (DSC: 0.59--0.64), while the sCTavg retained a DSC of 0.70-0.77. The dose distributions were comparable between plans calculated on the U-Net-based synthetic CT (sCTUNet) and on the &#xb5;CT. The largest deviation occurred in the near-maximum bone dose (D2%) with 0.16Gy for both, protons and for carbon ions. **CONCLUSIONS:** This study demonstrated the feasibility of MRI-only treatment planning for preclinical brain irradiation using a U-Net-based sCT generator, reducing the need for dedicated &#xb5;CT acquisitions. However, limited training data due to ethical constraints challenges cross-cohort generalization, while a rigidly aligned sCTavg provided competitive performance but requires an additional registration step.
