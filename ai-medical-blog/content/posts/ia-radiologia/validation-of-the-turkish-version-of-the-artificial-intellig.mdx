## Resumen

BackgroundIn the context of the growing integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into radiology, AI-assisted systems are increasingly presented as tools for improving diagnostic accuracy, workflow efficiency, and clinical decision-making. Yet discussions of AI in radiology often focus on technical performance or clinician perspectives, while patients' attitudes toward these technologies remain less well understood.ObjectiveThis study aimed to cross-culturally adapt the Artificial Intelligence Attitude in Radiology Scale (AIARS) for patients into Turkish and evaluate the psychometric properties of the modified Turkish version among patients receiving radiology services.MethodsThis cross-sectional validation study included 659 adult patients aged 18-69 years (Mage = 34.50, SD&#x2009;=&#x2009;11.21). The factor structure was examined using exploratory factor analysis (EFA) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA). Measurement invariance was tested across age groups, gender, and educational level using multi-group CFA.ResultsEFA supported a modified four-factor, 23-item structure derived from the original 39-item, five-factor AIARS. CFA confirmed that the four-factor model was a good fit for the data, &#x3c7;2(224)&#x2009;=&#x2009;640.16, p&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;.001, CFI = .985, TLI = .983, RMSEA = .075, 90% CI [.068, .082], SRMR = .072. The total scale demonstrated high internal consistency, with &#x3b1;&#x2009;=&#x2009;.87 and &#x3c9;&#x2009;=&#x2009;.88. Test-retest reliability was excellent (total-scale ICC = .97, 95% CI [.94, .99]). Measurement invariance was supported across age, gender, and educational level.ConclusionThe modified Turkish version of the AIARS demonstrated acceptable psychometric performance at the total-scale level and may be used to assess patients' attitudes toward AI in radiology. However, the Worries and Ethical Concerns factor requires further evaluation.
