## Resumen

**BACKGROUND:** The Trial of Org 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment (TOAST) classification remains the dominant framework for ischaemic stroke aetiology, yet inter-observer agreement rarely exceeds &#x3ba; 0.6. Deep learning algorithms trained on diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) have shown retrospective promise, but prospective multicentre validation is lacking. **METHODS:** We conducted a prospective diagnostic accuracy study across nine stroke centres in Korea. Consecutive patients with acute ischaemic stroke who underwent DWI within seven days of onset were enrolled between May and September 2023. A commercially deployed convolutional neural network classified each case into four modified TOAST categories (large-artery atherosclerosis [LAA], small-vessel occlusion [SVO], cardioembolism [CE], and other/undetermined) and generated a continuous CE probability score. The reference standard was site-specific expert adjudication based on the complete diagnostic workup. **RESULTS:** Among 617 patients (mean age 68.9 years, 62.2% male), overall agreement was 75.7% (Cohen's &#x3ba; 0.643, 95% CI 0.594-0.691). CE sensitivity reached 95.5% and SVO sensitivity 90.6%. Among 426 patients without prior atrial fibrillation (AF), the CE probability score predicted newly detected AF with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.722 (95% CI 0.634-0.806). The highest-risk quintile showed an AF rate of 17.1% (number needed to screen [NNS] 5.9) versus 4.9% (NNS 20.2) in the remaining quintiles. **CONCLUSIONS:** A DWI-based deep learning algorithm achieved substantial agreement with expert stroke neurologists for stroke classification. The CE probability score predicted occult AF from a single DWI sequence. This exploratory finding warrants confirmation in adequately powered prospective studies before clinical implementation.
