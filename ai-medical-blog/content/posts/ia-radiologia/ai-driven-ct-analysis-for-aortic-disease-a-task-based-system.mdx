## Resumen

Background/Objectives: Computed tomography (CT) and CT angiography (CTA) are central to assessing aortic aneurysm and dissection, but manual segmentation and geometric analysis remain time-consuming and operator-dependent, while evidence on artificial intelligence (AI) is fragmented. This task-based systematic review synthesised AI applications to CT/CTA of the thoracic and abdominal aorta by primary task: segmentation, measurement, or detection and classification. Methods: PubMed/MEDLINE and Europe PMC were searched from 2015 to September 2026 following PRISMA 2020; the protocol was registered retrospectively (INPLASY2026110004). Segmentation and measurement studies were appraised with CLAIM and detection studies with QUADAS-2/QUADAS-AI, and results were synthesised without meta-analysis. Results: Of 118 included studies (42 segmentation, 38 measurement, 38 detection), half were published since 2024. Median Dice similarity coefficient was 0.90 or higher for the outer wall, lumen and true and false lumina, but 0.86 for intraluminal thrombus and 0.63 for false lumen thrombus. Automated maximum diameter showed a median absolute bias of 0.26 mm (95% limits of agreement about &#xb1;5 mm). Detection of aortic dissection reached a median AUC of 0.96-0.97, mostly in enriched samples. External validation was reported in 14%, 34% and 55% of segmentation, measurement and detection studies, 33 of 38 detection studies were at high risk of bias, and only two systems were evaluated prospectively. Conclusions: AI supports technical automation of aortic CT/CTA segmentation and measurement and is progressing toward multistructure geometric phenotyping and clinical decision support, but independent multicentre validation, consecutive cohorts and prospective reader-in-the-loop studies are required before clinical integration. Detection/classification tools require independent diagnostic and workflow validation.
