## Resumen

Cardiovascular disease remains a leading cause of death and disability worldwide. Adipose tissue dysfunction is a key driver, and perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT) exerts location&#x2011;specific effects on the cardiovascular system. This review focuses on PVAT in atherosclerosis, with particular attention to pericoronary PVAT, epicardial adipose tissue (EAT), carotid PVAT, and peri-aortic adipose tissue (PAAT). PVAT homeostasis relies on UCP1&#x2011;mediated thermogenesis for metabolic modulation and on paracrine signaling via adiponectin and apelin. Under aging, obesity, or diabetes, PVAT switches from an anti&#x2011;inflammatory to a pro&#x2011;inflammatory phenotype, accelerating plaque formation through bidirectional inflammatory crosstalk. For clinical assessment, this review highlights progress in multimodal imaging technologies, including CT fat attenuation index, MRI water&#x2011;lipid separation, PET/CT, and echocardiography, for noninvasive quantification of PVAT density, composition, and metabolic activity, and compares their advantages and limitations. This review further discusses artificial intelligence (AI)-driven radiomic and radiotranscriptomic approaches that extend PVAT assessment beyond conventional single-parameter metrics. We systematically review the research progress of these techniques in evaluating dynamic PVAT changes and their correlation with atherosclerosis, aiming to clarify the intrinsic correlation between imaging&#x2011;derived PVAT functional status and AS development.
