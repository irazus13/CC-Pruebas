## Resumen

**RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES:** Neurodegenerative Parkinson syndromes show regionally distinct tissue loss involving several brainstem and midbrain structures. Reliable volumetry is constrained by atlas-based segmentation in atrophy-affected brains. We developed a multiclass convolutional neural network (CNN) for the segmentation of 16 brainstem-related regions on routine magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** In this retrospective single-center study, 200 manually annotated MRI datasets of patients with Parkinson's disease, atypical Parkinson syndromes, and healthy controls were used for model development and validation. A Deep Neural Patchwork model was trained on 150 datasets and evaluated in an independent test cohort of 50 participants against atlas-based segmentation using the Dice coefficient, 95% Hausdorff distance, and average symmetric surface distances (ASSD). In an extended validation cohort of 264 participants, CNN-derived regional volumes were analyzed across Parkinson's disease, progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP), and multiple system atrophy (MSA). **RESULTS:** CNN segmentation outperformed atlas-based segmentation across accuracy metrics. Overall medians were 0.75 vs 0.33 for Dice, 2.0 vs 5.0 mm for 95% Hausdorff distance, and 0.30 vs 1.02 mm for ASSD. CNN-derived volumetry recapitulated established disease-associated patterns, including predominant mesencephalic, superior cerebellar peduncle, and red nucleus atrophy in PSP and more marked pontine and middle cerebellar peduncle volume loss in MSA. **CONCLUSION:** The principal contribution of this study is an atrophy-robust multiclass brainstem segmentation framework. This framework may enable anatomically more faithful extraction of quantitative MRI metrics than atlas-based approaches and may reduce contamination from adjacent CSF spaces, particularly in small atrophy-affected structures.
