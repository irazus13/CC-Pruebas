## Resumen

**OBJECTIVE:** To determine whether deep learning can distinguish younger from older adult women on hand radiographs and to identify candidate anatomical regions for age-group discrimination using explainable artificial intelligence and quantitative cortical measurements. **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** This retrospective, single-center, exploratory study included 2,221 women sampled consecutively within each age group during 2020-2023 (1,134 aged 20-45 and 1,087 aged 55-80&#xa0;years at radiography). Patients contributed one posteroanterior radiograph and were assigned to mutually exclusive training, validation, or test sets. Five architectures were evaluated on the same 334 held-out test radiographs (171 younger; 163 older), with performance averaged across five random-seed runs. DenseNet121 was selected by descriptive comparison for gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) and ray-based analyses with retraining. Manual cortical measurements of test radiographs yielded metacarpal and proximal phalangeal indices for candidate rays identified by Grad-CAM. **RESULTS:** DenseNet121 achieved the highest mean area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC, 0.996; 95% CI, 0.992-0.999). Grad-CAM showed prominent activation around the third proximal phalanx, with additional activation in the second and fourth rays. Performance remained high after ray masking; the third-ray-only model achieved the highest mean accuracy among single-ray models. The third proximal phalangeal index had the highest AUC among the six indices (0.956; 95% CI, 0.937-0.976). **CONCLUSION:** Grad-CAM, ray-based analyses, and quantitative cortical measurements support the third proximal phalanx as an anatomically interpretable candidate region for age-group discrimination in adult women. Integrating explainable artificial intelligence with quantitative radiographic assessment provides an exploratory workflow for investigating hand skeletal aging, with potential relevance to broader musculoskeletal imaging research.
