## Resumen

AI methods are used in proteolysis-targeting chimera (PROTAC) discovery for candidate generation, ternary-complex modelling and property prediction. These tasks are organised here into Designer, Connector and Predictor roles to examine how computational outputs can inform experimental decisions in cancer drug discovery. Published studies connect selected computationally generated compounds to biological testing and show how new measurements can inform chemical optimisation and property-model refinement. Case studies of linker modifications and PKMYT1 stereoisomers illustrate discrepancies between binding and cellular degradation. Further preclinical and clinical findings show why target depletion must be interpreted alongside pathway suppression, tissue exposure and tolerability. The analysis identifies measurements that could distinguish competing explanations and guide changes to candidate chemistry, structural hypotheses, prediction models or experimental conditions. Predictive performance depends on chemical and biological context, so validation needs to reflect the compounds, targets and endpoints encountered in the intended application. Exposure-response models can help interpret pharmacological findings when supported by relevant tissue and pathway measurements. Clinical outcomes are also distinguished from evidence of AI involvement in individual candidates. Current evidence supports specific connections between computation and experiment. Prospective comparisons are needed to determine whether coordinating these methods improves candidate selection and pharmacological outcomes.
