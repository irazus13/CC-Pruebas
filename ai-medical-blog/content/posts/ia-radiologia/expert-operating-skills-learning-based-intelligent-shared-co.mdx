## Resumen

Accurate and minimally invasive percutaneous biopsy is critical for early lung cancer diagnosis, yet conventional manual procedures suffer from repeated radiation exposure, high reliance on doctor expertise, and risk of tissue injury. This study presents a 5 degree-of-freedom robotic system combined with an intelligent shared control framework guided by expert skills learning. Expert skills were modeled using a variational autoencoder-hidden Markov model to segment latent procedural phases, and dynamic motion primitives reconstructed representative expert trajectories. These learned skills were integrated into a phase-adaptive shared controller, enabling online modulation of impedance and trajectory parameters. Both phantom and in vivo porcine experiments validated the system's feasibility, safety, and accuracy, with animal experiment studies confirming minimal tissue trauma and stable physiological responses. Compared with manual, leader-follower, and admittance control methods, the intelligent shared control substantially reduced force fluctuations and trajectory deviations.
