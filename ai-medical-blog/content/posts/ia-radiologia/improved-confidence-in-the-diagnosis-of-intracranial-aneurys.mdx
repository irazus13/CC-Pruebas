## Resumen

**PURPOSE:** Conventional MR angiography (MRA) may yield indeterminate findings when evaluating small suspected aneurysmal lesions and anatomical variants, such as infundibular dilations (IDs). This study aimed to compare lesion classification and diagnostic confidence between high-resolution MRA with 0.4&#x2009;mm isotropic voxels reconstructed using deep learning-based reconstruction (DLR) and conventional MRA (C-MRA). **METHODS:** This study included 79 patients who underwent both C-MRA and high-resolution DLR MRA, with 107 suspected aneurysmal lesions. Two neuroradiologists categorized the lesions into 4 groups: definite aneurysm (dAN), probable aneurysm (pAN), definite ID (dID), and probable ID (pID). **RESULTS:** On C-MRA, the 107 lesions were classified as 78 dANs, 11 pANs, 5 dIDs, and 13 pIDs. On high-resolution DLR MRA, 5 lesions were reclassified as normal vascular structures. The remaining 102 lesions were reclassified as 78 dANs, 1 pAN, and 23 dIDs. Probable lesions decreased from 24 to 1, whereas definite lesions increased from 83 to 101. McNemar's test revealed a statistically significant shift from the aneurysmal group to the non-aneurysmal group between the 2 methods (P&#x2009;&lt;&#x2009;0.01). Interobserver agreement (Cohen's kappa) was 0.72 for C-MRA and 0.88 for high-resolution DLR MRA. **CONCLUSION:** High-resolution MRA with DLR improved visualization of microvascular structures and shifted most indeterminate lesions to higher-confidence categories, suggesting its potential utility in differentiating aneurysms from IDs.
