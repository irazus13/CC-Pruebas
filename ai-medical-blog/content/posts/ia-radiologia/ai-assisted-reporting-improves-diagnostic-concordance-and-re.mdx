## Resumen

**AIMS:** To evaluate whether Artificial Intelligence (AI)-integrated reporting improves diagnostic concordance, fracture detection performance, and report turnaround time compared with conventional interpretation and standalone AI in patients undergoing Computed Tomography (CT) for rib fracture assessment, thereby clarifying the clinical value of AI-assisted workflow integration in routine trauma radiology practice. **METHODS:** This retrospective study included 410 consecutive chest CT examinations from patients with rib fractures confirmed by an expert-consensus reference standard. Pre-AI cases were assigned to the conventional reporting group (Group III, n = 150), post-implementation cases to the AI-assisted group (Group I, n = 260), and all examinations were additionally processed by the AI model for standalone evaluation (Group II). Diagnostic performance and reporting efficiency were assessed against a senior-radiologist consensus reference standard. Primary outcomes focused on concordance and turnaround time; secondary outcomes included fracture detection yield, morphology-specific sensitivity, localization concordance, report quality, and subgroup performance. **RESULTS:** Compared with conventional reporting, AI-assisted reporting detected a significantly higher mean number of fractures per patient (P = 0.029). Reporting efficiency was significantly improved, with shorter mean and median turnaround times (both P &lt; 0.001), a higher proportion of reports completed within 30 minutes (P &lt; 0.001), and shorter time to first fracture identification (P &lt; 0.001). AI-assisted reporting also demonstrated better concordance with expert consensus (P &lt; 0.001), with a higher rate of complete concordance (84.6% vs. 66.0%) and fewer major discrepancies (3.8% vs. 12.0%), together with superior laterality-, rib-, and segment-level concordance (P = 0.007, P = 0.001, and P = 0.001, respectively). Report completeness and linguistic quality were also significantly improved with AI integration (all P &lt; 0.001). Across fracture types, AI-assisted interpretation consistently showed the highest agreement with the reference standard (&#x3ba; = 0.89-0.94), outperforming both standalone AI (&#x3ba; = 0.76-0.83) and manual interpretation (&#x3ba; = 0.45-0.71). **CONCLUSION:** AI-assisted rib fracture reporting improved diagnostic agreement and reduced turnaround time, supporting the clinical value of AI-assisted reporting in routine trauma CT interpretation.
