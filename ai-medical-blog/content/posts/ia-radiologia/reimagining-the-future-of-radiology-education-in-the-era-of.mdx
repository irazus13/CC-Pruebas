## Resumen

Rising clinical workloads and productivity pressures are reducing faculty time for teaching, mentorship, and educational innovation in radiology. This Perspective proposes that clinical efficiency gains from artificial intelligence (AI) be strategically reinvested in education through two complementary concepts. The educational dividend involves intentionally reinvesting a portion of time savings in teaching and mentorship, curricular innovation and scholarship, and faculty development. The educational multiplier describes how AI can extend the reach and impact across five domains: content creation, cognitive and perceptual learning, learning embedded in the clinical workflow, continuous assessment and feedback, and educational analytics. Neither the dividend nor the multiplier will be realized automatically; both require deliberate, sustained institutional commitment to strengthen radiology education.
