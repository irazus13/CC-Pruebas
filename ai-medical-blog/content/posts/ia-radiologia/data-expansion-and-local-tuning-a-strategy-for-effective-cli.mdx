## Resumen

BackgroundThe clinical performance of pulmonary nodule screening systems may be influenced by training data size and differences in imaging characteristics across institutions. Improving model performance and cross-center adaptability is important for clinical deployment.ObjectiveTo investigate the effects of data expansion and localized fine-tuning on a pulmonary nodule screening system (PNSS) and evaluate its clinical utility for pulmonary nodule detection and classification.MethodsLung CT scans from Weifang People's Hospital and Shandong Provincial Hospital were retrospectively analyzed to assess PNSS performance with different training dataset sizes. Localized fine-tuning was further applied to improve cross-center adaptability. A prospective study at Weifang People's Hospital compared PNSS with experienced radiologists in routine clinical practice.ResultsData expansion increased detection sensitivity from 0.7703 to 0.9428 and classification accuracy from 0.7029 to 0.9486, with performance gains gradually approaching saturation. Localized fine-tuning improved cross-center diagnostic accuracy from 53.57% to 85.36% and sensitivity from 0.5495 to 0.8736. In the prospective evaluation, PNSS achieved a sensitivity of 0.9302 for malignant nodules, 13.95 percentage points higher than radiologists. Overall diagnostic accuracy was 0.92 compared with 0.85 for radiologists. PNSS required only 34&#x2005;s per case, nearly eight times faster than radiologist review.ConclusionData expansion and localized fine-tuning significantly improved PNSS performance, adaptability, and efficiency, supporting its clinical application in pulmonary nodule screening and early lung cancer detection.
