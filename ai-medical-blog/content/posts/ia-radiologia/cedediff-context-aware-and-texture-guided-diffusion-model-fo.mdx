## Resumen

Low-dose CT (LDCT) significantly reduces patient radiation exposure but introduces substantial noise and artifacts. LDCT denoising aims to achieve image quality matching normal-dose CT (NDCT). Although deep learning methods have achieved promising results in LDCT denoising, they often suffer from over-smoothing and loss of fine structural details. Diffusion models have emerged as a powerful class of generative models, demonstrating a remarkable ability to capture complex data distributions and generate high-quality images. In this paper, we propose a contextual edge-enhanced diffusion model (CedeDiff) for LDCT denoising. First, we introduce a stochastic perturbation strategy into the degradation operator. Compared with conventional deterministic degradation, the proposed stochastic degradation strategy better captures the complexity and variability of noise patterns in LDCT images. This design makes the simulated degradation more realistic during training and improves the robustness and generalization of the model. Second, we propose a texture-conditioned diffusion model that allows the denoising network to exploit texture and structural information to guide restoration. Third, to further strengthen guidance during sampling, we introduce disentangled classifier-free guidance, which amplifies the influence of the conditional priors and yields more accurate restoration. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art LDCT denoising methods in both quantitative metrics and visual quality, with superior noise suppression and detail preservation.
