## Resumen

Sludge moisture content (MC) is a key parameter affecting sludge dewatering, drying, transportation, disposal, and resource recovery. Its rapid, accurate, and continuous determination is important for optimizing sludge treatment processes. Conventional oven drying and thermogravimetric analysis can provide relatively reliable reference values, but their long measurement time, delayed response, and poor compatibility with online regulation limit their application in dynamic operating conditions. In recent years, electrical-property detection, electromagnetic-wave detection, optical detection, nuclear magnetic resonance, and radiographic techniques have been increasingly investigated for sludge moisture characterization, offering potential routes for non-destructive, near-real-time, and multi-scale monitoring. However, sewage sludge is a complex heterogeneous medium composed of organic flocs, inorganic particles, extracellular polymeric substances, pore water, bound water, dissolved salts, and gas phases. Therefore, the measured signal is rarely governed by moisture alone, but is also affected by organic composition, physical structure, electrical conductivity, and temperature. The fundamental challenge is therefore not simply detecting water but reliably identifying moisture-related information from complex and evolving sludge responses. This review critically evaluates the current development of sludge MC detection methods, within a cross-method analytical framework linking sensing mechanisms, signal interpretation, management of non-MC-related signal contributions, and engineering translation, analyzes the major factors governing moisture identifiability across sensing modalities, and discusses strategies for selectively managing non-MC-related signal contributions, including measurement standardization, auxiliary-variable compensation, calibration adaptation, and coupled information exploitation. The role of machine learning as a data-processing approach for extracting informative features, improving prediction robustness, and supporting engineering applications is also reviewed. Finally, online monitoring scenarios, dynamic process control, engineering translation bottlenecks, long-term reliability, and future research directions are discussed, with the aim of guiding the development of sludge moisture sensing systems that move beyond empirical calibration toward physically interpretable, transferable, and decision-relevant monitoring of evolving sludge states.
