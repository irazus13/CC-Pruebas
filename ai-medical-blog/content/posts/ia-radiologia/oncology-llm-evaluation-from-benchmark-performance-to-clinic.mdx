## Resumen

Large language models (LLMs) perform increasingly well on medical benchmarks, yet benchmark success does not necessarily establish clinical readiness in oncology. Building on recent critiques of medical LLM evaluation, this Perspective identifies four benchmark-practice mismatches: complete-information benchmarks versus active information gathering, single reference answers versus preference-sensitive decisions, cross-sectional snapshots versus longitudinal disease trajectories, and standalone model performance versus model-in-system readiness. To address these gaps, we propose a four-stage evaluation paradigm progressing from a structured knowledge and reasoning baseline to interactive and dialogue-based evaluation, longitudinal trajectory assessment, and model-in-system evaluation. Grounded in the longitudinal, multimodal, and multidisciplinary nature of oncology care, the framework emphasizes the need to calibrate evaluation to intended use and clinical risk and to sustain assessment through continuous monitoring across the model life cycle. Together, these principles offer an operational and oncology-specific pathway for building progressively stronger evidence of clinical readiness.
