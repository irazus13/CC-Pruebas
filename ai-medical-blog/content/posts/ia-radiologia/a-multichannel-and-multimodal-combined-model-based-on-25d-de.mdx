## Resumen

**RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES:** Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for 90% of primary liver cancers and ranks as the fourth leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide. This study aimed to promptly identify high-risk patients for early recurrence after liver transplantation (LT), thereby optimizing personalized follow-up and intervention strategies for patients with HCC. **MATERIALS AND METHODS:** A retrospective cohort of 143 patients with HCC was selected. Following the collection of imaging investigations, clinical data, and postoperative follow-up information, we developed a combined model based on 2.5-dimensional (2.5D) deep learning (DL). This model integrates clinical features, radiomics characteristics, and DL data across multiple modalities, utilizing multichannel 2.5D data comprising coronal, sagittal, and peritumoral slices of the largest tumor area. Model performance was assessed through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA). **RESULTS:** The 2.5D DL model demonstrated superior performance over the 2D DL model, achieving area under the ROC curve (AUC) values of 0.784/0.842, 0.799/0.843, and 0.824/0.827 in the training and testing cohorts, compared to the 2D model's 0.736/0.819, 0.751/0.818, and 0.724/0.803. The combined model significantly surpassed other models, attaining C-index values of 0.789/0.826 in the training and testing cohorts, along with 1-year, 2-year, and 3-year AUC values of 0.842/0.931, 0.865/0.891, and 0.853/0.885, respectively. Furthermore, the combined model demonstrated the highest consistency in calibration curves and provided the greatest clinical net benefit in DCA. **CONCLUSION:** The combined model based on 2.5D DL demonstrates robust applicability and high predictive performance in identifying early postoperative recurrence of HCC. This strategy facilitates proactive and individualized intervention for high-risk patients, thereby improving clinical outcomes.
