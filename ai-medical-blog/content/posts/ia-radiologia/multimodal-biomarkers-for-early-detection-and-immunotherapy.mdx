## Resumen

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) commonly develops in chronically diseased liver and remains challenging because of late detection, heterogeneity, recurrence and limited hepatic reserve. Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-based combinations improve outcomes in advanced HCC, but durable responses occur in only a subset of patients. This structured narrative review evaluates biomarkers by intended use: surveillance and early detection, pretreatment prognostic stratification, treatment selection, on-treatment monitoring, neoadjuvant response assessment and postoperative minimal residual disease (MRD) detection. Serum alpha-fetoprotein and clinical scores are accessible but largely reflect tumor burden, inflammation or liver function. Cell-free DNA methylation and fragmentomics provide early-detection and tissue-of-origin signals, while circulating tumor DNA tracks molecular burden and clonal evolution. Tissue and spatial profiling clarify immune responsiveness and resistance, whereas radiomics and deep learning noninvasively assess whole-tumor and peritumoral heterogeneity. These modalities are complementary but require distinct evidence standards. Most early-detection studies use case-control or enriched cohorts, and most immunotherapy associations are retrospective, prognostic or monitoring-related. No biomarker yet supports routine selection among ICI regimens. Unlike modality-specific summaries, this review aligns each biomarker with a defined clinical decision and evidentiary category and proposes a longitudinal framework integrating baseline blood, imaging and tissue features with on-treatment dynamics, preoperative reassessment and postoperative molecular surveillance. Clinical translation requires standardized assays, representative multicenter cohorts, locked external validation and biomarker-guided trials demonstrating value beyond established criteria.
